Executive Summary

The Laboratory for Incentivization, Coordination and Cooperation (LICC) is proposed as a joint research institution. Its sponsors would be the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and the Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI). Coordination science studies how economic agents, institutions, and jurisdictions can be designed for optimal collective outcomes through programmable incentive mechanisms. LICC will develop this emerging field using mathematical and computational methods.

The Opportunity

Cryptographic primitives, programmable compliance infrastructure, and Special Economic Zones used for experimentation now support the development of a new academic discipline. Institutions governing international trade and jurisdictions still use frameworks designed before the digital era. LICC will develop the theory and tools for "programmable institutions" that operate at software speed while preserving the legitimacy and stability of traditional governance.

Why MIT-MBZUAI?

The dual-sponsorship model combines MIT's theoretical expertise in mechanism design, institutional economics, and network science with MBZUAI's applied AI infrastructure. MBZUAI also provides direct routes to government implementation through the UAE's 27 free zones. The MIT-MBZUAI Collaborative Research Program, announced in October 2025, provides an institutional framework that could expand to include LICC's coordination science mandate.

The recommended sequence begins with research inside the MIT-MBZUAI collaboration, then establishes LICC as an independently governed affiliated institute. Selected government partnerships would follow to support implementation research.

Part I: Institutional Context and Rationale

1.1 What is LICC?

LICC is a proposed interdisciplinary research institute for the mathematical, computational, and empirical study of coordination mechanisms. Its scope ranges from individual economic transactions to international institutional design. The research agenda includes:

  • Cryptoeconomic Mechanism Design: Formal methods for designing incentive-compatible protocols that align individual rationality with collective welfare in decentralized systems
  • Spectral Coordination Theory: Mathematical frameworks using spectral graph theory and category theory to analyze multi-agent coordination dynamics
  • Jurisdictional Competition and Decentralization: Empirical and theoretical analysis of how regulatory competition shapes institutional evolution
  • Programmable Compliance Infrastructure: Technical architectures for "Smart Assets" that carry compliance, identity, and operational intelligence across jurisdictions
  • Multi-Scale Coordination Dynamics: Study of how coordination mechanisms interact across individual, organizational, and societal scales

The first programs would study UAE free zone federation coordination, Smart Asset compliance architecture, and jurisdictional competition. Appendix B specifies the work for Years 1-3.

1.2 MIT: Institutional Profile and Research Resources

MIT has expertise in the theoretical foundations LICC requires. MIT's research enterprise totals $2.1 billion annually. In fiscal year 2024, industry-sponsored research totaled $175 million from over 300 companies, approximately 20% of total MIT research expenditures. According to the National Science Foundation, MIT consistently ranks first or second in industry-funded research and development expenditures among universities without a medical school.

Adding LICC to the Collaboration

The MIT-MBZUAI collaboration explicitly addresses "social and ethical responsibilities of computing in shaping global impact". This aligns with LICC's coordination science mandate. The current program emphasizes foundation models, robotics, and healthcare AI. To add coordination science as a fourth research area, LICC must show how it complements these priorities and secure the current program directors' agreement.

1.5 Complementary Strengths and Gaps

The dual-sponsorship structure must account for the expertise, resources, and access each institution provides or lacks.

MIT's Contributions

  • Domain Expertise in Mechanism Design: MBZUAI has virtually no faculty in game theory, mathematical economics, or auction theory. MIT's Daskalakis, Acemoglu, and affiliated researchers lead research in these fields.
  • Network Science and Social Physics: Pentland's Connection Science initiative has no MBZUAI equivalent. Its work is essential to understanding coordination dynamics at scale.
  • Institutional Economics: Acemoglu's Nobel-winning research on how institutions shape prosperity provides the empirical foundation LICC requires.
  • Cryptographic Foundations: Micali's Turing Award work on zero-knowledge proofs and verifiable computation underlies programmable compliance infrastructure.
  • Publication Prestige: MIT publications carry greater weight in economics journals (Econometrica, AER, QJE) than MBZUAI's ML-focused venues (NeurIPS, ICLR, CVPR).

MBZUAI's Contributions

  • Direct Government Channel: MBZUAI's board includes Sultan Ahmed Al Jaber (UAE Minister of Industry) and Khaldoon Al Mubarak (Mubadala CEO). MIT has no equivalent government access.
  • Implementation Laboratory: The UAE's 27 free zones provide sites for testing coordination mechanisms. MIT research remains theoretical without such testing grounds.
  • Sovereign Wealth Funding: MBZUAI's state funding model provides long-term stability that MIT's grant-dependent research cannot match.
  • Applied AI Infrastructure: MBZUAI's Foundation Models Hub and computing infrastructure exceed what MIT can provide for applied coordination research.
  • Speed of Decision-Making: As a young institution, MBZUAI can approve new initiatives faster than MIT's established bureaucracy.

Part II: Dual-Sponsorship Model Analysis

2.1 Structural Options

Three structures could establish LICC within the MIT-MBZUAI framework. Each has advantages and constraints that must be assessed against LICC's long-term research independence requirements.

Option A: Expansion of Existing MIT-MBZUAI Collaboration

Mechanism: Propose LICC as an additional research theme within the MIT-MBZUAI Collaborative Research Program. "Coordination Science" would become a fourth research area alongside scientific discovery, human thriving, and planetary health.

Advantages

Constraints

Uses existing infrastructure and governance mechanisms

Must convince current directors (Isola, Song) to expand scope beyond AI/ML focus

MBZUAI funding already committed under 5-year agreement

May be constrained by existing program priorities and steering committee composition

Steering committee provides a clear decision process

Limited independence for LICC-specific research agenda

Fastest path to initial research funding

Risk of being absorbed into broader AI research portfolio

Assessment: High access to resources, low autonomy. Suitable for the bootstrap phase (Months 1-12), but insufficient for LICC's long-term independence requirements.

Option B: Affiliated Independent Institute (RECOMMENDED)

Mechanism: Establish LICC as a legally distinct research institute with formal affiliation agreements with MIT and MBZUAI. It would govern itself independently and access institutional resources through partnership agreements.

Advantages

Constraints

Full research agenda autonomy protected by affiliation agreement

Must secure separate funding streams beyond MIT-MBZUAI program

Broader hiring flexibility across disciplines (economics, mathematics, law)

Less direct access to MBZUAI computing infrastructure

Allows partnerships with other institutions (Santa Fe Institute, ETH Zürich, etc.)

Requires independent governance structure and administration

Geopolitical insulation through neutral governance

Longer timeline to full operations (18-24 months)

Preserves LICC brand independently of either institution

More complex negotiation with both universities

Assessment: Best suited to LICC's interdisciplinary mandate and long-term independence, and recommended for mature operations. Appendix C sets out the proposed governance.

Option C: Joint Venture with UAE Government

Mechanism: Establish LICC as a trilateral joint venture between MIT, MBZUAI, and UAE government entities (ADGM, Dubai Free Zone Council, or similar). LICC would operate as a quasi-governmental research institution with a direct regulatory pathway.

Advantages

Constraints

Direct regulatory pathway for implementation research

Complex governance structure with multiple stakeholders

Government funding mandate ensures long-term sustainability

Potential for political interference in research agenda

Access to 27 UAE free zones for research and testing

Slower decision-making due to government processes

Maximum legitimacy for policy-relevant research

UAE-specific focus may limit global research applicability

Assessment: Greatest capacity to support implementation, with the most complex structure. Suitable for Phase 2 after proof-of-concept through Options A or B.

2.2 Recommended Hybrid Strategy

The recommended approach combines all three options in phases to launch quickly while preserving long-term independence:

Phase 1 (Months 1-12): Start through Option A. Launch initial research projects within the MIT-MBZUAI program to build faculty relationships and demonstrate research value.

Phase 2 (Months 12-24): Move to Option B. Establish LICC as an affiliated independent institute, maintain the MIT-MBZUAI partnership, and secure independent funding and governance.

Phase 3 (Months 24-48): Adopt selected elements of Option C. Formalize government partnerships for implementation research and expand research to jurisdictions beyond the UAE.

Appendix A: MIT Research Agreement Framework

MIT offers standard templates for research partnerships through OSATT:

  • Master Research Agreement (MRA): Covers multiple projects over several years. Recommended for LICC's ongoing relationship.
  • Individual Research Agreement (IRA): Covers a single research project.
  • Consortium Agreement: Allows multiple industry sponsors to join LICC research programs.
  • Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA): Required for initial discussions involving proprietary research.
  • Data Use Agreement (DUA): Governs data sharing between institutions and implementation partners.

Standard MIT overhead rate: 58% on-campus, 26% off-campus. LICC research conducted primarily at MBZUAI may qualify for reduced rates.

Appendix B: LICC Research Agenda Summary

Initial research programs for Years 1-3:

Program 1: UAE Free Zone Federation Coordination

Develop a mathematical framework for optimal coordination among the UAE's 27 free zones. Design incentive mechanisms that align individual zone policies with federation-wide objectives, and model inter-zone capital flows and regulatory arbitrage dynamics.

Program 2: Smart Asset Compliance Architecture

Formally specify programmable compliance primitives for cross-jurisdictional assets. Develop cryptographic mechanisms for privacy-preserving regulatory reporting and an implementation framework for Mass Protocol integration.

Program 3: Jurisdictional Competition Dynamics

Analyze empirically how regulatory competition affects institutional quality. Model jurisdiction selection by mobile capital and talent using game theory, and assess the implications for optimal regulatory design.

Appendix C: Governance Structure Template

Proposed governance for LICC as an affiliated independent institute:

Board of Directors (7 members):

  • 2 seats: MIT-appointed (faculty representatives)
  • 2 seats: MBZUAI-appointed (including 1 UAE government representative)
  • 1 seat: Momentum-appointed (Founding Sponsor)
  • 2 seats: Independent academics (non-affiliated)

Research Advisory Committee (9 members):

  • External academics with relevant expertise
  • Rotating 3-year terms
  • Veto authority over research agenda changes

Executive Leadership:

  • Founding Director: Appointed by Board, 5-year renewable term
  • Deputy Director (MIT): Based in Cambridge
  • Deputy Director (MBZUAI): Based in Abu Dhabi
  • Chief Operating Officer: Administrative leadership

## E — Recalibrations (the frictionless-migration register)

**E1 — Ch. 1, "The Structural Constraint on Isolated Zones," final paragraph.**
Find: `The key insight is that assets themselves—not just the records of assets—can be programmable and portable across jurisdictional boundaries.`
Replace: `The key insight is that a company's verified standing — its registration, its licences, its compliance history — can be made portable across jurisdictional boundaries, so that proof earned in one jurisdiction is recognized in the next.`

**E2 — Ch. 2, "The Limits of Geographic Monopoly," final paragraph.**
Find: `In the network model, assets can migrate between jurisdictional nodes in hours rather than years. This acceleration of competitive pressure drives jurisdictional quality improvements at software speed rather than diplomatic pace.`
Replace: `In the network model, once two jurisdictions agree to recognize one another's verifications, a company's standing carries between them at machine speed. The agreements themselves remain acts of statecraft, negotiated government to government; what changes is that the years of re-proving which followed every move collapse to the moment of verification. Competitive pressure sharpens accordingly, at the pace governments choose to connect.`

**E3 — Ch. 4, "From Geographic Advantage to Network Position," third paragraph.**
Find: `A company incorporated in one zone can establish presence in other zones instantly through the protocol. If a jurisdiction begins imposing arbitrary restrictions or deteriorating service quality, Smart Assets can migrate automatically to alternative nodes based on pre-defined triggers. This transforms jurisdictional competition from a slow-moving process requiring physical relocation into a dynamic system where capital flows instantly to optimal harbors.`
Replace: `A company incorporated in one zone can establish recognized presence in a connected zone in days, because the verification work travels with it. Jurisdictions that provide excellent service attract and keep companies; jurisdictions that deteriorate watch new formations go elsewhere. The competition runs on service quality, measured and visible, at the speed companies actually decide.`

**E4 — Ch. 5, "The Isolation Trap," fifth paragraph.**
Find: `In the network model, political instability in one jurisdiction triggers automatic asset migration to stable alternatives. This doesn't mean companies flee at first sign of difficulty, but it provides insurance against catastrophic state failure. Jurisdictions know that predatory behavior or collapse will trigger capital flight, creating powerful incentives to maintain stability and rule of law.`
Replace: `In the network model, a company harbored in more than one jurisdiction holds its standing in each of them, so the failure of one government does not erase the company's provable existence. That is insurance against catastrophic state failure — for the company, and for the jurisdiction's own citizens, whose enterprises survive their state's worst moments. Stability becomes an asset a government can offer, and be chosen for.`

**E5 — Ch. 3, "Zones as Jurisdictional Experiments," final paragraph.**
Find: `Smart Assets that operate in one zone can operate in all zones because they carry their governance and compliance with them. Innovations that work in one jurisdiction spread instantly to others through shared protocols. The competitive pressure that drove China's zones improves continuously rather than episodically, because assets can migrate seamlessly to better-performing jurisdictions.`
Replace: `A company that proves itself in one zone carries that proof to every connected zone, because its governance and compliance record travel with it. A policy that works in one jurisdiction can be adopted by another as working configuration rather than as a study tour's notes. The competitive pressure that drove China's zones becomes continuous, because the cost of recognizing a proven company, or adopting a proven policy, falls toward zero.`

**E6 — global terminology sweep across Part I.** Replace every remaining occurrence of `Smart Asset` / `Smart Assets` / `smart assets` with the concrete noun the sentence needs (`a company's recognized standing`, `companies`, `verified records`); replace `smart contract` with `executable rules` or delete; the capitalized product-vocabulary of the October draft does not survive into the book.

## Appendix A: Interpreting the Data

### Global Zone Counts

UNCTAD's *World Investment Report 2019* reported nearly 5,400 zones worldwide and more than 500 additional zones in preparation. This is a dated global inventory. Counts from different years require comparable definitions before they can be treated as a growth series. [UNCTAD, 12 June 2019](https://unctad.org/press-material/global-competition-investment-prompts-surge-special-economic-zones-un-report-finds).

Zone numbers alone do not measure performance. Employment needs a date, geographic boundary, sector, and distinction between direct and indirect jobs. Investment needs a distinction between commitments, expenditure, and assets held. Environmental comparisons need common units and a stated boundary for energy, water, waste, and emissions.

A comparison between an existing zone and the proposed network should separate observed data from targets. Every reported outcome needs a source, period, and method. Chapter 12 sets out the measurement framework for such comparisons.

## Appendix B: Case Study Dossiers

### Mauritius Export Processing Zone

Mauritius established its EPZ framework in 1970, and the first companies began operating in 1971. The regime permitted export manufacturers across the island. It therefore reduced the need to concentrate all participating firms in a newly built enclave. [Statistics Mauritius, 1999, §4.1](https://statsmauritius.govmu.org/Documents/Statistics/Digests/Productivity/Digest_Productivity_Yr99.pdf); [World Bank, 2011, p. 99](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/304221468001788072/pdf/634310PUB0Yes0061512B09780821387450.pdf).

The EPZ Development Authority's historical table records 89,080 workers at December 1988, 89,906 at December 1990, and 90,861 at December 1991. These year-end observations differ from a within-year peak. [EPZ Development Authority historical series](https://training.itcilo.org/actrav_cdrom1/english/global/frame/epzmaur.htm).

The case combines enterprise-based eligibility with market access, investment, and administrative capacity. Its relevance to a connected-zone design is the possibility of giving eligible businesses access to a regime without first requiring a separate physical development.

### China–Africa Economic Cooperation Zones

UNIDO's 2018 case study identifies Jiangsu Qiyuan Group as the owner and manager of Ethiopia's Eastern Industrial Park. Developer interviews reported US$200 million invested by 82 resident companies and approximately 10,000 jobs at the end of 2017. These are resident-company investment and reported employment, rather than developer expenditure or an undated current total. [UNIDO, 2018, pp. 23–24](https://ipp.unido.org/sites/default/files/knowledge/2020-11/Industrial%20park%20development%20in%20Ethiopia%20case%20study%20report.pdf).

The Zambia–China Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone comprises Chambishi and the Lusaka subzone, developed by China Nonferrous Metal Mining Group. A World Bank account records eleven Chambishi enterprises representing US$760 million of investment by July 2009. Its US$1.5 billion figure was a target for annual Chambishi output by 2011. [World Bank, 2011, pp. 73–74](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/752011468203980987/pdf/638440PUB0Exto00Box0361527B0PUBLIC0.pdf).

Lekki's southwest concession covers 3,000 hectares. Its operator, Lekki Free Zone Development Company, is the joint venture of Lekki Worldwide Investments and China Africa Lekki Investments. The wider Lekki development has separately managed quadrants; the Dangote refinery is in the southeast. [LFZDC, 19 May 2015](https://lfzdc.org/signing-of-lfzdc-concession-agreement/); [Lekki Worldwide Investments quadrant directory](https://www.lekkizone.com.ng/quadrants/).

These cases require attention to the unit being measured. A developer, a resident company, a concession, and a wider development area have different accounts and responsibilities. Comparing them requires evidence at the same level, with clear distinctions between plans and completed activity.

### Suzhou Industrial Park

Suzhou Industrial Park began in 1994 as a government-to-government project between China and Singapore. Its 278-square-kilometer administrative area includes an 80-square-kilometer China–Singapore cooperation area. Those are nested geographic scopes. [Singapore Prime Minister's Office, 25 November 2024](https://www.pmo.gov.sg/newsroom/sm-lee-hsien-loong-at-the-suzhou-industrial-park-30th-anniversary-commemorative-event/).

The park reported GDP of RMB368.6 billion in 2023. In November 2024, it reported more than 5,100 foreign-invested projects, including 174 projects backed by 104 Fortune Global 500 companies. Project counts and company counts measure different things. [SIPAC, 24 November 2024](https://www.sipac.gov.cn/szgyyq/yqmtjj/202411/11a6d9ecca854cd4b0cd0019b88610da.shtml); [SIPAC, 28 November 2024](https://www.sipac.gov.cn/szgyyq/dthg202411/202411/296c9a2177b9466a944b65ac8fb74998.shtml).

The partnership offers a case of administrative cooperation and industrial development sustained over decades. Applying its lessons elsewhere requires examining the institutions, commitments, and local conditions that supported it.

## Appendix C: Glossary

**Attestation.** A signed, tamper-evident record of an action, its source, time, rule, and authority. Its integrity is distinct from the truth of every external fact it records.

**Corridor.** An agreement specifying which verifications two jurisdictions recognize, for what uses, and under which conditions. Recognition may be asymmetric and subject to review or withdrawal.

**Recognized standing.** Evidence of an entity's registration, licenses, identity, compliance history, or good standing that an authority is entitled to rely on for a stated purpose.

**Multi-harbored company.** A company holding recognized standing in several jurisdictions, with coordinated records and operations subject to each jurisdiction's applicable law and authority.

**Mass.** The AI-native operating system that runs approved work for legal entities, organizations, and institutions end to end, keeps records current, and exposes permitted state through APIs. Autonomous agents execute the work under human supervision, while licensed institutions retain regulated powers.

**Momentum.** The company that builds the network and organizes the businesses, partnerships, and services needed to develop zones using Mass.

**The five primitives.** Entities, ownership, Fiscal, identity, and consent: the categories used to organize the actors, rights, value, verification, and authorization involved in regulated work.

**LICC.** The Laboratory for Incentivization, Coordination and Cooperation proposed in this essay. Its role is independent research, measurement, and publication under applicable consent and ethics requirements.

**Polycentric governance.** An arrangement in which each authority governs its own responsibilities while coordinating with others through agreed rules.

**Regulatory sandbox.** A bounded framework for testing specified activities under the permissions, limits, supervision, and safeguards established by the competent regulator.

**Network effects.** Benefits that an additional useful connection can provide to participants. Their magnitude depends on demand, recognition, services, and costs.

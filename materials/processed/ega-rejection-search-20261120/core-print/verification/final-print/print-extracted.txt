     Now put the disk in the boundary of a three-dimensional space. An interior insertion and a boundary experiment
can be specified independently. Suppose the interior contains a line ending at the centre of the disk. What is its charge,
how does it act on a second line, and what state does the filling of the disk prepare? A boundary description ought to
predict each answer using operations available on the disk. The comparison has to hold also when two ends come
together. Otherwise two successive calculations of the same measurement could give different answers according to
when the ends were joined.
     This is the reconstruction problem in its simplest form. Given a boundary condition, construct its fields on open
subsets of a curve, their collision operations and the sectors on which they act. Given a second boundary condition and
an interface between the two, ask which sectors cross the interface and which measurements one can recover on the
return journey. Finally cut a filling across that boundary. Does pairing the two independently prepared states reproduce
the amplitude computed before the cut? An affirmative answer would identify far more than a space of charges. It
would identify positions and their collision limits, spaces of maps between sectors, products of lines, and the value of
an experiment with its insertions still present. There is an algebraic reconstruction theorem on a curve. For an
augmented algebra of coefficient complexes on ordered configurations, with scalar coefficient in arity zero, consecutive
cuts recover its multiplication, all its associativity homotopies and the full spaces of maps to other such algebras. There
is also a physical calculation. At level one, a pair of opposite abelian charge lines and an independently sourced
harmonic disk give the same normalized joint Gaussian law for the positive harmonic coordinates on the boundary. Do
all compatible actions on a boundary arise from the physical bulk? Does the agreement of normalized states extend to
the absolute pairing of two fillings? Those questions require further constructions.

Recovering the algebra from cuts
     There is a short reason why putting the cuts back gives the original object. A term in cobar of bar is a word of
words. Flatten it to a string of 𝑟 nonempty pieces. In the associated graded complex each gap is either inside one word
or between two words. The differential which changes that choice gives the two copies of the same coefficient, joined by
the identity, at every gap. For 𝑟 > 1, pair terms at the first gap. They contract in pairs with opposite incidence signs. The
single piece survives. At a configuration of 𝑛 points there can be at most 𝑛 nonempty pieces, so this calculation finishes
in each arity even for a coefficient complex unbounded in both cohomological directions. It is this finiteness which
allows one to say that maps and their homotopies also come back. There is no operation in the argument which assigns
a state space to an arbitrary line or prescribes a value for an independent field at the collision.

## What a contour measures {#opening-contours}

A contour on a surface can measure more than the charge it encloses. Let \(J(z)\,dz\) be a holomorphic current in a coordinate disk. Suppose that two insertions of this current have the singular product
\[
J(z)J(w)\sim\frac{\kappa\,\mathbf 1}{(z-w)^2}.
\]
Here \(\mathbf 1\) is the vacuum field, \(\kappa\) is the level, and the two sides have the same polar part at \(z=w\). Integrate around \(w\) with a holomorphic weight \(f\). The residue of \(f(z)J(z)J(w)\,dz\) is \(\kappa f'(w)\mathbf 1\). A constant weight gives zero. The weight \(f(z)=z-w\) measures \(\kappa\). Replacing the two fields by a value at coincidence would lose the quantity the contour measures. [The free current](#heisenberg-current)

Draw a second contour around the first. Integration in the two possible orders defines two operations on any state inserted at the centre of the disk. As one contour passes the other, their difference is the integral of the polar part at the crossing. In a coordinate centred at the insertion, put \(J_m=\operatorname{Res}_z z^mJ(z)\,dz\). The inner residue with weight \(z^m\) is \(\kappa m w^{m-1}\mathbf1\). The remaining residue with weight \(w^n\) consequently gives
\[
[J_m,J_n]=\kappa m\,\delta_{m+n,0}\,\mathrm{id}.
\]
Positive and negative modes of the same frequency can detect one another. A current acting on its vacuum, a contour enclosing two fields, and an operator on states are three uses of the same local product. Even in this example it matters on which side of an insertion a contour lies.

Now put the disk in the boundary of a three-dimensional space. An interior insertion and a boundary experiment can be specified independently. Suppose the interior contains a line ending at the centre of the disk. What is its charge, how does it act on a second line, and what state does the filling of the disk prepare? A boundary description ought to predict each answer using operations available on the disk. The comparison has to hold also when two ends come together. Otherwise two successive calculations of the same measurement could give different answers according to when the ends were joined.

This is the reconstruction problem in its simplest form. Given a boundary condition, construct its fields on open subsets of a curve, their collision operations and the sectors on which they act. Given a second boundary condition and an interface between the two, ask which sectors cross the interface and which measurements one can recover on the return journey. Finally cut a filling across that boundary. Does pairing the two independently prepared states reproduce the amplitude computed before the cut? An affirmative answer would identify far more than a space of charges. It would identify positions and their collision limits, spaces of maps between sectors, products of lines, and the value of an experiment with its insertions still present. There is an algebraic reconstruction theorem on a curve. For an augmented algebra of coefficient complexes on ordered configurations, with scalar coefficient in arity zero, consecutive cuts recover its multiplication, all its associativity homotopies and the full spaces of maps to other such algebras. There is also a physical calculation. At level one, a pair of opposite abelian charge lines and an independently sourced harmonic disk give the same normalized joint Gaussian law for the positive harmonic coordinates on the boundary. Do all compatible actions on a boundary arise from the physical bulk? Does the agreement of normalized states extend to the absolute pairing of two fillings? Those questions require further constructions.

## What can be recovered by cutting {#opening-ordered-cuts}

For three fields in a fixed order there are two ways to make successive binary collisions. One brings \(a\) to \(b\) and their product to \(c\), the other brings \(b\) to \(c\) first. Each prescription starts near its own diagonal in the space of three marked points. On a domain where both can be compared, write their products as \((ab)c\) and \(a(bc)\). When the fields form a cochain complex with differential \(d\), the comparison itself can be an operation. For closed inputs a degree-minus-one operation \(H\) gives
\[
dH(a,b,c)=(ab)c-a(bc).
\]
Thus the products agree on cohomology. A fourth field can be multiplied with \(H(a,b,c)\), so the choice of \(H\) still enters the next calculation. The five parenthesizations of four fields form a pentagon. Its two paths must be related by a further homotopy. An \(A_\infty\) multiplication keeps these operations and their compatibility at every number of inputs.

At two points one can already see why the extension to a collision requires a choice. Hold the centre fixed, and let the difference of the two positions describe a small circle. If parallel transport on a fibre \(E\) around that circle is \(C\), its cellular cochain complex is \(E\xrightarrow{C-1}E\). For a one-dimensional complex fibre and \(C=-1\), this complex is acyclic. For \(C=1\), a copy of the fibre survives in each of degrees zero and one. There is also the possibility of placing a complex at the collision itself. Its restriction to the punctured disk vanishes. Knowing every value and every continuation at distinct points therefore leaves a question even when every continuation is trivial. One must specify what is placed on the diagonal and how it attaches to the surrounding fields. [The normal link of a collision](#ch04-normal-link)

What makes this multiplication *chiral* is the geometry on which it acts. On a smooth complex algebraic curve \(X\), use complexes of right algebraic \(\mathcal D\)-modules on each \(X^n\). Their underlying sheaves are quasi-coherent, differential operators act in the marked positions, and the complexes have compatible extraordinary pullbacks along the diagonals that merge consecutive labels. The word \(1,\ldots,n\) records the order of multiplication. A cut after \(p\) separates two blocks. It is available on the open set \(U_{p,n-p}\) where each point of the first block is distinct from every point of the second. Points within a block may coincide. In the ordered word model, form the *bar construction* \(BA\) of an augmented coherent multiplication \(A\). It records strings of inputs and applies the products and their homotopies to consecutive entries. On degree-zero elements of the augmentation ideal of an ordinary associative algebra its product differential sends \([a|b|c]\) to \([ab|c]-[a|bc]\). The next differential gives the difference of the two triple products. For an associative chiral algebra in this model, cutting the string gives an equivalence on the separating open. Writing \(q=n-p\) and using \(\boxtimes\) for derived external tensor product, the factorization condition is
\[
(BA)_n|_{U_{p,q}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\bigl((BA)_p\boxtimes(BA)_q\bigr)|_{U_{p,q}}.
\]

Choose a convolution for ordered configurations. It combines two coefficient systems by placing their inputs in separate blocks and extending the external product from the open where those blocks are disjoint. At two points let \(j:X^2\setminus\Delta\hookrightarrow X^2\) be that open inclusion. The binary multiplication of positive inputs is an actual map of complexes on \(X^2\),
\[
Rj_*j^*(A_1\boxtimes^{\mathbf L}A_1)\longrightarrow A_2 .
\]
The source allows the two input fields to develop a singularity as they approach each other. We must specify the target \(A_2\) on all of \(X^2\), its collision identification \(\Delta^!A_2\simeq A_1\), and the arrow across the diagonal. Knowing the map off the diagonal alone cannot say how a class supported at the collision attaches. In all arities an **associative chiral algebra** is an augmented \(E_1\)-algebra for the chosen convolution whose bar coalgebra factorizes. Here \(E_1\) means an ordered multiplication with the associativity homotopies just described. The augmentation is to the unit supported on the empty configuration. Its ideal is positive, meaning that the ideal has zero empty coefficient. Factorization has the following meaning. For each admissible division \(I=S\sqcup T\), the coproduct on the bar coefficients must induce the restriction equivalence displayed for \((BA)_n\), on the open where every point of \(S\) is distinct from every point of \(T\). Equivalently, a separated pair of clusters can be read independently by its two sets of bar coefficients.

Which divisions are admissible depends on the geometry. For a word in one fixed order, convolution uses consecutive cuts, and a monotone collision combines neighbours. The full ordered configuration space glues a chart for every total order and allows collision maps with ordered fibres. Its symmetric monoidal convolution sums over all ordered decompositions into subsets. Restrict to a single increasing chart and project onto the consecutive summands to recover the word coalgebra. This projection need not be an equivalence. One can see what a nonconsecutive collision contributes without constructing a coalgebra. Put \(u=z_1-z_3,\ v=z_2-z_3\) and consider \(du\wedge dv/[u(u-v)]\). Around \(u=v\) the first residue, in \(u-v\), is \(dv/v\), giving iterated residue \(1\). Around \(v=0\) first the form has no generic pole, giving \(0\). Around \(u=0\) first the coefficient is \(-dv/v\), giving \(-1\). The three flags sum to zero, the residue identity for the chiral Lie bracket. Equating the first two answers would erase the contribution from the first and third points. The associativity of consecutive cuts is a different comparison, between the two bracketings of an ordered product. [Associative products on configurations](#ch12-associative-products-on-configurations)

To see where a class of the consecutive-cut coalgebra can survive in cobar, keep the fields in the order \(1,2,3\). When the first two points meet away from the third, the cut \(12\mid3\) is still available. When the last two meet away from the first, one can use \(1\mid23\). At a meeting of the first and third points every cut separates a pair at the same position. The locus is the entire diagonal \(z_1=z_3\), even where the middle point lies elsewhere.

How much of the multiplication survives the passage to cuts? Give the empty configuration coefficient \(\mathbb C\), with its specified augmentation on the algebra and coaugmentation on the coalgebra. The reduced coefficients then have positive arity. A word of length \(n\) admits at most \(n\) nonempty blocks, so the iterated reduced constructions terminate at each arity. For a smooth separated algebraic curve of finite type, the resulting inverse theorem concerns the sequence of coefficient complexes. Bar and *cobar* are inverse equivalences between connected augmented algebras and connected counital coaugmented coalgebras for consecutive cuts, with their full derived spaces of maps. For a factorizing coalgebra the reduced three-input cobar coefficient is supported where both consecutive cuts fail, precisely on \(z_1=z_3\). The middle point can lie elsewhere. To carry the construction through a collision, observe that a cut inside a block of labels being identified disappears, while a cut between two such blocks descends to the corresponding cut of the shorter word. A comparison with fields constructed from a different theory must in addition identify their vacuum, translations and collision operations. [Recovering the algebra from cuts](#ch05-ordered-cuts-give-a-finite-geometric-bar-construction)

There is a short reason why putting the cuts back gives the original object. A term in cobar of bar is a word of words. Flatten it to a string of \(r\) nonempty pieces. In the associated graded complex each gap is either inside one word or between two words. The differential which changes that choice gives the two copies of the same coefficient, joined by the identity, at every gap. For \(r>1\), pair terms at the first gap. They contract in pairs with opposite incidence signs. The single piece survives. At a configuration of \(n\) points there can be at most \(n\) nonempty pieces, so this calculation finishes in each arity even for a coefficient complex unbounded in both cohomological directions. It is this finiteness which allows one to say that maps and their homotopies also come back. There is no operation in the argument which assigns a state space to an arbitrary line or prescribes a value for an independent field at the collision.

## A symmetry on a pair of states {#opening-spectral-symmetry}

A different question arises when a symmetry acts on two independently prepared systems. Which current acts on their tensor product? If \(J_1\) and \(J_2\) have levels \(\kappa_1,\kappa_2\) and their mixed product is regular, their sum has double-pole coefficient \(\kappa_1+\kappa_2\). Writing \(H_\kappa\) for the algebra of a free current of level \(\kappa\), the simultaneous action comes from
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{\kappa_1+\kappa_2}&\longrightarrow H_{\kappa_1}\otimes H_{\kappa_2},\\
J&\longmapsto J_1\otimes\mathbf1+\mathbf1\otimes J_2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
The direction and levels of this map are forced by the double pole. A unital field map from \(H_\kappa\) to the scalars would have to preserve it, so the regularity of scalar fields would force \(\kappa=0\). The augmentation at the empty configuration in the bar theorem belongs to the geometric coefficient system. In a chiral quantum group the coproduct must preserve the field products, give compatible actions on three systems and respect exchange when their positions move. These requirements concern the tensor product of systems. Cutting a string in the bar construction alone supplies no such action.

The plus sign in the level has a representation-theoretic meaning. Let the two currents act on spaces \(M\) and \(N\). On \(M\otimes N\), their sum acts by \(J_m^{(1)}\otimes1+1\otimes J_m^{(2)}\). The mixed commutators vanish, so the contour commutator on the tensor product has central coefficient \(m(\kappa_1+\kappa_2)\). Pullback along the displayed algebra map is the tensor product of these representations. Three such systems give the same current whichever pair is combined first. Moving two insertion points around one another asks a further question. The interchange should be an operator on the two representations, should respect their fields as the positions vary, and should agree with moving past a pair one point at a time. These are the reasons to ask for a coproduct, translation and an exchange operator in one structure.

To formulate this requirement for any associative chiral algebra, specify how two independent systems are combined *at the same positions*, writing their tensor product as \(A\diamond A'\). To multiply in it, one must interchange the middle two inputs before multiplying in the respective factors. In chiral geometry this interchange acts on the configuration extensions, on a common domain for their poles and supports. A **chiral coproduct** is then an algebra map \(D:A\to A\diamond A\), with coherent coassociativity and a counit to the unit of \(\diamond\), or a family which combines levels. For collision coefficients one can tensor the two bar coalgebras on the diagonal and transport back by cobar, obtaining \(A\diamond A'=\Omega(BA\otimes^! BA')\). To identify this product with the tensor of fields carrying an exchange, one still has to construct an interchange with those fields. The free current illustrates why cutting \(BA\) at separated positions and acting on a pair of systems solve different problems.

There is a class in which this compatibility is a theorem. Take a Hopf algebra over \(\mathbb C\) with bijective antipode and a translation \(T\) that is both a derivation and a coderivation and satisfies \(\varepsilon T=0\), where \(\varepsilon\) is its counit. A unitary spectral coquasitriangular form \(\rho_z\) pairs two inputs in Laurent polynomials in their separation \(z\). It is multiplicative against the coproduct, differentiates under translation, and implements an exchange whose reverse is its inverse. For Hopf automorphisms \(\eta,\theta\) preserving the translation and the form, these data construct a field algebra \(V_\eta\) at each such level \(\eta\). Writing \(\Delta\) for the Hopf coproduct, the maps for combining levels form a coassociative family
\[
D_{\eta,\theta}=(\theta\otimes 1)\Delta:
V_{\eta\theta}\longrightarrow V_\eta\boxtimes_\rho V_\theta .
\]
The tensor product on the right uses \(\rho\) when a field in one factor passes a field in the other. With that contraction included, applying \(D\) to a product gives the product of the images, with its full dependence on \(z\). Exchanging the product with a third field is the composite of the two exchanges with its factors. This is the field hexagon. [The spectral field and coproduct](#ch12-a-spectral-construction-of-products-and-coproducts)

Write \(Y(a,z)b\) for the field of \(a\) evaluated on \(b\), and \(Y_{12}\) for the field on the two factors with their cross-contraction. The equation tested by a coproduct \(D=D_{\eta,\theta}\) and the equation tested by exchange \(\mathcal S\) have the following forms. Coproduct compatibility is
\[
D\bigl(Y_{\eta\theta}(a,z)b\bigr)=Y_{12}(Da,z)Db .
\]
For \(Y(w)(a\otimes b)=Y(a,w)b\), the hexagon reads
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal S(z)(Y(w)\otimes1)\\
=(Y(w)\otimes1)\mathcal S_{23}(z)\mathcal S_{13}(z+w).
\end{gathered}
\]
The counit for this family is at the identity level. The last factor of the hexagon is expanded in nonnegative powers of \(w\). It exchanges the first and third fields at their actual separation. The other exchange uses the separation of the second and third. Thus the translation between the two centres of expansion enters the symmetry even before one chooses a representation.

The range of this construction matters for familiar fields. In the \(W_3\) algebra let \(L\) be the stress field, let \(W\) have spin three and write \(:LL:\) for the regular product obtained after subtracting the singular terms. At central charge \(c\) with \(22+5c\ne0\), the double pole of \(W(z)W(w)\) contains
\[
\frac{32}{22+5c}\left(:LL:-\tfrac3{10}\partial^2L\right).
\]
A description by single modes and scalars now encounters an elementary difficulty. In the regular normalization of the spin-three generator \(U\), a commutator on the vacuum has a term \(2L_{-2}^2\mathbf1\) independent of the term proportional to \(L_{-4}\mathbf1\). A basis of ordered creation modes separates these two states even at \(c=-22/5\), where the displayed normalization by \(W\) breaks down. Retaining the quadratic term changes the dual fields too. Their regular product acquires a derivative correction, and the differential of the curved dual squares to an operation of its curvature. For universal algebras with finitely many free even generators and homogeneous quadratic, linear and scalar brackets under the stated weight hypotheses, this construction recovers maps of fields from the corresponding coupling equation. A description of continuous modules or of the fusion of physical lines would require more. [The spin-three calculation](#ch12-nonlinear-fields-the-w3-example)

## Which representations cross an interface {#opening-interfaces}

One way of finding representations is to pass through an interface at which two theories meet. The simplest example is an \(A\)-\(B\) bimodule \(P\). A state in a left \(B\)-module \(M\) passes to \(P\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}M\), a left \(A\)-module. Going back from an \(A\)-module \(N\) gives the complex of responses \(R\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,N)\). Denote these operations by \(F\) and \(G\). The unit \(M\to GFM\) sends \(m\) to the response \(p\mapsto p\otimes m\). The counit \(FGN\to N\) evaluates a response on an element of the interface. These maps ask two different experimental questions. Can every original state and every map between original states be recovered? Which states on the far side can actually be prepared in this way? If \(B=k\), \(A=k\times k\) and \(P=(k,0)\), every complex comes back unchanged on the first journey. On the second, \((N_1,N_2)\) comes back as \((N_1,0)\). Thus even perfect recovery in one direction specifies a sector on the other side.

There is a theorem with this shape for the nonabelian currents. Let \(\mathfrak g\) be simple, \(h^\vee\) its dual Coxeter number and \(s\) an irrational shifted level, meaning \(s\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Q\). Write \(A\) for the affine mode algebra at level \(s-h^\vee\) and \(B=Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\) for the polynomial differential graded line algebra. Induce from a vector killed by the strictly positive currents, leaving the constant currents free to act. The resulting affine module is
\[
W=A\otimes_{U(t\mathfrak g[t])}\mathbb C .
\]
Supply its coherent right \(B\)-action, the smooth comparison on its endomorphisms and the compatible Taylor cutoffs and tensor–Hom adjunction of the affine realization. The unit is then an equivalence for *every* unbounded \(B\)-complex. Its image consists precisely of the smooth affine complexes generated by \(W\) under shifts, cones, coproducts and retracts, including energy shifts in the graded convention.

The reason a finite calculation reaches unbounded complexes here is quite different from the arity bound for bar and cobar. Let \(I\) be the positive-weight ideal of \(B\), \(R=U(\mathfrak g)\), and \(D\) the number of positive roots. Each successive nonzero factor of \(I\) raises the length in a finite Bruhat order. The derived tensor power of \(I\) over \(R\) of length \(D+1\) vanishes, while the power of length \(D\) can survive. For \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) one has a nonzero cube and a zero fourth power. This gives a finite resolution of the unit comparison by comparisons on coefficients inflated from \(R\). It also explains why one must ask again at rational shifted level. At raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) level \(-3\), there is an infinite direct-limit complex killed by reduction to \(R\) which still passes through the interface and returns intact. At the critical raw level \(-h^\vee\), assuming its smooth realization and the unit theorem on complexes bounded below in energy, there are nonzero complexes killed by the affine realization itself. An independently defined physical induction and restriction would have to realize these maps on their original domains and carry the product of two lines through the interface. [The affine interface](#affine-reconstruction-scope)

A different route begins with the geometry of the modules. For an ordinary unital chiral algebra on a smooth separated algebraic curve, take the complete families of ordinary modules with arbitrary classical parameter schemes. Inserting the empty unit in those families gives the vacuum on all configurations. The two-point residue of that vacuum retrieves the original chiral multiplication, including its extension to a collision. Classical parameters matter. A module supported at the origin can have zero ordinary fibre and a nonzero first derived fibre. In the coordinate model \(\delta_0=\mathbb C[u]\), the transverse parameter acts as \(-d/du\). Its cokernel is zero and its kernel is the constants. Ordinary restriction sees the cokernel, whereas derived restriction retains both. This round trip therefore proves something different from the equivalence on consecutive-cut coefficient sequences. It starts with the complete ordinary family, including the collision information that the open configuration alone does not specify. [Vacuum and geometric reconstruction](#ch13-the-ordinary-categorical-round-trip)

## Joining two insertions {#opening-joining}

Suppose we know exactly which sectors cross. To join two operations, the outgoing sector of the first must agree with the incoming sector of the second. This principle gives an explicit tensor construction for the simple Virasoro minimal models at coprime integers \(2\le p<q\). Let \(L_i\) be their finitely many simple modules and work with finite direct sums. For a marked point the full rational stress Ward identities impose the action of vector fields meromorphic at the insertions. Taking the part which obeys the relations of the simple algebra and then taking the restricted contragredient gives the representing tensor product \(M\boxtimes N\). Each fusion channel now has two ends. If \(I\) is the set of simples, put
\[
\mathcal F(M)_{ij}=\operatorname{Hom}(L_i,M\boxtimes L_j).
\]
The fibre is a bimodule over \(B_0=\mathbb C^I\). In its tensor product over \(B_0\), the two adjacent idempotents satisfy \(e_je_k=0\) unless \(j=k\). Precisely the composable channels survive. Composition with the associator gives the tensor comparison, and semisimplicity makes it invertible. This reconstructs a finite weak Hopf algebra acting on the fibres. Its tensor unit is \(B_0\), and the coproduct of the identity projects an ordinary tensor onto the matching end labels. In the Ising model, the two ways through \(\sigma\boxtimes\sigma=\mathbf1\oplus\psi\) really are different intermediate states. An ordinary vector-space fibre which requires the dimension of \(\sigma\) to be a positive integer would have to solve \((\dim\sigma)^2=2\). [The two ends of a channel](#ch13-finite-rational-categories-and-a-non-scalar-base)

For a continuum of intermediate labels there is an analytic question before one can even compare two ways of joining. Take the Virasoro positive principal representations at real \(c>1\), with highest weight \(h_p=(c-1)/24+p^2\) for \(p>0\). If the amplitudes on opposite sides of a circle take values \(u_1,v_1\) on the descendant \(L_{-1}\Omega_p\), that level contributes \(u_1v_1/(2h_p)\). The denominator comes from commuting \(L_1\) past \(L_{-1}\) on the normalized highest vector. At every higher level one must use the inverse of its full bilinear Gram matrix, and then integrate over the intermediate labels. Fix a compact set \(K\) of positions with \(0<|a_2|<\cdots<|a_d|\) and an output energy exponent \(s<-\log|a_d|\) throughout \(K\). Under the normalized boundary hypothesis and the two-radius estimates, a finite input energy exponent \(t_K\) makes the left-associated sewing of these \(d\) positive insertions a holomorphic Hilbert–Schmidt operator. No intermediate spectral or descendant cutoff remains. The strict gaps between radii supply the energy estimates which make that assertion possible. With a normalized unitary four-point comparison and pentagon on the positive labels, changing the finite tree acts unitarily on the intermediate labels and fixes the output representation. Each initial tree expansion converges in its own radial region.

One might try to feed the result of such a sewing straight into the next operation as an elementary input. In the positive entire-energy source, even a collision of two highest vectors can fail that test after any fixed finite propagation. Its primary coefficient decays at best exponentially in \(h_p\), while the source tests *all* positive exponential weights. There is a simpler algebraic instance of the same issue. The series \(\sum_{n\ge0}q^n\lambda^{-n^2}\) is defined with coefficients rational in the shape variable \(\lambda\). Setting \(\lambda=zq\) gives outer exponents \(n-n^2\), which have no lower bound. Thus agreement of the integrands on separated points, or convergence for one prescribed sewing, leaves a genuine domain question when a cluster is used again. A common coefficient space for both compositions is part of the comparison. [The domain of a collision](#ch10-collision-and-the-two-conditions-for-descent)

## From an interior operation to a centre {#opening-central-actions}

We can now return to the disk, with an interior of its own. A commutative algebra of classical fields equipped with a singular Poisson bracket on the curve is called a coisson algebra. At one point the singular bracket with a fixed insertion is a derivation of the commutative product. Exchanging the two points translates the expansion coordinate and gives the corresponding rule in the other argument. The Jacobi identity relates the residues of three insertions. In the abelian mixed theory on \(\mathbb C_z\times\mathbb R_{\ge0,t}\) one can see how a boundary bracket arises from an action. Put \(d'=\bar\partial+d_t\). At degree zero there is a partial connection \(a\) in the \(d\bar z,dt\) directions and a field \(b\) with a \(dz\) factor. The quadratic density is
\[
 b\wedge d'a+\frac{\lambda}{2}a\wedge\partial a .
\]
The full field complex includes gauge parameters and antifields. Set the tangential restriction of the first field to zero and leave the second field free. Contracting a compactly supported normal profile gives a boundary current. With the residue integral divided by \(2\pi i\) and the sign of this action, its double-pole coefficient is \(-\hbar\lambda\). The boundary contour thus reads a coefficient of the interior action. Translation in the real direction is part of the differential, yet bringing two insertions to equal height creates a contact term in the Ward identity. Thus the prescription for moving an interior operation to the boundary must specify its support even in a free theory. [The mixed half-space](#ch09-a-boundary-current-from-a-mixed-field-theory)

Holography asks which operations and states on a boundary can be obtained from a field theory in the space it bounds. In a free holomorphic–topological half-space, a test field supported strictly above the boundary has zero boundary restriction. Multiply it by a boundary input before integrating out the transverse fields and there can be a contraction. On polynomial current states \(f(p_1,p_2,\ldots)\), a test tuned to harmonic \(j\) acts by \(\partial f/\partial p_j\) at quantum current level one. Each occurrence of \(p_j\) gives one possible contraction. This computes an interior action on the boundary cochains. To determine a state one must also specify its preparation.

What single object could receive *every* such compatible action? For an ordinary algebra \(A\), a natural operation on all left modules is determined by its value on the regular module. If that value on \(1\) is \(z\), naturality under the maps \(A\to M\) sending \(1\) to \(m\) gives \(m\mapsto zm\). On the regular module, \(A\)-linearity sends \(a\) to \(az\), while naturality under right multiplication sends it to \(za\). Thus \(z\) lies in the ordinary centre. Passing to complexes gives the derived centre \(R\operatorname{Hom}_{A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}(A,A)\). On a curve an insertion has a position. One can bring it to a second insertion and then split the surrounding configuration. A recipient for such actions must compare both operations, their orders and their homotopies. A scalar in the ordinary centre has no argument in which to record a moving point.

There is a positive calculation between the ordinary centre and the proposed geometric one. Take the two-colour Chevalley coalgebra associated to the algebra of two orthogonal idempotents, and let the operations on it be parametrized by the Chevalley coalgebra of an unbounded differential graded chiral algebra on the affine line in characteristic zero, with a closed horizontal unit. Choose a coherent pointing on this parameter coalgebra. To a character respecting the pointing, associate multiplication by that character. Evaluating an action on the target unit goes back. For this family the two maps are inverse equivalences of the full pointed mapping spaces. This recovers the action and its homotopies from its value on the unit. A terminal recipient for every holomorphic field theory would have to account for a larger class of actions. [An action recovered from its unit](#ch07-recognition-on-chevalley-coalgebras)

This leads to the holomorphic–topological centre conjecture. For a unital holomorphic factorization algebra \(\mathcal A\) on a smooth complex \(d\)-fold, with specified supported density coefficients, there should be a mixed factorization algebra \(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{diag}}(\mathcal A)\), topological in one real direction and holomorphic in \(d\) complex directions. It is to act unitally at each point and be terminal among the diagonal actions preserving the coefficients, collisions, factorization and supports. If it exists, any such action of a bulk \(\mathcal B\) factors through it by a map \(\beta:\mathcal B\to\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{diag}}(\mathcal A)\), with a contractible space of choices. The independent assertion that a given physical bulk *is* this centre asks whether its own map \(\beta\) is an equivalence. The category in the existence question is differentiable. For the prescribed algebraic pointed-action functor of the nonzero-level Heisenberg coalgebra, representability already fails. It is open whether that obstruction persists in the differentiable setting. [Pointed actions and collision compatibility](#problem-universal-centres)

What then does an observer following a line encounter? Two small insertions on it may be composed in their order along the line. A local operator can approach the line from a transverse complex direction. Describing the line by a module should preserve both experiments, including their dependence on separation. The line-operator conjecture begins with the hypothesis that the local algebra is geometrically realized by the convolution cobar of an augmented transverse monoid \(F\), with its vacuum, product and local insertions on the specified collision domains. Form its bar coalgebra and realize its one-position coefficient in complete complexes by a strong monoidal functor \(\mathsf R\) preserving the bar realization. If continuous duality transposes the completed tensor powers used by the operations, one can form
\[
\mathcal Y=\mathsf R\bigl((\operatorname{Bar}^{!}F)_{\{1\}}\bigr)^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}} .
\]
The conjecture gives this algebra the structure of a shifted differential graded Yangian and identifies lines, under matching finiteness conditions, with its admissible continuous modules. Line fusion is to be tensor product via the spectral coproduct. The comparison should carry translation, the vacuum line and the local insertions at a line to their module operations. Under bar duality the degree-minus-one bracket of local fields should correspond to the degree-one twist of the coproduct. The abelian sum of levels gives a case where one can write the action on two states. The nonrational affine theorem gives a case where the original unit on complexes is invertible. Combining these conclusions for a physical line would require the same interface to preserve the two orders of tensor product on their actual domains. [Lines, products and geometric insertions](#problem-chiral-quantum-groups)

## What is retained at an open end {#opening-open-ends}

A brane provides another way to compare the operators seen on the two sides of a boundary condition. On a stack of \(N\) D3 branes, protected operators on a complex line \(\mathbb C\) in the four-dimensional worldvolume form a vertex algebra. There are two independent constructions within this sector. One imposes the gauge constraint and takes degree-zero relative BRST cohomology of polynomial fields. The other first goes to the stable moment quotient, the Hilbert scheme \(\operatorname{Hilb}^N(\mathbb C^2)\) of points on an auxiliary complex surface, and then takes the torus-finite global sections of its sheaf of chiral fields and specializes the quantum parameter to one. The polynomial operators inject into these sections. Their image is the kernel of a fermionic zero mode \(\chi^-_{(0)}\). There is a weight-zero odd section \(\tau\) with \(\chi^-_{(0)}\tau=N\). This single evaluation detects the missing global field. At rank one it is the distinction between the full \(b,c\) pair and the algebra generated by \(b,\partial c\), since differentiating loses the constant mode of \(c\). Thus putting the same local contractions into two constructions can give different spaces of global operators. An identification with a separately constructed bulk must determine which space it acts on. [The polynomial and global brane operators](#ch14-d3-branes-chiral-operators-and-hilbert-scheme-sections)

For the surface defect of the M5 system there is also an explicit product. For independent currents in two groups, the corresponding Miura differential operators multiply in their written order. Factoring the rank-\(N_1+N_2\) operator as \(L_{N_1}L_{N_2}\) gives a coassociative rank-addition map. Composition of three differential operators proves its associativity without moving the surfaces. Identifying this map with fusion of the actual defects in five dimensions calls for their quantization, the Ward identities and the normalization of their mixed insertion. There is a reason to insist on the intermediate defect. If \(R\to A\) is its algebra of operators, joining two copies along \(R\) gives \(A\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}A\). Even a surjection can leave a class invisible in the ordinary product and in its first derived correction. For the quiver \(1\xrightarrow a2\xrightarrow b1\) with \(ab=0\) in traversal order, kill vertex 2. The resolution at vertex 1 has projectives at vertices \(1,2,1\). After tensoring with the remaining simple module it becomes \(k\to0\to k\) in degrees \(-2,-1,0\). The algebraic M2 particle junction exhibits this same separation for \(N\ge1\), nonzero numerical \(\hbar\) and zero Cherednik deformation. Its coupling sends a Weyl observable to the sum of its actions on the \(N\) particles. The first Tor group vanishes. Capping a class in the second Tor group with the scalar central extension of the Weyl Lie algebra maps onto the full particle algebra, with a bimodule splitting. It detects the flux left by joining the two ends. Identifying this cap with an insertion in the five-dimensional interval is a further comparison. [What the joined interface retains](#ch14-what-a-joined-interface-remembers)

One can keep such intermediate choices in geometry by recording the object together with the way it was assembled. In Hall multiplication the two inputs prescribe the quotient and the subobject in an exact sequence. The output is obtained by integrating over the possible subobjects of the middle term. Over \(\mathbb F_q\), every extension of two one-dimensional vector spaces splits. Nevertheless the coefficient of their product on \(\mathbb F_q^2\) is \(q+1\), one for each line in the plane. In dimension three either bracketing integrates over the same flags of a line in a plane, which proves associativity of this product. With sheaves as coefficients one needs an actual map on the extension correspondence before integrating. If \(p\) records the ends and \(q\) the middle, the critical Hall multiplication calls for a morphism \(p^*(\mathscr P_\alpha\boxtimes\mathscr P_\beta)\to q^!\mathscr P_{\alpha+\beta}\). Comparing it with a local field operation requires transporting the square roots of the determinant lines and the moment parameters on the flags. The scalar product after integration cannot specify this map. [Extensions and their flags](#ch15-extensions-and-the-reason-for-associativity)

This distinction can be measured on a compact surface. For a K3 surface \(S\), the two ends of a relative Donaldson–Thomas tube carry states in \(H_d=H^*(S^{[d]},\mathbb Q)\), the cohomology of its Hilbert scheme of \(d\) points. Pair a state at each end and insert a divisor \(D\) in the interior. The reduced virtual correspondence defines an operator \(\widehat B^D_{\beta,d}\) in curve class \(\beta\). Closing the tube contracts its two ends against the Poincaré diagonal. The resulting reduced scalar series is \((\beta\cdot D)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}\widehat B^D_{\beta,d}\) when \(\beta\cdot D\ne0\). Closure even changes the multiple-cover coefficient. After the specified cohomological identifications, each cover in the operator formula has coefficient one. The factor \(1/k\) for a cover of degree \(k\) appears upon dividing by the divisor intersection at closure.

What can one do with three open ends that this trace cannot do? At \(d=1\), choose a rational section \(C\) and a fibre \(F\) on an elliptic K3, so \(C^2=-2\), \(F^2=0\) and \(C\cdot F=1\). The first reduced multiplication in section class has an explicit expression on all twenty-four states. For the Poincaré pairing \(\eta\), the expression is
\[
\mu(a,b)=\eta(a,C)\eta(b,C)C .
\]
In particular \(\mu(F,F)=C\). Choose isotropic classes \(u,v\in H^2(S,\mathbb Q)\) perpendicular to \(C,F\) and satisfying \(\eta(u,v)=1\). Add the cyclic first-order term \(\nu(a,b)=\eta(a,u)\eta(b,u)u\). The fibre row is unchanged. For fixed \(a\), the change in the multiplication operator has trace \(\eta(a,u)\eta(u,u)=0\). Yet the three-boundary experiment with \(v,v,v\) changes from zero to one. The geometric three-boundary calculation selects \(\mu\). Every first multiplication trace together with the fibre row would still have allowed the rival. A comparison between Hall multiplication and compact states must recover this uncontracted operation and its orientation, even if it already reproduces the closed Igusa series. [The three-boundary reading on a K3](#ch15-a-cyclic-product-that-all-these-traces-can-miss)

## Preparing a state at the boundary {#opening-state-preparations}

The compact example keeps three states at their respective ends. In a field theory one must also be able to prepare the states used to make such a distinction. For the free current the two calculations can be done on different spaces. In the current Fock space the negative mode \(J_{-n}\), \(n>0\), multiplies by a variable \(p_n\). Its adjoint \(J_n=n\partial_{p_n}\) satisfies the contour commutator at level one. On the disk, let \(Z_n\) be independent centred complex Gaussian coordinates of variance \(n\). The substitution \(p_n\mapsto Z_n\) is an isometry on holomorphic polynomials. Indeed both norms of the occupation monomial \(\prod p_n^{m_n}\) are the square root of \(\prod n^{m_n}m_n!\). It extends to the two Hilbert completions. One can test the isometry on an observable sensitive to phase. The unit vectors \((p_1^2+p_2)/2\) and \((p_1^2-p_2)/2\) have the same separate occupation statistics. The operator \(2p_1^2\partial_{p_2}+p_2\partial_{p_1}^2\) interchanges \(p_1^2\) and \(p_2\) with coefficient two, so its expectations in those vectors are \(+2\) and \(-2\). On the disk the same two values are the expectations of \(2\operatorname{Re}(Z_1^2\overline{Z_2})\). It is a joint measurement of two harmonics. The isometry alone, however, supplies no field-theoretic preparation of a given state. To compare preparations one must find a state on each side by its own equations.

In the free abelian Chern–Simons half-space theory at level one, put two oriented charge lines of strengths \(a,-a\) above the points \(r,0\) of a marked unit disk, with \(a\) real and \(0<r<1\). Use constant framing, subtract self-contractions, and take the prescribed limit of outgoing separations in a fixed real direction. The current on an outer contour sees \(a/(z-r)-a/z\). The unweighted residue is zero. Weighting by \(z^n\), as in the first contour calculation, gives the eigenvalue \(ar^n\) of the positive current mode for \(n\ge1\). Independently, take a real harmonic field \(\phi=h+\bar h\) on the disk with constant mode zero, and write \(h(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}Z_nz^n/n\). Weight its Gaussian law by \(\exp(a\phi(r))\) and normalize. Completing the square gives \(\mathbb E Z_n=ar^n\) and \(\mathbb E|Z_n-ar^n|^2=n\). The squared modulus of the normalized ray wavefunction, multiplied by the vacuum Gaussian density, gives this same law on every finite set of harmonics and hence on the full sequence. [Neutral rays and a sourced disk](#ch11-neutral-ray-pair)

Here one can derive the equality of laws directly. The contour gives \(n\partial_{p_n}\Psi=ar^n\Psi\) for each \(n>0\). Their common solution is fixed up to its constant term and has the form
\[
\Psi\ \propto\ \exp\!\left(a\sum_{n\ge1}p_n r^n/n\right).
\]
On the disk, set \(h_N=\sum_{n=1}^N Z_n r^n/n\) and \(V_N=\sum_{n=1}^N r^{2n}/n\). The squared modulus of this exponential under the Fock isometry, divided by its vacuum expectation, gives the probability measure
\[
 e^{2a\operatorname{Re}h_N-a^2V_N}\,d\mu .
\]
Independently, the real harmonic source at \(r\) adds \(2a\operatorname{Re}h_N\) to the logarithm of the disk weight. Completing the square in each coordinate gives precisely the same measure. Since \(V_N\uparrow-\log(1-r^2)<\infty\), the holomorphic exponentials converge in the disk Hilbert norm and their normalized squared moduli converge in \(L^1(\mu)\). It is the whole density, including correlations between modes, which the two preparations agree on.

Propagating through a cylinder of length \(L\ge0\) damps harmonic \(n\) by \(q^n\), \(q=e^{-L}\). The normalized overlap of the prepared state with its propagated state is \(\bigl((1-r^2)/(1-qr^2)\bigr)^{a^2}\) on both sides. The factor \(r^{-a^2}\) in the ray amplitude cancels from this measurement. An absolute cutting law must keep the vacuum amplitude as well.

For \(a\ne0\) these lines have zero total charge but prepare a state different from the vacuum. In fact the first two harmonic means determine where the marked line ended and what charge it carried. With \(\mu_1=ar\) and \(\mu_2=ar^2\), its position is \(r=\mu_2/\mu_1\) and its charge is \(a=\mu_1^2/\mu_2\). Varying the collar length separates frequencies because the successive powers \(q^n\) damp them at different rates. At \(q=1\) the normalized overlap is one, as it must be for a unit vector. Sending \(q\) down to zero leaves \((1-r^2)^{a^2}\), its squared vacuum overlap. Neither measurement can fix a common scalar multiplying an unnormalized filling. An absolute amplitude asks a question which the normalized state, even if known on every mode, cannot decide.

Arithmetic supplies an independent test of what a trace can measure. On logarithmic scale, dilation becomes translation. For a compact smooth test \(f\) write \(\widehat f(w)=\int f(u)e^{iwu}\,du\). The Weil explicit formula evaluates the same functional \(W(f)\) by its polar, real-place and prime-power terms, or as \(\sum_\rho m_\rho\widehat f(w_\rho)\) over zeros of the completed zeta function, where \(w_\rho=-i(\rho-1/2)\). These descriptions suggest taking a quotient by the relations from integer theta summation before any individual zero has been specified. For the test algebra with all derivatives integrable against \(e^{a|u|}\), \(a>1/2\), and a Hilbert weight \(1/2<s<\min(a,1)\), the quotient operator is trace class and its trace is \(W(f)\). This applies in particular to each compact smooth test above. A smoothing homotopy computes the same number as a supertrace. On a second Hilbert space one can realize the zeros for which \(w_\rho\) is real, equivalently \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\), by a positive star representation of the same test algebra modulo its closed theta ideal. A zero of multiplicity \(m\) contributes on \(m\) separate layers. The difference of the two traces is exactly
\[
W(f)-\operatorname{Tr}\pi_{\mathrm{crit}}(f)
 =\sum_{w_\rho\notin\mathbb R}m_\rho\widehat f(w_\rho).
\]
If the two traces agreed on every compact smooth test, then \(W(g*g^*)\) would be the trace of a positive operator for each such \(g\), where \(*\) denotes convolution and \(g^*(u)=\overline{g(-u)}\). By the Weil positivity criterion this would imply the Riemann hypothesis. Conversely the remainder vanishes if every zero lies on the critical line. The quotient trace is a theorem without that assumption. It supplies neither positivity on every test nor a product for colliding arithmetic insertions. Here the distinction between a trace, a positive state and local operations can be posed without assuming any bulk dual. ## The amplitude before and after a cut {#opening-filling}

Return finally to the physical filling. If the amplitude takes values in a line, its value on the empty insertion is itself a section of that line. Where this section is nonzero, dividing every correlation by the empty value gives a normalized state. To undo this division when gluing two pieces one needs an identification of their amplitude lines, the pairing on the states at the common boundary, and the unnormalized value on the vacuum. A bulk observable \(O\) may be measured before the cut or passed to the boundary by its action. An absolute cutting law says that the two *independently* defined functionals have equal values on these corresponding insertions, including \(O=1\). This is the additional information lost by the normalized cylinder measurement.

On a solid torus with fixed metric and density, a quadratic gravitational model admits a normalized calculation after removal of the mode cutoff. Take a torus multiplier of modulus strictly between zero and one, nonzero coupling, positive \(\hbar\), and a sufficiently small smooth integrable source of zero normal trace admitting a smooth solution of the Ward equations. The cutoff even fields converge in mean square and almost surely in a space of distributions. Their limit has a Gaussian law, and graded Wick contraction gives the moments involving odd fields. In a reflected realization with a spectral gap, smooth source bounds and finite action integrals, heat regularization gives a nonzero local section of the vacuum line, the one-dimensional space in which the unnormalized vacuum amplitude takes its values. This determines how the normalization varies near a reference. The absolute geometric cutting law remains open already in this quadratic model. Each geometric half must prepare its own state, and pairing those states must reproduce the uncut amplitude, including its phase and its response to insertions. For interacting quantum gravity that comparison must also respect the interaction terms. A common phase of the entire amplitude cancels in every probability. A relative phase between components of a single normalized state can survive, even when the occupations of its modes have identical distributions in two preparations. [The gravitational cutting problem](#problem-gravitational-cap)

A precise conjecture formulates this question for gravity. Fix a formal neighbourhood of an Einstein–Cartan solution with invertible coframe, its real form and boundary polarization. Fix the admitted regions, collars and cuts and a common ring for the quantum parameters. The full reconstruction conjecture in this perturbative sector asks first for independently renormalized bulk observables satisfying the quantum master and boundary Ward identities. Write \(\mathcal C_\partial\) for the geometric category of the admitted boundary conditions and their operators in the matched reduced current theory. Its geometric centre would represent coherent actions on this category, with their dependence on position, compatible with composition and collision. The local diagonal centre instead acts on the boundary field algebra \(\mathcal A_\partial\) at coincident points. For each admitted region \(U\), the conjecture asserts both equivalences in the diagram
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Obs}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(U)
&\xrightarrow{\beta_U} Z_{\mathrm{geom}}(\mathcal C_\partial)(U)\\
&\simeq\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{diag}}(\mathcal A_\partial)(U)
\end{aligned}
\]
where the first map is the coherent action of the independently defined bulk and the second identifies the two recipients. Both comparisons must preserve contacts, their homotopies and assembly of regions. Second, the admitted boundary objects should generate the prescribed physical sector, with its products of lines, vacuum and insertions. Third, there should be an intertwiner between independently defined bulk and boundary state spaces which carries the filling functionals and intertwines their sewing pairings. For an admitted filling let \(\lambda_{\mathrm{bulk}}\) and \(\lambda_\partial\) be those two functionals. In the identified amplitude line one should have
\[
\lambda_\partial(\beta_U O)=\lambda_{\mathrm{bulk}}(O)
\]
for every admitted insertion \(O\), and the same equality after sewing. At \(O=1\) it compares the absolute vacuum amplitudes. The conjecture is made in the fixed perturbative sector, with its specified cuts. Summing over topologies is a further physical question. [The state and the gravitational cap](#problem-gravitational-cap)

The first contour measured a derivative which vanished with constant weight. The three-boundary K3 experiment distinguished two products on which every specified trace agreed. The sourced neutral disk can be distinguished from the vacuum even though they have the same total charge, and its entire normalized law still leaves the absolute vacuum amplitude free. In each case a boundary calculation acquires its force from knowing which insertion was made, which operation was kept, and which states it can distinguish. For connected ordered coefficients there is a theorem that the cuts suffice. For the level-one disk one can give the independent preparations and compare them on every harmonic coordinate. For a gravitational filling the action, the states it can prepare, and the amplitude of a cut must all be identified in the same theory. That is what it would mean to recover an interior from its boundary.

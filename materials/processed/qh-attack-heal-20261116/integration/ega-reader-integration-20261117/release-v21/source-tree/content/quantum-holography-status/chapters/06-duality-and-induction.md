# Derived adjunctions and induction

How much of a representation can be recovered by mapping one chosen representation into it? Take a complex vector space \(M\) with a symmetry \(g\) satisfying \(g^2=1\). A map from the one-dimensional trivial representation to \(M\) is determined by the image of \(1\), which must be fixed by \(g\). These images span \(M^g\), the image of the projector \((1+g)/2\). Starting with a vector space \(L\), giving it the trivial action and then taking fixed vectors returns \(L\). Starting with a representation \(M\), doing these operations in reverse order gives the inclusion \(M^g\hookrightarrow M\). It misses every vector on which \(g\) acts by \(-1\).

For affine currents the chosen representation \(W\) is generated by a vector killed by the strictly positive current modes. The zero modes still act freely on its lowest-energy states. Maps out of \(W\), together with their higher extension groups, are computed by a resolution. The algebra acting on that resolution has modules of its own. Induction builds an affine representation from such a module, and mapping \(W\) into it provides the return map. Under the smooth realization hypotheses in (6.1), this return recovers every module complex at a complex shifted level outside \(\mathbb Q\), even one unbounded in both directions. The comparison in the reverse direction recovers the part of the affine category generated by \(W\). Which of these conclusions survives at other levels depends on the action of the positive currents.

## What the unit and counit measure {#adjunction-comparison-maps}

For a differential graded (dg) algebra \(B\), the ordinary derived category \(D(B)\) is obtained from complexes of \(B\)-modules by inverting quasi-isomorphisms. We retain its enhancement, so derived mapping complexes and their composition remain available. There is no bound on cohomological degrees. As before, differentials raise degree by one and \(L[1]^n=L^{n+1}\).

Let \(A\) be another dg algebra and let \(W\) be an \(A\)-\(B\) bimodule. Derived tensor and derived Hom give an adjunction
\[
\Phi=W\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-:D(B)
\rightleftarrows D(A):
\Psi=\operatorname{RHom}_A(W,-).
\]
Its defining property is the natural equivalence
\[
\operatorname{RHom}_A(\Phi L,M)
\simeq\operatorname{RHom}_B(L,\Psi M).
\]
The **unit** \(\eta_L:L\to\Psi\Phi L\) corresponds to the identity of \(\Phi L\); the **counit** \(\varepsilon_M:\Phi\Psi M\to M\) corresponds to the identity of \(\Psi M\). In degree zero the counit is evaluation. Derived resolutions supply its meaning on complexes. These are adjunction maps, distinct from an algebra unit or a coalgebra counit.

The two triangle identities are
\[
\varepsilon_{\Phi L}\circ\Phi(\eta_L)=1_{\Phi L},
\qquad
\Psi(\varepsilon_M)\circ\eta_{\Psi M}=1_{\Psi M}.
\]
They express compatibility of the two reconstructions. The functor \(\Phi\) is fully faithful precisely when every \(\eta_L\) is an equivalence. The functor \(\Psi\) is fully faithful precisely when every \(\varepsilon_M\) is an equivalence. An equivalence of the two whole categories requires both.

::: figure fig-adjunction-triangles

Already extension of scalars requires the derived tensor. Put \(R=\mathbb C[x]\) and \(K=R/(x)\). The free resolution
\[
\bigl[R\xrightarrow{x}R\bigr]\longrightarrow K,
\]
with the two free terms in degrees \(-1,0\), gives
\[
K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}K
\simeq\bigl[K\xrightarrow{0}K\bigr]
\simeq K\oplus K[1].
\]
Ordinary tensor retains only the degree-zero copy. The other copy records the relation \(x=0\). This is the simplest instance of the information carried by a bar resolution. Extension and restriction of scalars, with their derived adjunction, are described in the [Stacks Project, Example 22.33.6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BYX).

A right dg module \(P\) is **K-flat** if \(P\otimes_B X\) is acyclic whenever the left dg module \(X\) is acyclic. Such a module computes derived tensor on arbitrary coefficients. Dually, a complex \(J\) is **K-injective** if its Hom complex from every acyclic complex is acyclic. This condition concerns the whole complex; injectivity of each term alone does not suffice in the unbounded case. See the [Stacks Project, §13.31](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/070G).

### Which representations does the vacuum see? {#ch06-vacuum-generated-sector}

For the group algebra \(H=\mathbb C[C_2]\) of the two-element group, the projector \((1+g)/2\) splits the invariant subspace of every representation. Taking invariants is therefore exact. For the one-dimensional sign representation \(S\), maps from the trivial representation vanish in every derived degree. Yet \(g\) acts on \(S\otimes S\) by \((-1)^2=1\), so its invariants are \(\mathbb C\). Taking vacuum cochains on two inputs separately can lose a product whose output lies in the vacuum sector.

There is a corresponding positive statement for a strictly augmented dg bialgebra \(H\) over a characteristic-zero field \(F\), with an ordinary chain coproduct \(\Delta\). Let \(T\) be a locally nilpotent degree-zero algebra derivation and coalgebra coderivation commuting with the differential, with \(\varepsilon T=0\). For \(u\in F\), use \((e^{uT}\otimes1)\Delta\) for the action on \(M\otimes_u N\). Put \(C(M)=\operatorname{RHom}_{H}(F,M)\), \(B'=C(F)\). Translation on the bar resolution induces a cochain algebra automorphism \(\Theta_u\) of \(B'\). The translated cup product gives a natural map
\[
 C(M)\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{B'}{}_{\Theta_u}C(N)
 \longrightarrow C(M\otimes_u N).
\]
The subscript twists the left \(B'\)-action on \(C(N)\). Use full products for Hom and algebraic direct sums for the tensor totalizations. The map is an equivalence whenever either module is built from \(F\) by finitely many shifts, cones and retracts. This subcategory, \(\operatorname{thick}(F)\), is identified by \(C\) with the perfect \(B'\)-modules. Indeed the bar diagonal gives the map, its unit identities give an equivalence at \(F\), and exactness propagates it through the finite constructions. If \(F\) is compact as an \(H\)-module, \(C\) also preserves coproducts, extending the conclusion to the subcategory \(\operatorname{Loc}(F)\) generated using arbitrary coproducts.

In the sign example \(F=\mathbb C\) is compact, and \(S\) lies in the other idempotent summand. The map just displayed on \(S,S\) is \(0\to\mathbb C\). The same failure occurs with nonzero differential and translation. Tensor the group algebra with \(F[x,y]\otimes\Lambda(e,f)\) on primitive generators, with \(dx=e,\ dy=f,\ Ty=x,\ Tf=e\), and all other generator differentials and translations zero. The polynomial and exterior generators have degrees zero and one respectively. The homotopy taking \(e\mapsto x,\ f\mapsto y\) as a derivation contracts each positive total polynomial-plus-exterior weight after division by that weight. Hence the augmentation of this extra factor is a quasi-isomorphism. Both signs inflated from the group algebra give the same test at every \(u\). In the affine problem the regular horizontal top is what permits testing all horizontal highest weights.

## Affine currents and the algebra acting on their resolution {#ch06-affine-currents-and-the-algebra-acting-on-their-resolution}

Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a nonzero finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebra. Choose an invariant form \(\kappa\), normalized so that long roots have squared length two, and write
\[
\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}x\,\operatorname{ad}y)
=h^\vee\kappa(x,y).
\]
The integer \(h^\vee\) is the dual Coxeter number. For a scalar parameter \(k\), set \(\ell=(-k-h^\vee)\kappa\). The affine mode algebra \(A_\ell\) has relations
\[
[xt^r,yt^q]=[x,y]t^{r+q}
+r\delta_{r+q,0}\ell(x,y)1.
\]
The scalar \(-k-h^\vee\) is its **raw affine level**, the coefficient of the central cocycle in this normalization. Write \(s=-k\) for the **shifted level**, so that the raw scalar level is \(s-h^\vee\) and \(B=Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\). The mode algebra here has no degree derivation. Its internal energy grading assigns energy \(-r\) to \(xt^r\).

Put
\[
\mathfrak n=t\mathfrak g[t],\qquad
\mathfrak l=\mathfrak g[t^{-1}],\qquad
R=U(\mathfrak g),\qquad H=U(\mathfrak l).
\]
A module is **smooth** if each vector is annihilated by \(t^N\mathfrak g[t]\) for some \(N\), which may depend on the vector. Consider the induced smooth module
\[
W=A_\ell\otimes_{U(\mathfrak n)}\mathbb C.
\]
The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem identifies its underlying vector space with \(H\). Its energy-zero part is the regular \(R\)-module. This distinguishes \(W\) from the scalar-top vacuum
\[
V=A_\ell\otimes_{U(\mathfrak g[t])}\mathbb C,
\]
whose energy-zero part is \(\mathbb C\).

The polynomial dg algebra \(B=Y_k(\mathfrak g)\) has even generators \(b_m^a\) and odd generators \(c_{a,m}\), for \(m\ge0\). Fix a basis \(t_a\), write \([t_a,t_b]=f_{ab}{}^ct_c\), and sum repeated Lie-algebra indices. Its relations and differential are
\[
\begin{aligned}
[b_m^a,b_n^b]&=f_{ab}{}^cb_{m+n}^c,\\
[b_m^a,c_{b,n}]&=-f_{ac}{}^bc_{c,m+n},
&c_{a,m}c_{b,n}&=-c_{b,n}c_{a,m},\\
dc_{a,m}&=\tfrac12f_{bc}{}^a
 \sum_{r+p=m-1}c_{b,r}c_{c,p},\\
db_m^a&=mk\kappa_{ab}c_{b,m-1}
+\tfrac12f_{ab}{}^c
 \sum_{r+p=m-1}(c_{b,r}b_p^c+b_p^cc_{b,r}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Negative indices contribute nothing. The cohomological degrees of \(b,c\) are \(0,1\); their internal weights are \(m,m+1\). The differential preserves weight. The odd generators are often called ghosts; here that word means these specified degree-one algebra generators.

Write \(B_w\) for the entire weight-\(w\) complex. Then
\[
B=\bigoplus_{w\ge0}B_w,\qquad B_0=R,\qquad
I=\bigoplus_{w>0}B_w.
\]
There are two useful quotients:
\[
B\xrightarrow{q}H\xrightarrow{e_0}R,\qquad
q(c_{a,m})=0,\quad q(b_m^a)=t_at^{-m}.
\]
The map \(e_0\) kills the strictly negative modes. Thus \(p=e_0q\) is weight-zero projection and \(I=\ker p\). These quotients forget different information. An \(H\)-module inflated along \(q\) has zero ghost action but may have nonzero negative-current action. Inflation from \(R\) kills both.

The affine Chevalley resolution of \(W\) has
\[
P^{-p}=A_\ell\otimes\Lambda^p\mathfrak n.
\]
Its differential combines current multiplication with the Lie bracket. For \(x\in\mathfrak l\), let \(a_x\) be the exterior derivation induced by \(y\mapsto\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak n}[x,y]\). The generators act on this resolution through
\[
\rho(b_m^a)=R_{t_at^{-m}}-a_{t_at^{-m}},
\qquad
\rho(c_{a,m})=\iota_{a,m+1},
\]
where \(R_x\) is right multiplication on the affine factor and \(\iota_{a,m+1}\) is contraction with the indicated positive mode. This specified generator action is called the **native action**. The correction \(a_x\) is essential: its commutator supplies the half-Killing-form shift in \(\ell\). With the graded opposite composition, these operators give the algebraic derived endomorphism comparison. This construction is developed in *A derived endomorphism model for the line algebra*, the affine-resolution and generator-lift sections.

We must specify an additional smooth realization before using ordinary smooth derived Hom. Assume that the operators above are realized by a coherent right-\(B\) object \(\mathcal W\) in
\(\mathscr S_\ell=D(\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell))\), with underlying object \(W\). Assume the prescribed homogeneous endomorphism comparison and the compatible smooth Taylor resolutions
\[
P_N=(A_\ell/A_\ell\mathfrak n_N)
\otimes\Lambda^\bullet(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n_N),
\qquad \mathfrak n_N=t^N\mathfrak g[t],
\]
including their action comparisons on a common sufficiently large cutoff for each finite operator diagram. “Coherent” here includes all the higher compatibility homotopies for the right action. Finally assume the resulting enhanced adjunction
\[
\Phi=\mathcal W\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-:
D(B)\rightleftarrows\mathscr S_\ell:
\Psi=\operatorname{RHom}_{\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell)}(\mathcal W,-).
\tag{6.1}
\]
These are the smooth realization hypotheses used below. They identify a particular action and adjunction; they do not assert the desired unit theorem. They also keep smooth derived Hom distinct from derived Hom in all algebraic \(A_\ell\)-modules.

There is a parallel internally graded version. Modules are algebraic direct sums of their energy components, with arbitrary support. Set \(L\langle r\rangle_w=L_{w-r}\). Mapping complexes are formed at a specified homogeneous shift; their outer sum is algebraic. Neither convention introduces an energy completion.

## Cofree coefficients give the first unit theorem {#ch06-cofree-coefficients-give-the-first-unit-theorem}

The restricted positive-current dual
\[
C_{\mathfrak g}=\bigoplus_{E\ge0}U(\mathfrak n)_E^\vee
\]
uses the positive current degree \(\deg(xt^r)=r\). Each summand is finite dimensional, and every element of \(C_{\mathfrak g}\) has finite support in this degree. Put
\[
(L_x\phi)(u)=\phi(xu),\qquad
(D_x\phi)(u)=\phi(ux).
\]
Both actions lower the support bound of a functional. For a homogeneous positive-current basis \(x_i=t_at^r\), write \(c_i=c_{a,r-1}\). The complex
\[
\mathcal Q=C_{\mathfrak g}\otimes B,\qquad
d_{\mathcal Q}(\phi\otimes b)
=\phi\otimes db+\sum_iL_{x_i}\phi\otimes c_i b
\tag{6.2}
\]
has a pointwise finite differential. Its support filtration makes it semifree as a right \(B\)-module: the differential of a new free generator uses generators of smaller support degree.

The full affine action is restricted coinduction. Using the even \(H\)-action on the underlying graded algebra \(B\), identify \(\mathcal Q\) with
\[
\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_H(A_\ell,B):
f|_{U(\mathfrak n)_{\ge d}}=0\text{ for some }d\},
\qquad (af)(b)=f(ba).
\]
The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt decomposition gives the preceding tensor description. The twist in (6.2) makes its differential affine-linear at precisely the level \(\ell=(-k-h^\vee)\kappa\).

Under the smooth realization hypotheses, \(\mathcal Q\) is equivalent to the whole coherent right-\(B\) object \(\mathcal W\), with its affine action retained. Consequently it computes \(\Phi L\) for every ordinary unbounded \(L\). This is the cofree-model theorem in *The cofree native model and inflated-base unit for every simple Lie algebra*. The comparison preserves the specified action on \(P\).

If \(T\) is an arbitrary unbounded \(H\)-complex inflated to \(B\), every ghost acts by zero. Formula (6.2) becomes
\[
\Phi T\simeq(C_{\mathfrak g}\otimes T,1\otimes d_T).
\]
Its positive-current action is \(D\) on the first factor. The restricted dual with arbitrary vector-space coefficients is injective in the smooth positive-current category. More is needed for an unbounded \(T\): split its underlying complex over \(\mathbb C\) into its cohomology and a contractible complex. Tensoring this splitting with \(C_{\mathfrak g}\) is positive-current-linear. It exhibits the restriction as homotopy equivalent to a product of injectives placed in single degrees, hence as K-injective. At each degree only one of those factors contributes; no completion of \(C_{\mathfrak g}\) is involved.

The positive-current invariants are exactly \(\delta_1\otimes T\), where \(\delta_1\) extracts the scalar coefficient of \(U(\mathfrak n)\). Evaluation on this vector identifies the original signed tensor–Hom unit with the identity of \(T\).

**Conditional unit theorem for inflated coefficients.** For every simple \(\mathfrak g\) and every \(k\in\mathbb C\), under the smooth realization hypotheses of (6.1), the actual unit
\[
T\xrightarrow{\eta_T}\Psi\Phi T
\]
is an equivalence for every ordinary unbounded \(H\)-complex inflated along \(q\), and therefore for every inflated \(R\)-complex. This holds with arbitrary algebraic internal support or without an internal grading.

The theorem includes critical affine level. It also includes raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) level \(-3\). Its proof uses the zero ghost action on these coefficients; the cofree realization formula alone does not establish the unit for a general \(B\)-module.

## Finite descent from a quotient {#ch06-finite-descent-from-a-quotient}

To reach modules with nonzero ghost action, consider first a general split dg algebra extension
\[
R\longrightarrow B\xrightarrow{p}R,\qquad p|_R=1,
\qquad I=\ker p.
\]
Assume \(R\) is an ordinary algebra, \(B\) is K-flat on both \(R\)-sides, and for a finite integer \(N\),
\[
I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}N}\simeq0
\quad\text{as a complex of \(R\)-bimodules}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
The two multiplication actions are part of this assertion; \(R\) need not be central.

Let \(F=R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-\), and let \(U\) be restriction of scalars along \(p\), also called inflation. A relative bar resolution converts (6.3) into vanishing of the \(N\)-fold derived tensor of \(I\) over \(B\). For every \(L\), put
\[
T_j(L)=I^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}j}
\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L,\qquad T_0(L)=L.
\]
There is a functorial tower
\[
0\simeq T_N(L)\longrightarrow T_{N-1}(L)
\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow T_0(L)=L
\tag{6.4}
\]
whose triangles are
\[
T_{j+1}(L)\longrightarrow T_j(L)
\xrightarrow{u_{T_j(L)}}UF(T_j(L))
\longrightarrow T_{j+1}(L)[1].
\tag{6.5}
\]
Here \(u\) is the scalar-extension unit, distinct from \(\eta\) in (6.1). Every cofiber is inflated from an unrestricted \(R\)-complex with its actual outer \(R\)-action.

The bar argument explains the change of base from \(R\) to \(B\). Resolve an \(I\)-factor by terms
\[
\bigl(I\otimes_R I^{\otimes_R q}\otimes_R B\bigr)[q],
\qquad q\ge0.
\]
In an \(N\)-fold derived tensor over \(B\), each resulting bar term contains at least \(N\) copies of \(I\) tensored over \(R\). Such a term is acyclic by (6.3) and the two-sided K-flatness. Finite bar-length filtrations and their exact algebraic union prove the asserted vanishing without a cohomological bound.

For an exact enhanced adjunction \(\Phi\dashv\Psi\), the class of objects on which its unit is invertible is closed under finite triangles and retracts. Thus (6.4) proves:

**Finite descent theorem.** Under the split-extension, K-flatness and tensor-vanishing hypotheses above, the actual unit is invertible on every \(L\in D(B)\) if it is invertible on every unrestricted inflated \(R\)-complex.

The proof propagates the same natural transformation through (6.5). It never assumes that \(\Psi\) preserves an infinite coproduct. The construction and its map identifications are given in *A finite filtration from derived tensor nilpotence*, Theorems A–B.

Ordinary ideal multiplication cannot replace (6.3). For
\(B=\mathbb C[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), \(R=\mathbb C\) and \(I=(\epsilon)\), one has \(I^2=0\), but
\[
I^{\otimes_{\mathbb C}N}\cong\mathbb C
\qquad(N\ge1).
\]
The multiplication of two ideal elements vanishes; the tensor of those elements remains nonzero.

There is a second, weaker sufficient criterion. For a dg algebra map \(B\to H\), form its fibre \(J\to B\to H\) in the derived category of genuine \(B\)-bimodules. It suffices that one tensor power of the **fibre-inclusion map**
\[
\mu_N:J^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}N}\longrightarrow B
\tag{6.6}
\]
be zero as a bimodule morphism, together with invertibility of the unit on all inflated unbounded \(H\)-complexes. The tensor object in (6.6) need not vanish. A fixed bimodule nullhomotopy makes \(B\) a retract of \(\operatorname{cofib}(\mu_N)\); the latter is a finite extension of bimodules whose left action factors through \(H\). Tensoring this one diagram with \(L\) gives a coefficient-functorial retract and proves the unit theorem. See *Finite bimodule descent for the actual adjunction unit*, Theorem 1.1.

## The sharp exponent in every simple affine type {#ch06-four-factors-suffice-for-nonrational-mathfraksl3}

Let \(\Phi_0^+\) be the positive roots of the finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\), with long roots of squared length two. Put \(D=|\Phi_0^+|\) and take shifted level \(s\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Q\). Recall \(R=U(\mathfrak g)\), \(B=Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\) and \(I=\bigoplus_{m>0}B_m\).

In \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) the Weyl group permutes three coordinates. Exchanging adjacent entries in positions \(1,2\), then \(2,3\), then \(1,2\) sends the half-sum of positive roots \(\rho=(1,0,-1)\) through the sequence
\[
 (1,0,-1)\longmapsto(0,1,-1)\longmapsto(0,-1,1)
 \longmapsto(-1,0,1).
\]
The differences of the entries being exchanged are \(1,2,1\). All three pairs have now reversed order. The proof below identifies these differences with the weights of three successive positive-current factors and explains why a fourth factor cannot survive. In higher rank the same bound counts the positive roots.

**Theorem (sharp tensor exponent).** The first vanishing derived tensor power of \(I\) over \(R\) is \(D+1\):
\[
 I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}D}\not\simeq0,\qquad
 I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}(D+1)}\simeq0
 \quad\text{in }D(R\otimes R^{\mathrm{op}}).
\]
This is an assertion about an object with its two multiplication actions. It holds in the algebraic internal grading and after forgetting that grading.

Why should a root count bound a tensor length? A **horizontal Verma module** \(M_\tau\) is the \(R\)-module induced from a highest-weight line for a Borel subalgebra of \(\mathfrak g\). For highest-weight calculations the degree derivation is restored by its action \(-E\) on energy \(E\). At fixed input energy \(a\), induce a horizontal module \(N\) to the affine algebra, declaring its positive currents to kill its top. On horizontal category \(\mathcal O\), the complex \(B_m\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}N\) computes the derived positive-current invariants of this induced module in energy \(a+m\). Here category \(\mathcal O\) uses finitely generated weight modules with locally finite action of the positive Borel. At each energy the negative-mode and exterior-degree sums are finite. Putting the odd generators first in a Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis identifies them with the cochains on positive modes. The zero modes act on the left both on these cochains and on their coefficients, and act on the right by coefficient multiplication. These are the two actions used in the tensor power.

Fix an affine highest weight and let \(\mu\) be its horizontal part shifted by the finite Weyl vector \(\rho=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha\). A real root \(\alpha+n\delta\), where \(n\) is its loop mode, is integral at this weight precisely when
\[
  (\mu,\alpha)+ns\in\tfrac{(\alpha,\alpha)}2\mathbb Z .
\]
There is at most one such mode for each \(\alpha\). More is true. On the rational vector space spanned by \(1,s\) and the finitely many simple-root pairings, choose a rational-linear functional \(f\) with \(f(1)=0,\ f(s)=1\). Root lengths in the chosen normalization are rational. Thus the mode of every integral root is \(n=-f((\mu,\alpha))\). All the integral roots lie in the graph of one linear function on their projected root span. Reflection in that graph projects faithfully to reflection in a finite root subsystem. The integral Weyl group \(W'\) is finite and its longest length is at most \(D\). This step includes the short roots.

In a fixed affine block, horizontal linkage classes at their assigned energies are indexed by double cosets \(W_P\backslash W'/W_K\). The zero-mode integral roots generate \(W_P\), and \(W_K\) fixes a dominant representative of the affine weight. Each coset has a largest element for the Bruhat order, the order on Weyl elements defined by reduced subwords. The direction of transition can be read from highest-weight modules. A nonzero derived map between standard Verma modules requires downward linkage. This follows by resolving the source with projectives having Verma filtrations and applying the composition-factor criterion to the target. On reflection labels this forces a weak increase in Bruhat order. Taking the largest double-coset representative preserves that order. A surviving \(B_m\) with \(m>0\) increases the assigned energy, hence changes the coset and strictly increases the length of its largest element. Such a chain has at most \(D\) steps. The finite-block and Verma-embedding theorems used here are given in Fiebig's treatment of affine category \(\mathcal O\). [Theorems 2.2, 2.3 and 3.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305378v2).

To pass from tests on modules to actual bimodules, fix the positive weights \(m_1,\ldots,m_j\). Each \(B_{m_i}\) is bounded, free on either \(R\)-side in each degree, and locally finite for the adjoint action \(xv-vx\). The tensor word has finitely generated Harish–Chandra bimodule cohomology, meaning that it retains this local finiteness and both \(R\)-actions. If its highest nonzero cohomology were \(M\), a nonzero quotient of \(M\) with fixed right central character would survive tensoring with the corresponding dominant horizontal Verma by Bernstein–Gelfand's theorem on Harish–Chandra bimodules. Right exactness in the highest degree detects the original word. Thus vanishing on all these Verma tests implies bimodule vanishing. There are finitely many positive tuples at each total weight, and the algebraic direct sum of the acyclic words is acyclic. This proves the upper bound.

For sharpness choose the linear function \(d_0\) on the root lattice taking value one on each finite simple root. Write \(d_0(\alpha)=(\gamma,\alpha)\) and take \(\mu=\rho-s\gamma\). Every finite root now has its integral lift \(\alpha+d_0(\alpha)\delta\). All positive roots have positive mode, and the weight is regular. Follow a reduced expression for the longest Weyl element from the identity. At the \(j\)-th reflection the positive coroot pairing is an integer \(q_j\). Each step gives an actual injection of affine Vermas that sends its horizontal top into the degree-zero cycles of \(B_{q_j}\otimes_R M_{\tau_{j-1}}\). The composite of the injections remains nonzero. Multiplying the native degree-zero coefficients in the order \(q_D,\ldots,q_1\) acts on the initial top by this composite. The complex has no negative cochain terms, so its nonzero degree-zero cycle survives in cohomology. This realizes \(D\) positive factors on one choice of top.

**Conditional full-unit theorem.** At nonrational shifted level \(s\), in every finite-dimensional simple complex type, assume the smooth realization (6.1) with its specified right action, common Taylor cutoffs and enhanced homogeneous tensor–Hom comparison. For every ordinary unbounded \(B\)-complex \(L\) the original unit is an equivalence
\[
 \eta_L:L\xrightarrow{\sim}\Psi\Phi L .
\]
This holds for the ungraded adjunction and for algebraic internal grading with arbitrary support. Algebraically, (6.4) has at most \(D+1\) inflated \(R\)-complex factors. The scalar-extension functor \(R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-\) is conservative, since zero base change makes every map \(T_{j+1}(L)\to T_j(L)\) an equivalence and \(T_{D+1}(L)=0\). The affine assertion uses the additional fact that (6.1) has its unit invertible on every one of those inflated factors. The naturality squares and the finite triangles propagate precisely that unit to \(L\).

### Four factors for \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) {#ch06-a2-four-step-example}

For the trace form on \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) the raw affine level is \(s-3\). The positive roots are \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_1+\alpha_2\). The three adjacent transpositions computed above give the nonzero tensor word \(B_1\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}B_2\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}B_1\). The longest Weyl element has length three, so a word of four positive-weight factors would require one more strict increase in length. Thus
\[
 I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}4}\simeq0,\qquad
 I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}3}\not\simeq0 .
 \tag{6.7}
\]
The four-step tower below is this case of (6.4). It also follows from *The fourth derived tensor power of the native sl3 positive ideal* and *The full ordinary native unit for sl3 at nonrational parameter*. In type \(A_1\) two factors suffice. In \(A_3\) a surviving six-factor word has weights \(1,2,3,1,2,1\) in tensor order and every seven-factor word vanishes. In \(B_2\), \(G_2\), \(F_4\) and \(E_8\) the least vanishing exponents are \(5,7,25,121\), respectively.

## The image and its original counit {#ch06-the-image-and-its-original-counit}

Write \(\operatorname{Loc}(W)\) for the smallest full stable subcategory containing \(W\) and closed under arbitrary coproducts and retracts. In the internally graded case use all energy shifts:
\[
\mathscr C=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{Loc}_{\mathscr S_\ell}(W),&\text{ungraded},\\
\operatorname{Loc}_{\mathscr S_\ell}
\{W\langle r\rangle:r\in\mathbb Z\},&\text{graded}.
\end{cases}
\]
Under the preceding full-unit theorem,
\[
D(B)\simeq\mathscr C.
\tag{6.8}
\]
The source is generated by \(B\), or by all its internal shifts, using semifree resolutions. The left adjoint preserves coproducts and sends these generators to \(W\) and its shifts. Full faithfulness also lifts cones and retracts between image objects. These facts identify the image exactly with \(\mathscr C\).

For every smooth \(M\), the original counit gives the **coreflection** into this image,
\[
\Phi\Psi M\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_M}M.
\]
This means that for every \(C\in\mathscr C\), it induces an equivalence on derived maps from \(C\). To prove it, the second triangle identity and the full-unit theorem give
\[
\Psi(\varepsilon_M)=\eta_{\Psi M}^{-1}.
\]
The cone of \(\varepsilon_M\) is therefore killed by \(\Psi\), hence is right-orthogonal to \(\mathscr C\). In particular, (6.8) identifies a specified part of the smooth category; it does not assert that every smooth object lies there.

An object is compact in a category if maps from it commute with arbitrary coproducts. The free module \(B\) is compact in \(D(B)\), so \(W\) is compact **inside \(\mathscr C\)**, with all shifts included when graded. Compactness against arbitrary smooth targets would require an additional statement about \(\Psi\) on coproducts in the ambient category.

## Infinite extensions at other levels {#ch06-infinite-extensions-at-other-levels}

The right adjoint \(\Psi\) need not preserve sequential homotopy colimits. At critical level there is a telescope \(L\ne0\) with \(\Phi L=0\), even though the unit is invertible on each stage.

At critical raw level \(-h^\vee\), take \(B=Y_0\) and
\[
E=B\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}\mathbb C,\qquad \Phi E\simeq V.
\]
Assume the smooth realization and its actual lower-energy unit theorem. Critical vacuum cohomology supplies extension classes
\[
V\longrightarrow V[1]\langle-(n+2)\rangle,\qquad n\ge0,
\]
whose finite consecutive products are all nonzero. Their construction uses the quadratic central field and its successive derivatives. The level-deformation classes correspond to differentials of independent polynomial coordinates under the Frenkel–Teleman theorem: [*Self-extensions of Verma modules and differential forms on opers*, Theorem 4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401428).

Lift these classes through the unit on the individually lower-weight-bounded objects and form
\[
L=\operatorname*{hocolim}_{r\ge0}
E[r]\left\langle-\frac{r(r+3)}2\right\rangle.
\tag{6.9}
\]
A sequential homotopy colimit is the cone of \(1-\mathrm{shift}\) on the direct sum of its terms. Compactness of \(E\) detects a nonzero map \(E\to L\): its successive representatives are precisely the nonzero extension products. Thus \(L\ne0\).

After realization, the terms are shifted copies of the module \(V\), concentrated in different cohomological degrees. Every transition induces zero on cohomology, so \(1-\mathrm{shift}\) induces the identity on the cohomology of the direct sum. Its cone is zero. Consequently
\[
L\ne0,\qquad \Phi L=0,\qquad \eta_L:L\longrightarrow0.
\]
The lower weight bounds in (6.9) tend to minus infinity. Each finite stage keeps its valid unit. This is the theorem of *A critical-level kernel of the ordinary first-kind realization*, §§1–4, conditional on its specified smooth realization and lower-energy comparison.

Related extension telescopes give kernels at raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) levels \(-4,-5,\ldots\), and at raw level \(-2h^\vee\) for every simple type, under the corresponding realization and lower-energy hypotheses. They also show that \(W\) can fail to be compact even in its own generated category. The precise sources are *Actual-unit kernels at negative integral shifted sl2 levels* and *A noncritical ordinary-unit kernel for every simple Lie algebra*.

These ordinary-derived statements coexist with an all-type, all-level induction equivalence using different weak equivalences. For dg \(Y_k\)-modules that are termwise algebraically smooth for the even modes, twisted induction at raw level \(k-h^\vee\) has an explicit restricted twisted-coinduction inverse after inverting exactly the maps that induction sends to affine quasi-isomorphisms. That class is a proper subclass of the ordinary quasi-isomorphisms. This source localization therefore retains information that localization at all ordinary quasi-isomorphisms discards. This is the equivalence in *Corrected induction: the inverse, the adjunction and the equivalence*, Theorem 8.1. Its functor, level convention and source localization are part of the statement.

There is also a polarized duality for the universal mode algebra. Its input is a Tate Lie algebra with continuous bracket, an open linearly compact positive subalgebra with a countable basis of finite-codimensional ideals, countable-dimensional quotient, and a continuous central cocycle \(\eta\) vanishing on two positive inputs. Its smooth complexes at central generator one are taken modulo those coacyclic after restriction to the positive subalgebra. Here coacyclic means generated from totalizations of short exact sequences by triangles, coproducts and retracts. The dual consists of curved contramodules, with projective underlying graded contramodule and the corresponding Becker-coacyclic positive reductions killed. The curved differential on the cochain algebra has curvature \(+\eta\), and a free contramodule uses an inverse limit of direct sums in each total degree. Over \(\mathbb C\), the complementary category in the semi-infinite pairing is at affine scalar level \(-\ell_0-2h^\vee\) for original scalar level \(\ell_0\), or at Virasoro charge \(26-c\). For instance \(\ell_0=-h^\vee\) is a fixed point of the affine involution, while the cocycle \(m\ell_0\kappa(x,y)\delta_{m+n,0}\) still acts on opposite modes. The ordinary unit (6.1), these polarized quotients and the passage from universal modes to simple vertex-algebra quotients each require their specified functors.

### At rational shift the zero-weight test has no finite exponent {#ch06-rational-root-staircase}

For any rational shifted level \(s=a/b\), with integers \(a,b\), \(b>0\), choose a long root \(\alpha\). Both \(\alpha+b\delta\) and \(-\alpha+b\delta\) are positive affine roots. Choose an integral initial pairing \(u=(\mu,\alpha)\), and reflect in these roots alternately. In the first step the shifted coroot pairing is \(u+a\) and the new horizontal pairing is \(-u-2a\). The next coroot pairing is \(u+3a\). In general the \(j\)-th pairing and its energy increase are
\[
 q_j=u+(2j-1)a,\qquad m_j=bq_j .
\]
For any prescribed finite length \(N\) one can take \(u\) large enough that all \(q_j>0\) for \(1\le j\le N\). The required step is an **injection** \(M(\xi-q\beta)\hookrightarrow M(\xi)\) when a positive real root \(\beta\) has \(\langle\xi+\widehat\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle=q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Here \(\widehat\rho\) pairs to one with the affine simple coroots. Kübel's Theorem 4.1 gives the ordinary Verma composition-factor criterion. For the single-root injection one can argue directly, including at a critical or singular intersection. On the whole coroot hyperplane take the fixed finite PBW space of negative words of weight \(-q\beta\). The matrix of the simple raising operators on that space has polynomial entries in the highest weight. Existence of a nonzero singular vector is closed, by its maximal minors. Away from the critical hyperplane and the countably many other real-root hyperplanes, the ordinary noncritical block has just the two labels \(\xi,\xi-q\beta\). Fiebig's Verma-embedding theorem there gives a nonzero kernel on a dense subset of the coroot hyperplane, so it persists everywhere on it. Central level varies in this density argument. On specializing to the desired level, multiplication by any surviving singular vector injects the Verma, since the enveloping algebra of the negative part has a polynomial associated graded and hence no zero divisors. Only existence of a singular vector passes to this specialization.

These injections give a positive-weight word with nonzero composite on a horizontal highest-weight vector. This includes \(a=0,\ b=1\). Hence at every rational shift and in every simple type \(I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}N}\ne0\) for all \(N\). In fact the genuine \(B\)-bimodule map \(I^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}N}\to B\) is nonzero. Its restriction receives the \(R\)-tensor word by inserting zero-length terms at the intermediate relative bar resolutions, and the composite is exactly the tested multiplication. These finite nonvanishings exclude the descent proof through \(B\to R\). The ghost quotient has a different fibre, and neither finite test alone decides the unit at a rational level.

## Raw level minus three distinguishes the two quotients {#ch06-raw-level-minus-three-distinguishes-the-two-quotients}

For \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) at raw level \(-3\), the line algebra is \(B=Y_1\). Let
\[
V_j=V(-2j-1),\qquad
F_j=(B\otimes_RV_j)\langle-j^2\rangle,
\]
where \(V(\lambda)\) is the horizontal Verma module of highest weight \(\lambda\). There are actual degree-zero maps \(\beta_j:F_{j-1}\to F_j\) represented by positive-weight cycles. Their affine realizations send highest vectors to
\[
v_{j-1}\longmapsto
f_0^{\,2j-2}e_{-1}^{\,2j-1}v_j.
\tag{6.10}
\]
Here \(f_0\) is the horizontal lowering current and \(e_{-1}\) the mode-\(-1\) raising current. These are injective maps of affine Verma modules.

For \(L=\operatorname{hocolim}_j(F_j,\beta_j)\), the two quotients have different effects:
\[
R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L=0,\qquad
H\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L\ne0,\qquad
\Phi L\ne0.
\tag{6.11}
\]
Projection to \(R\) kills every positive-weight transition, and a telescope with zero transitions is zero. Projection to \(H\) retains the ghost-free word (6.10). The resulting system consists of injective Verma maps and has a nonzero filtered colimit; the affine realization has the same property.

Moreover, for \(I=\ker(B\to R)\), every finite map
\[
I^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}N}\longrightarrow B
\]
induced by the ideal inclusion is nonzero. Thus both the tensor-object and fibre-map descent routes through \(B\to R\) fail at this parameter. The proof is *A nonzero native telescope killed by the zero-weight augmentation*, §§2–4.

The unit on the telescope itself can be determined. There is an all-level sufficient condition under the smooth realization (6.1). Suppose a native complex has a strict dg \(B\)-module representative \(X\) with \(X^q=0\) below a fixed cochain degree. On positive currents the model (6.2) for \(\Phi X\) has terms \(C_{\mathfrak g}\otimes X^q\), which are injective in the smooth positive-current category. This bounded-below complex is K-injective there. Its positive-current invariants are \(\delta_1\otimes X\). Every \(L_{x_i}\) kills \(\delta_1\), so its differential on these invariants is \(d_X\). A smooth affine K-injective replacement restricts to a positive-current K-injective resolution, and evaluation of the tensor–Hom unit on the inducing vector is the inclusion of these very invariants. This proves that \(\eta_X\) is invertible. The hypothesis bounds the actual cochain representative. Internal energies can be arbitrarily negative.

Each \(F_j\) above has nonnegative cochain terms, so the cone of \(1-\mathrm{shift}\) representing \(L\) has no terms below degree \(-1\). Thus the same \(L\) satisfies (6.11) **and** \(L\xrightarrow{\sim}\Psi\Phi L\). It is detected by realization even though zero-weight base change kills it. More generally, in every simple type and at every \(s=a/b\le0\) rational, choose \(u+a<0\) in the alternating-root construction. All pairings \(u+(2j-1)a\) now stay negative. The embeddings point from the old Verma into the new one, with successively lower top energies. Inducing their horizontal top maps gives a telescope of nonnegative native complexes. Zero-weight base change kills all the transitions. Ghost base change retains their injective even-current maps, hence gives a nonzero union. The cochain bound again proves that this telescope has invertible unit. For positive rational \(s\), the finite staircase above allows the initial weight to depend on \(N\).

The fibre \(J=\ker(B\to H)\) retains a different obstruction. At \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) raw level \(-3\), let \(c\) be the weight-one ghost dual to \(ft\), where \([e,f]=h\). On a horizontal Verma top \(v_\lambda\), the differential between the unique weight-\((\lambda+2)\) vectors \(e_{-1}v_\lambda\) and \(c\otimes v_\lambda\) has coefficient, up to the common cochain sign,
\[
 f_1e_{-1}v_\lambda=(-3-\lambda)v_\lambda .
\]
At \(\lambda=-3\) this is zero. Thus \(c\) survives in \(H^1(B)_1\) and the first map \(J\to B\) is nonzero. For every simple type at every level, the highest long root gives the same test by taking \(\lambda(h_\theta)\) equal to the raw scalar level. Nevertheless any strict representative annihilated by a fixed \(J^d\) has good unit under (6.1), because the quotients of its finite ghost filtration are inflated from \(H\). In \(B/J^2\) the first ghost still acts nontrivially on cohomology, giving a concrete example beyond the inflated objects. At raw \(-3\) the unit also holds on \(B\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}T\) for arbitrary unbounded horizontal \(T\), by *A finite cofree resolution for arbitrary horizontal coefficients at raw level minus three*. Invertibility for **every** ordinary \(B\)-complex at this level remains open. At the parameters with a realization kernel, the full kernel, its localization and the essential image on the quotient also remain to be determined.

## Cellularity and derived torsion {#ch06-cellularity-and-derived-torsion}

There is another plausible description of the image in (6.8), expressed directly in positive-current modules. To keep its ideal distinct from \(I\subset Y_k\), put
\[
U_+=U(t\mathfrak g[t]),\qquad
\mathfrak a=\ker(U_+\to\mathbb C).
\]
A smooth module is **jointly augmentation-torsion** if every vector \(v\) is killed by one whole power \(\mathfrak a^r\). The exponent may depend on \(v\), but it must work for every element of that ideal power. Let \(\mathcal T\subset\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell)\) be this torsion category and set
\[
D_{\mathfrak a}=
\{M\in\mathscr S_\ell:H^q(M)\in\mathcal T
\text{ for every }q\}.
\]
The cellular category \(\mathscr C\) generated by \(W\) and its required shifts lies in \(D_{\mathfrak a}\).

Finite augmentation quotients explain a substantial converse. Since \(\mathfrak g\) is simple, \(U_+\) is generated in positive current degree one, and
\[
\mathfrak a^r=\bigoplus_{d\ge r}(U_+)_d.
\]
The quotient \(U_+/\mathfrak a^r\) is finite dimensional. The induced module
\[
E_r=A_\ell\otimes_{U_+}(U_+/\mathfrak a^r)
\]
has a finite filtration with factors
\[
W\langle-j\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb C}(U_+)_j,
\qquad 0\le j<r,
\]
where the last factor is a multiplicity space. Resolutions by such modules show that every complex whose terms are torsion is cellular. Every object with bounded-below torsion cohomology is also cellular. These statements, and the cellular coreflector on the whole unbounded smooth category, are proved in *Augmentation torsion and unbounded affine cellularization*.

The torsion inclusion has its own derived adjunction,
\[
i:D(\mathcal T)\rightleftarrows
D(\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell)):R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}.
\tag{6.12}
\]
It differs from both (6.1) and the inclusion of \(\mathscr C\). In particular, the exact inclusion of abelian categories need not induce a fully faithful functor on their unbounded derived categories.

This failure occurs already for positive-current \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), before adjoining negative affine modes. Let \(\mathcal S_+=\operatorname{Sm}(t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t])\), let \(\mathcal T_+\) be its joint-torsion subcategory, and put
\[
e_n=\frac{n(n+1)}2,\qquad
X=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\mathbb C[n]\langle-e_n\rangle.
\]
For the actual derived torsion adjunction one has
\[
H^0R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(iX)_0
\cong
\frac{\prod_{n\ge1}K_n}{\bigoplus_{n\ge1}K_n}\ne0,
\qquad \dim K_n=2n+1.
\tag{6.13}
\]
But \(H^0X=0\). Hence the unit of this torsion adjunction is not invertible. Its counit on the cellular object \(iX\) also fails to be an equivalence, so it cannot be the cellular coreflection there. Without an energy grading the corresponding quotient still injects into degree-zero cohomology. An explicit positive-current splitting transfers the failure to affine raw level zero; no transfer to arbitrary affine level is asserted. See *An unbounded joint-torsion unit failure for positive-current sl2*.

Finite quotients nevertheless admit a precise local-duality theorem. For an integer \(N\ge1\), put
\[
\mathfrak n=t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\quad
\mathfrak g_N=\mathfrak n/t^N\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\quad
R_N=U(\mathfrak g_N),\quad d_N=3(N-1).
\]
Let \(J_N\) be the augmentation ideal of \(R_N\) and
\(E_N^{\mathrm{cof}}=\varinjlim_r(R_N/J_N^r)^\vee\), with action
\((b\phi)(a)=\phi(ab)\). In the actual positive-current smooth category,
\[
R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(R_N)
\simeq E_N^{\mathrm{cof}}\otimes
(\det\mathfrak g_N)^\vee[-d_N].
\tag{6.14}
\]
The comparison identifies the actual torsion counit. In the graded convention the determinant-dual line has energy \(3N(N-1)/2\). The proof first uses finite-dimensional Lie-algebra duality, then compares the inflated counit cone through finite Chevalley complexes with bounded coefficient terms. It proves (6.14) for these objects, not a formula on arbitrary unbounded coefficients. See *Actual torsion of the smooth regular quotients* and its finite-quotient local-duality argument.

The full unbounded equality \(\mathscr C=D_{\mathfrak a}\) remains a separate question. Non-full-faithfulness of (6.12) does not decide that equality of object classes. Nor does termwise vanishing of a torsion functor prove vanishing of its derived value: a concrete quotient complex in the graded positive-current category has zero torsion in every term but is cellular and has nonzero actual derived torsion. K-injective replacement changes the calculation. This example is computed in *The graded quotient does not give an outside or kernel object*.

### A common current tail gives actual completeness {#ch06-common-tail-completeness}

Smooth-vector completeness is a different comparison from affine induction and from derived augmentation torsion. Put
\[
\mathfrak n=t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\qquad
\mathfrak n_L=t^L\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\qquad
F:D(\mathcal S)\longrightarrow D(\mathfrak M),
\quad G=R\operatorname{sm},
\]
where \(\mathcal S\) is the smooth positive-current module category and \(\mathfrak M\) is the category of all positive-current modules. Use algebraic integer-energy gradings, with currents lowering energy and maps preserving it. Products are taken energy by energy before the outer algebraic direct sum.

Suppose one tail annihilates all sufficiently low cohomology: there are \(L\ge1\) and an integer \(b\) such that
\[
\mathfrak n_LH^q(X)=0\qquad(q\le b).
\]
Then the actual unit is an equivalence,
\[
X\xrightarrow{\sim}GF(X)
\simeq\operatorname{holim}_n\tau^{\ge-n}X,
\]
with no bound on higher cohomology, multiplicities or energy support. For every smooth derived source \(Y\), the original map
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathcal S)}(Y,X)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathfrak M)}(FY,FX)
\]
is an isomorphism, including all shifts. [Common-tail completeness](#atlas-c-induction-common-tail-completeness).

The proof first restricts to a tail on which the cohomology layers are genuinely trivial. A sufficiently deeper restriction splits the Postnikov extensions coherently enough to identify the original completion map. If the same annihilating tail works in every degree, the whole restricted complex becomes noncanonically formal; \(M=4L-2\) is a sufficient deeper-tail index. With the hypothesis only for \(q\le b\), this formality statement applies to the lower truncation. No formality over the original positive-current algebra is asserted.

Completeness does not settle generation by the shifted trivial modules. Writing
\[
\mathcal L=\operatorname{Loc}\{\mathbb C\langle e\rangle:e\in\mathbb Z\},
\]
the equality of \(\mathcal L\) with the full subcategory of joint augmentation-torsion cohomology remains open. A potential nonzero torsion-cohomology object in \(\mathcal L^\perp\) may be complete and may have a common annihilating tail; its current action must be nontrivial in arbitrarily low cohomological degrees. The smooth derived category is itself not left-complete. The common-tail theorem locates a large complete class within it, rather than identifying completeness with cellularity. [The separate generation question](#atlas-c-induction-common-tail-generation-residual).

### Energy bounds for the smooth unit and the torsion counit {#ch06-energy-window}

Another criterion reaches both comparisons. Continue with positive-current \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), algebraic integer-energy gradings and smooth \(X\). Suppose \(H^qX\) vanishes below energy \(b_q\), taking \(b_q=+\infty\) for a zero group. Write \(e_p=p(p+1)/2\) as in (6.13). If, for each fixed integer \(t\),
\[
 b_{t-p}+e_p\longrightarrow+\infty\qquad(p\to\infty),
\]
then \(X\to GFX\) and \(iR\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}X\to X\) are equivalences, and \(X\) belongs to the localizing subcategory generated by the energy-shifted trivial modules. There is no common-tail hypothesis here.

The estimate comes from the minimal resolution for the tail \(t^N\mathfrak{sl}_2[t]\). At homological degree \(p\) its generators lower energy by at least \(Np+\binom p2\ge e_p\). For a cohomology layer with floor \(b_q\), a degree-preserving map from the resolution shifted to energy \(e\) can contribute to \(\operatorname{Ext}^p\) only if \(e\ge b_q+e_p\). This bound also applies to finite augmentation quotients by filtering them by shifted trivial modules. Thus for fixed output degree and energy the tests for both functors stabilize at a single finite truncation, uniformly in the tail and the quotient. The smooth unit follows first from the ambient module truncations. Its identification then permits the same finite calculation for the torsion counit.

For instance \(b_q\ge-cq^2-C|q|-D_0\) in sufficiently negative degrees suffices if \(0\le c<1/2\). At the quadratic boundary in (6.13) the layers instead have \(b_{-p}=-e_p\). Infinitely many align in energy zero, and the quotient of a product by a direct sum displayed there measures the failure of the torsion comparison. The object in that example is still cellular. The unrestricted question about generation by the shifted trivial modules therefore survives this calculation.

Under (6.1), nonrational affine induction identifies \(D(Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g))\) with \(\operatorname{Loc}(W)\) in every simple type, with energy shifts understood. To compare this category with physical lines one must construct their boundary module functor and its compatibility with fusion. A bulk insertion would give a natural operation on those modules. The complexes representing such actions are the subject of [Chapter 7](07-actions-and-centres.md).

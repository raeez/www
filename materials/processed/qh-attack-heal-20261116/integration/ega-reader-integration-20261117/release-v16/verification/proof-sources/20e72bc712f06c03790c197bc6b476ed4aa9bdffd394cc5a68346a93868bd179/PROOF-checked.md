## The theorem as it stands

The statement the chain started from ("Lemma H": for every rank N and non-zero hbar the coupling map
phi_N: U_N -> A_N is a homological epimorphism) is FALSE at every rank. The corrected theorem, proved in sections 1-5:

THEOREM (the junction with its flux class). Let hbar be a non-zero complex number and N >= 1. U_N is the bulk side at
flux N, A_N the brane algebra, phi_N: U_N -> A_N the coupling map and I_N its kernel; F_N = Sym^N(C[x]) is the N-particle
module, omega the class of the central extension of g; X_N = A_N (x)^L_{U_N} A_N (sewing) and E_N = RHom_{U_N}(A_N, A_N)
(reconstruction).
 (I)   The kernel. I_N is generated by one element, e_{N+1} (the (N+1)-st elementary symmetric polynomial in the J(x^k)),
       and I_N = I_N^2, i.e. Tor_1 = 0. For a number nu, U_nu/(e_{N+1}) is A_nu if nu is one of 0, .., N and zero otherwise.
       (Lemma 3.9, Theorem 5, Corollary 3.12.)
 (II)  Rigidity. F_N has no first-order deformation as a module of the Lie algebra g: none at fixed flux, and none to
       flux N + e. Every extension of F_N by a module M_tau of N particles with statistics tau, over U(g), is a split
       extension of A_N-modules. (Theorem 3, with two proofs; Propositions 3.3, 3.8; Corollary 3.10.)
 (III) What replaces sewing and reconstruction. The sewing map X_N -> A_N and the reconstruction map A_N -> E_N are
       isomorphisms in degrees zero and one (H_1(X_N) = 0, H^1(E_N) = 0), and neither is a quasi-isomorphism. In degree
       two H_2(X_N) = Tor^{U_N}_2(A_N, A_N) is non-zero, the cap product with omega maps it onto A_N, and omega.id is
       non-zero in H^2(E_N) and in Ext^2_{U_N}(F_N, F_N). The category of A_N-modules is a full subcategory of the category
       of U_N-modules, closed under extensions, and Ext^2 injects. HH^2(A_N; A_N) -> HH^2(U_N; A_N) is injective and
       omega.1 is not in its image: no first-order deformation of A_N receives a coupling of flux N + e. In the adopted
       algebraic frame these statements persist to every finite order in a deformation of the junction.
       (Theorem 4, Corollary 4.2.)
 (IV)  Rank one, exactly. U_1 = W (x) C, Tor^{U_1}_n(W, W) = W (x) T_n with T_n = H_n(gbar; W_sigma), computed by an explicit
       small complex; T_0 = C, T_1 = 0, T_2 = C.omega, and omega: T_2 -> T_0 is an isomorphism. With the central element
       not specialised, Tor_1 and Tor_2 of (W, W) over U(g) vanish. (Theorem 1, 1.7, 1.8, Theorem 4(e),(f).)
 (V)   Relation to the transport theorem of the neighbouring chain: Proposition 4.3.
 (VI)  Structure in every degree. Every Tor^{U_N}_n(A_N,A_N) is a possibly infinite direct sum of the simple
       Harish-Chandra bimodules D(tau) = (tau (x) W^{(x)N})^{S_N}, and is projective as a left and as a right
       A_N-module. The flux-cap surjection in (III) splits as an A_N-bimodule map, with kernel canonically
       Tor^{U(g)}_2(A_N,A_N). No finite-generation hypothesis on Tor is needed. (Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 6.)
Not proved (OPEN.md): whether the cap product H_2(X_N) -> A_N is injective for N >= 2; Tor_n for n >= 3 at any rank
(at rank one: T_n, n >= 3); the Tor groups with the odd partners. Not claimed: any comparison of derived
endomorphisms in low weights; any statement about the physical field theories.

## 0. Setting and notation
hbar is a non-zero complex number. W = W_hbar is the Weyl algebra C<x,y>/([x,y] = hbar); g = W as a Lie
algebra (commutator), gbar = g/C.1. For a complex number nu, U_nu = U(g)/(1_g - nu), where 1_g is the unit of
W regarded as an element of g (central); J: g -> U_nu is the canonical map. For an integer N >= 1,
A_N = (W^{(x)N})^{S_N} and phi_N: U_N -> A_N, J(f) -> sum_i f(x_i, y_i), is the coupling map (ghost number zero;
inscribe/fab-T85/ADOPTION.md, section 2). "Lemma H at rank N" is the statement that
A_N (x)^L_{U_N} A_N -> A_N is a quasi-isomorphism, i.e. Tor^{U_N}_n(A_N, A_N) = 0 for n > 0.

## 1. Rank one

**Theorem 1.** For every non-zero hbar, Tor^{U_1}_2(W, W) != 0. More precisely
Tor^{U_1}_n(W, W) = W (x) T_n as W-bimodules, with T_n = H_n(gbar; W_sigma) (W_sigma := W with the action
f.w = f w - w sigma(f), sigma the parity automorphism (x,y) -> (-x,-y)), and T_0 = C, T_1 = 0, T_2 != 0.
So Lemma H is false at rank one, and the first non-vanishing Tor group is in degree two. The non-zero
class in T_2 is the obstruction to changing the flux: the one-particle representation of U_1 does not
deform, even to first order, to a representation of U_{1+eps}.

The proof is 1.1-1.6; T_2 = C.omega is proved in 1.8. Exact computation in finite pieces of a filtration (1.7)
gives dimensions 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1 in degrees 0..5 for filtration 2 to 8 and 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 2 at filtration 10; these
are observations in truncations, not statements about T_3, T_4, T_5.
Published inputs: the Lie-derivation statement 1.5 is a case of Joseph's theorem (all derivations of the Lie
algebra W are inner); with the splitting 1.1 and obstruction theory it gives T_1 = 0 and T_2 != 0. New here, as
far as the search of STATE-OF-THE-ART.md shows: the identification of the Tor groups with H_*(gbar; W_sigma)
(1.2-1.3), the small complex and dim T_2 = 1 (1.7-1.8).

### 1.1 A Weyl algebra splits off  [P]
iota: W -> U_1, x -> J(x), y -> J(y), is an algebra map, because [J(x), J(y)] = hbar J(1) = hbar, and
phi_1 iota = id. On generators, [J(x), J(f)] = J([x,f]) = hbar J(df/dy) and [J(y), J(f)] = - hbar J(df/dx), so
ad iota(x) and ad iota(y) are commuting locally nilpotent derivations of U_1, with slices: delta_x := -(1/hbar) ad iota(y)
and delta_y := (1/hbar) ad iota(x) satisfy delta_x(iota x) = 1, delta_y(iota y) = 1, delta_x(iota y) = delta_y(iota x) = 0. Hence
[C: V. Bavula, arXiv:math/0604083, Theorems 2.2-2.3] U_1 is the direct sum of the iota(x)^a iota(y)^b C, C the common
kernel, i.e. U_1 = iota(W) (x) C with C := the centraliser of iota(W) (an explicit form of the splitting is in 1.2).
The same holds for U_nu, nu != 0, with [iota x, iota y] = nu hbar, and over C[e]/(e^2). phi_1 maps C into the centre of W, so phi_1 = id (x) eps for a character eps: C -> C. By the Kunneth
formula Tor^{U_1}_n(W, W) = Tor^W_*(W,W) (x) Tor^C_*(C_eps, C_eps) = W (x) T_n, T_n := Tor^C_n(C_eps, C_eps).

### 1.2 C inside U(gbar)  [P]
Notation: s := (+)_{d>=2} S^d (Weyl symbols without constant and linear part), g_+ := S^1 (+) s, so g = C.1 (+) g_+ and
gbar = C xi (+) C eta (+) sbar with xi = xbar, eta = ybar (they commute in gbar).
Psi: U_1 -> W (x) U(gbar), J(f) -> f (x) 1 + 1 (x) fbar, is an algebra map: the commutator of the images is
[f,g] (x) 1 + 1 (x) [fbar,gbar], and [f,g] in W equals (a lift of [fbar,gbar]) + omega(f,g).1, which is the
relation of U_1. (id (x) eps_{U(gbar)}) Psi = phi_1. Psi is injective: filter U_1 by the number of factors J(f), f in g_+,
and W (x) U(gbar) by the Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt degree of the second factor (W in degree 0); then
Psi(J(f_1)..J(f_k)) = 1 (x) fbar_1..fbar_k + lower terms, so gr Psi: Sym^k(g_+) -> W (x) Sym^k(gbar) is s -> 1 (x) s, injective.
theta := exp ad((x (x) eta - y (x) xi)/hbar) is an automorphism of W (x) U(gbar) (the derivation is locally nilpotent),
theta(x (x) 1) = x (x) 1 + 1 (x) xi = Psi(J(x)), likewise for y. Hence theta^{-1} Psi maps iota(W) onto W (x) 1 and C into the
centraliser 1 (x) U(gbar) of W (x) 1: C is isomorphic to a subalgebra C' of U(gbar), and eps is the restriction of
the augmentation of U(gbar). For f in S^d, d >= 2, the component of J(f) in C is mapped to
   gamma(f) = sum_alpha ((-1/2)^{|alpha|}/alpha!) xi^alpha (d^alpha f)bar + f(-xi,-eta)        (f homogeneous of degree >= 2; extended linearly to s);
gamma(q) = qbar for quadratic q. (For f = x^2 + 1 the formula would be wrong: gamma is defined on s only.)
**Lemma 1.2.** Let f_1, f_2, .. be a basis of s consisting of homogeneous elements. The ordered monomials
gamma(f_{i_1})..gamma(f_{i_k}) form a basis of C', and the monomials xi^a eta^b gamma(f_{i_1})..gamma(f_{i_k}) (and also the monomials
gamma(f_{i_1})..gamma(f_{i_k}) xi^a eta^b) form a basis of U(gbar). So U(gbar) is free as a left and as a right C'-module, with
basis xi^a eta^b.
Proof. Give fbar, f in S^d, the weight d - 1 (so xi, eta have weight 0). Since [S^a, S^b] lies in the sum of the S^{a+b-2-4m},
the weight of a bracket is less than the sum of the weights, so the weight defines an algebra filtration G of
U(gbar) with gr_G U(gbar) = Sym(gbar) = C[xi,eta] (x) Sym(sbar). gamma(f) = fbar + terms of weight <= d - 2. Give J(f), f in S^d
(d >= 1), the weight d - 1 and W the weight 0. Then gr U_1 = iota(W) (x) Sym(s) (the part of weight 0 is the Weyl algebra
generated by J(x), J(y)), and theta^{-1} Psi: U_1 -> W (x) U(gbar) is filtered: for f in S^d, d >= 2,
theta^{-1} Psi(J f) = sum_alpha (1/alpha!) ( (-1)^{|alpha|} d^alpha f (x) xi^alpha + z^alpha (x) (d^alpha f)bar ), whose part of top weight d - 1 is 1 (x) fbar.
Its associated graded map W (x) Sym(s) -> W (x) C[xi,eta] (x) Sym(sbar) is w (x) f -> w (x) 1 (x) fbar, injective with image
W (x) 1 (x) Sym(sbar). Hence gr(W (x) C') = W (x) Sym(sbar); so gr C' lies in Sym(sbar), contains the symbols fbar of the gamma(f), and is
a subalgebra: gr C' = Sym(sbar). The ordered monomials in the gamma(f_i), whose symbols are a basis of Sym(sbar), are therefore
a basis of C'; and the symbols of the monomials xi^a eta^b gamma(f_{i_1}).. are a basis of gr_G U(gbar). QED

### 1.3 The module W_sigma  [P]
By Lemma 1.2 (freeness) and Shapiro's lemma, T_n = Tor^{U(gbar)}_n(C, V_0) = H_n(gbar; V_0) with V_0 = U(gbar) (x)_C C_eps, a
cyclic gbar-module, free of rank one over C[xi,eta], in which the generator v satisfies gamma(f) v = 0. Solving
recursively, fbar.v = (f(xi/2, eta/2) - f(-xi/2, -eta/2)) v. The module W_sigma has the same property with
v = 1: f.1 = f - sigma(f) and xbar.w = xw + wx = 2xw on symbols, so xi/2 acts as multiplication by x. Both
modules are free of rank one over C[xi, eta] on such a vector, so V_0 = W_sigma. Hence T_n = H_n(gbar; W_sigma).
T_0 = W/<fw - w sigma(f)> = C (the twisted trace, Weyl symbol at 0) [C: Pinczon-Ushirobira, arXiv:math/0507092,
Proposition 3.4 and Theorem 3.7(2), for this module and its coinvariants].
(Check against Hochschild homology: for any W-bimodule M, H_n(gbar; M^ad) = (+)_{p+q=n} HH_p(W; M) (x) T_q;
for M = W_sigma, HH_*(W; W_sigma) = C in degree 0 [C: Alev-Farinati-Lambre-Solotar, J. Algebra 232 (2000), Proposition 5.1].)

### 1.4 T_1 = 0  [P]
T_1 = I_C/I_C^2 with I_C = ker eps, and hbar I_1 lies in I_1^2 for the kernel I_1 = W (x) I_C of phi_1
(frontier/fable/T85, Theorem 4(b)3', adopted in inscribe/fab-T85). So I_C = I_C^2.
Second proof, from 1.5 (given in STATE-OF-THE-ART.md): a linear form on I_C/I_C^2 is an eps-derivation d: C -> C; then
w (x) c -> w d(c) is a derivation U_1 -> W, its restriction to J(g) a Lie derivation of W, inner by Joseph's theorem;
an inner derivation of W vanishes on C, which acts on W by scalars; so d = 0.

### 1.5 The flux does not deform  [C; a second proof P]
**Lemma.** There is no linear map D: g -> W with D([f,g]) = [Df, g] + [f, Dg] for all f, g and D(1) != 0.
[C: A. Joseph, Derivations of Lie brackets and canonical quantisation, Comm. Math. Phys. 17 (1970) 210-232,
Theorem 4.1: every derivation of the Lie algebra W is inner; an inner derivation kills 1.] A short direct proof:
Proof. sp_2 = span(x^2, xy, y^2) acts on g and on W by ad, locally finitely and semisimply, and W = (+)_d S^d
with S^d (homogeneous symbols of degree d) irreducible and pairwise non-isomorphic. The condition on D is a
system of sp_2-equivariant linear equations, each involving finitely many finite-dimensional components
of D, and D(1) is invariant; so the invariant part of D (component by component) is again a solution with
the same D(1). An invariant D acts on S^d by a scalar c_d. Write the commutator of f in S^a, g in S^b as
sum_m hbar^{2m+1} B_m(f,g), B_m(f,g) in S^{a+b-2-4m}. The equation gives c_{a+b-2-4m} = c_a + c_b whenever
B_m does not vanish on S^a x S^b. From m = 0: c_a = lambda (a - 2), in particular c_0 = -2 lambda and
c_3 = lambda. From m = 1 on S^3 x S^3 (B_1 = P^3/24 != 0): c_0 = 2 c_3 = 2 lambda. So lambda = 0 and D(1) = 0. QED
(This is the non-triviality of the Vey class; it fails for the Poisson bracket, where c_d = d - 2 works. The
identity behind m = 1: [x^3, y^3]_* - 3 [x^2 y, x y^2]_* = 3 hbar^3, while the Poisson brackets cancel.)

### 1.6 T_2 != 0  [P, using standard obstruction theory]
Let R = C[e]/(e^2) and U~ = U(g)/((1_g - 1)^2), a flat R-algebra with e = 1_g - 1 and U~/e = U_1 (the
first-order part of the family U_nu at nu = 1). As in 1.1, U~ = W~ (x)_R C~ with W~ = R<X,Y>/([X,Y] = hbar(1+e)),
which is isomorphic to W (x) R by Y -> (1+e)y, and C~ a flat deformation of C. A C-module F lifts to an R-flat
C~-module iff an obstruction class o(F) in Ext^2_C(F, F) vanishes [C: W. Lowen, Obstruction theory for objects in
abelian and derived categories, arXiv:math/0407019, Theorem A; for modules it is read off the bar complex]. For F = C_eps a lift is a
character C~ -> R lifting eps; composing with W~ (x) C~ = U~ it gives a Lie map g -> W (x) R of the form
id + e D with (id + eD)(1_g) = 1 + e, i.e. a Lie derivation D with D(1) = 1, which does not exist by 1.5.
So o(C_eps) != 0, Ext^2_C(C_eps, C_eps) != 0; over a field Ext^2_C(C_eps, C_eps) = Hom(Tor^C_2(C_eps, C_eps), C) = (T_2)^*,
hence T_2 != 0. QED

### 1.7 The small complex and the computed groups  [P for the complex; X for the numbers]
**Lemma 1.7.** T_n = H_n(Lambda^* s, del), s = (+)_{d>=2} S^d, with
  del(s_1^..^s_p) = sum_{i<j} (-1)^{i+j} [s_i,s_j]_s ^ rest + sum_{i: deg s_i odd} (-1)^{i-1} 2^{1-deg s_i} s_i(ad x, ad y)(rest_i)
(i counted from 1; [ , ]_s the commutator projected to s; ad x = hbar d/dy, ad y = - hbar d/dx on s, projected to s, acting
on Lambda^* s as derivations).
Proof. t := C xi (+) C eta is an abelian subalgebra of gbar; it is not an ideal, and sbar is not a subalgebra
([S^3, S^4] has a component in S^1). Filter the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex Lambda^* gbar (x) W_sigma by the number of
factors outside t (the filtration of Hochschild and Serre for a subalgebra): E^1_{p,q} = H_q(t; Lambda^p(gbar/t) (x) W_sigma)
converges to H_{p+q}(gbar; W_sigma). On Lambda^p(gbar/t) (x) W_sigma, with W_sigma = C[x,y] in Weyl symbols, xi acts as
ad xi (x) 1 + 1 (x) 2x and eta as ad eta (x) 1 + 1 (x) 2y (x.w + w.x = 2xw on symbols), and ad xi, ad eta are commuting locally
nilpotent operators on Lambda^p(gbar/t). So the two operators form a regular sequence: the Koszul homology
H_q(t; -) vanishes for q > 0, and the coinvariants are Lambda^p(gbar/t) = Lambda^p s, a class lambda (x) P(x,y) being equal to
P(-ad xi/2, -ad eta/2) lambda (x) 1. The spectral sequence has one row, so T_n is the homology of (Lambda^* s, d^1). d^1 is
the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential computed modulo terms containing a factor in t and reduced to coinvariants: the
bracket terms give [s_i,s_j]_s, and the coefficient terms give rest_i (x) (s_i - sigma(s_i)) = rest_i (x) 2 s_i(x,y) for deg s_i
odd (zero for even degree), which equals 2 (-1/2)^{deg s_i} s_i(ad xi, ad eta)(rest_i) (x) 1. With the signs of the
Chevalley-Eilenberg differential this is the formula. QED
del is filtered by w = sum (deg s_i - 2), and gr(del) is the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential of the Lie algebra
sp_2 |x (polynomials of degree >= 3) with the Poisson bracket (Hamiltonian vector fields of the plane vanishing at
the origin).
Computed [X]. scripts/small_complex.py (hbar = 1, torus weight 0; ranks computed over the prime field with
p = 2^61 - 1 elements): del^2 = 0 in degrees 2..6; homology of the finite pieces F_w of the filtration:
   w = 2, 4, 6, 8:  dim H_n(F_w) = 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1 for n = 0..5;   w = 10: 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 2.
The same dimensions for w = 2..8 were obtained in exact rational arithmetic by an independent program
(REQUESTS/1-attack.d/homology_checks.py). The classes in degree 2 and 3 are omega_3 = x^3 ^ y^3 - 3 x^2y ^ xy^2 (weight 2)
and u_3 = x^2 ^ xy ^ y^2 (the fundamental class of sp_2; weight 0); neither is a boundary in F_10 (for u_3: the test
in the first version of the script was void, SUPERSEDED.md item 4; repaired and confirmed independently).
These are statements about finite pieces of a filtration over a prime field. They do not determine T_3, T_4, T_5:
the homology of F_w need not inject into T, and nothing is claimed about T_n for n >= 3. What is proved about T is
T_0 = C, T_1 = 0 (1.4), T_2 = C.omega (1.6, 1.8).

### 1.8 A second, independent proof of T_2 != 0, and dim T_2 = 1  [C + P]
The other family's attack on the same question (frontier/astra/T145/THEOREM.md, unchecked at the time of
writing, check started) proves Tor_2 != 0 at rank one by a different route, and the two proofs are
independent:
* Mine (1.5-1.6): the class is the obstruction to deforming the flux; it is non-zero because the Weyl
  algebra has no Lie derivation D with D(1) = 1.
* Theirs: the map Psi(a (x) b (x) f ^ g) = a omega(f,g) b on two-chains (omega the cocycle of the central
  extension g -> gbar) kills every boundary, and an explicit 54-term rational two-cycle Z has Psi(Z) = 1
  (their rank_one/global_tor2_certificate.json, verified there in two implementations). In the language
  used here Psi is the Gysin map S: T_2 -> T_0 of the central extension, i.e. the cap product with omega; so
  their certificate proves more than non-vanishing: S: T_2 -> T_0 = C is onto. They also give a second
  argument, which is the Lie-derivation lemma 1.5 proved in a different way (integrating D(x), D(y) and using
  the quadratics instead of averaging over sp_2), and they name the mechanism: the Groenewold-Van Hove
  obstruction. The name is historically apt, but the theorem that matches 1.5 exactly is Joseph's (1970): Gotay's
  form of Groenewold-Van Hove (arXiv:math-ph/9809015, Theorem 4) concerns quantisations of the Poisson algebra.
* Their record also contains Tor_1 = 0 at rank one with the odd partners, and at rank two the exact
  hbar-torsion exponent three of the cubic conormal classes.
**dim T_2 = 1 [P].** T_2^* is the space of sp_2-invariant relative two-cocycles of the small complex of 1.7, and

### 2.1 Hochschild form  [C, with the short proof]
U_N is a twisted enveloping algebra of gbar: generated by gbar with [a,b] = [a,b]_gbar + N omega(a,b). For a
U_N-bimodule B let B^ad be B with the action a.b = ab - ba of gbar (the two fluxes cancel).
**Lemma 2.1.** HH_n(U_N; B) = H_n(gbar; B^ad) and HH^n(U_N; B) = H^n(gbar; B^ad), naturally in B. For B = Q (x) P
(Q a left, P a right module) this is Tor^{U_N}_n(P, Q). [C: J. A. Guccione, J. J. Guccione, J. Algebra 243 (2001)
596-614, Corollaries 4.2.3 and 5.2.3, with A = C, the Lie algebra gbar and the scalar cocycle N omega.]
Proof. a -> a (x) 1 - 1 (x) a is a Lie map gbar -> U_N (x) U_N^op (the cocycles cancel), so U(gbar) maps to the
enveloping algebra U_N^e; by the Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt theorem U_N^e is free over U(gbar) on both sides and
U_N^e (x)_{U(gbar)} C -> U_N, (a (x) b) (x) 1 -> ab, is an isomorphism of bimodules. Hence
Tor^{U_N^e}(U_N, B) = Tor^{U(gbar)}(C, B) and Ext_{U_N^e}(U_N, B) = Ext_{U(gbar)}(C, B) (change of rings). QED
**Lemma 2.2** [C: Geigle-Lenzing; D. Pauksztello, arXiv:0705.0118, Theorems 3.7 and 3.9, applied to bimodules].
If phi_N is a homological epimorphism, then HH_n(U_N; B) -> HH_n(A_N; B) and
HH^n(A_N; B) -> HH^n(U_N; B) are isomorphisms for every A_N-bimodule B and every n. Conversely Lemma H at
rank N is the case B = A_N (x) A_N: Tor^{U_N}_n(A_N, A_N) = HH_n(U_N; A_N (x) A_N).
Proof. B (x)^L_{U^e} U = B (x)^L_{A^e} (A^e (x)^L_{U^e} U) and A^e (x)^L_{U^e} U = A (x)^L_U A, which is A by assumption. Dually
for Ext. QED

## 3. Every rank: the N-particle module does not deform in the flux

F_N := Sym^N(C[x]) = C[x_1..x_N]^{S_N}, the N-particle module of A_N (y_i = -hbar d/dx_i), a U_N-module through phi_N.
Ext^n_{A_N}(F_N, F_N) = 0 for n > 0 (Morita equivalence of A_N with W^{(x)N} # S_N, under which F_N is C[x_1..x_N];
Ext_W(C[x], C[x]) = C in degree 0).

**Definition (flux lift).** R = C[e]/(e^2). U~_N = U(g)/((1_g - N)^2) is flat over R with e := 1_g - N (U(g) is free over
C[1_g] by the Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt theorem), and U~_N/e = U_N. A flux lift of F_N is a U~_N-module M~, flat over R,
with an isomorphism of U_N-modules M~/eM~ = F_N. Equivalently: a module M~ over U(g) with an exact sequence
0 -> F_N -> M~ -> F_N -> 0 in which 1_g - N acts as the composite M~ -> F_N -> M~. Two flux lifts are equivalent if they
are isomorphic as U~_N-modules compatibly with the identifications modulo e.

**Theorem 3.** For every N >= 1 and every non-zero hbar, F_N has no flux lift: the N-particle module F_N does not
deform, even to first order, to a module on which the central element acts by N + e. (The statement concerns this
module; U_nu has other modules for every nu.)
Consequently Ext^2_{U_N}(F_N, F_N) != 0, phi_N is not a homological epimorphism, and Tor^{U_N}_1(A_N, A_N) or
Tor^{U_N}_2(A_N, A_N) is non-zero (Lemma 2.7). (By Theorem 5 in 3.6, Tor_1 = 0 at every rank, so Tor_2 != 0 at every rank.)

Two proofs are given. The first is 3.1 (every flux lift is one of an explicit one-parameter list of candidates),
3.2 (two identities on the vacuum) and 3.3. The second is 3.5 (Proposition 3.8): it uses only the first two steps of
3.1 in a simpler form and one cubic identity, and does not use the classification of 3.1 or the identities of 3.2.

### 3.1 The structure of a lift  [P]
Notation. p_k = sum_i x_i^k, Lambda_N = C[p_1..p_N] = F_N as a ring. D_a := sum_i x_i^a d_i (a >= 0), derivations of
Lambda_N. For a number c, Delta_c := sum_i d_i^2 + 2c sum_{i<j} (d_i - d_j)/(x_i - x_j), an operator on Lambda_N (the
Calogero-Moser Laplacian). For numbers m != 0 and c define a linear map Phi_{m,c}: g -> End(Lambda_N) by
   Phi_{m,c}(x^k) = m p_k,   L := (hbar^2/m) Delta_c,   Phi_{m,c}(x^a y^b) = (a!/(a+b)!) (-1/(2 hbar))^b (ad L)^b (m p_{a+b}).
Phi_{1,0} = phi_N (the action on F_N): indeed [y^2, x^a y^b]_* = -2 hbar a x^{a-1} y^{b+1} exactly, and J(y^2) = hbar^2 Delta_0.
**Lemma 3.1 (every flux lift is a candidate Phi_{m,c}).** Let M~ be a flux lift of F_N. Then there are t in C and an
R-linear isomorphism M~ = Lambda_N (x) R, reducing to the given identification modulo e, under which J(f) acts by Phi_{m,c}(f),
m = 1 + e/N, c = t e, for every f in g.
What the lemma says and does not say. It lists all candidates: a flux lift, if there is one, is equivalent to the
operators Phi_{1+e/N, te} for some number t, and no flux lift of any other form exists. It does not say that Phi_{m,c}
is a Lie map for some t; Theorem 3 says that it is not. The proof fixes the operators in the order J(x^k), J(x^a y),
J(y^2), all J(x^a y^b). At each stage it uses only relations that hold in U~_N and R-linear changes of the
identification M~ = Lambda_N (x) R, and it lists every parameter that remains free:
   after (1), (2):  J(x^k) = (1 + e/N) p_k;                         no free parameter (the charges mu_i are equal with sum 1);
   after (3):       J(x^a y) = - hbar D_a + w_a,  w a cocycle;      two free parameters b', t';
   after (4):       J(y^2) = (hbar^2/m) Delta_c;                    b' = 0 is forced, one free parameter t = - t'/hbar;
   after (5):       every J(x^a y^b) is determined by J(y^2) and J(x^{a+b});   no further parameter.
Proof. (0) Grading. [xy, x^a y^b]_* = hbar (b - a) x^a y^b exactly, and J(xy) = - hbar (E + N/2) on F_N (E the degree).
J(xy) is locally finite on M~: for m in M~ a polynomial in J(xy) sends m into eM~, and eM~ = F_N as a U(g)-module, so a
further polynomial kills it. Let M~_d be the generalised eigenspace for the eigenvalue - hbar (d + N/2). Then M~ is the
direct sum of the M~_d, M~_d is R-free of rank dim (F_N)_d, and J(x^a y^b) maps M~_d to M~_{d+a-b}. Every identification
made below is homogeneous for this grading (p_k has degree k); the vacuum v~ is a generator of M~_0, which is free
of rank one, and M~_d = 0 for d < 0.
(1) The commuting algebra. The operators J(x^k), k >= 1, on M~ commute; let P~ be the R-subalgebra of End_R(M~) they
generate. P~ v~ + eM~ = M~ (modulo e, Lambda_N.1 = F_N), hence M~ = P~ v~ + e(P~ v~ + eM~) = P~ v~. The map P~ -> M~, a -> a v~,
is also injective: if a v~ = 0 then a M~ = P~ a v~ = 0. So M~ = P~ as a P~-module, P~ is R-flat, and P~/eP~ = Lambda_N
(an element a with a M~ in eM~ has a v~ = e a' v~, so a = e a'). The map of R-algebras R[P_1..P_N] -> P~, P_k -> J(x^k), is
an isomorphism modulo e between R-flat modules, hence an isomorphism: P~ = Lambda_N (x) R, homogeneously, and
M~ = Lambda_N (x) R by v~ -> 1. Write J(x^k) = p_k + e Q_k, Q_k in Lambda_N homogeneous of degree k (k >= 1), J(1) = N + e.
(2) For f = x^a y: [J(x^a y), J(x^k)] = - hbar k J(x^{a+k-1}), so D~_a := -(1/hbar) ad J(x^a y) is an R-linear derivation
of P~ with D~_a(p_k + e Q_k) = k (p_{a+k-1} + e Q_{a+k-1}) for all k >= 1 (Q_0 := 1). Write D~_a = D_a + e D'_a. For a
polynomial phi(u) = sum phi_k u^k put P(phi) = sum_i phi(x_i), q(phi) = sum phi_k Q_k; for v = u^a d/du the first order
of the last identity is
   (*)  D'_v(P(phi)) + D_v(q(phi)) = q(v phi').
Fix a point x with distinct coordinates and let I_x be the ideal of {x_1..x_N} in C[u]. If v is in I_x, (*) at
x reads sum_i eta_i phi'(x_i) = q_x(v phi') (eta_i the coefficients of D'_v at x), for every phi; hence q_x vanishes on
I_x^2 (every element of I_x^2 is a sum of products v phi' with v, phi' in I_x, and every polynomial is a derivative), i.e.
q_x(phi) = sum_i (mu_i phi(x_i) + beta_i phi'(x_i)) with uniquely determined mu_i, beta_i, rational functions regular on
the locus of distinct coordinates (Hermite interpolation), and sum_i mu_i = q(1) = 1. Inserting this in (*) for
arbitrary v and comparing the coefficients of the independent quantities phi(x_i), phi'(x_i) gives D_v mu_i = 0 for all
v and D'_v = [xi, D_v], xi := sum beta_i d_i. The D_v span the tangent space at every point with distinct coordinates,
and that locus is connected; so the mu_i are constants, equal by symmetry: mu_i = 1/N. Then Q_k = p_k/N + xi(p_k); the
beta_i may have poles where two coordinates meet, but xi(p_k) = Q_k - p_k/N is a polynomial for every k, so xi is a

J(x^k) = (1 + e/N) p_k and D~_a = D_a. No freedom is left at this stage.

w_0 = 0. The relations [J(x^a y), J(x^b y)] = hbar (a - b) J(x^{a+b-1} y)
say that w is a 1-cocycle of the Lie algebra of the D_a with values in Lambda_N. A homogeneous cocycle is fixed by

### 3.2 Two identities on the vacuum  [P for the first; X for the second]
On functions of the power sums (d_k := d/dp_k, p_0 := N):

**Lemma 3.4 (a faithful rigid module detects deformations).** Let phi: U -> A be a surjection of algebras over a
field and F a faithful left A-module with Ext^2_A(F, F) = 0 and Ext^1_U(F, F) = 0. Then HH^2(A; A) -> HH^2(U; A) is

### 4.3 The exact finite-rank dual, and the relation to the transport theorem
(1) The exact finite-rank dual. A_N is Morita equivalent to W^{(x)N} # S_N, whose Koszul dual is the exterior algebra

reconstruction and sewing of the rank-N brane hold exactly. The junction K_N of the bulk is the restriction of the
exact kernel along phi_N [A: inscribe/fab-T85]. Theorem 4(b) describes this restriction on modules in degree zero: it is
bijective on Hom and on Ext^1 and injective on Ext^2, for all pairs of A_N-modules and at every rank; it is not
surjective on Ext^2 for the pair (F_N, F_N), where the cokernel contains the flux class. The classes that restriction
adds belong to the bulk side; the exact dual does not see them.
(2) The transport theorem. Inputs [A: inscribe/chain-brane-transport/ADOPTION.md, item 3; the text is
chains/brane-transport/PROOF.md, Theorems 8.3 and 8.6, with the repairs of that record]. Let k = C(h) and K_{N,k} the

### 4.4 What the obstruction class is, physically
(1) It is the quantisation of the number of branes. omega is the class of the central extension of the bulk
symmetry algebra; the family U_nu is the bulk side at flux nu, flat in nu. The rank-N brane algebra is the quotient of
U_nu at nu = N by the single relation e_{N+1} = 0 (Lemma 3.9), and that quotient is zero unless nu is one of 0, .., N
(Corollary 3.12): the brane does not move with nu. To first order: the N-particle module F_N has no flux lift
(Theorem 3), and no first-order deformation of A_N receives a coupling of flux N + e (Theorem 4(d)). The class omega.id
in Ext^2 is the first-order form of that integrality. In the stable limit (nu a free parameter, no trace
relations) there is no such obstruction.
(2) On the worldline it is the Vey class (the phenomenon of Groenewold, Van Hove and Joseph). In a flux lift every
particle would carry the charge 1 + e/N (Lemma 3.6). A particle of charge m satisfies the relations of the Moyal
Lie algebra only for m = +-1: the relation [y^3, g(x)] = - 3 hbar g' y^2 - (hbar^3/4) g''' fails by (hbar^3/4)(m - 1/m) g''', which is
the Vey cocycle, and no interaction between the particles can compensate, because an interaction changes only the
terms in g and g' (Lemma 3.7). For the Poisson bracket every charge is allowed. (In the first proof the same is seen
on the vacuum: the Calogero-Moser interaction cancels the cubic identity at N(N-1)c = 2e and the quartic one at
N(N-1)c = (15/4)e, never both; Lemma 3.2.)
(3) Sewing does not see it in low weight: in oscillator weights <= N the two-sided bar multiplication is exact
[A: inscribe/ast-T145, item 6], so the classes of H_2(X_N) have weight > N; at rank one the class omega has weight six
(the cubics x^3 ^ y^3 - 3 x^2 y ^ x y^2). The weight of the class for N >= 2 is not determined (OPEN.md).

## Counterpart

Brane side (what is proved). For N particles on a line (the two-matrix brane at rank N, ghost number zero) the
algebra of observables A_N is the quotient of the bulk-side algebra U_N by the trace relations, which begin in
weight N + 1 and are generated by one of them, e_{N+1} = 0. The N-particle state space is rigid as a representation of
the bulk symmetry algebra g: it deforms neither at fixed flux (in particular not into its Calogero-Moser
deformation) nor in the flux. The category of modules of the brane is a full subcategory of the bulk-side category,
closed under extensions, at every rank; it is not a full subcategory on the derived level: the self-extensions of
the brane over the bulk side contain, in degree two, the class of the central extension of g.
Bulk side (the corresponding statement, and its status). The bulk side at flux nu is a flat family in nu, and
its tangent at nu = N is the class omega. The statement proved here is that this tangent direction is not
tangent to any family of finite-rank branes: the quotient of the bulk side by e_{N+1} = 0 is non-zero only at the
integer fluxes 0, .., N, and the first-order junction equation phi_*(beta) = phi^*(a) has no solution for beta = omega. For
the one-loop deformation of the bulk the same equation has been verified on finitely many components only (the
adopted first-order identity: derivative of the brane's bracket in the Calogero-Moser parameter = bulk kernel, nine
commutators); if it holds globally its solution a is unique. Not proved: any statement about the five- or
six-dimensional field theories themselves; that the vacuum segment of the physical bulk line is A_N (x)^L_{U_N} A_N beyond
the algebraic frame of the adopted record; the Tor groups with the odd partners; the higher Tor groups.

## 5. Harish-Chandra structure of the sewing homology

All tensor products in this section are algebraic over C. Fix N >= 1 and hbar != 0, and put
U = U_N, A = A_N, R = W_hbar^{(x)N}, G = S_N and T_{N,n} = Tor^U_n(A,A).
The two commutative subalgebras of A used below are P_x = C[x_1,...,x_N]^G and
P_y = C[y_1,...,y_N]^G. For an A-bimodule M, ad p means m -> pm-mp.
Finite generation, when stated for a bimodule, means finite generation over A^e = A (x) A^op.

### 5.1 The adjoint action on the two-sided bar  [P]

**Lemma 5.1.** On every T_{N,n}, ad p is locally nilpotent for every p in P_x or P_y.
This holds in every homological degree and does not require finite generation of T_{N,n}.

Proof. Use the unnormalised two-sided bar complex

    B_n = A (x) U^{(x)n} (x) A,
    d(a[u_1|...|u_n]b)
      = a phi(u_1)[u_2|...|u_n]b
        + sum_{i=1}^{n-1} (-1)^i a[u_1|...|u_i u_{i+1}|...|u_n]b
        + (-1)^n a[u_1|...|u_{n-1}]phi(u_n)b.

Its homology is T_{N,*}, and the outer A-actions induce its bimodule structure. For p in P_x,
Newton's identities give a lift p~ in the commutative subalgebra of U generated by J(x),...,J(x^N).
Let D_{p~} be the chain derivation applying ad p to the two A factors and ad p~ to each U factor.
Let E_p be the outer action a[u]b -> pa[u]b-a[u]bp. These are not equal on chains.
Define the degree-one operator

    h_n(a[u_1|...|u_n]b)
      = sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i a[u_1|...|u_i|p~|u_{i+1}|...|u_n]b.

Then

    d h + h d = E_p - D_{p~}.                                      (5.1)

Indeed, terms multiplying two entries away from p~ cancel in pairs. The terms adjacent to p~
give minus the commutator [p~,u_i] in each interior slot, while the two endpoint terms give
a p[u]b-a[u]p b. These are exactly E_p-D_{p~}. Thus the endomorphism of homology induced by
the outer adjoint action is the one induced by D_{p~}.

Give J(x^a y^b) degree b, with Weyl symbols as in section 0, and use total degree on products.
The enveloping relations respect this nonnegative filtration. The commutator
[J(x^k),J(x^a y^b)] = J([x^k,x^a y^b]) has degree at most b-1. By the Leibniz rule,
ad J(x^k) lowers this filtration by at least one on all of U. Since the J(x^k) have degree zero,
the same is true of ad p~ for any polynomial p~ in them. On A use total y-degree in the N Weyl factors;
ad p also lowers it by at least one. On B_n the sum of these degrees is a nonnegative filtration,
and D_{p~} lowers it by at least one. Every chain has finite filtration degree, so D_{p~} is locally
nilpotent on chains and hence on their homology. Equation (5.1) gives the assertion for E_p.
Interchanging x and y proves the assertion for P_y. QED

### 5.2 Every sewing Tor bimodule is semisimple  [P + C]

For an irreducible complex representation tau of G put D(tau) = (tau (x) R)^G, as in Lemma 3.11.
These are pairwise nonisomorphic simple A-bimodules, with End_{A^e}(D(tau)) = C.

**Theorem 6.** For every N >= 1, hbar != 0 and n >= 0, the evaluation map gives an isomorphism
of A-bimodules

    (+)_{tau in Irr(G)} D(tau) (x) V_{N,n,tau}  ->  T_{N,n},
    V_{N,n,tau} := Hom_{A^e}(D(tau),T_{N,n}).                       (5.2)

The spaces V_{N,n,tau} may be infinite-dimensional. Every T_{N,n} is projective as a left
A-module and as a right A-module. In particular, the surjection of Theorem 4(c) splits in
the category of A-bimodules:

    T_{N,2} = A (+) K_N^{flux},
    S(a,k)=a,       K_N^{flux} := ker(S:T_{N,2}->A).                (5.3)

The identification in (5.3) involves a choice of section. The kernel K_N^{flux} is a direct
sum of the D(tau), is projective on each side, and is canonically isomorphic to
Tor^{U(g)}_2(A,A) by Theorem 4(f). At N=1 it is zero. Formula (5.3) does not assert that it
vanishes at larger rank.

Proof. Let M be any A-bimodule on which the two families of adjoint actions are locally nilpotent.
For m in M the cyclic sub-bimodule A m A has the same nilpotence property and is finitely
generated over A^e. It therefore satisfies precisely the hypotheses of the Harish-Chandra
classification [C: Berest-Etingof-Ginzburg, *Cherednik algebras and differential operators on
quasi-invariants*, Theorem 8.5 at c=0; Definition 3.2 and its spherical version after Lemma 4.4,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0111005]. Thus A m A is a finite direct sum of the simple D(tau).
In particular m lies in the sum of the simple sub-bimodules of M. Since this holds for every m,
M is its socle and is a semisimple A^e-module. This argument uses finite generation only for
each cyclic sub-bimodule, never for M. Schur's lemma and the stated classification now give
the evaluation isomorphism (5.2). Lemma 5.1 applies this conclusion to M=T_{N,n}.

For projectivity, write H=R#G and e=|G|^{-1} sum_{g in G} g. The Morita equivalence
A=eHe with H used in sections 2-4 makes eH a finitely generated projective left A-module
and He a finitely generated projective right A-module. The maps r -> er and r -> re identify
these modules with R on the indicated sides: their injectivity follows by taking the
identity-group coefficient, and their surjectivity follows from eg=ge=e. Hence R is
finitely generated projective on either side over A. Averaging the diagonal G-action
on tau (x) R is an A-bimodule idempotent with image D(tau). Thus each D(tau) is finitely
generated projective on both sides. An arbitrary direct sum of projective modules is
projective, proving the assertion for T_{N,n} without a multiplicity bound.

Finally, a quotient of a semisimple A^e-module is split. Apply this to S:T_{N,2}->A, which is
surjective by Theorem 4(c). Its kernel is again semisimple. The central-extension exact
sequence in Theorem 4(f) identifies that kernel with Tor^{U(g)}_2(A,A), and Theorem 4(e)
gives its vanishing at N=1. QED

At rank two this specializes to

    T_{2,n} = A_2 (x) V_{2,n,triv} (+) D(sign) (x) V_{2,n,sign}.

The flux map selects a nonzero linear functional V_{2,2,triv}->C and vanishes on the sign
summands. This is a structural decomposition, not a determination of the two multiplicities.
At rank one (5.2) recovers the bimodule form W (x) T_n of Theorem 1 without identifying T_n.

The published input classifies finitely generated Harish-Chandra bimodules; Lemma 5.1 supplies
the hypothesis for these Tor groups, and the cyclic-submodule argument removes the finite-
generation proviso in the earlier OPEN item 1(b). This is an application of that classification
and the elementary bar homotopy, with no claim of priority. The decomposition is a statement
about homology bimodules; it supplies neither a formality theorem for X_N nor its higher
sewing operations.

### 5.3 Base change and regular-module reconstruction in every degree  [P]

**Corollary 5.3.** For every left A-module Q and every n >= 0 there is a natural isomorphism

    Tor^U_n(A,Q) = T_{N,n} (x)_A Q.                                (5.4)

For the right-module reconstruction convention of section 4 there is a natural isomorphism
of cohomology groups

    H^n(RHom_{right U}(A,A)) = Hom_{right A}(T_{N,n},A).             (5.5)

The corresponding left-module statements hold with the handedness exchanged. Formula (5.5)
specifies the groups, not the multiplication or higher operations of the endomorphism algebra.

Proof. Derived associativity gives A (x)^L_U Q = X_N (x)^L_A Q. Its homology spectral sequence is

    Tor^A_p(T_{N,q},Q) => Tor^U_{p+q}(A,Q).

It is a first-quadrant spectral sequence and Theorem 6 kills all columns p>0. Its edge map
therefore gives (5.4) in every degree. For right modules, extension-restriction adjunction gives
RHom_{right U}(A,A) = RHom_{right A}(A (x)^L_U A,A). The corresponding first-quadrant
hyper-Ext spectral sequence has entries Ext^p_{right A}(T_{N,q},A) and total degree p+q.
Right projectivity from Theorem 6 kills all columns p>0, giving (5.5). QED

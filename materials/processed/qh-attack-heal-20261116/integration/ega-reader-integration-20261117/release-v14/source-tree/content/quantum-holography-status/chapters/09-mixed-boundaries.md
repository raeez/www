# Ordered boundary operations and mixed theories

Moving one insertion past another can change an operation even when the fields vary smoothly. The ordering region has a boundary, and differentiating its indicator produces a current on that boundary. When the insertions also have a holomorphic separation, this real boundary can meet a complex pole. The resulting contact term belongs to the operation.

The problem is therefore more precise than extending a formula to coincident points. We need a space of distributions on which the formula, its differential and its compositions are all defined. We also need to distinguish a collision in the complex coordinate from a collision in both the real and complex coordinates. These distinctions lead to the relative complexes and height homotopies in this chapter.

## A boundary current from a mixed field theory {#ch09-a-boundary-current-from-a-mixed-field-theory}

On \(M=\mathbb C_z\times\mathbb R_{\geq0,t}\), let

\[
d'=\bar\partial+d_t,\qquad \partial=dz\wedge\partial_z.
\]

A holomorphic–topological theory is holomorphic in \(z\) and topological in the real direction. In its cohomological description, the antiholomorphic and real derivatives enter \(d'\); holomorphic derivatives remain genuine operations.

An abelian example has fields \((\alpha,\beta)\), where \(\alpha\) is a form generated by \(d\bar z,dt\), while \(\beta\) has one additional factor \(dz\). An ordinary form of degree \(p\) in either summand has cohomological degree \(p-1\). The differential is

\[
Q_\kappa(\alpha,\beta)
 =\bigl(d'\alpha,d'\beta+\kappa\partial\alpha\bigr).
\]

Since \(d'\partial+\partial d'=0\), its square is zero for every \(\kappa\). The degree-zero fields are the partial connection \(a\) and holomorphic one-form \(b\). Their quadratic action density is

\[
\mathcal L_\kappa=b\wedge d'a+\frac{\kappa}{2}a\wedge\partial a.
\]

At \(\kappa=0\), this is the holomorphic BF presentation: the field \(b\) multiplies the partial curvature \(F=d'a\).

The integrated action requires compact support. The Batalin–Vilkovisky pairing pairs the two field summands by wedge product and integration and is independent of \(\kappa\). This pairing includes the odd ghost and antifield components of the complex; retaining only \(a,b\) would omit the differential needed for the quantum identities.

Impose the boundary condition \(\iota^*\alpha=0\), with \(\beta\) free at the boundary. A compactly supported contraction in the normal direction identifies the boundary observables with their supported bulk realization. This is an instance of the free bulk–boundary construction of Gwilliam, Rabinovich and Williams, [*Factorization algebras and abelian CS/WZW-type correspondences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07888). With residue integration normalized by \((2\pi i)^{-1}\int\), the resulting current has singular product

\[
J(z)J(w)\sim-\frac{\hbar\kappa}{(z-w)^2}.
\]

At \(\kappa=0\), that singular product vanishes, but the bulk pairing is still nondegenerate and all polynomial current fields and holomorphic jets remain. Thus the level of a boundary current is not the same datum as the nondegeneracy of the bulk field pairing. The construction and signs are developed in *Holomorphic BF theory and its boundary current*, particularly the theorems on the boundary map and current at every level.

## Fields as joint currents {#ch09-fields-as-joint-currents}

The boundary calculation fixes a local operator product. A distributional current model makes the support data for moving and composing such operations explicit. On the full space \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb C\), write \(X_I=(\mathbb R\times\mathbb C)^I\) for the external insertion positions. The variables in the coefficient distributions remain independent, even when two external points coincide.

This coefficient complex has two polarizations, called \(x\) and \(p\). An internal mixed form of degree \(r\) has cohomological degree \(r-2\) in the \(x\)-polarization and \(r-1\) in the \(p\)-polarization. On every compact set of external parameters, the \(x\)-variables have a common compact support. A \(p\)-variable can extend upward in height, but above a common collar height its current is independent of that height and has no \(dt\)-component. This is the meaning of *upward-proper support* here. Particle number is finite, and these finite operations are defined over \(\mathbb C[\hbar]\).

On one coefficient variable, the internal differential is

\[
Q(f_x)=(d'f)_x,\qquad Q(g_p)=-(d'g)_p,\qquad
\delta(f_x)=-(\partial_zf)_p,\qquad \delta(g_p)=0.
\]

With external forms placed first, the full differential is

\[
\mathcal D=d_{X_I}+Q+\delta,
\qquad
(Q+\delta)(\omega\otimes n)
 =(-1)^{|\omega|}\omega\otimes(Q+\delta)n.
\]

The notation \(d_{X_I}\) denotes the mixed exterior derivative in the position variables. The coefficient differential and its shifts are part of this formula.

The degree-zero ray and current families are denoted by \(\widehat W_i,\widehat J_i\); their degree-one descendant is \(\widehat p_i\). They obey

\[
\mathcal D\widehat W_i=-\widehat p_i,\qquad
\mathcal D\widehat p_i=\mathcal D\widehat J_i=0.
\]

They are smooth families of distributions in the internal variables. Normal ordering means multiplication in independent internal variables followed by particle symmetrization. It introduces no extra contraction at coincident external endpoints.

For \(n_{ij}=(t_j-t_i,z_i-z_j)\), the nonzero cross contractions are

\[
\langle W_i,J_j\rangle=-\hbar b_1(n_{ij}),\qquad
\langle p_i,J_j\rangle=\hbar Q_1(n_{ij}),\qquad
\langle J_i,J_j\rangle=\hbar E_2(z_i-z_j).
\]

Here \(E_m(z)\) is the principal-value current \(z^{-m}\), \(Q_m\) is a scalar one-form kernel, and, off \(z=0\),

\[
b_1(t,z)=\frac{1}{2z}
 \left(1+\frac{t}{\sqrt{t^2+|z|^2}}\right).
\]

The height distinguishes the two sides of a complex collision. At positive height its singular part approaches \(1/z\); at negative height it becomes regular near \(z=0\).

A scalar distribution \(K\) of finite order multiplies a smooth coefficient family \(F\) by the joint-test formula

\[
\langle KF,\varphi\rangle
 =\left\langle K,\ q\longmapsto
       \langle F(q),\varphi(q,\cdot)\rangle\right\rangle.
\]

Uniform support, collar and finite-order estimates make this a joint distribution. In particular, a relation between two expressions is an equality after all external and internal tests. A graph presentation is a way to compute the current, not additional data attached to it. This distinction is what permits the product in *Composition on actual Gaussian images and their finite contact strata* to accept its earlier output as a new input.

## Ordered poles and their complete differential {#ch09-ordered-poles-and-their-complete-differential}

Let \(H(t)\) be the Heaviside distribution and set

\[
r_m(z)=\frac{\pi(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}
 \partial_z^{m-1}\delta_{\mathbb C}(z)\,d\bar z,
\qquad
C_m(t,z)=\delta(t)\,dt\wedge r_m(z).
\]

Thus \(\bar\partial E_m=r_m\). The first bulk kernel is the distributional one-form represented off the origin by

\[
Q_1(t,z)=
\frac{\bar z}{2(t^2+|z|^2)^{3/2}}\,dt
-\frac{t}{4(t^2+|z|^2)^{3/2}}\,d\bar z.
\]

For every \(m\geq1\), define the higher kernels by holomorphic distributional differentiation:

\[
b_m=\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\partial_z^{m-1}b_1,
\qquad
Q_m=\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\partial_z^{m-1}Q_1.
\]

This is the same normalization as \(E_m\). The scalar ray identities are

\[
D b_m=Q_m+H(t)r_m,\qquad DQ_m=-C_m,
\qquad D=d_t+\bar\partial.
\]

The term \(H(t)r_1\) is the distributional contribution at the complex pole. Differentiating it gives \(C_1\), which cancels \(DQ_1\). This is the full identity \(D^2b_1=0\). Omitting the real wall would destroy it.

With many rays, the whole arrangement must be extended across its dependent collision loci. Multiplying independently extended factors on a common singular normal need not define a distribution. The scalar arrangement kernels use the following extension class.

Work on a coordinate space \(V=V_t\times V_z\), with \(V_t\) real and \(V_z\) complex. Let \(\mathscr L\) be the finite intersection-closed lattice of proper subspaces generated by the complex collision loci, mixed collision loci and gate walls. Its members are products of real-linear and complex-linear subspaces. At \(y\in V\), let \(L(y)\) be the intersection of the lattice members containing \(y\), or \(V\) if there are none, and let \(\pi_y:V\to V/L(y)\) be the quotient. If its real factor has dimension \(p_y\) and its complex factor has complex dimension \(k_y\), the quotient has mixed dimension \(p_y+k_y\).

A normal current \(\omega\) is bihomogeneous of bidegree \((\alpha,\beta)\) if, including its form factors,

\[
g_{\lambda,\theta}^*\omega
 =\lambda^\alpha e^{i\beta\theta}\omega,
\qquad
g_{\lambda,\theta}(t,z)=(\lambda t,\lambda e^{i\theta}z),
\quad\lambda>0.
\]

Its excess is \(\alpha-\beta\). A current is *tame* if near each \(y\) it has a finite presentation

\[
\sum_j\eta_j\wedge\pi_y^*\omega_j,
\]

where \(\eta_j\) are smooth mixed forms and \(\omega_j\) are bihomogeneous normal currents of nonnegative excess. It is *subcritical* if, at every point of the lattice union, these models can be chosen with \(\deg\omega_j<p_y+k_y\). This bounds the normal model degree, which can be smaller than the total degree after multiplication by \(\eta_j\). The whole scalar arrangement has a unique extension in this class agreeing with its regular kernel; see *Subcritical Q cycles and finite native Gaussian kernels*, equation (1.4), Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 3.2. This fixes the scalar extension; a physical principle selecting that extension is a separate question.

A supporting flat is described by a partition of the real coordinates and a partition of the complex coordinates. Compatibility means that the real partition refines the complex one: equality in height is imposed only within a complex block. Scalar rays and marked one-forms \(Q_m\) join distinct complex blocks. Gates \(H(h)\) compare real blocks inside a complex block. One also allows finite holomorphic derivatives of the top normal delta currents.

For a set \(A\) of marked edges, let \(p_A\) be the real rank of its height forms and \(k_A\) the complex rank of its complex forms. The nonzero admissible case requires \(|A|<p_A+k_A\) for every nonempty marked subset; violating this inequality gives the zero whole kernel. A critical contact has

\[
|A|=p_A+k_A-1.
\]

The two ranks can differ. For example, \(Q_1(s,u)\wedge Q_1(t,u)\) has two independent real normals and one complex normal. With

\[
\Omega_2=\pi\delta(s)\delta(t)\delta_{\mathbb C}(u)
 \,ds\wedge dt\wedge d\bar u,
\]

the complete gated current satisfies

\[
D\bigl[H(t-s)Q_1(s,u)\wedge Q_1(t,u)\bigr]
 =-\frac12\partial_u\Omega_2.
\]

The factor \(1/2\) is determined by the two complementary chambers. Their currents sum to the ungated current, whose derivative is \(-\partial_u\Omega_2\); interchanging \(s,t\) makes their two coefficients equal. This computation never assigns a value to \(H(0)\). A ray weight changes the residue: on the corresponding whole arrangement,

\[
D\bigl[Q_1(s,u)\wedge Q_1(t,u)b_1(s,u)\bigr]
 =\frac14\partial_u^2\Omega_2.
\]

These formulas illustrate the weighted normal theorem, rather than multiplication of an existing point contact by a singular ray.

Let \(\mathcal M_I\) be the actual finite span of these compatible scalar currents multiplied by finite normally ordered \(W,p,J\) families and smooth external forms, including all allowed holomorphic normal jets. The following closure theorem is the basic algebraic input to moving the operations.

**Theorem — finite marked Ward calculus.** With the stated compatible flats, internal gates, finite pole and jet orders, upward-proper coefficient support and canonical scalar extension, \(\mathcal D\mathcal M_I\subseteq\mathcal M_I\). For a scalar coefficient \(K\) with \(q\) marked factors, its derivative consists of four finite sums:

\[
DK=\mathcal B(K)+\mathcal S(K)+\mathcal V(K)+\mathcal C(K).
\]

Here \(\mathcal B\) replaces a ray by an additional mark with sign \((-1)^q\); \(\mathcal S\) takes the residues of entire complex-parallel scalar classes; \(\mathcal V\) takes the oriented real boundaries of whole simplified gates; and \(\mathcal C\) is the sum over closed critical marked flats. Its coefficients are the annular moments of the whole weighted, gated normal kernel. The derivative of \(KF\) also includes \((-1)^qK\mathcal DF\). Old normal degrees contribute their Koszul sign, and every residue differentiates the entire remaining coefficient and field family to the required finite normal order.

The proof classifies all active normal strata. Removing an entire vanishing scalar class leaves proper tangent factors; the critical-normal calculation supplies the missing mixed contacts. Finite-order estimates justify each transpose against a joint test. Since the formula equals the actual distributional differential, it respects every relation between presentations of the same current. The precise statement is *The finite marked Ward calculus on compatible collision flats*, Theorems 4.1 and 7.1.

The disjoint-label Gaussian product \(\star\) also closes on this space and is associative with its labelled signs. Its Ward defect

\[
\mathfrak W(X,Y)=\mathcal D(X\star Y)
 -(\mathcal DX)\star Y-(-1)^{|X|}X\star(\mathcal DY)
\]

is computable from the same formulas and is supported on cross complex collisions. Consequently \(\mathcal D\) is not a derivation of this product. The defect is retained as operation data.

## Relative currents and a radial homotopy {#ch09-relative-currents-and-a-radial-homotopy}

Let \(Z_I\) be the union of physical pair diagonals, where both height and complex position coincide. Write \(\mathcal M_Z\) for currents in \(\mathcal M_I\) supported there. The relative complex is

\[
\mathcal F_{\mathcal M}^n
 =\mathcal M_I^n\oplus\mathcal M_Z^{n+1},
\qquad
d_F(\alpha,\gamma)=(\mathcal D\alpha+\gamma,-\mathcal D\gamma).
\]

Its second component records the supported correction to the first. For example, if \(G\) is a gated complex normal current and \(F\) its smooth tangent coefficient, the pair

\[
(GF,-DG\,F)
\]

retains the real boundary of the gate. Its differential includes the derivative of \(F\); scalar closedness alone does not make the pair closed.

The decomposition by supporting flats supplies a projection \(P_Z\) on this finite current space. It need not commute with \(\mathcal D\). The correct chain map is

\[
E(x)=\bigl((1-P_Z)x,\mathcal DP_Zx-P_Z\mathcal Dx\bigr).
\]

With \(\mathsf R(\alpha,\gamma)=E(\alpha)\) and
\(\mathsf H(\alpha,\gamma)=(0,P_Z\alpha)\), direct calculation gives

\[
\mathsf R^2=\mathsf R,\qquad
1-\mathsf R=d_F\mathsf H+\mathsf H d_F.
\]

This retracts the displayed relative complex onto \(E(\mathcal M_I)\). It leaves purely complex intersection currents in the first component. It does not contract the entire ambient space of coefficient distributions.

A geometric homotopy requires an actual family. Consider the three-field circuit
\(:W_1^2:,\ :W_2J_2:,\ :J_3^2:\), together with any finite disjoint set of mutually contracting, initially unmarked \(W,J\) spectators. Set

\[
t_0=t_3,\quad \tau=t_2-t_1,\quad \sigma=t_3-t_2,
\qquad u=z_1-z_2,\quad v=z_2-z_3.
\]

Scale the circuit heights by

\[
t_1\mapsto t_0-\lambda(\tau+\sigma),\qquad
t_2\mapsto t_0-\lambda\sigma,\qquad
t_3\mapsto t_0,\qquad 0<\lambda\leq1,
\]

leaving spectator heights and all complex coordinates unchanged. At positive scale this gives a distributional pullback. Its extension to zero is constructed as a limit of the full family, including parameter forms. In particular,

\[
d(t_0-\lambda\sigma)
 =dt_0-\lambda\,d\sigma-\sigma\,d\lambda.
\]

The walls remain present:

\[
\delta(\lambda\ell)\,d(\lambda\ell)
 =\lambda^{-1}\delta(\ell)
    (\lambda\,d\ell+\ell\,d\lambda)
 =\delta(\ell)\,d\ell.
\]

The last equality uses \(\ell\delta(\ell)=0\); it does not discard the parameter differential by convention.

For this family the fixed relative support \(Z\) is the union of the three circuit mixed diagonals, times the full spectator parameter space. It does not include every spectator collision. The first component may acquire spectator contacts; only the second component is required to remain supported on \(Z\).

**Theorem — radial circuit transport.** On the actual span \(\mathscr S\) of the complete first and successive circuit contacts, their stated spectator compositions, smooth form multiples and finite holomorphic derivatives, the positive-scale pullback has a unique continuous joint-current extension to zero. It extends as a chain family to
\(\mathscr V=\mathscr S+d_F\mathscr S\). Its proper parameter integral gives

\[
H_F(\alpha,\gamma)=(h\alpha,-h\gamma),\qquad
h=\int_0^1\iota_{\partial_\lambda}\mathcal P\,d\lambda,
\]

\[
d_FH_F+H_Fd_F=\operatorname{id}-\operatorname{sp}_0.
\]

Both endpoints and the homotopy take values in the original ambient relative current complex with the same \(Z\). The three successive contact branches retain their full sum-zero relation, including unmatched fields, vacuum terms and all finite normal jets.

The source restriction makes the limit possible. Unmarked tangent coefficients are strongly \(C^1\) on the required joint-current stages. Positive-scale equality of two presentations then extends uniquely to zero, so the construction descends to actual currents. The chain identity on \(\mathscr S+d_F\mathscr S\) uses the combined full derivative, including the \(p\)-descendants. It requires no separate endpoint value for every term in that derivative.

There is a sharp limit to a stronger trace claim. For the whole kernel \(K_t=[b_1(t,v)^2]\), its symmetric second difference, tested against \(v^2\chi(|v|^2)\), contains

\[
\frac{1}{2(t^2+\rho^2)}.
\]

Its radial integral is \(\pi\log(1/|t|)+O(1)\). Thus a general \(C^2\) trace without a retained real normal is false. When every ray retains an independent spectator-height form, a stronger \(C^2\) theorem and continuous single-mark traces hold. The radial circuit theorem uses the weaker bounds it needs. Its endpoint still remembers the original gates, walls and labels; it is not an operation depending only on the fused point. See *A common radial height family for the first circuit and its spectator compositions*, Theorem 1.1.

## Changing endpoint profiles {#ch09-changing-endpoint-profiles}

A normal contraction also depends on a compact profile. Let \(p\) integrate along an interval and let \(s_a,s_b\) be sections defined by normalized profiles \(\theta_a,\theta_b\). Then

\[
T_{ba}=1+(s_b-s_a)p,\qquad
T_{cb}T_{ba}=T_{ca},\qquad T_{ab}=T_{ba}^{-1}.
\]

This transports the entire source and root map, not only their cohomology classes. A quantum operation must also transport its local pairing.

For a planar disk \(D\) and interval \(I\), take the endpoint coefficient complex \(\mathcal E_c=\Omega_c^\bullet(D\times I)[2]\) with
\(b(u,v)=\nu\int u\wedge v\), with \(\nu\ne0\). Put
\(A=(s_b-s_a)p\) and \(T=1+A\). Since both \(Au\) and \(Av\) contain \(dt\),

\[
b(Tu,Tv)-b(u,v)=b(Au,v)+b(u,Av).
\]

This defect can be nonzero. Choose a compact planar function \(\phi\), a compact planar two-form \(\psi\), and real distinct profiles, with \(\int_D\phi\psi\ne0\). For
\(u=\phi(\theta_b-\theta_a)\) and \(v=\psi\theta_a\,dt\), it equals

\[
\nu\left(\int_D\phi\psi\right)
       \left(\int_I(\theta_b-\theta_a)^2dt\right)\ne0.
\]

The bare symmetric extension of \(T\) therefore does not generally preserve the unchanged quantum differential.

There is an explicit corrected transport. Let \(\Pi_a,\Pi_b\) be the signed local primitives. The operator \(\partial_\Pi\) contracts pairs of symmetric field factors with \(\Pi\), and \(E_a=\exp(\partial_{\Pi_a})\) changes from quantum to creation coordinates. Retain the cumulative cross pairings \(c_a,c_b\). Under the actual identity \(T^*c_b=c_a\), the map is

\[
G_T=E_b^{-1}\operatorname{Sym}(T)E_a
 =\operatorname{Sym}(T)
    \exp\!\bigl(-\partial_{\,T^*\Pi_b-\Pi_a}\bigr).
\]

It acts on the full fixed-field completed kernels, transports each source \(\eta\) to \(T\eta\), and preserves the full source–target matrix differential. The contraction exponential is finite at fixed field number. A word operation \(f\) in these coordinates has linear part \(T_Y^{-1}fT_X\) and the corresponding pairing correction. It agrees with the unchanged operation only when the actual unmatched-map square and primitive identities hold. Independent changes in two disjoint input bands generally cannot be absorbed by one output profile shear. These statements are proved in *Local pairing defects and profile-labelled Gaussian operations*.

Higher refinement homotopies have their own continuity conditions. The normalized finite cubes and their evaluation mates act on mixed words with one Laurent lower bound before all state-energy and bar-length products. Profile paths and exponent distributions are algebraic at fixed indices. Finite coalgebra supports are chosen from right to left, before the still-free state products to their left; terminal coefficient and form slots remain independent until balancing. Constant finite-dimensional commuting charge profiles, including Jordan profiles, retain their full matrix Hom complexes on this domain.

This positive theorem is compatible with a concrete obstruction on the larger energy-product domain. In the neutral creation algebra, let \(J_{i,n}\) have energy \(n+1\), let \(c_{i,n}\) be a ghost of energy \(n\), and let \(s\) denote bar suspension. The specified normalized binary refinement cell satisfies

\[
\overline K\!\left(
 sc_{1,0}\mid
 s\!\left(3(z_1-z_3)^{n+1}(z_2-z_3)^{n+1}
             c_{2,0}J_{3,n}^{\,2}\right)\right)=s1.
\]

Here \(s1\) is the length-one bar state of the algebra unit; it is distinct from the empty word. The inputs tend to zero in the product over energy blocks, while the output is the same nonzero unit. Hence this cell has no continuous extension preserving those values to a Hausdorff target that retains the unit. In the logarithmic frame \(z_i=b+t^{-1}t_i\), the multiplier has order \(-2(n+1)\). The sequence therefore has no common Laurent lower bound. The obstruction concerns the larger source topology, and does not invalidate the normalized cubes and cap mates on their stated mixed Laurent domain.

## Two complex dimensions {#ch09-two-complex-dimensions}

The geometry changes when the holomorphic space is \(\mathbb C^2\). Its volume form \(dz_1\wedge dz_2\) couples two holomorphic directions, and current anomalies involve a cubic invariant rather than the quadratic level of a curve current. Higher current algebras and their free-field realizations are developed by Gwilliam and Williams in [*Higher Kac–Moody algebras and symmetries of holomorphic field theories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06534).

A concrete family uses internal formal variables \(x,y\), distinct from the spatial \(z_1,z_2\). Put \(\mathfrak m=(x,y)\subset\mathbb C[[x,y]]\) and

\[
V_N=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{N+1},\qquad
\mathfrak g_N=\mathfrak m^2/\mathfrak m^{N+2},\qquad
\rho_N(f)g=f_xg_y-f_yg_x.
\]

The estimate
\(\{\mathfrak m^a,\mathfrak m^b\}\subseteq\mathfrak m^{a+b-2}\)
makes the action well defined. Quadratic Hamiltonians preserve internal degree; higher ones raise it. On the first jet, the quadratic \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) action has
\(h=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), so \(\operatorname{Tr}(h^2)=2\) while \(\operatorname{Tr}(h^3)=0\). The full symmetric cubic trace vanishes on every \(V_N\). Degree-raising blocks cannot contribute to a closed trace, and the invariant pairings on the remaining quadratic blocks give the required reversal identity.

For every finite \(N\) and positive heat scale, this cancellation produces a nontrivial quantum higher Lie current map from
\(\Omega_c^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb C^2,\mathfrak g_N)\)
to the compact quadratic distribution observables of the free matter theory. Its components satisfy the master equation, with formal \(\hbar\) and completion in background weight. This is the finite-current theorem of *Hamiltonian jets and quantum currents*. The cancellation concerns complete scalar wheels; individual ordered traces need not vanish.

The ambient observables cannot simply be projected by deleting higher matter modes. For a removed dual pair \(b,g\) with Batalin–Vilkovisky bracket \(\{b,g\}=1\), deletion gives \(r(b)=r(g)=0\) but \(r(1)=1\). It is not a bracket-preserving map. The current-generated inverse systems use their own degree-triangular structure.

There is a further geometric distinction. The internal matrix action fixes the spatial point. For actual holomorphic \(F,g\), let \(H_F=(\partial_{z_1}F)\partial_{z_2}-(\partial_{z_2}F)\partial_{z_1}\). Taylor expansion in internal jet variables \(\xi_1,\xi_2\) gives

\[
j_N^+(H_Fg)
 =H_F(z)\cdot\partial_zj_N^+g
   +\rho_N(F_z^{\geq2})j_N^+g.
\]

Here \(j_N^+\) is the positive Taylor jet through order \(N\), and \(F_z^{\geq2}\) is the Taylor part of \(F\) of degrees two through \(N+1\). If \(F=z_1\), \(g=z_2^2\), and \(N=1\), the matrix term vanishes but the transport term sends \(2z_2\xi_2\) to \(2\xi_2\). A comparison with geometric Hamiltonian currents must retain that transport term and the relations defining holonomic jets.

## Compact comparison and prescribed lower homotopies {#ch09-compact-comparison-and-prescribed-lower-homotopies}

Local current identities can isolate a global comparison without settling it. In the six-field elliptic cubic construction, let \(A_\epsilon:N\to\mathcal Y\) be the original compact output and \(E_\epsilon:J_q\to\mathcal Y\) the separately specified evaluation. The subscript \(q\) labels a selected collision normal. The signed contraction \(N\rightleftarrows J_q\) has section \(j_q\), projection \(g_q\), and homotopy \(H_q\). A further root–central contraction \(J_q\rightleftarrows\mathscr G\) has section \(j_r\), projection \(g_r\), and homotopy \(h_r\). The target retains the central outputs, both harmonic components and the full parameter-form coefficients. Let \(\delta_u\) be the degree-one unreduced contact from the prescribed six-field source, and \(\delta_c=g_q\delta_u\) its projected contact. On their common actual domain, the comparison is

\[
A_\epsilon\delta_u-E_\epsilon\delta_c
 =\partial\mathcal L_\epsilon+\gamma_\epsilon g_r\delta_c,
\qquad
\gamma_\epsilon=A_\epsilon j_qj_r-E_\epsilon j_r,
\]

\[
\mathcal L_\epsilon
 =A_\epsilon H_q\delta_u+(A_\epsilon j_q-E_\epsilon)h_r\delta_c.
\]

The boundary \(\partial\) is the full Hom differential, so it includes the source differential on nonclosed inputs. The unresolved compact map is \(\gamma_\epsilon\) on the original supported contact image. A trace that kills its value would prove only a traced statement.

Finite polynomial Green-boundary limits and their four-supported compositions are available with fixed finite polynomial and jet degrees, the original order of width limits and all source-contact terms. Selected scalar unit and section rows also have vanishing objectwise first-comparison values. That scalar sector is not a subcomplex: its coefficient differential produces antiholomorphic descendants. Those descendants remain in the Hom identities.

The cylinder map from the canonical normal complex to the original normal complex is a separate required arrow, especially on unsupported homotopy outputs. A coordinate isomorphism of finite normal currents does not define it. A cubic compatibility for an operation \(\Omega=\sum_i\hbar^i\Omega_i\) and map \(P=\sum_j\hbar^jP_j\) retains the entire coefficient

\[
\Omega_0P_3+\Omega_1P_2+\Omega_2P_1+\Omega_3P_0.
\]

Likewise, a cubic homotopy must preserve the prescribed lower homotopies. If the Hom differential is \(\partial_0+\hbar\partial_1+\cdots\), correcting lower coefficients \(S_0,S_1,S_2\) contributes

\[
-\partial_1S_2-\partial_2S_1-\partial_3S_0
\]

at order \(\hbar^3\). These terms explain why a local normalization or one scalar contact cannot settle the relative cubic attachment. The remaining question is an equality of specified maps with their supported domains and lower homotopies. Analytic sewing asks for exactly this kind of control when the local constructions are glued.

# The strongest remaining questions

The preceding constructions leave several exact comparison problems. Some ask for a map between independently defined objects; others ask whether a known map is an equivalence on a larger domain. A counterexample to one proposed extension can coexist with a theorem on another domain. The problem statements below retain that distinction, so that a successful construction has an unambiguous conclusion and a failed comparison has a precise meaning.

The shared objective is to recover local operations, representations, states and amplitudes together. The questions are connected by that objective, but their coefficients and categories differ. A solution in one family transfers to another only through an actual comparison preserving the data named in the statement.

## The physical adjunction and its full domain {#problem-physical-adjunction}

**Problem 18.1.** Compare the functor defined by the prescribed physical boundary or interface with the tensor–Hom realization of the line algebra, including its right adjoint, unit and counit on the original complexes. Determine its essential image and its compatibility with the position-dependent product of line operators.

The algebraic starting point is strong. At nonrational \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) parameter, the fourth derived tensor power of the positive ideal vanishes and its third power does not. Under the stated smooth realization data, the actual unit is invertible on every ordinary unbounded line module. Its image is the localizing category generated by the regular-top affine object and all required internal shifts. The corresponding nonrational \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) theorem has a two-step descent. Inflated coefficients have a unit theorem for every simple type and level under their realization hypotheses. [Chapter 6](06-duality-and-induction.md)

The unresolved comparison is not supplied by those hypotheses themselves. One must identify the field-theoretic right adjoint with the constructed one, with its support, signs, action and higher naturality. For other simple types at nonrational parameter, a finite descent mechanism would need a theorem on the actual bimodule tensor powers or on the tensor powers of the fibre-inclusion map. Ordinary ideal nilpotence is insufficient.

The native precision-three example gives a specific action test. Its all-input dg quantum vertex fields induce ordinary local cohomology fields at every numerical level. Yet, at noncritical level, the two marked evaluations have the same nonzero derived bimodule obstruction for the specified quotient-inflated source action. A source resolution cannot remove it. The next physical comparison must change the obstructed action or recipient and compare the replacement with the original operations. [The field theorem and exact endpoint obstruction](index.html#ch17-native-r3-fields).

There is now a comparison before this physical step. Retained kernels give continuous evaluations on the ordered rooted carrier at every numerical level. At \(K\ne-2\), a homotopy with zero scalar pairing aligns the prescribed and field-defined evaluations through middle degree one. Let \(\Phi\) be the prescribed retained kernel, let \(\mathcal Y^{\sharp,1}\) be its first-root-corrected field counterpart, and let \(\mathscr C\) be the retained Hom subcomplex imposing homogeneity, both translation laws and zero scalar pairing. The higher retained question is
\[
[\Phi-\mathcal Y^{\sharp,1}]=0
\quad\text{in }H^0(\mathscr C).
\]
Its displayed representative vanishes on inserted ghost degrees zero and one. A reduction to the two trace-free state generators must still extend over every trace-current polynomial and trace-ghost monomial with these constraints. Scalar normalization does not give strict creation-unit preservation, and the correction need not begin at order \(\hbar^2\). Even a solution of this higher retained problem must still be extended to the full marked and whole-series coefficient source, the proper unit cones and the independent physical operations. [Retained kernels and the first rooted comparison](index.html#ch17-retained-first-root).

Raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) level \(-3\) provides a discriminating case. Its weight-zero quotient kills a nonzero telescope whose affine realization survives; every finite fibre-inclusion power for that quotient is nonzero. This rules out finite descent through that quotient. It leaves open the full unit theorem and the different descent route through the ghost quotient. Critical and specified negative levels have genuine realization kernels, so a theorem covering all levels cannot retain the same ordinary source category without accounting for those kernels.

The unbounded essential-image problem is equally precise. The cellular category, the full subcategory with torsion cohomology, and the derived category of the torsion abelian category are three different constructions. A proposed identification must survive the product-modulo-sum class of Chapter 6. Termwise torsion and bounded-below torsion cohomology already give positive cellularity theorems; those results do not remove the unbounded comparison.

For algebraically energy-graded positive-current \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), a common annihilating tail on all sufficiently low cohomology now makes the actual smooth-vector completion unit invertible. This does not prove generation by the shifted trivial modules: a nonzero torsion-null object could still be complete. [The common-tail theorem and its generation boundary](index.html#ch06-common-tail-completeness).

## General chiral quantum groups and completed curved duality {#problem-chiral-quantum-groups}

**Problem 18.2.** For the independently defined chiral field and mode constructions, construct a compatible associative product, same-position tensor, coproduct and exchange on the required category of representations. Identify the relevant curved dual category and the exact localization in which its comparison functors are inverse.

The spectral class already has explicit fields, a cross-contracted tensor product, level-indexed coproducts, their coassociativity and counit identities, and a field hexagon. The rational determinant-one matrix comparison holds in every rank \(N\ge2\) and at every numerical level, with divided integral relations and \(\hbar\)-adic fields. Nonlinear quadratic–linear–scalar algebras have derivative-corrected curved transposes. The regular universal \(W_3\) mode algebra has actual curved bar adjoints at every finite charge. [Chapter 12](12-chiral-quantum-groups.md)

The remaining module comparison must preserve the full mixed curvature, rather than only the regular half-ghost quotient. It must specify the summation monad, completed tensors and weak equivalences. A contramodule need not come with an underlying topology, and an ordinary state-field theorem does not decide those choices. For \(W_3\), the known smooth objects and iterated-continuous derived Hom provide concrete tests for the proposed localization.

Exceptional \(W_4\) fibres give another finite test. Their complete dimension table and three primitive products are known, including \(\eta_5\eta_7=0\). The products involving the extra classes, the positive torsion exponents and associative formality at those fibres remain to be determined. Replacing them by the generic exterior algebra would erase the observed jump. A construction of the full exceptional algebra must reproduce the known products before drawing a conclusion about formality.

## Universal actions and higher residue compatibility {#problem-universal-centres}

**Problem 18.3.** Decide whether multiplication and vacuum evaluation identify the full character and action spaces for every admitted pointed factorizing actor, and construct a comparison with the ordinary vertex-algebra mapping spaces wherever the two descriptions are claimed to agree.

For the geometric spaces of Chapter 7, the maps are
\[
\Theta(g)=m(g\otimes^!1),
\qquad
\Psi(\alpha)=\alpha(1\otimes^!u_B).
\]
Their types, pointings and homotopy fibres are fixed. Recognition is established on the stated unital, module-extension and nonunital families. The original pointed three-block boundary is filled for arbitrary unbounded coassociative actors without a cocommutativity hypothesis. A different separated-base primitive comparison uses coherent cocommutativity of the underlying actor. The general question still requires all root changes, collision restrictions and arbitrarily long refinements.

The ordinary binary exterior source has a separate complete theorem, including unbounded noncommutative targets. Its exact interpolation uses actual quotients and strict factorizations of transitions. To use that theorem in the geometric action problem, the comparison must retain both legs of roofs, evaluation and the full refinement diagrams. Identifying tangent complexes or singleton cohomology does not construct that map.

The residue complexes supply a concrete higher-dimensional problem. For
\(D_{r,q}(b,N;\mu)\), the strict statement is
\[
H^n(D_{r,q})=0\qquad(q\ge2,\ n<q),
\tag{18.1}
\]
with positive odd \(\mu\) and the specified pole bounds. It holds for three spectators with arbitrary nonnegative bounds, for two spectators, and for arbitrary spectator count when the marked–spectator bounds vanish; further fixed positive-bound cases are known. The uniform first compatibility ideal is also computed. The second and later compatibility defects at arbitrary spectator count remain distinct questions. A fixed-bound class at \(n=q\) prevents replacing the strict inequality by \(n\le q\), while its possible death under enlarged pole bounds remains compatible with an eventual comparison. [Chapter 7](07-actions-and-centres.md)

The genuine Heisenberg actor comparison has a more concrete remaining test. Let \(F^0\) be the whole zero sector of its charge and constant detector, and let \(U\) be the genuine pointed automorphism space of \(H\oplus\epsilon H\) acting trivially on state cohomology. The original \(q_B\) is an equivalence exactly when \(F^0\) is connected and \(U\) is contractible. Neither condition is settled; based loop calculations alone cannot see an additional zero-sector component. [Genuine fibre reduction](index.html#ch07-genuine-heisenberg-fibre).

## Integral collisions and independently completed intermediate objects {#problem-composition-domains}

**Problem 18.4.** Construct the comparison between the integral collision objects, the common radial current construction and independently defined nested tensor products, preserving the original rational evaluations and all incoming and outgoing actions.

Under the coherent realization, full-unit, bounded-below cellularity and two-term inflated-descent hypotheses stated in Chapter 8, its theorem covers every finite number of radial inputs on the declared \(F((q))\) domain and original jointly nilpotent smooth tests. The complete incoming bar has its own perturbative contraction; the outgoing realization uses a separate algebraic bar. Finite integral heads have actual derived base-change and collision maps. These are different pieces of the comparison, not absent operations waiting to be defined from scratch.

The obstruction is equally explicit: the radial field admits coefficient sequences for which substitution into a nested separation has no lower Laurent bound. A valid extension must prove a uniform support condition on its own space, or construct a different map that retains the same evaluations. Collision specialization must be performed before inverting the discriminant. An argument that substitutes a collision into the separated Laurent field has not reached the integral question.

The full three-pole rational tensorator and native joint comparison hold for arbitrary constant ordinary inputs under the retained realization hypotheses. Reaching the independently factorized endpoint retains finite-total spectator cohomology. For arbitrary constant \(T=T_{12}\otimes M_3\), there is nevertheless an actual common domain \(L_D^{\mathrm{fac}}=L_D\cap N_1\): at each outer Laurent coefficient the spectator has finite algebraic support across the inner series. It maps to the radial object and the independently completed pair-first object. Finite total cohomology of \(M_3\) makes its inclusion into \(L_D\) a quasi-isomorphism; the raw comparison cones remain, and the inflated regular \(U\)-module still gives a nonzero joint/factorized defect. [The defined carriers and common-domain theorem](index.html#ch08-factorized-common-domain).

Two independently completed pairs also have an algebraic comparison, with separate factorized, symmetric and ordered carriers. Finite total cohomology of both pairs gives equivalences after the specified extensions to either ordered field. The factorized conic intersection uses the smaller ring \(D_*=D_{\mathrm{con}}\cap\Lambda_{\mathrm{fac}}((q))\), defined by the conic and separated-cluster support conditions; it is generally not a module over all of \(D_{\mathrm{con}}\). The full four-pole rational tensorator has its algebraic bar identities. A native full-bar interchange, its compatibility with associativity in larger trees and its original topology remain further constructions. [Two independently completed clusters](index.html#ch08-incomparable-clusters).

The next current comparison must evaluate the full regular kernel on an incoming bar adapted to the factorized intersection, with support uniform in incoming bar length for each fixed outgoing word. It must also retain the original integral collision heads, a nonzero normal jet, the actual comparison cones and the native outer topology. Ordinary \(\operatorname{Hom}_D\) on arbitrary ring-defined tests fails quasi-isomorphism invariance; explicit finite \(W_D\)-cell models give a positive comparison without that false extension. [The test-category distinction](index.html#ch08-common-ring-tests).

Normalized-vacuum deletion is available in all three slots on full ambient field tests and constant ground-field tests for \(D\). The third slot retains its nonanchored output chart. Constant spectral inputs at any finite number of points have a flat chain connection, with outgoing horizontality up to specified homotopies. These gains leave the anchored test-coordinate comparison, integral deletion and opposite insertions, the normalized full-complex gauge with its inverse and action homotopies, and the current/native comparisons over polynomial parameter forms with all facets and interchanges. [Deletion and the spectral connection](index.html#ch08-deletion-and-spectral-connection).

For mixed boundary currents, the required domain also includes real height gates and distributional contacts. The finite marked Ward calculus, radial circuit transport and normalized endpoint refinements are established with their stated support and regularity. A general height trace without a retained real normal is false; stronger spectator hypotheses give positive trace results. The next comparison must retain these different domains rather than erase them by calling every operation a distribution. [Chapter 8](08-completions.md) and [Chapter 9](09-mixed-boundaries.md)

**Problem 18.5.** Extend analytic conformal-block composition to the required unsmoothed source and target spaces, with both descent conditions and all composition maps retained.

Under the normalized boundary hypothesis of Chapter 10—convergent algebraic four-point channel sewings, canonical spectral \(L^2\) channel vectors, the prescribed real unitary fusion transformation and normalized positive-label pentagon—and its two-radius estimates, the available analytic theorem constructs finite uncut tree compositions and identity insertions on specified completions. Its obstructions distinguish a missing diagonal limit from failure of descent through a quotient. A proposed unrestricted sewing theorem must control the actual noncut domains, outgoing quotient and convergence of the original maps. Compatibility on a dense algebraic core is not sufficient without the continuity and closedness statements used in the extension. [Chapter 10](10-analytic-sewing.md)

## The compact elliptic attachment {#problem-elliptic-attachment}

**Problem 18.6.** Identify the original compact output with the independently specified elliptic evaluation on the full supported contact image, preserving the cylinder comparison and all prescribed lower homotopies.

The reduction in Chapter 9 isolates the actual remaining map:
\[
A_\epsilon\delta_u-E_\epsilon\delta_c
=\partial\mathcal L_\epsilon
+\gamma_\epsilon g_r\delta_c,
\qquad
\gamma_\epsilon=A_\epsilon j_qj_r-E_\epsilon j_r.
\tag{18.2}
\]
All terms have the original coefficient and support domains described there. Thus the task is to control \(\gamma_\epsilon\) on that image and to provide the cylinder map on the homotopy outputs where it is needed. A coordinate identification of finite normal spaces is not that cylinder map.

The full Hom differential retains the antiholomorphic descendants of scalar rows. At cubic order, every term in
\[
\Omega_0P_3+\Omega_1P_2+\Omega_2P_1+\Omega_3P_0
\]
survives unless an actual identity removes it. Adjusting lower homotopies contributes
\(-\partial_1S_2-\partial_2S_1-\partial_3S_0\). The finite polynomial boundary limits, supported compositions and vanishing scalar comparison values remain useful theorems. They do not determine these remaining maps after a scalar trace has discarded their outputs.

## The gravitational state, line and cap {#problem-gravitational-cap}

**Problem 18.7.** Compare the numerical bulk state, the original based vacuum line and the state prepared by the prescribed geometric filling, on a common field and source domain and with cutting and gluing preserved.

The normalized numerical Gaussian and its full graded Wick moments are constructed on the original continued cycles. The coefficient heat has a finite part and a based local line section, with the correct covariant source counterterm. The ordinary zero-height finite-stage Ward identity and first quantum heat cells retain the complete first transport term. None of these results needs to be replaced by a formal covariance chosen after the integral. [Chapter 11](11-states-and-lines.md)

On the square-zero reflected Ward family, a common gapped positive-level interval and the fixed-scale uniform inserted heat expansion give the relative formula \(W_{\mathrm{fp}}(x_1)-W_{\mathrm{fp}}(x_0)=\int_{x_0}^{x_1}c_F(x)\,dx/x\). The absolute Mellin comparison retains \(\gamma_E C\). This positive-interval theorem supplies neither a zero-level endpoint estimate nor an independent geometric cap. On a sufficiently small common based family the local line and normalized numerical state do form a continuous line-valued functional; identifying it with the cap remains the stated problem. [Positive-level variation and its endpoint](index.html#ch11-positive-level-variation).

The missing cap comparison must supply the map from uncut coefficient fields to the fields on the geometric cut, identify the amplitude lines and transport the normalized observable functionals together with the base amplitude. A global line statement must determine holonomy and anomaly data beyond one local source neighbourhood. An interacting boundary theorem must extend the insertion identities to its actual normal derivatives, positive-height roots, singular higher collisions and quantum orders. The existing ordinary unary result does not assert those extensions.

One precise issue is the relation between formal perturbation and the numerical cycle limit. At a finite spectral window, the covariance correction and determinant-line ratio give an actual formal comparison. An infinite-dimensional conclusion needs convergence of the original series in the same topology and identification of its sum with the numerical state. Equality of finite formal coefficients alone does not establish that analytic statement.

## Brane junctions and geometric state operations {#problem-branes-and-compact-states}

**Problem 18.8.** Construct the open–closed map from the independently specified bulk fields to the derived operations on branes and interfaces, preserving both outer actions, junction composition and the flux class.

For the M2 particle junction, the degree-two cap onto \(A_N\) is a split bimodule surjection at every positive rank and nonzero numerical \(\hbar\). Its kernel multiplicities for \(N\ge2\) and the higher algebra structure on the full derived tensor product remain unknown. The cap does not make the derived multiplication an equivalence. The integral curved matrix comparison and polynomial Hamiltonian transport supply different positive constructions on their declared rank and degree ranges. The physical comparison must carry the actual fields into these objects. [Chapter 14](14-branes.md)

For the D3 sector, the exact chiral image and simplicity are known, together with specific higher torsion and nonseparated phenomena. A comparison to an independently defined Kodaira–Spencer bulk must preserve that derived information. For surface fusion, the rank-addition Miura map is algebraically coassociative, but its identification with the five-dimensional fusion operation needs the quantized defect, Ward identities and normalization. Higher-current and celestial constructions likewise have explicit finite or special nonabelian scopes; their extension must retain the central and anomaly terms present there.

**Problem 18.9.** Compare the critical Hall coefficient theory with the full compact state theory before taking traces, preserving orientation roots, relative moment values, the ordinary unit, pairing and reduced three-boundary operation.

The ordinary K3 composition Hall comparison is faithful, strong orientation exists on the stated two-charge component group, and the spherical reduced coefficient maps are constructed in every positive length. Relative Donaldson–Thomas operators, untraced multiple covers and the primitive inverse-Igusa trace are also established. The global flag comparison must still retain its common lifting gerbes and relative moment parameters; imposing separate zero moments on every intermediate object would change it.

The state-map target is \(H^*(S^{[d]})\) with its actual pairing and products. At \(d=1\), the trace-invisible cyclic deformation of Chapter 15 changes a nonzero three-state coefficient while keeping the stated trace data. A realization must distinguish those products and preserve the geometric one. Equivariant orientation descent and comparison with independently chosen compact orientations are further specified arrows, not consequences of an orientation sign on numerical charges. [Chapter 15](15-hall-and-compact.md)

## Arithmetic positivity and local operations {#problem-arithmetic}

**Problem 18.10.** Establish
\[
W(g*g^*)\ge0\qquad\text{for every }g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C),
\]
or construct an independently defined ordinary positive representation whose trace is the complete Weil functional on its stated test algebra.

The exact missing contribution in the existing positive representation is
\[
W(f)-\operatorname{Tr}\pi_{\mathrm{crit}}(f)
=\sum_{w_\rho\notin\mathbb R}m_\rho\widehat f(w_\rho).
\tag{18.3}
\]
Its vanishing on all tests has Riemann-hypothesis strength. The fixed whole-window theorem already proves positivity for every complex \(L^2\) test supported in an interval of width at most \(31/10\), with both parity certificates and full coupling bounds. A theorem on larger cofinal windows must retain the prime-power, polar, real-place and off-block terms. For a block decomposition, the relevant form is the Schur complement \(A-K^*D^{-1}K\); positivity of \(A\) alone is not its required lower bound. [Chapter 16](16-arithmetic.md)

**Problem 18.11.** Compare the independently defined arithmetic coefficient complexes with a local field theory and a common integrable trace domain, preserving multiplicity jets, normal operations, Frobenius variance, collisions and the vacuum.

Continuous integer-summation duality, the full theta trace, actual completed bar homotopies and injective integral normal comparisons are available. The linking construction gives a cyclic vector and nonlocal creation fields; it does not give locality. At an arithmetic point, the relation \(h_0\) vanishes on scalar evaluation but its first normal derivative is \(-p\), nonzero modulo \(p^2\). A local comparison factoring only through scalar points would destroy that operation. A full cyclic trace on the position–normal algebra is also constrained by \([D_0,M_{x_0}]=pI\), whereas proper trace ideals and specified finite residue functionals remain valid.

These problems return to the original reduction question in a sharper form. The theory has explicit objects, maps, finite filtrations, state operations and counterexamples. A reconstruction theorem must carry their operations through the proposed comparison and show exactly where it is invertible. The next decisive result is therefore an equality, equivalence or obstruction for one of the specified maps, on the domain where that map has a mathematical meaning.

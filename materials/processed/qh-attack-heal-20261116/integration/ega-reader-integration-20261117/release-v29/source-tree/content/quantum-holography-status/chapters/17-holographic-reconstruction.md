# From boundary operations to bulk reconstruction

If an observer can prepare states and insert operators only at a boundary, which bulk measurements can that observer recover? The arithmetic trace in Chapter 16 gives a reason to ask for the operations themselves. A zero of multiplicity two contributes twice to a trace whether or not a translation generator mixes its two generalized eigenvectors. A bulk field can likewise agree with another on a scalar expectation and still act differently on a boundary insertion.

Let \(U\) be a bulk open set above a boundary disk \(V\). In the abelian half-space of Chapter 7, multiply a regular bulk observable supported in \(U\) by a boundary field with disjoint profile and project the product onto the boundary currents. Varying that boundary field gives a continuous linear operator on their complex \(\mathcal A_\kappa(V)\). For compactly supported smooth test kernels at each finite field number, this defines the cochain map
\[
\beta_{U;V}:\operatorname{Obs}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(U)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal A_\kappa(V)).
\]
To reconstruct the bulk from actions one would have to recognize which compatible actions on all boundary conditions come from bulk fields.

There is an independent test using states. A defect between two theories can carry a module from one side to the other. Can a measurement made after crossing the defect be pulled back to the original module, and does joining two lines before crossing give the same result as joining them afterwards? The first question has an algebraic model with an explicit inverse on its image. A filling adds a further physical requirement: the transported measurement must have the same unnormalized amplitude in the line assigned to the cut.

## Interfaces give an explicit reconstruction map {#interfaces-and-reconstruction}

The ordinary algebraic model is useful because all its arrows can be written. Let \(A,B\) be differential graded algebras and let \(P\) be an \(A\)-\(B\) bimodule. The two actions commute with the differential. A vector in \(P\) can therefore be acted on by \(A\) from the left and by \(B\) from the right, as an interface can be acted on from its two sides.

The derived functors
\[
F=P\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-:D(B)\longrightarrow D(A),
\qquad
G=R\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,-):D(A)\longrightarrow D(B)
\tag{17.1}
\]
form an adjunction. Derived categories are used with their mapping complexes, as in Chapter 6. The right \(B\)-action on \(P\) produces the left \(B\)-action on the Hom complex by precomposition. The actual unit and counit are
\[
\eta_L:L\longrightarrow GF(L),
\qquad
\varepsilon_M:FG(M)\longrightarrow M.
\tag{17.2}
\]
The functor \(F\) is fully faithful exactly when \(\eta:\mathrm{id}_{D(B)}\to GF\) is an equivalence. In that case \(M\in D(A)\) lies in the essential image of \(F\) exactly when \(\varepsilon_M\) is an equivalence. A derived Morita equivalence requires this for every \(M\in D(A)\).

For a complete finite example, take \(A=M_n(k)\), \(B=k\), \(P=k^n\), and let \(E_{ij}\) be the matrix units. Put \(e=E_{11}\). Then \(G(M)=eM\), and the counit is
\[
\varepsilon_M:k^n\otimes eM\longrightarrow M,
\qquad v_i\otimes m\longmapsto E_{i1}m.
\tag{17.3}
\]
Its inverse is
\[
M\longrightarrow k^n\otimes eM,
\qquad m\longmapsto\sum_i v_i\otimes E_{1i}m.
\]
The two composites are identities because \(E_{1j}E_{i1}=\delta_{ij}e\) and \(\sum_iE_{i1}E_{1i}=1_A\). The unit sends a vector \(v\) to \(v_1\otimes v\) in \(e(k^n\otimes V)\), so it is also an isomorphism. This proves the equivalence of module categories directly, including its maps.

The same example shows why a centre alone is insufficient. The algebra of upper triangular two-by-two matrices over \(k\) has centre \(k\), just as \(M_2(k)\) does. It has two nonisomorphic simple modules, obtained by evaluating the two diagonal entries; \(M_2(k)\) has one simple module up to isomorphism. Their module categories are not equivalent. A centre comparison and an interface equivalence answer different questions.

For a field theory the prescribed defect must supply the differential and both actions of \({}_AP_B\) on its allowed supports. Identifying the resulting physical adjunction with (17.1) means giving natural equivalences that intertwine the units and counits. In the matrix example these equivalences can be checked on the matrix units.

## What the affine theorem reconstructs {#affine-reconstruction-scope}

Take a simple complex Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\), normalize its long roots to square length two, and write \(h^\vee\) for its dual Coxeter number. Let \(s\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Q\) be the shifted level and \(B=Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\) the polynomial differential graded line algebra. The affine mode algebra \(A\) has raw level \(s-h^\vee\). The module
\[
W=A\otimes_{U(t\mathfrak g[t])}\mathbb C
\]
is generated by a vector annihilated by the positive currents, with the horizontal zero modes acting freely on its top. Its smooth right \(B\)-action, homogeneous endomorphism comparison, coherent Taylor cutoffs on the Chevalley resolution, and enhanced tensor–Hom adjunction are the realization data of [Chapter 6](06-duality-and-induction.md). Under these hypotheses the ordinary unit is invertible on every unbounded source complex. The resulting equivalence is
\[
D(B)\simeq\operatorname{Loc}(W).
\tag{17.4}
\]
The right-hand category is generated inside the smooth affine target by \(W\) under shifts, cones, coproducts and retracts, with all internal energy shifts in the energy-graded convention. The counit is the coreflection onto this category.

Why can an unbounded conclusion follow from finite data? Write \(R=U(\mathfrak g)\), let \(I\) be the positive-weight ideal in \(B\), and let \(D\) be the number of positive roots of \(\mathfrak g\). The precise bimodule statement is
\[
I^{\otimes^{\mathbf L}_R(D+1)}\simeq0,
\qquad I^{\otimes^{\mathbf L}_R D}\not\simeq0.
\tag{17.5}
\]
Each surviving positive factor raises energy and moves strictly up the finite Bruhat order in its integral linkage block. The length can rise at most \(D\) times. A block in which all simple integral roots have positive mode realizes a longest word through actual embeddings of affine Verma modules, giving the nonzero power. For \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\), the three positive roots give a surviving cube and a vanishing fourth power. Equation (17.5) yields a tower with at most \(D+1\) coefficient layers inflated from \(R\). The original unit is already invertible on each such layer, so exact triangles give the conclusion on the source itself. [The sharp exponent](#atlas-c-ur-10)

At raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) level \(-3\), a nonzero telescope \(L\) satisfies \(R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L\simeq0\), \(F(L)\not\simeq0\) and \(\eta_L:L\xrightarrow{\sim}GF(L)\). Thus reduction to \(R\) loses a module detected by the affine realization. At critical raw level \(-h^\vee\) there are nonzero \(L'\) with \(F(L')\simeq0\). Determining \(\ker F\) and the full image at rational shifted levels, or identifying \(\operatorname{Loc}(W)\) with a specified unbounded category of torsion modules, remains open in general.

## Products must survive passage through the interface {#products-through-an-interface}

An equivalence of linear categories can forget the way systems combine. If \(\mathcal C\) and \(\mathcal D\) have specified tensor products, a monoidal comparison includes maps
\[
\gamma_{L,M}:F(L)\otimes_{\mathcal D}F(M)
\longrightarrow F(L\otimes_{\mathcal C}M)
\tag{17.6}
\]
and a unit comparison. For three modules \(L,M,N\), one may transport the product of \(L,M\) first and then join \(N\), or transport \(M,N\) first and then join \(L\). The associativity diagram equates these composites after the two categories' reassociation maps. The unit diagrams concern inserting the tensor unit. A strong monoidal functor requires these maps to be equivalences. A braided comparison also includes the exchange square and its compatibility with the associator.

To compare operations on a generated sector, fix a field \(k\). Let \(G_i\) generate a localizing subcategory \(\mathcal S_i\) of a presentable stable \(k\)-linear category and be compact in \(\mathcal S_i\), for \(0\le i\le r\) and \(r\ge1\). Write \(R_iX=\operatorname{RHom}(G_i,X)\) and \(E_i=R_iG_i\). Precomposition makes \(R_iX\) a right \(E_i\)-module, and \(R_i\) identifies \(\mathcal S_i\) with the derived category of right \(E_i\)-modules. In the ungraded affine equivalence (17.4), the unit on the free module identifies \(B^{\mathrm{op}}\) with the smooth derived endomorphisms \(\operatorname{RHom}_{\operatorname{Sm}(A)}(W,W)\) through the specified right action. Right modules over this algebra are left \(B\)-modules. The graded statement uses all energy shifts. A \(k\)-multilinear operation \(\mu:\prod_{i=1}^r\mathcal S_i\to\mathcal S_0\) preserving colimits in each input has a kernel \(P_\mu=R_0\mu(G_1,\ldots,G_r)\). Applying \(\mu\) to input endomorphisms gives a left action of \(E_{\mathrm{in}}=E_1\otimes_k\cdots\otimes_k E_r\). For two such operations one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_0\mu(X_1,\ldots,X_r)
&\simeq (R_1X_1\otimes_k\cdots\otimes_k R_rX_r)
           \otimes^{\mathbf L}_{E_{\mathrm{in}}}P_\mu,\\
\operatorname{Map}_k(\mu,\nu)
&\simeq \operatorname{Map}_{E_{\mathrm{in}}\text{-}E_0}(P_\mu,P_\nu).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the second line identifies the space of enriched natural transformations, including all higher homotopies. Resolve each input by free modules. Taking colimits in each variable extends the operation on the free generators with its endomorphism actions, and extends every equivariant map of kernels in the same way. Substitution becomes a derived tensor over the intermediate algebra. If the outputs leave \(\mathcal S_0\), the first formula for their projection requires \(R_0\mu\) itself to preserve colimits in each input. [B. Toën, derived Morita theory, Corollary 7.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408337)

With several probes the morphisms between them enter the kernel. Consider arrows \(X_1\xrightarrow{a}X_2\) of \(k[x]\)-complexes and use \(k\) for the residue module at the origin. The probes \(G_1=(k\xrightarrow{1}k)\) and \(G_2=(0\to k)\) give the two complexes \(\operatorname{fib}(x:X_i\to X_i)\). Precomposition by the map \(G_2\to G_1\) that is the identity at the second vertex induces \(a\) on these fibres. Together they generate the diagrams with \(x\)-power-torsion cohomology at both vertices. A diagram is right-orthogonal to \(G_1\) exactly when \(x\) acts invertibly on \(X_1\). With both probes the same condition holds at both vertices.

Write \(H\) for the algebra of lower triangular two-by-two matrices over \(k\), so these arrows are left \(H[x]\)-modules. The double centralizer of a probe \(G\) is its derived endomorphism algebra over \(\operatorname{RHom}_{H[x]}(G,G)\). At the origin \(G_1\) is the two-dimensional column and its endomorphisms as an \(H\)-module are scalars. The two columns together are the regular \(H\)-module, whose right multiplications have centralizer \(H\) acting on the left. The Koszul resolution at \(x=0\) contributes an exterior generator \(\epsilon\) in cohomological degree one. Its augmentation module has a free resolution with generators \(v_n\) of degree zero, \(dv_0=0\) and \(dv_n=\epsilon v_{n-1}\) for \(n>0\). Lowering \(n\) represents \(x\), and ordinary Hom permits an arbitrary series in this operator. The resulting double centralizers are \(M_2(k[[x]])\) for \(G_1\) and \(H[[x]]\) for \(G_1\oplus G_2\).

For local fields the products in (17.6) depend on positions and on the Laurent expansions used at collisions. In the spectral class of Chapter 12, a flat Hopf algebra with compatible translation, a rational spectral form \(\rho_z\) and levels \(\eta_i\) has field-preserving coproducts \(D_{\eta_1,\eta_2}=(\eta_2\otimes1)\Delta:V_{\eta_1\eta_2}\to V_{\eta_1}\boxtimes_\rho V_{\eta_2}\). The product \(\boxtimes_\rho\) contracts fields belonging to different factors. The field hexagon holds on the specified common expansion space, and the counit is defined at the identity level.

Chapter 8 supplies another positive result. For nonrational \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), under its realization and finite-descent hypotheses, every finite tuple of ordinary unbounded line modules has a comparison with the independently defined radial current complex. Scalar extension to the shape field \(F\) occurs before completion to \(F((q))\). Rational evaluation and its affine restriction preserve the outgoing action and all stated incoming actions. Composition of the original kernels along a finite tree agrees on this common radial domain.

That conclusion does not define substitution on every separately completed intermediate tensor. The series
\[
\sum_{n\geq0}q^n\lambda^{-n^2}
\]
is a valid element of \(\mathbb C(\lambda)((q))\), whereas substituting \(\lambda=zq\) produces outer orders \(n-n^2\) with no lower bound. The proposed substituted series is outside the iterated Laurent space. A larger tensor comparison must impose and prove the support estimates that exclude this failure, or choose a different independently justified domain.

An integral collision over a ring \(R\) with discriminant \(d\) uses the derived fibre \((R/(d))\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}C\) of its coefficient complex \(C\). Applying this construction to \(C[d^{-1}]\) gives zero. The finite integral quotients in Chapter 8 have collision maps before \(d\) is inverted. Their comparison with radial fusion must intertwine these maps through specialization and completion.

### Native fields on every algebraic input {#ch17-native-r3-fields}

The native four-colour construction has a dg quantum vertex structure on every algebraic input through the coefficient ring
\[
R_3=\mathbb C[\hbar]/(\hbar^3).
\]
Its state complex is \(M_3=V^{KB}(\mathfrak{gl}_2)\otimes_{R_3}F_0\), with the trace-current and trace-ghost factors retained. At every numerical level \(K\), including \(0\) and \(-2\), the field \(\mathbb Y_-\), translation, vacuum and exchange give a dg quantum vertex superalgebra on all algebraic states. The locality exponent depends on the first two states and works for every final state. The opposite field \(\mathbb Y_+\) is a weakly associative chain field, joined to \(\mathbb Y_-\) by the specified field homotopy. A separate full quantum vertex structure for the opposite exchange is not part of this conclusion. [All-input field theorem](#atlas-c-native-r3-all-input-qva).

Passing to cohomology removes the exchange correction. The exchange has an algebraically finite Laurent homotopy
\[
[d_M^{\otimes},\Lambda(u)]=S_M(u)-1.
\]
For cycle representatives of the first two inputs, a finite power of the separation, depending on those representatives, clears its poles. The difference of the two exchanged products then becomes a coefficientwise boundary for every final cycle. Consequently \(H^\bullet(M_3,d_M)\) carries an ordinary local vertex superalgebra, with \(R_3\)-bilinear fields and the original faithful central \(R_3\)-action. Both ordered fields induce that same operation. The argument needs no Künneth identification over \(R_3\), and ordinary locality does not make the singular products zero. [Cohomology fields](#atlas-c-native-r3-ordinary-cohomology).

There is a separate computation of the states at each fixed \(K\ne-2\). An algebraic chain frame preserves the native minimal inclusion and identifies
\[
H^\bullet(M_3)\cong
(R_3\mathbf1\oplus R_3[\omega_\hbar])
\otimes_{\mathbb C}
\left(\mathbb C[I_1,I_2,\ldots]
\otimes\Lambda(\xi^I_0,\xi^I_1,\ldots)\right),
\qquad |\omega_\hbar|=3.
\]
Write \(b=[\omega_\hbar]\) and let \(L\) denote the displayed trace factor. On the trace-free small complex the nontrivial differential sends a polynomial in translation \(T\) times a generator \(q\) to that polynomial times \(2T\omega_\hbar\). Multiplication by \(T\) is injective on \(R_3[T]\), so \(Tb=0\). The product of \(b\) with itself has degree six and stays in the trace-free factor, where the degree-six cohomology vanishes. The chain field factors with the commutative differential field of \(L\). Consequently all cohomology products at fixed \(K\ne-2\) have the form \(Y(x,u)y=(e^{uT}x)y\), with \(b^2=0\). This includes numerical level zero. Ordinary locality alone would not give that formula.

At the critical level \(K=-2\), let \(S\) be the quadratic Sugawara state of the trace-free current algebra. Its jets \(s_j=T^jS/j!\) and degree-one partners \(b_j\), obtained by differentiating the classical differential with respect to level, have weight \(j+2\). Together with the constant degree-three ghost class \(\omega\) they give a different state calculation. As a graded \(R_3\)-module, the full algebraic critical state cohomology is free on the classical monomials of
\[
\mathbb C[s_j:j\geq0]\otimes\Lambda(b_j:j\geq0)
\otimes\Lambda(\omega)\otimes L .
\]
The invariant \(S\) lifts as an actual cycle. Dividing its differential by \(K+2\) before specializing gives a lift of the partner, and the native binary field applied to the cycle lifts yields a cycle above every classical basis monomial. One can choose a translation-linear change of state coordinates intertwining the differentials through precision three. This determines the state module and its precision reductions. Determining the singular products and independently produced marked operations at this level remains a further comparison.

### Retained kernels and the first rooted comparison {#ch17-retained-first-root}

A scalar field forgets its ghost output. A retained kernel keeps that output until it is paired with a coefficient functional. Let \(V=M_3^0\), let \(\mathcal G\) be the completed ghost algebra, and let \(\widehat I_L\) be its closed left ideal generated by ghost modes of index at least \(L\). Put \(F_L=\mathcal G/\widehat I_L\). A retained kernel of degree \(n\) assigns to each algebraic current state \(w\in V\) a compatible Laurent series
\[
T_L(w)\in(V\otimes F_L^n)((u)).
\]
These \(R_3\)-linear families form the retained kernel space \(\mathcal E^n_{\rm ret}\) for \(n\ge0\); put \(\mathcal E^n_{\rm ret}=0\) for \(n<0\). Their Laurent lower bound may depend on \(L\) and \(w\). The combined current–ghost differential \(\mathscr D\) on these kernels squares to zero. The ghost differential itself is evaluated at a larger cutoff before projection; the individual \(F_L\) are not thereby dg quotient algebras. A retained cochain has an additional state input: in degree \(j\) it belongs to
\[
\mathcal Q^{\mathrm{ret},j}
=\prod_{p\ge0}\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}
\bigl(M_3^p,\mathcal E^{p+j}_{\rm ret}\bigr),
\qquad
\delta\Psi=\mathscr D\Psi-(-1)^j\Psi d_M.
\]
Thus \(\Psi(x)\) is a retained kernel on the current input \(w\), with output ghost degree \(p+j\) when \(x\in M_3^p\).

At every numerical \(K\), the dressed field gives a retained cocycle \(\mathcal Y_-\). The restricted exchange homotopy gives a second cocycle \(\mathcal Y^\sharp\), with the opposite scalar field; its polarization agrees with \(\mathbb Y_+\) up to a coboundary in the kernel of the corner projection. Write \(A_{\rm cur}\) for the full smooth native current algebra and \(A_{{\rm cur},R}\) for its specified right quotients. Coefficient pairing defines continuous dg \(A_{\rm cur}\)-bimodule evaluations on the ordered rooted carrier
\[
N_{\rm rt}^{1-n}
=\varprojlim_R\operatorname*{colim}_{L\ge1}
\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}(F_L^n,A_{\rm cur})
 \otimes_{R_3,\mathrm{alg}}A_{{\rm cur},R}\right).
\]
These source coefficients are independent of \(u\). At each right quotient they have one middle ghost cutoff and finite right PBW support; neither is required to be uniform over all right quotients. The recipient is the shifted scalar Hom complex
\[
Q_3=\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}
\bigl(M_3,\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}(V,V((u)))\bigr)[-1],
\]
with the pointwise Laurent topology in its two algebraic inputs. Its Laurent floor need not be uniform over all inputs. Homogeneity supplies the finite ghost box and Laurent floor needed for the pairing. The two evaluations \(E^\flat,E^\sharp:N_{\rm rt}\to Q_3\) retain both endpoint actions and both translation identities, and an actual bimodule homotopy joins them. At \(K=-2\) they are new rooted maps defined by the field, not an extension of the noncritical marked evaluation. [Retained kernels and rooted evaluations](#atlas-c-native-r3-retained-rooted-evaluations).

At a fixed \(K\ne-2\), including \(K=0\), let \(E_3\) be the prescribed pure-root evaluation, with retained cochain \(\Phi\in\mathcal Q^{\mathrm{ret},0}\), and put \(\Delta=\Phi-\mathcal Y^\sharp\). Their scalar kernels agree. Use the translation-compatible state retract with its full trace factor: if \(i\) and \(p\) are its inclusion and projection, its degree-minus-one homotopy \(k\) satisfies \(d_Mk+kd_M=1-ip\). Write \(\Delta_1\) for the restriction to inserted ghost degree one. The degree-minus-one retained cochain
\[
\kappa^{(1)}|_{M_3^2}=\Delta_1k|_{M_3^2},
\qquad
\kappa^{(1)}|_{M_3^p}=0\quad(p\ne2)
\]
has zero scalar pairing and obeys both translation laws. With \(\delta\) the retained Hom differential,
\[
\mathcal Y^{\sharp,1}=\mathcal Y^\sharp+\delta\kappa^{(1)}
\]
agrees with \(\Phi\) on inserted ghost degrees zero and one. The induced evaluation therefore agrees with \(E_3\) on the subcomplex of middle degrees at most one, and is globally homotopic to the evaluation defined by \(\mathcal Y^\sharp\) on \(N_{\rm rt}\). [The first rooted comparison](#atlas-c-native-r3-first-root-alignment).

The contraction explains why this correction exists. Its degree-one small image consists of trace-current polynomials times one trace ghost. The two kernels agree there, while the cocycle identity makes \(\Delta_1d=0\) on degree zero. The contraction identity then gives \(\Delta_1=\Delta_1kd\). The full trace factor is essential to this argument.

The first-root correction keeps the scalar fixed. Both its construction and its extension to every degree use the entire trace-current and trace-ghost factor.

### A marked comparison in every retained degree {#ch17-all-retained-marked}

A rooted operation has a distinguished input on which the coefficients act. To extend the preceding argument, keep the complete algebraic state at that input. Denote the two bulk retained kernels by \(\mathcal Y_-\) and \(\mathcal Y^\sharp\) and their exchange homotopy by \(h^\Lambda\), so that \(\delta h^\Lambda=\mathcal Y^\sharp-\mathcal Y_-\). At a fixed noncritical level the maps \(i,p,k\) contract the state complex onto the small complex while acting as the identity on its trace factor. On the small complex the prescribed pure-root kernel \(\Phi\) agrees with \(\mathcal Y_-\). The essential verification is equality as operators on arbitrary algebraic rooted states for the two trace-free generators. The trace variables are spectators in those equalities. With \(\Delta=\Phi-\mathcal Y^\sharp\), put
\[
K_{\rm rt}=\Delta k-h^\Lambda ip .
\]
The first term has differential \(\Delta(1-ip)\). The second has differential \((\mathcal Y_--\mathcal Y^\sharp)ip=\Delta ip\). Thus \(\delta K_{\rm rt}=\Delta\) in every rooted degree. Its scalar part vanishes because \(\Delta_0=0\) and the degree-one small states have only a trace ghost, paired with a nonnegative trace current which annihilates the rooted input. The homotopy satisfies both translation identities.

A marked coefficient can itself be a Laurent series. At each fixed right quotient and algebraic inserted input require one lower Laurent bound, one ghost cutoff and a common finite set of right current monomials for the whole series. For a series starting at exponent \(\nu\), let \(T_0\) bound the exponents of the retained kernel from below on this finite list of right translates of the rooted state. At output exponent \(t\) the convolution uses only \(\nu\leq j\leq t-T_0\). Homogeneity bounds the ghost indices in each of those coefficients. This supplies the continuity estimate which would fail if new right-current orders or unbounded cutoffs could enter at every coefficient.

On the trace-only inserted summand one extends the rooted homotopy by contraction with the dual trace monomials. Each fixed algebraic input meets only finitely many. On the positive traceless inserted summand take the prescribed state and Casimir operations on both sides. This gives a homotopy on these whole-series stages of the restricted marked source. The Cartan coordinate isomorphism carries the homotopy to the resolved source. On the original unsimplified source one may precompose with the specified current projection. These steps keep the marked operations that were given on the positive traceless summand. To realize them physically, both independent ordered products must produce those operations on their own inputs. The three proper unit cones and the tensorator also have to be compared. [Marked whole-series comparison](#atlas-c-r3-marked)

### The endpoint obstruction has a specified acting algebra {#ch17-native-endpoint-obstruction}

The intrinsic field theorem can coexist with an obstruction to a physical coefficient comparison because the latter must preserve an additional action. Write \(V=M_3^0\) for the ghost-degree-zero state module and \(A_3\) for its prescribed current algebra. Let
\[
A=\operatorname{End}^{\ge0}_{R_3}(M_3)
\times_{\operatorname{End}_{R_3}(V)}A_3,
\qquad q:A\longrightarrow A_3,
\]
be the actual dg fibre product, whose endomorphisms raise ghost degree by a nonnegative integer. Inflate the marked source \(\mathcal N_3[1]\) along \(q\). The exact corner sequence, with kernel \(\mathcal K\), is a sequence of dg \(A\)-bimodules, but it has no global chain section. The kernel of \(q\) contains a closed trace creator \(c\), with \(c^2=0\), which kills the source unit class. On every closed normalized bulk lift \(G\) of the prescribed scalar class,
\[
G(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)=\mathbf1,
\qquad [cG](\mathbf1,\mathbf1)=[\xi^I_0]\ne0.
\]
Thus the source unit and its required image cannot have the same \(c\)-action.

At every fixed \(K\ne-2\), the two ordered marked evaluations have the same nonzero derived obstruction:
\[
\beta_AE_3[1]=\beta_A\overline E_3[1]\ne0
\quad\text{in}\quad
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(A^e)}
\bigl(\mathcal N_3[1],\mathcal K[1]\bigr).
\]
Restriction to the exterior algebra generated by \(c\) detects a split free cyclic \(R_3\)-submodule. An equivalent resolution of the same source module cannot remove the obstruction, nor can standard uncurved \(A_\infty\)-bimodule coherence. This excludes the specified quotient-inflated lift. A replacement action, acting algebra or recipient must come with a comparison preserving the original operations. [The exact endpoint obstruction](#atlas-c-native-quotient-inflated-endpoint-obstruction).

The trace detector has a stronger module-theoretic form in a specified corner complex. Put \(F^1M_3=M_3^{\ge1}\). Let \(\mathcal B\) be the subcomplex of two-input Laurent cochains
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}(M_3\otimes M_3,M_3((u)))
\]
whose value lies in \((F^1M_3)((u))\) whenever the rooted, second input lies in \(F^1M_3\). Let \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal B\) consist of cochains whose every output lies in \((F^1M_3)((u))\). The corner map
\[
\pi:\mathcal B\longrightarrow
\mathcal C=\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}(M_3\otimes V,V((u)))
\]
restricts the rooted input to its ghost-free part \(V=M_3^0\) and projects the output to ghost degree zero; its kernel is \(\mathcal K\).

For a closed \(G\in\mathcal B^0\) with \([\pi G]=[W_\pm]\), multiplication of its output by exterior trace monomials gives a split copy of \(\Lambda_{R_3}(\xi^I_0,\xi^I_1,\ldots)\) in \(H^\bullet(\mathcal B)\); its positive-degree part splits into \(H^\bullet(\mathcal K)\). This is a splitting of graded \(R_3\)-modules, not a presentation of a product on Hom cohomology. Suppose a dg acting algebra preserves this corner exact sequence and contains a closed degree-one element acting by output multiplication by a trace ghost. If a dg module map from \(S\) to \(\mathcal C\) sends a closed degree-zero element to the normalized scalar class \([W_\pm]\) and \(H^1(S)=0\), it has no lift to \(\mathcal B\) in the derived module category or as a standard uncurved \(A_\infty\)-module map. The trace creator kills the source class and cannot kill its required image. The acting-algebra and diagram hypotheses are essential; this obstruction does not exclude the retained-kernel pairing or a relative cone with different cohomology. [Trace classes and the source-degree obstruction](#atlas-c-native-r3-trace-classes-source-obstruction).

Suppose a source with closed unit \(e\) carries the exterior algebra on the trace creators \(c_j\). A normalized endpoint intertwining these actions with multiplication by \(i_j=\xi^I_j\) makes \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha[e]\) a split injection on cohomology. A left inverse evaluates the endpoint on the two vacua, takes the regular coefficient and projects to the pure trace ghosts.

The identification of the first trace ghost can itself be measured. On the algebraic trace factor \(d=0\), and the weights of \(I_m,i_j,\hbar\) are \(m,j,-1\). An \(R_3\)-linear algebra automorphism \(U\) fixing the trace currents, commuting with \(T\), preserving ghost degree and adjusted weight, and equal to the identity modulo \(\hbar^2\), is uniquely given by
\[
 U(i_0)=i_0+\hbar^2w,\qquad
 w=\sum_{j=1}^4\lambda_j b_j,\qquad
 (b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)=(i_2,I_1i_1,I_2i_0,I_1^2i_0).
\]
These are all the monomials of ghost degree one and weight two. Translation gives \(U(i_j)=i_j+\hbar^2T^jw/j!\). Every choice of \(\lambda_j\in\mathbb C\) extends to the free supercommutative trace algebra and has inverse given by \(-w\).

At every numerical \(K\) there is a corrected closed traceless cubic ghost \(\Omega_K\) with coefficient one on \(\omega=h_0e_0f_0\). Take the closed inputs \(I_1U(i_0)\) and \(I_1\Omega_K\). In the regular product with the trace ghost first, let \(m_*\) be twice the coefficient of \(I_1^2i_0\omega\), and \(m_j\) the coefficient of \(I_1^2b_j\omega\). The two orders give
\[
 (m_*,m_1,\ldots,m_4)=\pm(2,\hbar^2\lambda_1,\ldots,\hbar^2\lambda_4).
\]
The trace factors multiply, and reversing the two odd inputs gives the minus sign. Each coefficient descends to cohomology. At its fixed trace monomial the classical traceless differential into the weight-zero top ghost vanishes, and higher terms would require negative input weight. The four ratios \(2m_j/m_*\) recover the preparation even with a common cup factor. For the even insertion \(I_1U(i_0i_1)\) the two orders have the same sign. The correction to \(i_0i_1\) is \(\hbar^2(wi_1+i_0Tw)\), whose coefficients on \(i_0i_3,i_1i_2\) are \(3\hbar^2\lambda_1,-\hbar^2\lambda_1\). An independently named creator acting on the unit by \(i_0\) forces all four \(\lambda_j\) to vanish. A physical determination requires its own marked preparation, output functionals, and absolute cup and cap on that pair.

There is an obstruction already after forgetting the bimodule action on a different pair of marked inputs. At noncritical level write \(e\) for the scalar mark and \(a_E=J_E(-1)\) for the creation mode of the raising current. Both \(a_E e\) and \(e a_E\) are degree-zero cycles of the prescribed marked source. For either ordering the constant Laurent coefficient of the corresponding scalar-corner evaluation at the two vacua is the degree-zero state \(v_E=a_E\mathbf1\). Boundaries in the corner cannot change this vacuum value. Any closed bulk lift would therefore require \(d_Mv_E=0\). If \(E_1,H_1\) denote negative-current states and \(h_0,f_0,f_1\) the corresponding ghost modes, its classical part is
\[
d_Mv_E\bmod\hbar=2E_1h_0-H_1f_0+Kf_1 .
\]
The coefficient of \(E_1h_0\) is two. A retained map can still use this current as a *coefficient* and contract its ghost output. A proposed physical realization must specify which source differential, insertion and output it gives to this marked current. One cannot lift the unchanged covariant endpoint map by simply forgetting its symmetries.

The noncritical marked evaluations themselves remain positive constructions. Their endpoint-bilinear homotopy restores the reversed scalar field, both Ward laws, the prescribed minimal pairings and the proper unit-cone diagrams. A separate scalar correction also aligns the two scalar homotopies while preserving translation. Scalar connecting classes can therefore vanish while the derived bimodule obstruction survives. Throughout, the coefficient source retains one Laurent floor, one ghost cutoff and finite right PBW support across each whole series at a fixed right quotient and algebraic inserted input. The independent physical right adjoint, its counit, both products and tensorator still require maps on that declared domain.

## A bulk action and a universal centre {#bulk-action-and-centre}

In the ordinary associative case, Hochschild cochains
\[
C^\bullet(B,B)=R\operatorname{Hom}_{B\otimes B^{\mathrm{op}}}(B,B)
\]
act coherently on derived \(B\)-modules. For example a bimodule morphism \(B\to B[n]\) induces \(M\simeq B\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}M\to M[n]\) for each \(M\). In the chiral case an action also has restrictions to configuration opens, factorization products, a vacuum and homotopies on collision strata. One must specify that action functor before asking for its representing object.

Chapter 7 defines one such geometric problem exactly. A pointed factorizing coalgebra \((F,u)\) is tested against the Chevalley coalgebra \(C\) of the two-idempotent algebra. The complete character and action spaces are \(\mathcal X(F,u)\) and \(\mathcal A_C(F,u)\). Multiplication and vacuum evaluation give
\[
\Theta:\mathcal X(F,u)\longrightarrow\mathcal A_C(F,u),
\qquad
\Psi:\mathcal A_C(F,u)\longrightarrow\mathcal X(F,u),
\qquad \Psi\Theta\simeq1.
\tag{17.7}
\]
Recognition asks whether the other composite is homotopic to the identity with all the required compatibilities. This is proved for the full families stated in Chapter 7: arbitrary pointings on genuinely unital Chevalley coalgebras, their specified module extensions, and the nonunital families covered by the horizontal-representative or commutative-connection theorems. The three-block filling for arbitrary coassociative actors requires no cocommutativity. The separate old-base closed-corner comparison has its own coherent cocommutativity hypothesis and remains on the separated locus.

Suppose the pointed geometric action functor is represented by a chiral centre \(Z_{\mathrm{ch}}\) in its specified coefficient category. A reconstruction would still require a bulk-to-centre morphism \(r:\mathcal O_{\mathrm{bulk}}\to Z_{\mathrm{ch}}\) whose induced operations agree with supported bulk insertions, their products and their restrictions. Representability and the ordinary vertex-algebra mapping-space calculation have different domains.

For a fixed unital chiral algebra \(A\), Chapter 13 proves \(\operatorname{Rec}(\mathcal C(A),F)\cong A\) for ordinary coefficient families over arbitrary classical parameter schemes. The two-point vacuum coefficient, with its separated factorization and collision isomorphisms, recovers \(j_*j^*(A\boxtimes A)\to\Delta_*A\) by the diagonal residue. The theorem uses ordinary base change with \(A\) fixed. A derived family or an independently defined bulk observable algebra would require its own comparison.

## States and amplitudes impose another comparison {#states-lines-and-caps}

Return to the Gaussian of Chapter 1. Normalization produces a linear functional \(\omega\) with \(\omega(1)=1\). The unnormalized functional has the form
\[
Z(O)=Z(1)\,\omega(O).
\tag{17.8}
\]
Knowing \(\omega\) leaves the factor \(Z(1)\) undetermined. If that factor varies in a line rather than as a scalar, one must also identify the line and its connection. This distinction survives when the finite integral becomes a field theory.

Chapter 11 constructs a numerical normalized Gaussian on the original continued source graph. Its domain includes the fixed metric and density, \(0<|q|<1\), \(\kappa\ne0\), \(\hbar>0\), a smooth integrable source in the stated small inverse neighbourhood, zero normal trace, and a supplied smooth Ward filling \(T_\mu\). The even finite-cycle fields converge to a Radon Gaussian; the complete odd and mixed moments are given by the graded Wick rule. Odd variables are not assigned an ordinary probability law.

The original weighted coefficient heat construction gives another object: a based local vacuum line with a nonzero finite-part section. It uses its own reflected realization, spectral gap, smooth source bounds and finite action integrals. In a finite spectral frame, the counterterm is added to the covariant connection because the response one-form enters that connection with a minus sign. This line result determines more than the normalized state. It does not claim a convergent bare infinite determinant product or a global holonomy theorem.

A **cap** is a chosen filling of a boundary together with fields, boundary conditions and normalization that prepare a vector or functional in the specified boundary state space. If an anomaly makes the amplitude line-valued, that line belongs to the data. For a bulk observable \(O\), an exact cap comparison would take the form
\[
Z_M^{\mathrm{bulk}}(O)
=Z_M^{\partial}\bigl(r_M(O)\bigr),
\tag{17.9}
\]
where \(r_M\) sends a bulk observable to a boundary observable on the cut. An isomorphism of amplitude lines lets one compare the two sides. The filling must determine \(Z_M^\partial\) independently. Gluing along a common boundary must intertwine its state-space pairing with the bulk integral, including the value on \(1\).

There is a Gaussian example for which one can follow an operation from the action all the way to an independently specified filling. Fix numerical \(\hbar,\kappa\ne0\) and \(k=\hbar\kappa\) on the marked half-space with oriented transverse coordinate \(t\) and tangential complex coordinate \(z\). In the mixed presentation the tests are partial forms in \(dt,d\bar z\), in two fields \(\Phi,\eta\). In abelian Chern–Simons theory they are full spatial forms. Every spatial form splits uniquely into a partial form and \(dz\) times a partial form. If \(s\) denotes the suspension of the linear tests, the comparison is
\[
s(\alpha e_\Phi)\longmapsto-\kappa^{-1}dz\wedge\alpha,\qquad
s(\beta e_\eta)\longmapsto(-1)^{\deg\beta+1}\beta .
\]
The mixed boundary condition kills the tangential trace of \(\beta\), precisely the condition that the spatial form restrict to a multiple of \(dz\). The change of variables identifies the unary differentials. On a mixed Wick contraction the factor \(k\kappa^{-1}=\hbar\) gives the correct quantum coefficient with the signs from the displayed map. Thus the two complexes of regular compact kernels agree at every fixed field number, with disjoint products and the empty unit. On a proper oriented graph ray the one-form tested by the Wilson source is \(\eta-\kappa^{-1}\Phi\,dz\). The second term contributes when the ray moves tangentially. The ray comparison transports the specified Cauchy propagator, framing and outgoing subtraction with these tests. [The marked Gaussian change of variables](#atlas-c-hz-cs)

At level one the neutral pair \((a,-a)\) at \((r,0)\), with real \(a\) and \(0<r<1\), gives a regulator factor \(r^{-a^2}\) times a coherent oscillator state. Independently coupling the harmonic disk to the real source \(2a\operatorname{Re}h(r)\) gives a Gaussian law in which mode \(n\) has mean \(ar^n\) and variance \(n\). After the Gaussian–Fock identification, the squared modulus of the ray wavefunction divided by its squared norm gives this same law. Acting on the wavefunction with the disk collar gives the same one-sided pairing. A different experiment uses positive-height cochain insertions, boundary creation modes and the two polynomial states \((p_1^2\pm p_2)/2\). Their one-mode distributions agree, but the mixed observable in [Chapter 11](11-states-and-lines.md) has expectations \(+2\) and \(-2\). This is an actual discriminator on the common preparation domain.

For numerical gravity the zero-height finite-stage Ward calculation and the full-source heat identities give specific operations on their own common domain. ## Independent realizations test what reconstruction preserves {#independent-realizations}

The interface comparison can fail even if its multiplication is correct on every ordinary state. Joining two M2 particle interfaces over their common algebra \(U_N\) gives a complex; multiplying their outputs in \(A_N\) forgets its negative degrees. At the M2 junction of Chapter 14, \(\operatorname{Tor}^{U_N}_1(A_N,A_N)=0\) while \(\omega\frown-:\operatorname{Tor}^{U_N}_2(A_N,A_N)\twoheadrightarrow A_N\) splits as an \(A_N\)-bimodule map for every \(N\ge1\) and numerical \(\hbar\ne0\). In \(H^0\) the derived multiplication is the ordinary isomorphism \(A_N\otimes_{U_N}A_N\cong A_N\). The flux summand in \(H^{-2}\) prevents this multiplication from being a quasi-isomorphism.

A different loss occurs if the operation is known only after closing a boundary. For compact K3 states, closing the relative Donaldson–Thomas cylinder gives \((\beta\cdot D)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}\widehat B^D_{\beta,d}(r)\) when \(\beta\cdot D\ne0\), and the primitive generating series gives the inverse Igusa form with the normalization of Chapter 15. At \(d=1\) the rational section \(C\) determines a three-state coefficient \(q(a,C)q(b,C)q(c,C)\). A nonzero cyclic Hochschild class changes this coefficient while fixing both the fibre-marked cylinder and the specified multiplication traces.

Even an exact trace need not be a positive expectation on a Hilbert space. For the arithmetic theta quotient \(K_s\) of Chapter 16 one has \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K_s}\Pi_{s,a}(f)=W(f)\). The ordinary positive representation \(\pi_{\mathrm{crit}}\) omits exactly \(\sum_{w_\rho\notin\mathbb R}m_\rho\widehat f(w_\rho)\). Vanishing of this difference on all tests is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis. Preserving the normal derivatives of arithmetic jets imposes an additional condition on any local field comparison.

A local collision presents the same question before any trace is taken. Knowing the complex of fields away from the collision and the vector space at the collision still leaves the map that continues a field between them. For constructible complexes that datum can be written explicitly. Let \(i:\{x\}\hookrightarrow X\) be a closed stratum and \(j:U=X\setminus\{x\}\hookrightarrow X\) its open complement. Given a constructible complex \(L\) on \(U\) and a complex \(V\) at \(x\), an attaching morphism \(\alpha:V\to i^*Rj_*L\) determines the glued complex by the homotopy pullback
\[
Rj_*L\mathop{\times}^{h}_{i_*i^*Rj_*L} i_*V .
\]
Restriction to \(U\) recovers \(L\), and restriction to the point recovers \(V\). Restricting the projection from the glued sheaf to \(Rj_*L\) gives the chosen arrow from \(V\) to the link. For the four charge-one rays at level two, different lines in the two-dimensional degree-two cohomology of the deepest link, \(H^2(i^*Rj_*L)\), give genuinely different perverse extensions with identical stalk and costalk dimensions. For identical rays, fix the coordinate framings and specify the positive half-exchange as \(+i\). With that linearization the permutation action on \(H^2(i^*Rj_*L)\) splits as the trivial character plus the sign character. Exactly their two lines in the projective family admit this equivariance. If the coefficient is further required to be the ordinary inverse image of a sheaf on the coarse unordered space, every stabilizer must act trivially on each stalk. This singles out the quotient by the trivial line, the pullback of the downstairs middle extension. The physical theory would have to supply that last requirement or decide between the two equivariant candidates in another way. [The four-point attachment](#atlas-c-cl-05)

A common bulk reconstruction would have to transport the attaching morphism \(\alpha:V\to i^*Rj_*L\) to the collision of supported boundary actions. Its interface would have to identify the two ordered products on the spaces where they can be iterated. At a filling it must in addition satisfy (17.9) and the gluing law in the identified amplitude line. In particular, an observer who can vary three K3 boundary states can test the section cubic; closing a cylinder first removes that test. At the four-ray collision the analogous measurement fixes the image of the attaching map in the link. The algebraic constructions determine the alternatives these measurements distinguish. An independent physical interface and filling must predict the measurements on the same states.

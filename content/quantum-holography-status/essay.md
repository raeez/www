# Ordered composition, duality and quantum states

## A coupling on one probe and on a pair {#essay-consistent-couplings}

Suppose that two infinitesimal symmetries \(h,e\) obey \([h,e]=e\). What operators can realise them on a probe with ungraded state space \(V\) over a field \(k\) of characteristic zero? To express the consistency condition, take two degree-one exterior generators \(\xi,\eta\), dual to \(h,e\). Their Chevalley–Eilenberg differential is \(d\xi=0,\ d\eta=-\xi\eta\). For operators \(H,E\) of degree zero on \(V\), the coupling \(\alpha=\xi H+\eta E\) changes the differential on the cochains with values in \(V\) to \(d+\alpha\). Its square acts by
\[
d\alpha+\alpha^2=\xi\eta(HE-EH-E).
\]
Thus a consistent coupling on \(V\) is precisely a representation of the algebra generated by \(h,e\) with the single relation \(he-eh=e\). This is the universal enveloping algebra of the two-dimensional Lie algebra. Its multiplication composes operations on one probe. [Lie algebra actions](#ch02-lie-algebra-actions-and-dual-representations).

Take \(V\) with basis \(u,v\). Let \(H\) fix \(u\) and kill \(v\), and let \(E\) send \(v\) to \(u\) and kill \(u\). Then \(HE=E,\ EH=0\), so the coupling is consistent. Apply the same infinitesimal symmetry simultaneously to two such probes. On their tensor product \(e\) acts by \(E_{\rm pair}=E\otimes1+1\otimes E\). Acting twice on \(v\) gives zero. Acting twice on \(v\otimes v\) gives
\[
E_{\rm pair}(v\otimes v)=u\otimes v+v\otimes u,\qquad
E_{\rm pair}^2(v\otimes v)=2u\otimes u.
\]
There are two orders in which the operation can act once on each probe. The coproduct \(\Delta(e)=e\otimes1+1\otimes e\) accounts for both in \(\Delta(e^2)=\Delta(e)^2\). This equality compares joining the probes with composing their operations. [The diagonal action and its compatibility with multiplication](#ch02-proposition-diagonal-action).

A crossing asks a further question about the pair. For lines graded by the group of order two, one may exchange two odd labels with sign \(+1\) or \(-1\). Both exchanges have the same rule for joining labels, addition modulo two. For lines ending on a curve, an exchange can also depend on their positions. A charged pair in a free theory lets us compute both the dependence on separation and the state prepared at the boundary. [Two exchanges with the same fusion operator](#f-failure-fusion-braid).

## Two preparations on a disk {#essay-disk-preparations}

Two Wilson rays end at \(z=r\) and \(z=0\) on the boundary of a free abelian theory. Give them real charges \(a,-a\), take \(0<r<1\), and fix the level of the boundary current to one. A contour enclosing both ends detects zero total charge. Weight the same contour by \(w^n\), however, and the current \(a/(w-r)-a/w\) has residue \(ar^n\) for every \(n>0\). Thus the positions leave a signal in every positive harmonic. Write \(p_n\) for its creation variable. The positive current mode is \(n\partial_{p_n}\), so these residues determine the outgoing vector up to a scalar. With constant framing, self-contractions removed and the outgoing separation renormalized in the positive real chamber, the mutual ray contraction fixes that scalar. The vector is
\[
\Psi_{a,r}=r^{-a^2}\exp\!\left(a\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{p_n r^n}{n}\right)|0\rangle .
\]

What part of this amplitude can be measured by sampling a field on a disk? Fix the angular coordinate on the Euclidean unit disk and set the constant mode to zero. Write the real harmonic field as \(h+\bar h\), with \(h(z)=\sum_{n>0}Z_nz^n/n\). At a finite mode cutoff the action \(S_D=(8\pi)^{-1}\int_D|\nabla(h+\bar h)|^2\,d^2z\) is \(\frac12\sum_n |Z_n|^2/n\). Normalizing the squared amplitude \(e^{-2S_D}\) gives independent circular complex Gaussians with \(\mathbb E|Z_n|^2=n\). Their finite marginals define the vacuum law \(\mu\). To sample the ray preparation, substitute \(p_n=Z_n\) and use the probability law \(|\Psi_{a,r}(Z)|^2\,d\mu(Z)/\|\Psi_{a,r}\|^2\). Completing the square in each mode gives mean \(ar^n\) and variance \(n\). The sum \(\sum r^{2n}/n=-\log(1-r^2)\) is finite, and the squared norm is \(r^{-2a^2}(1-r^2)^{-a^2}\). A real collar with parameter \(0<q\le1\) multiplies harmonic \(n\) by \(q^n\). In the overlap with the propagated state the covariance sum therefore contains \(qr^2\). Dividing by the squared norm gives \(((1-r^2)/(1-qr^2))^{a^2}\). The scalar \(r^{-a^2}\) cancels in both normalized readings. An absolute filling amplitude must recover it. [The neutral ray pair and its disk law](#ch11-neutral-ray-pair)

Two states can agree on every single harmonic and still differ in a joint reading. Put two quanta in the first harmonic, or one in the second. The corresponding boundary polynomials \(p_1^2,p_2\) are orthogonal and both have squared norm two. Their norm-one superpositions are \(u_\pm=(p_1^2\pm p_2)/2\). To exchange the two occupations, an interior test on a compact slab above the collar is normalized to contract with \(p_m\) by \(\delta_{jm}\). It acts by \(\partial_{p_j}\). Boundary creation multiplies by \(p_j\). With the repeated first-mode tests on disjoint ordered slabs, the operation is
\[
O=2p_1^2\partial_{p_2}+p_2\partial_{p_1}^2,\qquad
Op_2=2p_1^2,\quad Op_1^2=2p_2 .
\]
Here \(2\partial_{p_2}\) is the level-one second current mode. Differentiating \(p_1^2\) twice gives the same coefficient in the reverse direction. Thus \(O\) is twice the exchange on this shell, and its outcomes in \(u_+,u_-\) are \(+2,-2\), respectively. The slab calculation acts on boundary cochains and allows interior tests with nonzero differential.

On the disk sample \((Z_1,Z_2)\) with density \(|Z_1^2\pm Z_2|^2/4\) relative to \(\mu\). The interference term is proportional to \(C=2\operatorname{Re}(Z_1^2\overline{Z_2})\). Averaging over either angular phase removes it, which proves that both single-mode marginals agree. Measuring \(C\) on the same sample keeps the relative phase. Circular symmetry gives \(\mathbb E_\mu C^2=2\,\mathbb E_\mu|Z_1|^4\,\mathbb E_\mu|Z_2|^2=8\). Hence the two prepared laws give means \(+2,-2\) for a reading that varies from sample to sample. Both states have energy two, so propagating the state on one side of the insertion gives the unnormalized pairings \(+2q^2,-2q^2\). [The slab calculation and the joint disk reading](#ch11-relative-phase)

What changes if the circle measurement uses all the harmonics? At radius \(0<r<1\) its cubic average is \(\Phi_r=\operatorname{Re}\sum_{i,j\ge1}r^{2(i+j)}Z_iZ_j\overline{Z_{i+j}}\). The first term is \(r^4C/2\). All terms together have mean \(+r^4\) or \(-r^4\) in the corresponding energy-two preparation. On this shell the cubic current operator \(W\) exchanges the two occupations. For a homogeneous field \(A(z)\) of weight \(h\), the angular zero mode takes the constant Fourier coefficient of \(z^h A(z)\). The cylinder coordinate \(w=\log z\) has \(dw=dz/z\), so the corresponding plane contour is \(\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} z^{h-1}A(z)\,dz\). With \(J(z)=\sum_n J_nz^{-n-1}\) and \(J_0=0\), the angular zero modes for energy and twice this cubic are \(E=\tfrac12\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} z\,{:J(z)^2:}\,dz\) and \(2W=\tfrac13\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}z^2\,{:J(z)^3:}\,dz\). The factors \(z\) and \(z^2\) select terms whose current-mode indices sum to zero. [The weighted plane residues](#eq-current-momentum-zero-modes).

[The means tend to the two eigenvalues](#ch11-theorem-the-cubic-observable-on-the-disk) of \(W\) on this shell. A single filling gives a different kind of reading. At radii \(r,s\) on the same vacuum sample the [covariance of the full averages](#ch11-proposition-boundary-fluctuations-of-the-cubic-disk-observable) is \(2x^2(1+x)/(1-x)^5\), with \(x=(rs)^2\). Even in either of the two prepared laws, the probability of finding \(\Phi_r\) in any fixed bounded interval tends to zero as \(r\uparrow1\). This holds under every fixed law absolutely continuous with respect to the vacuum. [The fluctuation calculation](#ch11-proof-cubic-disk-fluctuations).

The disk calculation used a vacuum state to create the polynomials and a filling to pair them. Can either operation be replaced by a map from the fields to scalars? For the level-one current \(J\) the coefficient of \((z-w)^{-2}\) in \(J(z)J(w)\) is the vacuum \(\mathbf1\). A unital field map to scalars would send that coefficient to \(1\), although the product of two scalar fields has zero double pole. Thus the current algebra has a vacuum and has no such augmentation. The empty configuration supplies the disjoint-union unit. Inserting the vacuum at an occupied point is a different operation. A pointing gives a compatible map into the coalgebra, and a cap evaluates the state prepared by a filling. The next calculation asks what cutting an occupied word can recover before a cap is chosen.

## Ordered products and reconstruction {#essay-ordered-products}

A cut of the word splits it into two consecutive blocks, either \(1|23\) or \(12|3\). At positions \((x,y,x)\), with \(x\ne y\), both cuts put two copies of \(x\) on opposite sides. Conversely, when \(x_1\ne x_3\), both cuts could fail only if \(x_1=x_2\) and \(x_2=x_3\), which would give a contradiction. The underlying closed locus where both cuts fail is therefore \(\Delta_{13}=\{x_1=x_3\}\). A factorizing coalgebra can therefore contribute ternary cobar coefficients supported on this diagonal. ## The actions on an operation kernel {#essay-operation-kernels}

Can a binary operation be recovered from its value on two free modules? Let \(B=kp_1\oplus kp_2\), with \(p_ip_j=\delta_{ij}p_i\) and \(1=p_1+p_2\). A right \(B\)-module \(M\) splits as \(Mp_1\oplus Mp_2\). On two such modules consider the operations \(\mu_{11}(M,N)=Mp_1\otimes_k Np_1\) and \(\mu_{12}(M,N)=Mp_1\otimes_k Np_2\). At \(M=N=B\) both values are one-dimensional. Now apply the endomorphism of the second free input given by multiplication by \(p_1\). On the first value it fixes the basis \(p_1\otimes p_1\). On the second it kills \(p_1\otimes p_2\). Thus when both inputs are the summand \(p_1B\), the two outputs are \(k\) and zero. The input action on the common one-dimensional space decides what survives the projection.

More generally, let \(A,D\) be differential graded algebras over \(k\). Let \(\mu\) be an enriched \(k\)-bilinear operation from pairs of derived right \(A\)-modules to derived right \(D\)-modules that preserves colimits in each input. Its value \(P=\mu(A,A)\) has a right \(D\)-action. Left multiplication on the two free inputs gives it a left \(A\otimes_k A\)-action. Free resolutions then give
\[
\mu(M,N)\simeq (M\otimes_k N)\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{A\otimes_k A}P .
\]
The left action tells the resolution which maps to apply between free terms. Substituting one operation into another tensors their kernels over the intermediate algebra. Enriched natural transformations between the composites are derived bimodule maps between their kernels, including the higher homotopies. For boundary categories the formula applies in the sectors generated by the chosen probes, when the generators are compact there and the operation lands in the chosen output sector. If the fields vary with position, comparing the operations across a defect also requires their collision maps and a common domain for the next product. [Operation kernels on compactly generated sectors](#ch17-proposition-operation-kernels) and [products through an interface](#products-through-an-interface).

The same idempotents distinguish elements of \(B\) by their action. Multiplication by \(b=\lambda_1p_1+\lambda_2p_2\) on \(p_i\) has eigenvalue \(\lambda_i\). Evaluating on the unit \(p_1+p_2\) recovers \(b\) itself. For fields, a factorizing coalgebra supplies coefficients for all the occupied positions and all their compatible cuts. Regarded as a family of operations on boundary coefficients, it is called an actor. A pointing chooses the vacuum operation coherently over those configurations. In the Chevalley actor of an unbounded unital differential graded chiral algebra on the affine line, multiplication and evaluation on the canonical vacuum give inverse equivalences of the full pointed mapping spaces. Both colours of input, translation, cuts, collisions, refinements and parameter families are retained. In characteristic zero this recognition also holds for every coherent coassociative pointing on the specified genuine-unit actors. [The two idempotents and the pointing](#ch07-a-factorizing-actor-and-its-pointing) and [recognition on Chevalley coalgebras](#ch07-recognition-on-chevalley-coalgebras).

## Exterior generators, actions and residues {#essay-exterior-sources}

Let \(\epsilon\) be an exterior generator of degree one with differential and translation zero.

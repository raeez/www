# Derived adjunctions and induction

Let an involution \(g\) exchange two basis vectors \(e_0,e_1\) of a complex representation \(M\). What can a map from the trivial representation \(\mathbb C\) tell us about \(M\)? Its value at \(1\) has to be fixed by \(g\). The sum \(e_0+e_1\) can occur as that value. The difference \(e_0-e_1\) changes sign and cannot occur. Evaluation at \(1\) therefore identifies the space of maps with the single line \(\mathbb C(e_0+e_1)\) inside this two-dimensional representation.

Now start at the other end. Give a vector space \(L\) the trivial action. Each \(l\in L\) determines the equivariant map \(1\mapsto l\), and every equivariant map arises exactly once. This round trip recovers \(L\). Starting with \(M\) gave us the inclusion \(M^g\hookrightarrow M\). For any involution over \(\mathbb C\) its image is the image of \((1+g)/2\). The two round trips can therefore have different answers even when every map is known.

For affine currents, which probe should play the part of the trivial representation? Induce the trivial representation of the strictly positive currents. The resulting representation \(W\) is generated by a vector killed by the strictly positive current modes. Its lowest-energy states form the regular representation of the zero modes. Sending the generator to any vector of \(M\) killed by the positive currents therefore determines a unique affine map \(W\to M\). To include extensions as well as maps, resolve \(W\) and take the whole complex of maps. The algebra acting on the resolution lets us run the construction in reverse, starting from a complex \(L\) of coefficients. Under the smooth realisation hypotheses in (6.1), at a complex shifted level outside \(\mathbb Q\), inducing \(L\) and then taking derived maps from \(W\) recovers every \(L\), including complexes unbounded in both directions. In the opposite order, evaluation recovers precisely the affine complexes generated from \(W\) by shifts, cones, coproducts and retracts, with all energy shifts included in the graded case. A [smooth module induced from a nonzero character](#eq-ch06-first-mode-character) gives an ungraded example outside this sector.

## What the unit and counit measure {#adjunction-comparison-maps}

For a differential graded (dg) algebra \(B\), the ordinary derived category \(D(B)\) is obtained from complexes of \(B\)-modules by inverting quasi-isomorphisms. We retain its enhancement, so derived mapping complexes and their composition remain available. There is no bound on cohomological degrees. As before, differentials raise degree by one and \(L[1]^n=L^{n+1}\).

Let \(A\) be another dg algebra and let \(W\) be an \(A\)-\(B\) bimodule. Derived tensor and derived Hom give an adjunction
\[
\Phi=W\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-:D(B)
\rightleftarrows D(A):
\Psi=\operatorname{RHom}_A(W,-).
\]
Its defining property is the natural equivalence
\[
\operatorname{RHom}_A(\Phi L,M)
\simeq\operatorname{RHom}_B(L,\Psi M).
\]
**Definition (unit and counit).** The unit \(\eta_L:L\to\Psi\Phi L\) is the image of \(1_{\Phi L}\) under this equivalence. The counit \(\varepsilon_M:\Phi\Psi M\to M\) is the inverse image of \(1_{\Psi M}\). On degree-zero inputs the unit sends \(l\) to the map \(w\mapsto w\otimes l\), and the counit evaluates \(w\otimes f\mapsto f(w)\). For complexes these formulas use resolutions and the sign for interchanging homogeneous inputs. In the involution example the first map identifies \(L\) with the fixed vectors of its trivial representation. The second includes the fixed vectors in \(M\).

The two triangle identities are
\[
\varepsilon_{\Phi L}\circ\Phi(\eta_L)=1_{\Phi L},
\qquad
\Psi(\varepsilon_M)\circ\eta_{\Psi M}=1_{\Psi M}.
\]
The first composite evaluates the map just built from \(l\). The second sends a map out of \(W\) back to itself. To see what the unit detects, take a map \(f:K\to L\) in \(D(B)\). Under the adjunction, \(\Phi f\) corresponds to \(\eta_L\circ f:K\to\Psi\Phi L\). Thus the map that \(\Phi\) induces on derived Hom is postcomposition with \(\eta_L\). It is an equivalence for every source \(K\) precisely when \(\eta_L\) is an equivalence. Requiring this for every \(L\) is precisely full faithfulness of \(\Phi\).

In the other direction, take a map \(h:M\to N\) in \(D(A)\). Under the adjunction, \(\Psi h\) corresponds to \(h\circ\varepsilon_M:\Phi\Psi M\to N\). Here the map on derived Hom is precomposition with the counit. Testing it against every target \(N\) shows that \(\Psi\) is fully faithful precisely when every \(\varepsilon_M\) is an equivalence. An equivalence of the two whole categories requires both conditions.

On strict bar–cobar representatives the map \(\Omega BA\to A\) sends a one-letter generator to its letter. The connected intrinsic adjunction supplies an arrow \(A\to\Omega BA\). Both compare presentations of an algebra. Here evaluation takes a vector of \(W\) and a map from \(W\) to a module \(M\). For the exchange of \(e_0,e_1\), evaluation can land anywhere on the line spanned by their sum. Its image is the part of \(M\) generated by the test representation. [The twisting cochain](05-bar-and-coherence.md#ch05-cobar-reverses-the-construction) · [Connected consecutive-cut duality](05-bar-and-coherence.md#ch05-ordered-cuts-give-a-finite-geometric-bar-construction).

::: figure fig-adjunction-triangles

Already extension of scalars requires the derived tensor. Put \(R=\mathbb C[x]\) and \(K=R/(x)\). The free resolution
\[
\bigl[R\xrightarrow{x}R\bigr]\longrightarrow K,
\]
with the two free terms in degrees \(-1,0\), gives
\[
K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}K
\simeq\bigl[K\xrightarrow{0}K\bigr]
\simeq K\oplus K[1].
\]
Tensoring the resolution with \(K\) turns multiplication by \(x\) into zero. Its cokernel gives the ordinary tensor product in degree zero. Its kernel gives the second copy of \(K\) in degree \(-1\). A resolution can therefore detect a relation even after that relation acts by zero on the coefficients. Extension and restriction of scalars, with their derived adjunction, are described in the [Stacks Project, Example 22.33.6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BYX).

A right dg module \(P\) is **K-flat** if \(P\otimes_B X\) is acyclic whenever the left dg module \(X\) is acyclic. Such a module computes derived tensor on arbitrary coefficients. Dually, a complex \(J\) is **K-injective** if its Hom complex from every acyclic complex is acyclic. This condition concerns the whole complex; injectivity of each term alone does not suffice in the unbounded case. See the [Stacks Project, §13.31](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/070G).

### Which representations does the vacuum see? {#ch06-vacuum-generated-sector}

Give the missing line a name. For \(H=\mathbb C[C_2]\), let \(S=\mathbb C(e_0-e_1)\) carry the sign action. The projector \((1+g)/2\) splits off the fixed vectors in every representation, so taking invariants is exact and all derived maps from the trivial representation to \(S\) vanish. Now put two copies of \(e_0-e_1\) at the inputs. Their tensor is fixed because the two minus signs multiply to \(+1\). Thus the vacuum cochains of each input vanish, while those of their tensor product contain \(\mathbb C\) in degree zero.

What changes if the sign representation is also available as a probe? Put \(P=\mathbb C\oplus S\). An equivariant map between its two distinct summands equals its own negative, hence is zero. The two projections onto summands therefore span \(\operatorname{End}_H(P)\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\). Maps out of \(P\) now give separately the two eigenspaces \(M^+,M^-\) of \(g\). Evaluation is the sum map \(M^+\oplus M^-\to M\), with inverse
\[
 m\longmapsto\left(\frac{m+gm}{2},\frac{m-gm}{2}\right).
\]
These two probes recover a single representation. To combine two representations we must also specify the action of \(g\) on their tensor product.

The coproduct explains how the sign returns to the trivial representation. It sends \(g\) to \(g\otimes g\), so it tests the simultaneous action on the two inputs. For a strictly augmented dg bialgebra \(H\) over a characteristic-zero field \(F\), write \(\Delta\) for its ordinary chain coproduct and \(\varepsilon\) for its augmentation. Let \(T\) be a locally nilpotent degree-zero algebra derivation and coalgebra coderivation commuting with the differential, with \(\varepsilon T=0\). For \(u\in F\), use \((e^{uT}\otimes1)\Delta\) for the action on \(M\otimes_u N\). Put \(C(M)=\operatorname{RHom}_{H}(F,M)\), \(B'=C(F)\). Translation on the bar resolution induces a cochain algebra automorphism \(\Theta_u\) of \(B'\). The translated cup product gives a natural map
\[
 C(M)\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{B'}{}_{\Theta_u}C(N)
 \longrightarrow C(M\otimes_u N).
\]
The subscript twists the left \(B'\)-action on \(C(N)\). Use full products for Hom and algebraic direct sums for the tensor totalizations. Fix \(N\) and vary \(M\). At \(M=F\) the bar diagonal gives the left-unit isomorphism. Both sides of the comparison are exact in \(M\), so it remains an equivalence under shifts, cones and retracts. This proves the assertion for every \(M\) in \(\operatorname{thick}(F)\), the subcategory generated by \(F\) using these finite operations. Fixing \(M\) instead and using the other unit law proves the same assertion for every \(N\) in \(\operatorname{thick}(F)\). In either case the fixed input is arbitrary.

As a functor to right \(B'\)-modules, \(C\) takes \(F\) to the free module \(B'\) and identifies \(\operatorname{thick}(F)\) with the perfect modules. Passing to arbitrary coproducts requires one more hypothesis. If \(F\) is compact as an \(H\)-module, derived maps out of \(F\) commute with coproducts. Both sides of the cup-product comparison then preserve coproducts in the varying input. The comparison is therefore an equivalence whenever either input belongs to \(\operatorname{Loc}(F)\), the subcategory generated by \(F\) with arbitrary coproducts allowed. In this case \(C\) identifies \(\operatorname{Loc}(F)\) with the derived category of right \(B'\)-modules.

In the sign example \(F=\mathbb C\) is compact, and \(S\) lies in the other idempotent summand. For the pair \(F,S\) the comparison is \(0\to0\), since \(F\otimes S\) still has the sign action. For \(S,S\) the source is again zero, but \(S\otimes S\) is trivial and the comparison is \(0\to\mathbb C\). The same failure occurs with nonzero differential and translation. Tensor the group algebra with \(F[x,y]\otimes\Lambda(e,f)\) on primitive generators, with \(dx=e,\ dy=f,\ Ty=x,\ Tf=e\), and all other generator differentials and translations zero. The polynomial and exterior generators have degrees zero and one respectively. The homotopy taking \(e\mapsto x,\ f\mapsto y\) as a derivation contracts each positive total polynomial-plus-exterior weight after division by that weight. Hence the augmentation of this extra factor is a quasi-isomorphism. Both signs inflated from the group algebra give the same test at every \(u\). A map out of the trivial representation sees no sign vector. There is a similar choice for affine currents. One can require the image of the generating vector to be killed by the constant currents as well as the strictly positive ones, or leave the constant currents free to act. The second choice admits every horizontal highest weight as a test.

## Affine currents and the algebra acting on their resolution {#ch06-affine-currents-and-the-algebra-acting-on-their-resolution}

Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a nonzero finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebra. Choose an invariant form \(\kappa\), normalized so that long roots have squared length two, and write
\[
\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}x\,\operatorname{ad}y)
=h^\vee\kappa(x,y).
\]
The integer \(h^\vee\) is the dual Coxeter number. For a scalar parameter \(k\), set \(\ell=(-k-h^\vee)\kappa\). The affine mode algebra \(A_\ell\) has relations
\[
[xt^r,yt^q]=[x,y]t^{r+q}
+r\delta_{r+q,0}\ell(x,y)1.
\]
The central generator acts as \(1\). The scalar \(-k-h^\vee\) is the **raw affine level**, the coefficient of the central cocycle in this normalization. Write \(s=-k\) for the **shifted level**, so that the raw scalar level is \(s-h^\vee\). The mode algebra here has no degree derivation. Its internal energy grading assigns energy \(-r\) to \(xt^r\).

Put
\[
\mathfrak n=t\mathfrak g[t],\qquad
\mathfrak l=\mathfrak g[t^{-1}],\qquad
R=U(\mathfrak g),\qquad H=U(\mathfrak l).
\]
**Definition (smooth affine module).** An \(A_\ell\)-module is smooth if each vector is annihilated by \(t^N\mathfrak g[t]\) for some \(N\), which may depend on the vector. To test the vectors killed by *all* strictly positive modes in a smooth module, induce the trivial \(\mathfrak n\)-module to obtain
\[
W=A_\ell\otimes_{U(\mathfrak n)}\mathbb C.
\]
An affine map \(W\to M\) is determined by the image of \(1\otimes1\), and each vector of \(M\) killed by \(\mathfrak n\) gives such a map. The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem identifies the underlying vector space of \(W\) with \(H\). Its energy-zero part is the regular \(R\)-module. This distinguishes \(W\) from the scalar-top vacuum
\[
V=A_\ell\otimes_{U(\mathfrak g[t])}\mathbb C,
\]
whose generating vector is also killed by every zero mode. Its energy-zero part is \(\mathbb C\). Derived maps from \(W\) compute the higher positive-current invariants. Precomposition gives those maps their coefficient action.

A cochain model for these maps must remember two operations. Negative and constant currents can multiply the affine coefficient, and a linear form on the positive currents can remove a factor from an exterior word in the resolution. In the polynomial dg algebra \(B=Y_k(\mathfrak g)=Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\) these operations give the even generators \(b_m^a\) and the odd generators \(c_{a,m}\), respectively, for \(m\ge0\). Fix a basis \(t_a\), write \([t_a,t_b]=f_{ab}{}^ct_c\) and \(\kappa_{ab}=\kappa(t_a,t_b)\), and sum repeated Lie-algebra indices. The generator relations and differential are
\[
\begin{aligned}
[b_m^a,b_n^b]&=f_{ab}{}^cb_{m+n}^c,\\
[b_m^a,c_{b,n}]&=-f_{ac}{}^bc_{c,m+n},
&c_{a,m}c_{b,n}&=-c_{b,n}c_{a,m},\\
dc_{a,m}&=\tfrac12f_{bc}{}^a
 \sum_{r+p=m-1}c_{b,r}c_{c,p},\\
db_m^a&=mk\kappa_{ab}c_{b,m-1}
+\tfrac12f_{ab}{}^c
 \sum_{r+p=m-1}(c_{b,r}b_p^c+b_p^cc_{b,r}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The cohomological degrees of \(b,c\) are \(0,1\), and their internal weights are \(m,m+1\). Negative indices contribute nothing. In particular \(db_0^a=dc_{a,0}=0\). The \(b_0^a\) satisfy the relations of \(\mathfrak g\), while \(c_{a,0}\) already has weight one. Every term of the differential preserves weight. We call these specified degree-one generators ghosts.

Write \(B_w\) for the entire weight-\(w\) complex. Then
\[
B=\bigoplus_{w\ge0}B_w,\qquad B_0=R,\qquad
I=\bigoplus_{w>0}B_w.
\]
There are two useful quotients:
\[
B\xrightarrow{q}H\xrightarrow{e_0}R,\qquad
q(c_{a,m})=0,\quad q(b_m^a)=t_at^{-m}.
\]
The map \(e_0\) kills the strictly negative modes. Thus \(p=e_0q\) is weight-zero projection and \(I=\ker p\). These quotients forget different information. An \(H\)-module inflated along \(q\) has zero ghost action but may have nonzero negative-current action. Inflation from \(R\) kills both.

The affine Chevalley resolution of \(W\) has
\[
P^{-p}=A_\ell\otimes\Lambda^p\mathfrak n.
\]
Its differential combines current multiplication with the Lie bracket. For \(x\in\mathfrak l\), let \(a_x\) be the exterior derivation induced by \(y\mapsto\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak n}[x,y]\). The generators act on this resolution through
\[
\rho(b_m^a)=R_{t_at^{-m}}-a_{t_at^{-m}},
\qquad
\rho(c_{a,m})=\iota_{a,m+1}.
\]
Here \(R_x\) is right multiplication on the affine factor and \(\iota_{a,m+1}\) contracts with the linear functional dual to \(t_at^{m+1}\). On exterior degree zero the odd operators vanish. On \(1\otimes(t_bt^{m+1})\) the operator \(\rho(c_{a,m})\) gives \(\delta_{ab}\,1\). The even operator moves both the affine and exterior factors. Its exterior correction supplies the half-Killing-form shift in \(\ell\).

These operators give \(P\) a right \(B\)-action commuting with its left \(A_\ell\)-action. On homogeneous elements it is \(v\cdot b=(-1)^{|v||b|}\rho(b)v\). In the example just computed the exterior generator has degree \(-1\) and the ghost has degree \(1\), so right multiplication by \(c_{a,m}\) gives \(-\delta_{ab}\,1\). Equivalently, \(\rho(bb')=(-1)^{|b||b'|}\rho(b')\rho(b)\). With this graded opposite, the map \(B\to\operatorname{End}_{A_\ell}(P)^{\mathrm{op}}\) is a quasi-isomorphism for algebraic affine modules. At each homogeneous energy shift one takes the product over resolution degrees, then the algebraic direct sum over shifts. This is the endomorphism comparison of *A derived endomorphism model for the line algebra*, in the sections on the affine resolution and the generator lifts.

What changes for a smooth module? Each vector is killed by some tail \(\mathfrak n_N=t^N\mathfrak g[t]\). On that vector a positive current can be read modulo \(t^N\), keeping just the coefficients of \(t,\ldots,t^{N-1}\). This is the Taylor cutoff of order \(N\). For example, at \(N=2\) just \(xt\) survives for each \(x\in\mathfrak g\). Imposing the tail on the inducing vector and truncating the exterior inputs gives smooth resolutions of \(W\),
\[
P_N=(A_\ell/A_\ell\mathfrak n_N)
\otimes\Lambda^\bullet(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n_N)
\longrightarrow W.
\]
A negative mode changes the order needed at the source. For example, the bracket of \(t_at^{-1}\) with \(yt^r\) at sufficiently large \(r\) has mode \(r-1\). Hence the lift of \(b_1^a\) with output in \(P_N\) can be evaluated on \(P_{N+1}\). For a path of operators with weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_j\), the bound is \(N+w_1+\cdots+w_j\). In a finite diagram take the maximum of these bounds over the paths being compared, using the prescribed output order for each. Pull both sides of each identity back to that common source resolution. The comparisons at successively larger source orders are the compatibility needed for an action on smooth modules.

In \(\mathscr S_\ell=D(\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell))\), assume that these compatible operators and their higher homotopies realize \(W\) as a coherent right-\(B\) object \(\mathcal W\), with the prescribed comparison of homogeneous derived endomorphisms in the smooth category. This comparison concerns maps out of \(\mathcal W\) into itself. For maps into other smooth complexes also assume the enhanced tensor–Hom adjunction
\[
\Phi=\mathcal W\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-:
D(B)\rightleftarrows\mathscr S_\ell:
\Psi=\operatorname{RHom}_{\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell)}(\mathcal W,-).
\tag{6.1}
\]
These are the smooth realization hypotheses used below. They specify the functors and hence a unit \(L\to\Psi\Phi L\). Proving that this particular map is an equivalence requires an argument on the coefficients \(L\).

There is a parallel internally graded version. Modules are algebraic direct sums of their energy components, with arbitrary support. Set \(L\langle r\rangle_w=L_{w-r}\). Mapping complexes are formed at a specified homogeneous shift; their outer sum is algebraic. Neither convention introduces an energy completion.

In this graded category write \(C_{\rm cts}(M)=\varinjlim_N\operatorname{Hom}_{A_\ell}(P_N,M)\) for the continuous Taylor cochains. If \(M^q_e=0\) for all \(e<L\) and every \(q\), their natural map \(c_M:C_{\rm cts}(M)\to\Psi M\) is an equivalence. At input weight \(w\ge L\), restrict to the positive currents and apply the exact energy-window functors for \([L,w]\). The action there factors through the Lie algebra \(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n_N\) for \(N>w-L\). Its Chevalley resolution becomes a bounded complex of projectives on that window. Weights below \(L\) give zero. If only the cohomology has a common energy floor, the derived product-Hom model still computes \(\Psi M\). A smooth resolution \(X\to W\) can have \(H^0C_{\rm cts}(X)=0\) while \(\mathrm{id}_W\) survives in \(H^0\Psi X\). [The supported smooth comparison](#atlas-c-induction-first-kind-c-question).

## The unit on modules with zero ghost action {#ch06-cofree-coefficients-give-the-first-unit-theorem}

To test the unit on a module with zero ghost action, we want one positive-current invariant for each coefficient vector. There is a model in which that invariant can be evaluated on the empty word \(1\). Take the dual of each homogeneous piece of the enveloping algebra of the positive currents and form their direct sum,
\[
C_{\mathfrak g}=\bigoplus_{E\ge0}U(\mathfrak n)_E^\vee
\]
where \(E\) is the positive current degree, with \(\deg(xt^r)=r\). Each summand is finite dimensional, and every element of \(C_{\mathfrak g}\) has finite support in this degree. Put
\[
(L_x\phi)(u)=\phi(xu),\qquad
(D_x\phi)(u)=\phi(ux).
\]
If \(\phi\) vanishes above degree \(E\), a mode of degree greater than \(E\) acts by zero on it from either side. There are only finitely many basis modes of degree at most \(E\). Both operators lower the support bound. The two orders of applying \(L_y\) and \(D_x\) agree since both evaluate \(\phi\) on \(yux\). Right multiplication on the argument gives the positive-current representation \(D\). Left multiplication gives \([L_x,L_y]=-L_{[x,y]}\). For a homogeneous positive-current basis \(x_i=t_at^r\), write \(c_i=c_{a,r-1}\). The complex
\[
\mathcal Q=C_{\mathfrak g}\otimes B,\qquad
d_{\mathcal Q}(\phi\otimes b)
=\phi\otimes db+\sum_iL_{x_i}\phi\otimes c_i b
\tag{6.2}
\]
has a pointwise finite differential. For instance, let \(\phi\) extract the coefficient of one degree-one basis mode \(x_j\) and vanish in every other degree. Then \(L_{x_i}\phi\) vanishes for \(i\ne j\) and \(L_{x_j}\phi\) extracts the coefficient of the empty word. Thus the twist on \(\phi\otimes1\) lands in support degree zero. In general the twist lowers the support degree strictly. Filtering by this degree exhibits \(\mathcal Q\) as semifree on the right over \(B\).

The full affine action is restricted coinduction. Using the even \(H\)-action on the underlying graded algebra \(B\), identify \(\mathcal Q\) with
\[
\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_H(A_\ell,B):
f|_{U(\mathfrak n)_{\ge d}}=0\text{ for some }d\},
\qquad (af)(b)=f(ba).
\]
The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt decomposition gives the preceding tensor description. The twist in (6.2) makes its differential affine-linear at precisely the level \(\ell=(-k-h^\vee)\kappa\).

Under the smooth realization hypotheses, \(\mathcal Q\) is equivalent to the whole coherent right-\(B\) object \(\mathcal W\), with its affine action retained. Consequently it computes \(\Phi L\) for every ordinary unbounded \(L\). This is the cofree-model theorem in *The cofree native model and inflated-base unit for every simple Lie algebra*. The comparison preserves the specified action on \(P\).

If \(T\) is an arbitrary unbounded \(H\)-complex inflated to \(B\), every ghost acts by zero. Formula (6.2) becomes
\[
\Phi T\simeq(C_{\mathfrak g}\otimes T,1\otimes d_T).
\]
Its positive-current action is \(D\) on the first factor. The restricted dual with arbitrary vector-space coefficients is injective in the smooth positive-current category. More is needed for an unbounded \(T\): split its underlying complex over \(\mathbb C\) into its cohomology and a contractible complex. Tensoring this splitting with \(C_{\mathfrak g}\) is positive-current-linear. It exhibits the restriction as homotopy equivalent to a product of injectives placed in single degrees, hence as K-injective. At each degree only one of those factors contributes; no completion of \(C_{\mathfrak g}\) is involved.

A functional killed by every \(D_x\) vanishes on every positive-length word \(ux\). Only its value on \(1\) remains. Thus the invariants are \(\delta_1\otimes T\), where \(\delta_1\) is the augmentation functional on \(U(\mathfrak n)\). To identify the *map*, take a smooth K-injective replacement \(j:\mathcal Q\otimes_BT\to J\). On homogeneous \(t\in T,\ v\in\mathcal Q\), the unit sends \(t\) to \(v\mapsto(-1)^{|t||v|}j(v\otimes t)\). The inducing vector of \(W\) maps to \(\delta_1\otimes1\) in degree zero. Evaluation there gives \(j(\delta_1\otimes t)\), exactly the class of \(t\) under the invariant calculation. [The cofree realization and inflated unit](#atlas-c-induction-all-level-inflated-unit).

**Conditional unit theorem for inflated coefficients.** For every simple \(\mathfrak g\) and every \(k\in\mathbb C\), under the smooth realization hypotheses of (6.1), the actual unit
\[
T\xrightarrow{\eta_T}\Psi\Phi T
\]
is an equivalence for every ordinary unbounded \(H\)-complex inflated along \(q\), and therefore for every inflated \(R\)-complex. This holds with arbitrary algebraic internal support or without an internal grading.

At the critical level and at raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) level \(-3\) the same calculation gives the unit on inflated coefficients. If a ghost acts on \(L\), (6.2) contributes \(L_{x_i}\phi\otimes c_i l\) to the differential on \(\phi\otimes l\). The splitting used for \(T\) need no longer compute the derived maps. One way forward is to build \(L\) from complexes on which the ghosts act by zero.

## Finite descent from a quotient {#ch06-finite-descent-from-a-quotient}

If two terms of a triangle have invertible affine unit, naturality and exactness give an invertible unit on the third. We would like to recover a general coefficient complex by finitely many such triangles. What has to vanish to make this process terminate? The ideal \((\epsilon)\) of \(\mathbb C[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) has square zero, yet \(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\) spans its tensor square over \(\mathbb C\). A tower built by tensoring must control the tensor powers themselves. Consider a split dg algebra extension
\[
R\longrightarrow B\xrightarrow{p}R,\qquad p|_R=1,
\qquad I=\ker p.
\]
Assume \(R\) is an ordinary algebra, \(B\) is K-flat on both \(R\)-sides, and for a finite integer \(N\),
\[
I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}N}\simeq0
\quad\text{as a complex of \(R\)-bimodules}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
The two multiplication actions are part of this assertion; \(R\) need not be central.

Let \(F=R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-\), and let \(U\) be restriction of scalars along \(p\), also called inflation. A relative bar resolution converts (6.3) into vanishing of the \(N\)-fold derived tensor of \(I\) over \(B\). For every \(L\), put
\[
T_j(L)=I^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}j}
\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L,\qquad T_0(L)=L.
\]
There is a functorial tower
\[
0\simeq T_N(L)\longrightarrow T_{N-1}(L)
\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow T_0(L)=L
\tag{6.4}
\]
whose triangles are
\[
T_{j+1}(L)\longrightarrow T_j(L)
\xrightarrow{u_{T_j(L)}}UF(T_j(L))
\longrightarrow T_{j+1}(L)[1].
\tag{6.5}
\]
The first triangle comes from tensoring \(I\to B\to R\) on the right with \(L\). Its map out of \(L\) is the scalar-extension unit \(u_L:L\to U(R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L)\), represented by \(l\mapsto1\otimes l\) on a resolution. Repeating with \(T_j(L)\) gives (6.5). Each cofiber is inflated from an unrestricted \(R\)-complex with its actual outer \(R\)-action. It is the affine unit \(\eta\) of (6.1) that we test on these cofibers.

The bar argument explains the change of base from \(R\) to \(B\). Resolve an \(I\)-factor by terms
\[
\bigl(I\otimes_R I^{\otimes_R q}\otimes_R B\bigr)[q],
\qquad q\ge0.
\]
In an \(N\)-fold derived tensor over \(B\), each resulting bar term contains at least \(N\) copies of \(I\) tensored over \(R\). Such a term is acyclic by (6.3) and the two-sided K-flatness. Finite bar-length filtrations and their exact algebraic union prove the asserted vanishing without a cohomological bound.

For an exact enhanced adjunction \(\Phi\dashv\Psi\), the class of objects on which its unit is invertible is closed under finite triangles and retracts. Thus (6.4) proves:

**Finite descent theorem.** Under the split-extension, K-flatness and tensor-vanishing hypotheses above, the actual unit is invertible on every \(L\in D(B)\) if it is invertible on every unrestricted inflated \(R\)-complex.

Start at \(T_N(L)=0\), where the unit is invertible. At the \(j\)-th triangle it is invertible on \(T_{j+1}(L)\) by the preceding step and on \(UF(T_j(L))\) by the hypothesis on inflated coefficients. Exactness gives the assertion on \(T_j(L)\). After finitely many triangles one reaches \(L\), using only these values of \(\Psi\). [The tower and its unit maps, Theorems A–B](#atlas-c-induction-finite-derived-tensor-tower).

Return to \(B=\mathbb C[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), \(R=\mathbb C\) and \(I=(\epsilon)\). For every \(N\ge1\) the vector \(\epsilon^{\otimes N}\) spans
\[
I^{\otimes_{\mathbb C}N}\cong\mathbb C .
\]
Thus there is no exponent as in (6.3). Multiplying the images in \(B\) nevertheless kills this vector as soon as \(N\ge2\). The ordinary ideal filtration gives another way to prove finite descent. For a strict dg ideal \(I=\ker(B\to R)\) with \(I^N=0\), tensor the filtration \(0=I^N\subset I^{N-1}\subset\cdots\subset B\) on the right with \(L\) over \(B\), using the derived tensor product. Its successive cofibres are \((I^j/I^{j+1})\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L\). Their left action factors through \(R\), since \(I I^j\subset I^{j+1}\). Thus invertibility of the unit on all inflated \(R\)-complexes implies invertibility on \(L\) by finite induction. For the dual numbers this filtration has two pieces, although the derived tensor powers of \(I\) have nonzero degree-zero homology at every length. The multiplication map itself gives a third test.

The same example separates a zero tensor object from a zero morphism. For a dg algebra map \(B\to H\), form its fibre \(J\to B\to H\) in the derived category of \(B\)-bimodules. It suffices that one tensor power of the **fibre-inclusion map**
\[
\mu_N:J^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}N}\longrightarrow B
\tag{6.6}
\]
be zero as a bimodule morphism, together with invertibility of the unit on all inflated unbounded \(H\)-complexes. In the dual-number example \(H=\mathbb C\), \(J=(\epsilon)\). The product of the images of two copies of \(J\) in \(B\) vanishes strictly, so \(\mu_2=0\) even on a bimodule resolution. Yet \(H^0(J\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}J)=J\otimes_BJ\cong\mathbb C\). In general a chosen bimodule nullhomotopy of \(\mu_N\) identifies its cofiber with \(B\oplus (J^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}N})[1]\). In particular \(B\) is a retract of the cofiber.

To see which coefficients build this cofiber, put \(J_0=B\) and \(J_j=J^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}j}\) for \(j\ge1\). Apply \(J\to B\) to the leftmost factor at each step. The resulting maps \(J_{j+1}\to J_j\) compose to \(\mu_N:J_N\to B\). Tensoring the triangle \(J\to B\to H\) on the right with \(J_j\) identifies the cofiber of one step with \(H\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}J_j\). Its left \(H\)-action is multiplication on the first factor. The cofiber triangles for the successive composites build the cofiber of \(\mu_N\) from these \(N\) pieces. Tensor the entire diagram on the right with \(L\). Each piece is now inflated from an \(H\)-complex, and the retract \(B\) gives the retract \(L\). The unit is invertible on the pieces by hypothesis, hence on their finite extension and on its retract \(L\). [Finite bimodule descent for the actual adjunction unit, Theorem 1.1](#atlas-c-induction-finite-fibre-map-descent).

## The sharp exponent in every simple affine type {#ch06-four-factors-suffice-for-nonrational-mathfraksl3}

For affine currents the quotient is \(Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\to U(\mathfrak g)\). How many positive-weight factors can survive tensoring over \(R=U(\mathfrak g)\)? Fix \(s\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Q\), write \(I=\bigoplus_{m>0}B_m\), and choose positive roots \(\Phi_0^+\) in \(\mathfrak g\), with long roots of squared length two. We will compare the length of a surviving tensor word with \(D=|\Phi_0^+|\).

In \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) the Weyl group permutes three coordinates. Exchanging adjacent entries in positions \(1,2\), then \(2,3\), then \(1,2\) sends the half-sum of positive roots \(\rho=(1,0,-1)\) through the sequence
\[
 (1,0,-1)\longmapsto(0,1,-1)\longmapsto(0,-1,1)
 \longmapsto(-1,0,1).
\]
The differences of the entries being exchanged are \(1,2,1\). All three pairs have now reversed order. The proof below identifies these differences with the weights of three successive positive-current factors and explains why a fourth factor cannot survive. In higher rank the same bound counts the positive roots.

**Theorem (sharp tensor exponent).** For the positive-weight ideal \(I\) at \(s\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Q\), the first vanishing derived tensor power over \(R=U(\mathfrak g)\) is one more than the number \(D\) of positive roots. More precisely,
\[
 I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}D}\not\simeq0,\qquad
 I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}(D+1)}\simeq0
 \quad\text{in }D(R\otimes R^{\mathrm{op}}).
\]
This is an assertion about the bimodule with its two multiplication actions. It holds in the algebraic internal grading and after forgetting that grading. [The sharp tensor exponent](#atlas-c-ur-10).

Why should a root count bound a tensor length? A **horizontal Verma module** \(M_\tau\) is the \(R\)-module induced from a highest-weight line for a Borel subalgebra of \(\mathfrak g\). Take \(N\) in horizontal category \(\mathcal O\), the category of finitely generated weight modules with locally finite action of the positive Borel. Give \(N\) energy \(a\) and extend its action to \(\mathfrak g[t]\) by making \(\mathfrak n=t\mathfrak g[t]\) act by zero. Denote the induced affine module by \(\operatorname{Ind}_aN\). By the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem the ordered negative-mode monomials give a right basis over the inducing algebra, so induction is exact. The degree derivation acts by \(-(a+j)\) on a negative-mode word of energy \(j\) applied to \(N\). For \(N=M_\tau\) the induced module is the ordinary affine Verma \(M(\tau,a)\).

What does one factor \(B_m\) do to this top? It is bounded and free as a right \(R\)-module in each cochain degree, so \(B_m\otimes_R N\) computes its derived tensor with \(N\). Putting the ghosts first in the ordered basis identifies the horizontal complexes
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_m\otimes_R N
 &\cong C^\bullet_{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathfrak n,\operatorname{Ind}_aN)_{a+m}\\
 &=\bigl(\Lambda^\bullet(\mathfrak n^\vee_{\mathrm{cts}})
               \otimes\operatorname{Ind}_aN\bigr)_{a+m}.
\end{aligned}
\]
{#eq-ch06-positive-current-cochains}
The continuous dual \(\mathfrak n^\vee_{\mathrm{cts}}\) is the direct sum of the duals of the positive modes. A product of \(p\) distinct basis ghosts evaluates on their ordered wedge with sign \((-1)^{p(p-1)/2}\). Here the differential is the Hom differential from the Chevalley resolution. Multiplying cochains in degree \(p\) by \((-1)^{p(p+1)/2}\) gives the usual Chevalley–Eilenberg convention. For a zero mode \(x\), precomposition with \(R_x-a_x\) gives the left action \((xF)(\omega)=xF(\omega)-F(a_x\omega)\) on a cochain \(F\) with exterior input \(\omega\). Right multiplication on the coefficient is the \(R\)-action balanced against \(N\).

The degree-zero cycles of these cochains form \(\Gamma_b X=(X^{\mathfrak n})_b\) for \(X=\operatorname{Ind}_aN\) and \(b=a+m\). These are the vectors of energy \(b\) killed by every positive current. To use affine linkage in higher degrees we need the right derived functor of \(\Gamma_b\) in affine category \(\mathcal O\). At nonrational shift we can compute it one finite block at a time.

Fix an affine highest weight and let \(\mu\) be its horizontal part shifted by the finite Weyl vector \(\rho=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha\). A real root \(\alpha+n\delta\), where \(n\) is its loop mode, is integral at this weight precisely when
\[
  (\mu,\alpha)+ns\in\tfrac{(\alpha,\alpha)}2\mathbb Z .
\]
If two distinct modes \(n,n'\) satisfied this condition for the same \(\alpha\), subtraction would give \((n-n')s\in\tfrac{(\alpha,\alpha)}2\mathbb Z\). The squared root length \((\alpha,\alpha)\) is rational in the basic normalization, whereas \(s\notin\mathbb Q\). Thus at most one mode lifts each finite root. On the rational span of \(1,s\) and the finitely many simple-root pairings, choose a rational-linear functional \(f\) with \(f(1)=0,\ f(s)=1\). Applying \(f\) to the integrality condition now shows how the lifts fit together. In fact the mode of every integral root is \(n=-f((\mu,\alpha))\). All the integral roots lie in the graph of one linear function on their projected root span. Reflection in that graph projects faithfully to reflection in a finite root subsystem. The integral Weyl group \(W'\) is finite and its longest length is at most \(D\). This step includes the short roots.

The highest-weight labels of the affine block form an orbit of this finite group. Choose a highest-weight truncation containing all these labels. Its projective covers serve in the full block. Every nonzero subquotient of a Verma has a simple subquotient with one of the listed highest weights. The full Cartan weight spaces there are finite dimensional, so the sum of their dimensions bounds the length of any filtration. BGG reciprocity now gives finite Verma filtrations of the projective covers. Restricted duality, which dualizes each full Cartan weight space, turns these covers into injective hulls with finite filtrations by dual Vermas.

Consider one such dual Verma, with horizontal dual top \(M_\sigma^\vee\) at energy \(c\). Its restriction to the positive currents is
\[
 C_{\mathfrak g}\otimes M_\sigma^\vee,\qquad
 C_{\mathfrak g}=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}U(\mathfrak n)_j^*,
 \qquad (D_x\phi)(u)=\phi(ux).
\]
{#eq-ch06-dual-verma-positive-currents}
The negative-mode slices are finite dimensional. Their duals give the first factor, even when the horizontal top is infinite dimensional. The Chevalley anti-involution reverses products, so \(x\) acts by right multiplication on the argument \(u\) and acts trivially on the second factor. With this action \(C_{\mathfrak g}\) tensored with any vector space is [injective for smooth positive currents](#ch06-cofree-coefficients-give-the-first-unit-theorem). The finite filtrations therefore split on restriction, and the block injectives remain injective for smooth positive currents.

The cochains of a dual Verma show what this injectivity gives at one energy. At energy \(c+1\) the usual cochain differential sends \(\phi\in(\mathfrak g t)^*\) to \(x\mapsto(D_x\phi)(1)=\phi(x)\). It identifies the two terms, both tensored with \(M_\sigma^\vee\). In every weight the coefficient action is dual to right multiplication, and the bracket acts through the exterior boundary. Thus the cochains at energy \(c+j\) are the dual of the weight-\(j\) part of the free Chevalley–Eilenberg resolution \(U(\mathfrak n)\otimes\Lambda^\bullet\mathfrak n\to\mathbb C\), tensored with \(M_\sigma^\vee\). The augmented resolution is exact in each weight and each weight slice is finite dimensional. Its dual has no higher cohomology. The degree-zero invariants come from evaluation on the empty word and give \(M_\sigma^\vee\) at \(j=0\).

Measure energies relative to one label and let \(e_0\) be the least top energy in the block. Every finite-length object in the block has this lower bound. Its energy slices lie in horizontal \(\mathcal O\), as they do for a Verma by the ordered negative-mode basis and hence for subquotients and finite extensions. A cochain at energy \(b\) on inputs of total mode \(j\) takes values in \(X_{b-j}\). Each input has mode at least one, so the exterior degree \(p\) satisfies \(p\le j\le b-e_0\). At fixed \(b\) the cochain terms are finite sums of energy components tensored with finite-dimensional exterior duals, so they are exact in \(X\). Apply these cochains to a bounded-below resolution of \(X\) by block injectives. Taking resolution cohomology first recovers the cochains of \(X\). Taking current cohomology first leaves only \(\Gamma_b\) of the resolution, by the acyclicity just proved. The common cutoff makes every total diagonal finite. Thus the cochains represent \(\mathbf R\Gamma_b X\) in horizontal \(\mathcal O\). Applying this to \(\operatorname{Ind}_aN\), with the same finite totalizations for a bounded horizontal complex \(N\), gives
\[
 B_m\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}N\simeq
 \mathbf R\Gamma_{a+m}(\operatorname{Ind}_aN)
 \quad\text{in }D^b(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{hor}}).
\]
{#eq-ch06-derived-positive-current-invariants}

An affine map from \(M(\eta,b)\) to \(X\) is determined by the horizontal map from its inducing top \(M_\eta\) to \(\Gamma_b X\). Induction and projection to the chosen affine block are exact, so their right adjoint \(\Gamma_b\) sends block injectives to horizontal injectives. Deriving the adjunction gives
\[
 \mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_{\mathrm{hor}}}
        (M_\eta,\mathbf R\Gamma_b X)
 \simeq
 \mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_{\mathrm{aff}}}
        (M(\eta,b),X).
\]
{#eq-ch06-finite-block-induction-adjunction}
Horizontal Vermas generate the bounded derived category of each horizontal block by induction on its highest weights and on length. If \(B_m\otimes_R M_\tau\) has a nonzero component in such a block, a shift of some \(M_\eta\) in it therefore maps nontrivially to that component. By the adjunction this gives a nonzero affine Verma Ext from \(M(\eta,a+m)\) to \(M(\tau,a)\). Zero-mode integral reflections keep the horizontal linkage class at the new energy in this same affine block. Generation by the horizontal Vermas lets us apply the test again to a bounded intermediate complex.

In a fixed affine block, horizontal linkage classes at their assigned energies are indexed by double cosets \(W_P\backslash W'/W_K\). The zero-mode integral roots generate \(W_P\), and \(W_K\) fixes a dominant representative of the affine weight. Each coset has a largest element in Bruhat order, the order on Weyl elements defined by reduced subwords. Which way does the Verma Ext above move in this order? Write \(M(\nu)\) for the affine Verma of full highest weight \(\nu\), and \(L(\nu)\) for its simple quotient. A downward step sends \(\nu\) to \(s_\beta\cdot\nu\) for a positive real root \(\beta\) for which the pairing of \(\nu+\widehat\rho\) with \(\beta^\vee\) is a positive integer. Here \(\widehat\rho\) is the affine Weyl vector. Write \(\xi\preccurlyeq\tau\) if such steps lead from \(\tau\) to \(\xi\), allowing an empty chain. At this noncritical level the Kac–Kazhdan strong-linkage criterion says that \(L(\xi)\) occurs in \(M(\tau)\) precisely when \(\xi\preccurlyeq\tau\). [Kac and Kazhdan, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708%2879%2990066-5)

Let \(P(\xi)\) be the projective cover of \(L(\xi)\) in this finite block. Its map onto \(M(\xi)\) has a kernel filtered by Vermas of strictly higher linked weights, by BGG reciprocity and the projective Verma filtration. Resolving these Vermas in turn gives a projective resolution of \(M(\xi)\) with labels \(\sigma\) satisfying \(\xi\preccurlyeq\sigma\). A nonzero map \(P(\sigma)\to M(\tau)\) puts its simple head \(L(\sigma)\) among the composition factors of \(M(\tau)\). Thus a nonzero Ext from \(M(\xi)\) to \(M(\tau)\) forces \(\xi\preccurlyeq\sigma\preccurlyeq\tau\) for some \(\sigma\). Read from the dominant representative, each downward reflection increases Bruhat order on the orbit labels. Passing to the largest double-coset representatives preserves that order. A surviving \(B_m\) with \(m>0\) changes the assigned energy and hence the coset. Its largest representative strictly increases in length, so at most \(D\) such steps survive. [Fiebig, Theorems 2.2, 2.3 and 3.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305378v2)

To pass from the module tests to bimodules, fix positive weights \(m_1,\ldots,m_j\) and let \(T\) be the corresponding tensor word. Each \(B_{m_i}\) is bounded, free on either \(R\)-side in each degree, and locally finite for the adjoint action \(xv-vx\). The cohomology bimodules of \(T\) are finitely generated and retain this local finiteness. Suppose \(H^d(T)\) is the highest nonzero one. Take a simple bimodule quotient \(S\) of \(H^d(T)\). The right centre acts on \(S\) by a character, by Dixmier's lemma. Choose a horizontal weight \(\lambda\) of that character which is dominant for the shifted action of its integral Weyl group. Bernstein–Gelfand's theorem detects \(S\) by the dot-dominant Verma \(M_\lambda\), including when \(\lambda\) lies on a wall. Right exactness of tensor product at the highest cohomological degree gives
\[
 H^d(T\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}M_\lambda)
 \cong H^d(T)\otimes_R M_\lambda
 \twoheadrightarrow S\otimes_R M_\lambda\ne0 .
\]
For example, in \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) normalise the Casimir \(\Omega\) to act on \(M_\lambda\) by \(\lambda(\lambda+2)\). Tensoring \(R/(\Omega+1)\) with \(M_\lambda\) gives \(M_\lambda/(\lambda+1)^2M_\lambda\). Only the singular weight \(\lambda=-1\) detects this bimodule. [Bernstein and Gelfand, Theorem 5.9(ii)](https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1980__41_2_245_0.pdf#page=34)

Consequently vanishing on all the horizontal Verma tests forces the tensor word to vanish. At each total weight there are finitely many positive tuples. The algebraic direct sum of the acyclic words is acyclic, proving the upper bound.

For sharpness choose the linear function \(d_0\) on the root lattice taking value one on each finite simple root. Write \(d_0(\alpha)=(\gamma,\alpha)\) and take \(\mu=\rho-s\gamma\). Every finite root now has its integral lift \(\alpha+d_0(\alpha)\delta\). All positive roots have positive mode, and the weight is regular. Starting at the identity, multiply successively on the left by the simple reflections along a reduced path to the longest element. Each lifted simple root has mode one. At the \(j\)-th reflection the positive coroot pairing is an integer \(q_j\). Each step gives an actual injection of affine Vermas that sends its horizontal top into the degree-zero cycles of \(B_{q_j}\otimes_R M_{\tau_{j-1}}\). The composite of the injections remains nonzero. Multiplying the degree-zero coefficients of \(B\) in the order \(q_D,\ldots,q_1\) acts on the initial top by this composite. The complex has no negative cochain terms, so its nonzero degree-zero cycle survives in cohomology. This realizes \(D\) positive factors on one choice of top.

The same product detects the \(D\)-fold fibre-inclusion map \(I^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}D}\to B\). After restriction to \(R\), inserting the length-zero terms of the relative bars gives a map from the \(R\)-balanced tensor word. Composing with the restricted \(D\)-fold map gives the nonzero multiplication just computed. In length \(D+1\) the [relative bar argument](#ch06-finite-descent-from-a-quotient) gives zero even for the \(B\)-balanced source.

**Conditional full-unit theorem.** At nonrational shifted level \(s\), in every finite-dimensional simple complex type, assume the smooth realization (6.1) with its specified right action, common Taylor cutoffs and enhanced homogeneous tensor–Hom comparison. For every ordinary unbounded \(B\)-complex \(L\) the original unit is an equivalence
\[
 \eta_L:L\xrightarrow{\sim}\Psi\Phi L .
\]
This holds for the ungraded adjunction and for algebraic internal grading with arbitrary support. Algebraically, (6.4) has at most \(D+1\) inflated \(R\)-complex factors. If \(R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L=0\), (6.5) first gives \(T_1(L)\simeq L\). The same base change now vanishes on \(T_1(L)\), and induction gives \(L\simeq T_{D+1}(L)=0\). Thus scalar extension detects zero objects. The affine assertion uses the additional fact that \(\eta\) is invertible on every inflated factor. Its naturality squares and the finite triangles then give \(\eta_L\). [The full unit and its hypotheses](#atlas-c-ur-05).

### Four factors for \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) {#ch06-a2-four-step-example}

For the trace form on \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) the raw affine level is \(s-3\). The positive roots are \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_1+\alpha_2\). The three adjacent transpositions computed above give the nonzero tensor word \(B_1\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}B_2\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}B_1\). The longest Weyl element has length three, so a word of four positive-weight factors would require one more strict increase in length. Thus
\[
 I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}4}\simeq0,\qquad
 I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}3}\not\simeq0 .
 \tag{6.7}
\]
The four-step tower below is this case of (6.4). It also follows from *The fourth derived tensor power of the native sl3 positive ideal* and *The full ordinary native unit for sl3 at nonrational parameter*. In type \(A_1\) two factors suffice. In \(A_3\) a surviving six-factor word has weights \(1,2,3,1,2,1\) in tensor order and every seven-factor word vanishes. In \(B_2\), \(G_2\), \(F_4\) and \(E_8\) the least vanishing exponents are \(5,7,25,121\), respectively.

## Which affine representations does evaluation recover? {#ch06-the-image-and-its-original-counit}

Write \(\operatorname{Loc}(W)\) for the smallest full stable subcategory containing \(W\) and closed under arbitrary coproducts and retracts. In the internally graded case use all energy shifts:
\[
\mathscr C=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{Loc}_{\mathscr S_\ell}(W),&\text{ungraded},\\
\operatorname{Loc}_{\mathscr S_\ell}
\{W\langle r\rangle:r\in\mathbb Z\},&\text{graded}.
\end{cases}
\]
Under the preceding full-unit theorem,
\[
D(B)\simeq\mathscr C.
\tag{6.8}
\]
The source is generated by \(B\), or by all its internal shifts, using semifree resolutions. The left adjoint preserves coproducts and sends these generators to \(W\) and its shifts. Full faithfulness also lifts cones and retracts between image objects. These facts identify the image exactly with \(\mathscr C\).

Take the opening representation again. Every equivariant map from a trivial representation into \(M\) factors uniquely through the invariant line \(\mathbb C(e_0+e_1)\), even though \(M\) also contains the sign line. Now take any smooth complex \(M\) and test it by maps from objects of \(\mathscr C\). Under the full-unit hypotheses of (6.8), the original counit gives the **coreflection** of \(M\) into \(\mathscr C\),
\[
\Phi\Psi M\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_M}M.
\]
This means that for every \(C\in\mathscr C\), it induces an equivalence on derived maps from \(C\). To prove it, the second triangle identity and the full-unit theorem give
\[
\Psi(\varepsilon_M)=\eta_{\Psi M}^{-1}.
\]
The cone of \(\varepsilon_M\) is therefore killed by \(\Psi\). By adjunction all maps to that cone from \(\Phi L\) vanish in every degree. If \(M\) belongs to \(\mathscr C\), both ends of the counit and hence its cone belong to \(\mathscr C\). Taking the cone itself as test source shows that its identity vanishes, so the counit is an equivalence. Conversely, if the counit is an equivalence, it identifies \(M\) with \(\Phi\Psi M\), which belongs to \(\mathscr C\).

An object is compact in a category if maps from it commute with arbitrary coproducts. The free module \(B\) is compact in \(D(B)\), so \(W\) is compact **inside \(\mathscr C\)**, with all shifts included when graded. Compactness against arbitrary smooth targets would require an additional statement about \(\Psi\) on coproducts in the ambient category.

## Infinite extensions at other levels {#ch06-infinite-extensions-at-other-levels}

In (6.4) one starts at zero and works backwards through finitely many triangles. With infinitely many terms there is no last zero from which to start. An extension class can survive every finite composite, yet become invisible on cohomology after affine realization. The critical level gives a telescope with precisely these two properties.

At critical raw level \(-h^\vee\), take \(B=Y_0\) and
\[
E=B\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}\mathbb C,\qquad \Phi E\simeq V.
\]
Assume the smooth realization and its compatibility with forgetting smoothness. On objects whose cohomology is bounded below in energy, assume the full comparison with graded algebraic affine modules and the corresponding lower-weight unit on the line side. To form the telescope we need maps
\[
V\longrightarrow V[1]\langle-(n+2)\rangle,\qquad n\ge0,
\]
{#eq-ch06-critical-vacuum-extensions}
whose successive composites remain nonzero. Start with the quadratic central field at critical level. Write \(\Omega\) for the scalar-top vacuum vector, \(S_0=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\kappa^{ij}(t_it^{-1})(t_jt^{-1})\Omega\) for its state, and \(S_n=T^nS_0/n!\) for its translates, where \(T\) is translation and \((\kappa^{ij})\) is the inverse form. Differentiating the current action on \(S_n\) as the level varies in direction \(\kappa\) gives a relative one-cocycle \(\beta_n\) of energy \(n+2\). For instance \(\beta_0(xt)=xt^{-1}\Omega\). In relative vacuum cohomology, Frenkel and Teleman identify these classes with the differentials \(dS_n\) of independent polynomial coordinates on the space of opers on the disc for the Langlands dual group. The Yoneda product of \(r\) consecutive classes corresponds to a wedge of \(r\) distinct coordinate differentials and is therefore nonzero. [Frenkel–Teleman, Theorem 4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401428v2#page=18).

Why does this product survive as an ordinary extension of \(V\)? The vacuum extensions of Frenkel and Teleman have an action of \(G[[t]]\) integrating the nonnegative currents, where \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)=\mathfrak g\). Forget this integrability to obtain a smooth affine extension, then forget smoothness to obtain an extension of \(A_\ell\)-modules. Both functors are exact, so they preserve the order of splicing extensions. To test whether a splice becomes a boundary, put \(\mathfrak a=\mathfrak g[t]=\mathfrak g\ltimes\mathfrak n\). Since \(V=A_\ell\otimes_{U(\mathfrak a)}\mathbb C\), inducing the free Chevalley resolution of \(\mathbb C\) computes ordinary affine self-Ext by Shapiro's lemma. On a class of energy \(w\) the forgetful map takes the form
\[
 H^\bullet(\mathfrak g[[t]],\mathfrak g;V)_w
 \longrightarrow H^\bullet(\mathfrak a;V)_w
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Ext}^\bullet_{A_\ell}(V,V).
\]
{#eq-ch06-critical-relative-to-ordinary}
On extension cochains one restricts the current inputs to polynomial currents and evaluates the endomorphism coefficient on \(\Omega\). The resulting relative cochain is \(\mathfrak g\)-invariant and extends by zero on any constant-current input. These operations use the same splittings of the extension after forgetting. [Frenkel–Teleman, Proposition 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401428v2#page=6) and [Lemma 3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401428v2#page=15).

On inputs of total mode \(d\), an energy-\(w\) cochain takes values in \(V_{w-d}\). The vacuum has nonnegative energy and finite-dimensional energy slices. Thus \(d\le w\) and only finite jets occur. Split the inputs into \(p\) constant currents and \(q\) strictly positive currents. The resulting bicomplex is \(C^p(\mathfrak g; C^q(\mathfrak n;V)_w)\), with one differential increasing \(p\) and the other increasing \(q\). Project to \(p=0\), then to the trivial \(\mathfrak g\)-isotypic summand there. The latter projection commutes with the positive-current differential by complete reducibility of the finite-dimensional horizontal modules. The composite is a cochain retraction onto \(C^\bullet(\mathfrak n;V)_w^{\mathfrak g}\), the relative complex.

An ordinary algebraic primitive \(F\) could have components in arbitrarily many energies. For each homogeneous input \(u\) of total mode \(d\) set
\[
 (\operatorname{pr}_w F)(u)=\operatorname{pr}_{V_{w-d}}F(u).
\]
{#eq-ch06-critical-cochain-projection}
The current action and bracket preserve the total cochain energy, so this projection commutes with the differential. A primitive for a relative class of energy \(w\) would therefore give a primitive at \(w\), and the preceding retraction would give a relative primitive. This proves injectivity into ordinary algebraic self-Ext. At \(w=0\) the relative complex consists of the vacuum line in degree zero, while the absolute complex also has the Cartan class \((x,y,z)\mapsto\kappa(x,[y,z])\) in \(H^3(\mathfrak g;\mathbb C)\). The injection is proper. [Frenkel–Teleman, Proposition 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401428v2#page=21).

The source \(V\) and each shifted target separately have a lower energy bound. Full faithfulness of forgetting smoothness on these bounded-below energy subcategories lifts each homogeneous algebraic class uniquely to a graded smooth map. Its nonzero algebraic image also detects the ordered products after forgetting the grading. Since \(\Phi E\simeq V\) and the actual unit is invertible on each shifted \(E\), adjunction lifts these maps and their composites to the line modules. The first \(r\) extensions have total energy shift \(-(2+3+\cdots+(r+1))=-r(r+3)/2\) and total cochain shift \([r]\). Joining the lifted maps gives the telescope
\[
L=\operatorname*{hocolim}_{r\ge0}
E[r]\left\langle-\frac{r(r+3)}2\right\rangle.
\tag{6.9}
\]
A sequential homotopy colimit is the cone of \(1-\mathrm{shift}\) on the direct sum of its terms. The trivial \(R\)-module has the finite free Chevalley resolution \(R\otimes\Lambda^\bullet\mathfrak g\). After tensoring with \(B\) it presents \(E\) by finitely many free \(B\)-cells, so maps from \(E\) commute with this telescope. The identity of its initial copy of \(E\) is carried to a nonzero extension product at each later stage. It therefore gives a nonzero map \(E\to L\).

After realization, the terms are shifted copies of the module \(V\), concentrated in different cohomological degrees. Every transition induces zero on cohomology, so \(1-\mathrm{shift}\) induces the identity on the cohomology of the direct sum. Its cone is zero. Consequently
\[
L\ne0,\qquad \Phi L=0,\qquad \eta_L:L\longrightarrow0.
\]
The lower weight bounds in (6.9) tend to minus infinity. Each finite stage keeps its valid unit. This is the theorem of *A critical-level kernel of the ordinary first-kind realization*, §§1–4, conditional on its specified smooth realization and lower-energy comparison.

The same composites test compactness in the category generated by \(W\) and its energy shifts. Put \(V_r=V[r]\langle-r(r+3)/2\rangle\). Let \(\sigma\) send each summand of \(\bigoplus_r V_r\) to the next by its extension, and let \(j_0\) include the first summand. The map \(h=(1-\sigma)^{-1}j_0\) satisfies \(h=j_0+\sigma h\), so its \(r\)-th projection is the composite of the first \(r\) extensions. Every projection is nonzero. Compactness of \(V\) would force this map to factor through a finite subsum. Since the finite free model for \(E\) realizes \(V\) in \(\operatorname{thick}(W)\), compactness of \(W\) in the generated category would imply compactness of \(V\) there. The same argument gives the failure of both compactness assertions in \(\operatorname{Loc}(W)\) after forgetting the internal grading. [Compactness in the generated category](#atlas-c-induction-critical-noncompactness).

Related extension telescopes give kernels at raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) levels \(-4,-5,\ldots\), and at raw level \(-2h^\vee\) for every simple type, under the corresponding realization and lower-energy hypotheses. They also show that \(W\) can fail to be compact even in its own generated category. The precise sources are *Actual-unit kernels at negative integral shifted sl2 levels* and *A noncritical ordinary-unit kernel for every simple Lie algebra*.

An all-level equivalence does hold for a different induction functor. For dg \(Y_k\)-modules that are termwise algebraically smooth for the even modes, *twisted induction* at raw level \(k-h^\vee\) has an explicit restricted twisted-coinduction inverse after inverting exactly the maps sent to affine quasi-isomorphisms. These form a proper subclass of the ordinary quasi-isomorphisms. In (6.1) the same parameter \(k\) gives raw level \(-k-h^\vee\), and the source inverts all ordinary quasi-isomorphisms. At \(k=0\) even the levels coincide, while the choice of maps to invert still distinguishes the two statements. ### Curvature at one level and the complementary-level pairing {#ch06-polarized-mode-dualities}

Two questions about universal modes already differ on a pair of opposite affine currents. At raw level \(\ell_0\), choose \(x\) in the Cartan subalgebra with \(\kappa(x,x)\ne0\). Then \([xt,xt^{-1}]=\ell_0\kappa(x,x)1\). A cochain model for modules at this level must retain that scalar. Pairing two modules asks a second question. At what level must the other module lie for the differential on their semi-infinite cohomology complex to square to zero?

**Definition (polarized Tate Lie datum).** Over a discrete field of characteristic zero, take a complete separated linearly topologized Lie algebra \(\mathfrak t\) with continuous bracket, an open linearly compact Lie subalgebra \(\mathfrak p\), and a continuous scalar two-cocycle \(\eta\) vanishing on \(\mathfrak p\times\mathfrak p\). Require \(\mathfrak p\) to have a countable neighbourhood basis of ideals \(\mathfrak p_N\) of finite codimension, and \(\mathfrak t/\mathfrak p\) to have at most countable dimension. For affine modes one can take \(\mathfrak t=\mathfrak g((t))\), \(\mathfrak p=\mathfrak g[[t]]\). Each vector of a smooth module is killed by some \(\mathfrak p_N\), and the central generator acts as \(1\).

For a smooth representation at cocycle \(\eta\), the operators satisfy \([\rho(x),\rho(y)]-\rho([x,y])=\eta(x,y)1_M\). This difference is exactly what remains when the cochain connection is squared. At a fixed cocycle \(\eta\), Koszul cochains take a smooth complex \(M\) to the free graded contramodule \(K_\eta(M)\) on \(M\) over the complete Chevalley–Eilenberg algebra \(B_\eta\). A contramodule can sum families whose coefficients tend to zero in the specified topology. Here the free object uses an inverse limit of direct sums in each total degree. For a topological basis \(x_i\), its dual forms \(e_i\), and the action \(\rho\) on \(M\), the connection is \(d_B+d_M+\sum_i e_i\rho(x_i)\). Squaring it cancels the Lie bracket against the cochain differential and leaves precisely the central term \(+\eta\). Its right adjoint \(F_\eta\) is smooth coinduction.

In \(\mathcal S_\eta\) one kills the smooth complexes coacyclic after restriction to \(\mathfrak p\), where coacyclic complexes are generated from totalizations of short exact sequences by triangles, coproducts and retracts. In \(\mathcal K_\eta\) one takes curved \(B_\eta\)-contramodules with projective underlying graded contramodule. One kills those whose reduction to the continuous cochains of \(\mathfrak p\) is Becker-coacyclic, meaning that its Hom complex into every dg module with injective underlying graded module is acyclic. On these quotients the adjoints
\[
K_\eta:\mathcal S_\eta\rightleftarrows\mathcal K_\eta:F_\eta
\]
are inverse equivalences. To obtain the ordinary derived category of smooth modules from \(\mathcal K_\eta\), one must take a further quotient by the objects \(Q\) for which \(F_\eta(Q)\) is acyclic. The equivalence at this fixed level takes one module as input. To pair *two* modules, one must account for the central terms of both actions.

Choose a discrete complement \(D\) to \(\mathfrak p\) in \(\mathfrak t\). The ghost Fock space \(\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p}\) is the exterior algebra on the continuous dual \(\mathfrak p^\vee_{\rm cont}\) in degree \(1\) and on \(D\) in degree \(-1\), with vacuum \(\Omega=1\). A ghost \(c(\xi)\) for a continuous covector \(\xi\) acts by exterior multiplication by \(\xi|_{\mathfrak p}\) and contraction of the antighosts in \(D\) by \(\xi|_D\), adding the two operators. For a basis \(x_i\) adapted to the polarization, write \(c^i\) for the ghost of its dual coordinate. On \(\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p}\) the Clifford action is the linear map that sends a continuous two-form \(\alpha\) to the degree-two operator
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathfrak p}(\alpha)=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha(x_i,x_j)c^ic^j .
\]

**Theorem (complementary-level pairing).** Over \(\mathbb C\), let \(\gamma\) represent the Tate determinant extension in the lift given by normal ordering at \(\mathfrak p\), with its canonical splitting on \(\mathfrak p\). For smooth modules \(M,N\) with cocycles \(\eta,\eta'\), the normally ordered semi-infinite operator \(Q\) for the diagonal action on \(M\otimes N\) satisfies \(Q^2=1_{M\otimes N}\otimes\operatorname{cl}_{\mathfrak p}(\eta+\eta'+\gamma)\) on \(M\otimes N\otimes\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p}\). For the abelian pair \(x,y\) with lattice \(\mathbb Cx\), let \(x,y\) act by \(\partial_u,u\) on \(\mathbb C[u]\) and take \(N\) trivial. With matter coefficient \(1\), the vacuum satisfies \(Q^2\Omega=0\), while the state with one antighost satisfies \(Q^2(b_y\Omega)=c_x\Omega\ne0\). Here \(b_y\) creates \(y\) and \(c_x\) wedges by \(x^*\). Take the second central extension to be the negative of the sum of the first and the determinant extension. In compatible lifts its cocycle is \(\eta'=-\eta-\gamma\), so \(Q^2=0\). The resulting semi-infinite cohomology pairing identifies \(\mathcal S_{-\eta-\gamma}\) with the categorical dual of \(\mathcal S_\eta\). The complementary raw affine level is \(-\ell_0-2h^\vee\), and the complementary Virasoro charge is \(26-c\). For Witt modes the nonnegative lattice \(P_0\) gives the categorical pairing. Changing the normal ordering from modes \(r\ge-1\) to \(r\ge0\) adds \(d\lambda\) to the determinant cocycle, where \(\lambda(L_n)=\delta_{n,0}\). With the first lift fixed, the second uses \(L_0-1\) at the same charge.

In this abelian example the determinant cocycle vanishes and \([\partial_u,u]=1\). The ghost \(c_y\) contracts by \(y^*\). The Lie bracket contributes no term to \(Q\), so
\[
 Q=c_x\partial_u+c_y u,\qquad Q^2=c_xc_y .
\]
\[
 Q^2\Omega=0,\qquad Q^2(b_y\Omega)=c_x\Omega\ne0 .
\]
Indeed \(c_y\Omega=0\) and \(c_y b_y\Omega=\Omega\). Exterior multiplication by a fixed form is determined by its value on \(1\). The antighost thus detects a curvature that ordinary exterior multiplication on this Fock space could not give. Taking \(N=\mathbb C[v]\) with actions \(\partial_v,-v\) instead gives \([\partial_u+\partial_v,u-v]=0\) and makes the square vanish on every state.

For example, take \(\mathfrak t=\mathfrak p=\mathbb Cx\) abelian, with \(\eta=\gamma=0\). Pair the regular module \(\mathbb C[x]\) with the trivial module \(\mathbb C\). The vacuum has degree zero and the dual generator \(x^*\) has degree one. The polarized complex is
\[
\mathbb C[x]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[x]\,x^*,\qquad f\longmapsto xf\,x^* .
\]
The class of \(x^*\) spans its first cohomology. Ordinary relative Lie algebra cochains for \((\mathfrak t,\mathfrak p)\) would have only the \(\mathfrak p\)-invariants in degree zero, and multiplication by \(x\) has zero kernel.

*Proof.* Write \([x_i,x_j]=\sum_k f_{ij}^{\,k}x_k\) and let \(\rho_i\) be the diagonal action of \(x_i\) on \(M\otimes N\). The antighost operator \(b_k\) of \(x_k\) satisfies \(b_kc^i+c^ib_k=\delta_k^i\). On modules in degree zero the operator is
\[
 Q=\sum_i c^i\rho_i-\frac12\sum_{i,j,k}f_{ij}^{\,k}:c^ic^jb_k: .
\]
Normal ordering moves vacuum annihilators to the right with their graded signs. In the square, the single contractions cancel the noncentral Lie bracket in the diagonal action. The scalar parts of the two matter commutators give \(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathfrak p}(\eta+\eta')\). The double contractions give \(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathfrak p}(\gamma)\). By linearity their sum is the action of \(\alpha=\eta+\eta'+\gamma\). In the abelian example \(\alpha=x^*\wedge y^*\), so the two ordered summands give \((c_xc_y-c_yc_x)/2=c_xc_y\). It kills \(\Omega\) and sends \(b_y\Omega\) to \(c_x\Omega\). The second polynomial action \(\partial_v,-v\) contributes \(-1\) to the commutator. In the compatible complementary lift \(\alpha=0\), and the square vanishes on all states. If \(\pi\) projects to \(\mathfrak p\) along \(D\), then \(\gamma(x,y)\) is the trace on \(\mathfrak p\) of
\[
 \pi\,\operatorname{ad}x(1-\pi)\operatorname{ad}y\,\pi
 -\pi\,\operatorname{ad}y(1-\pi)\operatorname{ad}x\,\pi .
\]
The maps crossing from the linearly compact lattice to its discrete complement have finite rank.

For unbounded complexes use the direct sum
\[
 \bigoplus_{i+j+r=n} M^i\otimes N^j\otimes\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p}^{\,r}
\]
in degree \(n\), adding the internal differentials with their tensor signs. A cochain thus has finite support among the triples of degrees. Filter by the number of antighosts from \(D\). The differential preserves this increasing filtration because \([\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak p\). Its \(s\)-th graded piece consists of direct-sum totalized continuous \(\mathfrak p\)-cochains with coefficients in \(\Lambda^s(\mathfrak t/\mathfrak p)\otimes M\otimes N\), shifted by \([s]\). These cochains are exact on coefficient modules and preserve coproducts degree by degree. Totalizing by direct sums thus kills coacyclic complexes in either input on every graded piece. The finite filtration steps and their filtered union are then acyclic as well. This gives a pairing on the stated quotients that preserves colimits in each variable.

To identify the category on which the pairing is perfect, consider the localization \(q_\eta:\mathcal S_\eta\to D(\operatorname{Sm}_\eta)\) to the ordinary derived category of smooth modules. Put \(P_i=U(\mathfrak p/\mathfrak p_i)\). Inducing \(P_i\) from \(\mathfrak p\) gives \(G_i=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak p_i}^{\mathfrak t,\eta}\mathbb C\), with trivial \(\mathfrak p_i\)-action on the inducing line and central generator acting as \(1\). The \(P_i\) compactly generate the coderived category of smooth \(\mathfrak p\)-modules, the quotient by coacyclic complexes. Restriction to \(\mathfrak p\) preserves coproducts and detects zero in \(\mathcal S_\eta\) by its defining null class. Hence the induced \(G_i\) compactly generate \(\mathcal S_\eta\). By induction and restriction, maps \(G_i\to G_j[n]\) are maps \(P_i\to (G_j|_{\mathfrak p})[n]\) in this coderived category. The unshifted target is a module in degree zero. A bounded-below injective resolution computes the same maps in the coderived and ordinary derived categories. Induction is exact by the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem, so restriction preserves injectives. Ordinary derived adjunction now gives the maps \(q_\eta G_i\to q_\eta G_j[n]\) for every \(n\).

Thus \(q_\eta\) is fully faithful on the thick closure of the \(G_i\), obtained by shifts, finite cones and direct summands. Dhillon starts in \(D(\operatorname{Sm}_\eta)\) with modules induced from finite-dimensional smooth representations of the central inverse images of compact open subalgebras, at the prescribed central action. For a compact open \(\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak t\), choose \(\mathfrak p_i\subseteq\mathfrak h\). The vector space \(\mathfrak h/\mathfrak p_i\) is finite-dimensional. The finite relative Chevalley–Eilenberg resolution of the inducing module has terms induced from finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak p_i\)-modules. Each is killed by a deeper \(\mathfrak p_j\) and has a finite resolution by finitely generated projectives over \(U(\mathfrak p_i/\mathfrak p_j)\). Inducing these projectives to \(\mathfrak t\) gives summands of finite sums of \(G_j\). Conversely each \(G_i\) is itself induced from a line at a compact open subalgebra. The two thick closures therefore coincide.

The Ind-completion of this common compact category recovers \(\mathcal S_\eta\) by compact generation and is Dhillon's renormalized category by definition. The same comparison applies at \(-\eta-\gamma\), since both cocycles vanish on \(\mathfrak p\times\mathfrak p\). On the induced modules at the two levels, both evaluations use the same polarized semi-infinite cochains on the tensor product and the same induced maps of cochains. Exactness extends this identification to their thick closures. Every object on either side is a filtered colimit of compacts, and the pairing preserves colimits in each variable. Thus Dhillon's perfect pairing on the two renormalized categories gives the asserted duality. [Dhillon, §5.1, Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 5.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.06477v1#S5.SS1).

The Virasoro charge specifies an extension class. To see the effect of its lift, take \([L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}\) and \(\omega(L_m,L_n)=\delta_{m+n,0}(m^3-m)/12\). For the lattice \(P_k=\prod_{r\ge k}\mathbb C L_r\), write \(\gamma_k\) for the determinant cocycle in the mode splitting. In the crossed trace on \((L_m,L_{-m})\), with \(m>0\), only the modes \(L_r\) with \(k\le r<k+m\) leave \(P_k\) under \(L_{-m}\) and return under \(L_m\). Their contribution is
\[
 \gamma_k(L_m,L_{-m})=\sum_{r=k}^{k+m-1}(-m-r)(2m-r).
\]
Off the pairs of opposite modes the crossed operators have zero trace. For the full disc, \(k=-1\), the sum is \(-26\,\omega(L_m,L_{-m})\). Passing to \(P_0\), where the positive tails are ideals, replaces the \(r=-1\) term by the \(r=m-1\) term and changes the sum by \(-2m\). Thus \(\gamma_0=-26\omega+d\lambda\), where \(\lambda(L_n)=\delta_{n,0}\) and \(d\lambda(x,y)=-\lambda([x,y])\). Keeping the first lift at \(c\omega\), take the second at \((26-c)\omega-d\lambda\). This amounts to replacing the standard \(L_0\) action on the second module by \(L_0-1\). Its extra \(+2m\) cancels the change in the determinant term. In particular the two standard charge cocycles both vanish on \((L_1,L_{-1})\), where the \(P_0\) determinant contributes \(-2\).

For affine currents the determinant term contributes \(2h^\vee\) in the chosen normalization. Thus with both inputs at \(\ell_0=-h^\vee\) the three scalar contributions sum to \(-h^\vee-h^\vee+2h^\vee=0\). The commutator above still contributes \(-h^\vee\kappa(x,x)\) to the curvature at that level. Thus the fixed point gives a pairing of the category with itself while the one-input cochain functor retains nonzero curvature. ∎

These statements concern universal mode algebras. Applying either construction to a simple vertex-algebra quotient requires a comparison on its modules.

### At rational shift the zero-weight test has no finite exponent {#ch06-rational-root-staircase}

Return to the weight-zero quotient of \(Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\). Its nonrational exponent came from a finite integral Weyl group. At a rational shifted level \(s=a/b\), with integers \(a,b\), \(b>0\), choose a long root \(\alpha\). Both \(\alpha+b\delta\) and \(-\alpha+b\delta\) are positive affine roots. Choose an integral initial pairing \(u=(\mu,\alpha)\), and reflect in these roots alternately. In the first step the shifted coroot pairing is \(u+a\) and the new horizontal pairing is \(-u-2a\). The next coroot pairing is \(u+3a\). In general the \(j\)-th pairing and its energy increase are
\[
 q_j=u+(2j-1)a,\qquad m_j=bq_j .
\]
Fix a proposed length \(N\). Choosing \(u\) large enough makes every \(q_j\) positive up to that length. For negative \(a\) a longer word may require a different initial weight. It remains to show that the \(N\) successive steps act nontrivially on a single chosen top. Kübel's Theorem 4.1 detects composition factors of ordinary affine Verma modules. Here we need maps whose composite survives, so we use embeddings.

**Lemma (one real-root reflection).** Let \(\beta\) be a positive real affine root and let \(\widehat\rho\) pair to one with the affine simple coroots. If the complex highest weight \(\xi\) satisfies \(\langle\xi+\widehat\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle=q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), there is an injection of ordinary affine Verma modules \(M(\xi-q\beta)\hookrightarrow M(\xi)\).

*Proof.* On the whole coroot hyperplane take the fixed finite Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt space of negative words of weight \(-q\beta\). The matrix of the simple raising operators on that space has polynomial entries in the highest weight. Existence of a nonzero singular vector is closed, by its maximal minors. Away from the critical hyperplane and the countably many other integral real-root conditions independent of \(\beta\), the noncritical block has just the two labels \(\xi,\xi-q\beta\). Fiebig's Verma-embedding theorem gives a nonzero kernel on this dense subset, so the kernel persists on the entire coroot hyperplane. The central level varies in the density argument. At the desired level, multiplication by any surviving singular vector injects the Verma, since the enveloping algebra of the negative part has a polynomial associated graded and hence no zero divisors. The specialization proves existence. ∎

These injections give a positive-weight word with nonzero composite on a horizontal highest-weight vector. This includes \(a=0,\ b=1\). Hence at every rational shift and in every simple type \(I^{\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}N}\ne0\) for all \(N\). In fact the genuine \(B\)-bimodule map \(I^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}N}\to B\) is nonzero. Its restriction receives the \(R\)-tensor word by inserting zero-length terms at the intermediate relative bar resolutions, and the composite is exactly the tested multiplication. These finite nonvanishings exclude the descent proof through \(B\to R\). The ghost quotient has a different fibre, and neither finite test alone decides the unit at a rational level.

## Raw level minus three distinguishes the two quotients {#ch06-raw-level-minus-three-distinguishes-the-two-quotients}

Which part of one of these words is lost under \(B\to R\)? For \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) at raw level \(-3\), we have \(s=-1\) and \(B=Y_1\). There is an infinite sequence whose first step already separates the two quotients \(B\to H\to R\). Let
\[
V_j=V(-2j-1),\qquad
F_j=(B\otimes_RV_j)\langle-j^2\rangle,
\]
where \(V(\lambda)\) is the horizontal Verma module of highest weight \(\lambda\). There are actual degree-zero maps \(\beta_j:F_{j-1}\to F_j\) represented by positive-weight cycles. Their affine realizations send highest vectors to
\[
v_{j-1}\longmapsto
f_0^{\,2j-2}e_{-1}^{\,2j-1}v_j.
\tag{6.10}
\]
Here \(f_0\) is the horizontal lowering current and \(e_{-1}\) the mode-\(-1\) raising current. For \(j=1\) the vector on the right is simply \(e_{-1}v_1\). The quotient \(B\to H\) kills ghosts and keeps \(e_{-1}\), so it retains this nonzero highest-weight vector. The further projection \(H\to R\) kills \(e_{-1}\). For every \(j\ge1\), the energy difference \(j^2-(j-1)^2=2j-1\) is positive, and (6.10) gives an injection of affine Verma modules. Thus every stage distinguishes the same two quotients.

For \(L=\operatorname{hocolim}_j(F_j,\beta_j)\), the two quotients have different effects:
\[
R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L=0,\qquad
H\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L\ne0,\qquad
\Phi L\ne0.
\tag{6.11}
\]
Projection to \(R\) kills every positive-weight transition, and a telescope with zero transitions is zero. Projection to \(H\) retains the ghost-free word (6.10). The resulting system consists of injective Verma maps and has a nonzero filtered colimit; the affine realization has the same property.

Moreover, for \(I=\ker(B\to R)\), every finite map
\[
I^{\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}N}\longrightarrow B
\]
induced by the ideal inclusion is nonzero. Thus both the tensor-object and fibre-map descent routes through \(B\to R\) fail at this parameter. The proof is *A nonzero native telescope killed by the zero-weight augmentation*, §§2–4.

The unit on the telescope itself can be determined. The distinction in (6.11) concerns two changes of scalars. The affine unit tests a third map. To compute it, use the following sufficient condition at any level where (6.1) is realized. Suppose a coefficient complex has a strict dg \(B\)-module representative \(X\) with \(X^q=0\) below a fixed cochain degree. On positive currents the model (6.2) for \(\Phi X\) has terms \(C_{\mathfrak g}\otimes X^q\), which are injective in the smooth positive-current category. This bounded-below complex is K-injective there. Its positive-current invariants are \(\delta_1\otimes X\). Every \(L_{x_i}\) kills \(\delta_1\), so its differential on these invariants is \(d_X\). A smooth affine K-injective replacement restricts to a positive-current K-injective resolution, and evaluation of the tensor–Hom unit on the inducing vector is the inclusion of these very invariants. This proves that \(\eta_X\) is invertible. The hypothesis bounds the actual cochain representative. Internal energies can be arbitrarily negative. [The cochain bound and the original unit](#atlas-c-ur-13).

Each \(F_j\) above has nonnegative cochain terms, so the cone of \(1-\mathrm{shift}\) representing \(L\) has no terms below degree \(-1\). Thus the same \(L\) satisfies (6.11) **and** \(L\xrightarrow{\sim}\Psi\Phi L\). It is detected by realization even though zero-weight base change kills it. More generally, in every simple type and at every \(s=a/b\le0\) rational, choose \(u+a<0\) in the alternating-root construction. All pairings \(u+(2j-1)a\) now stay negative. The embeddings point from the old Verma into the new one, with successively lower top energies. Inducing their horizontal top maps gives a telescope of \(B\)-modules concentrated in nonnegative cochain degrees. Zero-weight base change kills all the transitions. Ghost base change retains their injective even-current maps, hence gives a nonzero union. The cochain bound again proves that this telescope has invertible unit. For positive rational \(s\), the finite staircase above allows the initial weight to depend on \(N\).

The fibre \(J=\ker(B\to H)\) retains a different obstruction. At \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) raw level \(-3\), let \(c\) be the weight-one ghost dual to \(ft\), where \([e,f]=h\). On a horizontal Verma top \(v_\lambda\), the differential between the unique weight-\((\lambda+2)\) vectors \(e_{-1}v_\lambda\) and \(c\otimes v_\lambda\) has coefficient, up to the common cochain sign,
\[
 f_1e_{-1}v_\lambda=(-3-\lambda)v_\lambda .
\]
At \(\lambda=-3\) this is zero. Thus \(c\) survives in \(H^1(B)_1\) and the first map \(J\to B\) is nonzero. For every simple type at every level, the highest long root gives the same test by taking \(\lambda(h_\theta)\) equal to the raw scalar level. Nevertheless any strict representative annihilated by a fixed \(J^d\) has good unit under (6.1), because the quotients of its finite ghost filtration are inflated from \(H\). In \(B/J^2\) the first ghost still acts nontrivially on cohomology, giving a concrete example beyond the inflated objects. At raw \(-3\) the unit also holds on \(B\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}T\) for arbitrary unbounded horizontal \(T\), by *A finite cofree resolution for arbitrary horizontal coefficients at raw level minus three*. Invertibility for **every** ordinary \(B\)-complex at this level remains open.

The telescope in (6.11) survives affine realization even though its zero-weight reduction vanishes. The algebraic test for realization uses the [Chevalley resolution with its right action](#ch06-affine-currents-and-the-algebra-acting-on-their-resolution). Write \(P\) for this affine–\(B\) bimodule. At any fixed level, in the ordinary unbounded derived category, put
\[
 \mathcal K_P=\{L\in D(B):P\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L\simeq0\}.
\]
{#eq-ch06-realization-kernel}
Call an object of \(\mathcal K_P\) null. Call \(Z\) local if \(\operatorname{Hom}_{D(B)}(K,Z[j])=0\) for every null \(K\) and every \(j\). The null objects are generated under cones, coproducts and retracts by a set of countable semifree null modules and their shifts, including energy shifts in the graded category. To see this, choose a semifree model \(F\) of a null object. In its free graded basis, each differential involves finitely many earlier basis elements. Close a countable set of basis elements under these differentials to obtain \(F_0\subset F\). The total vector spaces of \(B\) and \(P\) have countable bases, so the cycles of \(P\otimes_B F_0\) have a countable basis. Each of these basis cycles has a primitive in \(P\otimes_B F\) involving finitely many basis elements of \(F\). Adjoin their supports and close under differentials again. In the countable union every cycle has acquired a primitive. Thus each countable set of basis elements lies in a countable semifree null submodule. Their filtered union recovers \(F\).

For an arbitrary \(L\), start a new sequence with a semifree resolution \(F_0\to L\). Choose a set \(\mathcal S_P\) of the countable null generators including their shifts. At stage \(\alpha\) take one copy of \(S\) for each derived map \(f:S\to F_\alpha\) and use a chain representative of \(f\) to form the evaluation
\[
 \bigoplus_{S\in\mathcal S_P,\ f:S\to F_\alpha} S
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev}} F_\alpha
 \longrightarrow F_{\alpha+1}=\operatorname{Cone}(\mathrm{ev}).
\]
{#eq-ch06-null-evaluation-cone}
Use the cone inclusions and take unions at limit ordinals. A map from a countable semifree generator into the union at the first uncountable ordinal \(\omega_1\) factors through an earlier stage. Its basis images occur at countably many stages, whose supremum is still countable. The next evaluation makes the map nullhomotopic. Tensoring with \(P\) preserves the unions and makes each cone inclusion a quasi-isomorphism. The resulting map \(\ell_L:L\to\lambda_P L\) has local target and null cone. Applying derived Hom into any local \(Z\) now gives the universal property of the exact reflector
\[
 \lambda_P:D(B)\longrightarrow\mathcal K_P^\perp,\qquad
 D(B)/\mathcal K_P\simeq\mathcal K_P^\perp .
\]
{#eq-ch06-localization-at-the-realization-kernel}
It inverts precisely the maps that become equivalences under \(P\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-\). [Localization at the algebraic realization kernel](#atlas-c-induction-kernel-localization).

Assume (6.1) and its compatibility \(\operatorname{For}\Phi\simeq P\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-\), where \(\operatorname{For}\) forgets smoothness and detects zero objects. Then \(\mathcal K_P=\ker\Phi\). Adjunction makes \(\Psi M\) local for every smooth affine complex \(M\), since maps from \(K\) to \(\Psi M\) are maps from \(\Phi K\) to \(M\). Consequently the actual unit factors uniquely as
\[
 L\xrightarrow{\ell_L}\lambda_P L\xrightarrow{\beta_L}\Psi\Phi L .
\]
{#eq-ch06-unit-through-localization}
\(\Phi(\ell_L)\) is invertible. Using it to identify \(\Phi L\) with \(\Phi(\lambda_P L)\) identifies \(\beta_L\) with the unit on the local object \(\lambda_P L\). The cone of \(\ell_L\) is null. The triangle for the composite then identifies \(\operatorname{Cone}(\beta_L)\) with \(\lambda_P\operatorname{Cone}(\eta_L)\), the local part of the unit defect. The functor from the quotient to smooth affine complexes is conservative and takes values in the category generated by the regular-top \(W\), with all energy shifts when graded. [The factored unit](#atlas-c-induction-unit-factorization) and [its two cones](#atlas-c-induction-defect-triangle).

At nonrational shifted level the [full-unit theorem](#ch06-conditional-full-unit-theorem), under its smooth hypotheses, gives zero kernel. Equation (6.8) identifies the image with the entire generated category. At a positive rational shifted level the [finite staircase](#ch06-rational-root-staircase) detects products for the quotient \(B\to R\). Its initial highest weight may depend on the length of the word. Exhibiting a nonzero \(P\)-null object at such a level requires a calculation with the affine action. At the critical and specified negative raw levels the [extension telescopes](#ch06-infinite-extensions-at-other-levels) give nonzero null objects under their lower-energy comparison hypotheses. Whether they generate the full kernel remains open. In the unresolved cases one must still determine whether \(\beta_L\) is invertible on every local source and identify the essential image of the quotient.

## Cellularity and derived torsion {#ch06-cellularity-and-derived-torsion}

Every vector of \(W\) has finite energy above its lowest-energy space, and a strictly positive current lowers energy by at least one. A sufficiently long product of such currents therefore kills the vector. Does this property on cohomology characterize the smooth complexes generated by \(W\)? To keep its ideal distinct from \(I\subset Y_k\), put
\[
U_+=U(t\mathfrak g[t]),\qquad
\mathfrak a=\ker(U_+\to\mathbb C).
\]
Smoothness controls the index of a *single* current. For example, on the regular module of \(U(t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t]/t^2\mathfrak{sl}_2[t])\) the entire tail of index two acts by zero. Yet every power of the surviving generator \(et\) acts nontrivially on \(1\). This positive-current example distinguishes smoothness from a condition on products.

**Definition (joint augmentation torsion).** A smooth module is jointly augmentation-torsion if for each vector \(v\) some whole ideal power \(\mathfrak a^r\) kills \(v\). The exponent may depend on \(v\). Let \(\mathcal T\subset\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell)\) be this torsion category and set
\[
D_{\mathfrak a}=
\{M\in\mathscr S_\ell:H^q(M)\in\mathcal T
\text{ for every }q\}.
\]
The cellular category \(\mathscr C\) generated by \(W\) and its required shifts lies in \(D_{\mathfrak a}\).

This containment gives an affine test of how much one probe recovers. Choose a linear functional \(\lambda\) on \(\mathfrak g\) with \(\lambda(x)=1\) for some \(x\). Give the line \(\mathbb C_\chi\) the positive-current character \(\chi(yt)=\lambda(y)\), \(\chi(yt^j)=0\) for \(j\ge2\). Every bracket of two positive currents has mode at least two, so the character respects brackets. At any raw level \(\ell\), form the ungraded induced module
\[
 M_\chi=A_\ell\otimes_{U_+}\mathbb C_\chi,\qquad
 v=1\otimes1,\qquad (xt)^r v=v\quad(r\ge1).
\]
{#eq-ch06-first-mode-character}
The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis makes \(v\) nonzero. It also gives smoothness. On a word in modes of indices \(-p_1,\ldots,-p_j\), \(p_i\ge0\), acting on \(v\), commute a mode of index \(n>1+\sum_i p_i\) to the right. Every commutator retains index at least two, so no central term occurs and every surviving positive mode kills \(v\). Each finite sum of words admits a common such \(n\). Nevertheless \((xt)^r\in\mathfrak a^r\) fixes \(v\) for all \(r\). Thus \(M_\chi\) lies outside \(D_{\mathfrak a}\) and hence outside \(\mathscr C\). In particular, even at a nonrational shifted level where the smooth hypotheses give full faithfulness on coefficients, the counit on this smooth module is not an equivalence. [The counit on the generated sector](#ch06-the-image-and-its-original-counit)

Consider first a vector killed by \(\mathfrak a^r\). Sending \(1\) to this vector defines a map out of \(U_+/\mathfrak a^r\). Since \([\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]=\mathfrak g\), bracketing a degree-one current with a current of degree \(d\) spans the currents of degree \(d+1\). Thus \(U_+\) is generated in degree one. Words in the degree-one generators of length at least \(r\) span exactly
\[
\mathfrak a^r=\bigoplus_{d\ge r}(U_+)_d.
\]
Only finitely many finite-dimensional degrees remain in \(U_+/\mathfrak a^r\). The induced affine module
\[
E_r=A_\ell\otimes_{U_+}(U_+/\mathfrak a^r)
\]
has a finite filtration with factors
\[
W\langle-j\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb C}(U_+)_j,
\qquad 0\le j<r,
\]
where the last factor is a multiplicity space. Resolutions by such modules show that every complex whose terms are torsion is cellular. Every object with bounded-below torsion cohomology is also cellular. These statements, and the cellular coreflector on the whole unbounded smooth category, are proved in *Augmentation torsion and unbounded affine cellularization*.

The torsion inclusion has its own derived adjunction,
\[
i:D(\mathcal T)\rightleftarrows
D(\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell)):R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}.
\tag{6.12}
\]
On a module \(\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}\) takes the vectors killed by an ideal power. To compute \(R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}M\), take those vectors in a smooth K-injective resolution of \(M\). Inclusion gives the counit \(iR\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}M\to M\). The cellular coreflection is characterized by its effect on maps from the generating objects and their shifts. For affine modules under the full-unit hypotheses of (6.8), its counit is \(\Phi\Psi M\to M\). An unbounded sum of trivial positive-current modules lets us evaluate the torsion counit on an object we already know to be cellular.

First take just the strictly positive currents of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\). Let \(\mathcal S_+=\operatorname{Sm}(t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t])\), let \(\mathcal T_+\) be its joint-torsion subcategory, and put
\[
e_n=\frac{n(n+1)}2,\qquad
X=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\mathbb C[n]\langle-e_n\rangle.
\]
Resolve the trivial module in the torsion category by successive injective envelopes. In the \(n\)-th term let \(K_n\) be the subspace killed by all positive currents, in energy \(e_n\). It identifies with the continuous cohomology \(H^n_{\mathrm{cts}}(t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\mathbb C)\) in that energy. For the \(n\)-th summand of \(X\), use this resolution of \(\mathbb C\) shifted by \([n]\langle-e_n\rangle\). Its copy of \(K_n\) now lies in cochain degree zero and energy zero. For the actual derived torsion adjunction one has
\[
H^0R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(iX)_0
\cong
\frac{\prod_{n\ge1}K_n}{\bigoplus_{n\ge1}K_n}\ne0,
\qquad \dim K_n=2n+1.
\tag{6.13}
\]
A family with one nonzero vector in each \(K_n\) is killed by the augmentation ideal. A degree-minus-one primitive in the torsion product would need a single annihilating power for all its coordinates. At energy zero its \(n\)-th coordinate is \(e_n-e_{n-1}=n\) above the lowest energy of that term. A family killed by \(\mathfrak a^r\) must vanish in every coordinate \(n\ge r\). Conversely the differential onto each \(K_n\) supplies a primitive for any finite-support family. The boundaries therefore give precisely the direct sum in (6.13). The family thus represents a nonzero class, while \(H^0X=0\). In cohomological degree zero and energy zero the torsion unit \(X\to R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(iX)\) therefore maps zero to a nonzero group. For the counit \(iR\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(iX)\to iX\) the same two groups occur in reverse order, since inclusion of torsion modules is exact. This counit fails to be an equivalence on \(iX\), a direct sum of shifts of the trivial module. The cellular coreflection is an equivalence on that object. Without an energy grading the corresponding quotient still injects into degree-zero cohomology. An explicit positive-current splitting transfers the failure to affine raw level zero; no transfer to arbitrary affine level is asserted. [The joint-torsion counterexample and its affine range](#atlas-c-induction-intrinsic-joint-torsion-failure).

Finite quotients nevertheless admit a precise local-duality theorem. For an integer \(N\ge1\), put
\[
\mathfrak n=t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\quad
\mathfrak g_N=\mathfrak n/t^N\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\quad
R_N=U(\mathfrak g_N),\quad d_N=3(N-1).
\]
Let \(J_N\) be the augmentation ideal of \(R_N\) and
\(E_N^{\mathrm{cof}}=\varinjlim_r(R_N/J_N^r)^\vee\), with action
\((b\phi)(a)=\phi(ab)\). In the actual positive-current smooth category,
\[
R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(R_N)
\simeq E_N^{\mathrm{cof}}\otimes
(\det\mathfrak g_N)^\vee[-d_N].
\tag{6.14}
\]
At \(N=1\) the quotient Lie algebra is zero, and (6.14) gives the trivial module in degree zero. At \(N=2\) only \(t\mathfrak{sl}_2\) modulo \(t^2\) survives. Its three generators commute in the quotient, so the cofree module on the right occurs in cohomological degree three. In general the determinant-dual line has energy \(3N(N-1)/2\). Finite-dimensional Lie-algebra duality gives the right side for the quotient. Finite Chevalley complexes with bounded coefficient terms then compare the inflated counit cone with the one in the smooth category. This identifies the torsion counit on the displayed regular quotients. See *Actual torsion of the smooth regular quotients*, the finite-quotient local-duality argument.

The equality \(\mathscr C=D_{\mathfrak a}\) asks which objects are cellular in the unbounded smooth affine category. At the critical level a [complex built from quadratic currents](#ch06-theorem-a-critical-affine-complex-orthogonal-to-the-regular-top) gives strict inclusion. At other raw levels the unrestricted comparison remains open. Non-full-faithfulness of (6.12) does not decide that equality of object classes. Nor does termwise vanishing of a torsion functor prove vanishing of its derived value: a concrete quotient complex in the graded positive-current category has zero torsion in every term but is cellular and has nonzero actual derived torsion. K-injective replacement changes the calculation. This example is computed in *The graded quotient does not give an outside or kernel object*.

### A common current tail gives actual completeness {#ch06-common-tail-completeness}

Every cohomology module of the input \(X\) in (6.13) has trivial positive-current action. An infinite family representing a nonzero class in \(H^0R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(iX)_0\) has no primitive with a common torsion exponent. There is a different way to recover a smooth complex from its underlying module complex. Its truncations discard successively fewer low cohomological degrees. To compare their inverse limit with the original complex, first forget smoothness and then take derived smooth vectors. Put
\[
\mathfrak n=t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\qquad
\mathfrak n_L=t^L\mathfrak{sl}_2[t],\qquad
F:D(\mathcal S)\longrightarrow D(\mathfrak M),
\quad G=R\operatorname{sm},
\]
where \(\mathcal S\) is the smooth positive-current module category and \(\mathfrak M\) is the category of all positive-current modules. The functor \(F\) forgets smoothness. Its right adjoint derives the smooth-vector functor \(\operatorname{sm}(M)=\bigcup_{L\ge1}M^{\mathfrak n_L}\). Use algebraic integer-energy gradings, with currents lowering energy and maps preserving it. Products are taken energy by energy before the outer algebraic direct sum.

Suppose one tail annihilates all sufficiently low cohomology: there are \(L\ge1\) and an integer \(b\) such that
\[
\mathfrak n_LH^q(X)=0\qquad(q\le b).
\]
Then the actual unit is an equivalence,
\[
X\xrightarrow{\sim}GF(X)
\simeq\operatorname{holim}_n\tau^{\ge-n}X,
\]
with no bound on higher cohomology, multiplicities or energy support. For every smooth derived source \(Y\), the original map
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathcal S)}(Y,X)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathfrak M)}(FY,FX)
\]
is an isomorphism, including all shifts. [Common-tail completeness](#atlas-c-induction-common-tail-completeness).

The proof first restricts to a tail on which the cohomology layers are genuinely trivial. A sufficiently deeper restriction splits the Postnikov extensions coherently enough to identify the original completion map. If the same annihilating tail works in every degree, the whole restricted complex becomes noncanonically formal; \(M=4L-2\) is a sufficient deeper-tail index. With the hypothesis only for \(q\le b\), this formality statement applies to the lower truncation. No formality over the original positive-current algebra is asserted.

Let \(\mathcal L=\operatorname{Loc}\{\mathbb C\langle e\rangle:e\in\mathbb Z\}\). Suppose in addition that every \(H^qX\) is jointly augmentation-torsion. Then \(X\in\mathcal L\). Indeed, restriction to \(\mathfrak n_{4L-2}\) splits \(\tau^{\le b}X\) into shifted trivial cells. To pass back to \(\mathfrak n\), adjoin the missing currents one at a time in decreasing mode degree. At each step the smaller Lie algebra is a codimension-one ideal. For the added current \(z\), the two-term induction sequence from the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis makes the fibre of localization at \(z\) cellular whenever the restriction is cellular. Joint torsion makes this localization zero. The upper truncation is cellular by the bounded-below theorem, so the truncation triangle gives the result. The same cellularity conclusion holds in the ungraded category by repeating a complex in every energy and taking the cokernel of one minus the sheet shift.

In the graded category it suffices that \(\mathfrak n_L(H^qX)_{\le a}=0\) for some fixed \(a,b,L\) and all \(q\le b\). Apply the preceding argument to \(X_{\le a}\). In the quotient each positive mode lowers energy, so every vector is killed by a whole power of the augmentation ideal. Thus a nonzero object in \(\mathcal L^\perp\) with jointly torsion cohomology must escape all these bounds. The ambient smooth derived category can fail to be left-complete. To see what the cells themselves miss, we will compare cohomology at energy \(-2\) with maps from the trivial module at that same energy.

**Theorem (a complex orthogonal to all positive-current cells).** Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic zero and \(\mathcal S\) the category of smooth \(t\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)[t]\)-modules with algebraic integer energy grading and degree-preserving maps. In the unbounded category \(D(\mathcal S)\), the localizing subcategory generated by the trivial modules \(k\langle u\rangle\), \(u\in\mathbb Z\), is strictly contained in the subcategory of complexes with jointly augmentation-torsion cohomology. There is a complex \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\) with nonzero jointly torsion \(H^{-n}(X_{\mathrm{lin}})\) for every \(n\ge1\), zero cohomology in nonnegative degrees, and
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathcal S)}(k\langle u\rangle,X_{\mathrm{lin}}[j])=0
 \qquad(u,j\in\mathbb Z).
\]

*Proof.* Write \(U_+=U(t\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)[t])\) and \(x_s=xt^s\) for \(x=e,h,f\) in the usual \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) basis. On the graded dual \(C=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}(U_+)_d^*\), the left action is \((x_s\phi)(b)=\phi(bx_s)\). The operators \((\partial_s\phi)(b)=\phi(e_s b)\) commute with this action and with one another. Use them to form the Koszul complex
\[
 J_N=C\otimes\Lambda(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_N),\qquad
 D=\sum_{s=1}^N\epsilon_s\wedge\partial_s,\qquad
 M_N=\bigcap_{s=1}^N\ker\partial_s .
\]
Here \(\epsilon_s\) has energy \(s\) and form degree one. A column with parameters \((N,n,c)\) uses \(J_N\langle c\rangle\) and puts an \(r\)-form in cohomological degree \(r-n\).

Start with the column \((2,1,-3)\). Its arrow into \((4,2,-10)\) adjoins \(\epsilon_3\wedge\epsilon_4\). Index the latter column by the empty multiset. From a multiset \(\alpha\), adjoin any one integer \(a\) with \(3\le a\le N\), where \(N=4+|\alpha|\). The column at \(\alpha\) has \(n=N-2\) and \(c=-N(N+1)/2-\sum_{a\in\alpha}a\), counting multiplicities. The arrow adjoining \(a\) wedges with \(\epsilon_a\wedge\epsilon_{N+1}\). Each arrow on an \(r\)-form is right exterior multiplication with sign \((-1)^r\), and the internal differential is \(D\). The arrows preserve energy and raise cohomological degree by one. They anticommute with \(D\). Two successive arrows either repeat an exterior factor or cancel with the path that adds the same indices in the other order.

Keep finitely many layers and project away the deepest one. Let \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\) be the inverse limit of these stages in \(\mathcal S\). In each energy its product consists of families killed by one common current tail. Different energies are assembled by direct sum. To compute cohomology first take the subcomplex killed by \(\mathfrak n_K=t^K\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)[t]\). By the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem the enveloping algebra of the quotient is free over the polynomial ring in the surviving \(e_1,\ldots,e_{\min(N,K-1)}\), acting on the left. Dualizing the Koszul complex degree by degree gives \(M_N^{\mathfrak n_K}\) in form degree zero for these indices. The exterior factors with \(K\le s\le N\) have zero differential. Thus layer \(n\) contributes only between total degrees \(-n\) and \(\max(-n,3-K)\). For fixed degree \(q\), taking \(K\ge5-q\) makes the finite-stage cohomologies in degrees \(q\) and \(q-1\) eventually constant. For negative \(q\) only layer \(-q\) contributes in degree \(q\), and later projections act identically on that layer. The first derived inverse limit vanishes at each energy. Taking the union over \(K\) therefore gives \(H^{-n}(X_{\mathrm{lin}})\) as the sum of the shifted \(M_N\)'s of layer \(n\), and \(H^q(X_{\mathrm{lin}})=0\) for \(q\ge0\). The top and every layer below it contain a nonzero constant functional. In layer \(n\ge2\), for instance, take the column with \(n-2\) copies of \(3\). Every weight-\(d\) functional in \(C\) is killed by \(\mathfrak a^{d+1}\).

In particular \(H^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{lin}})=M_2\langle-3\rangle\). Energy \(-2\) picks out weight-one functionals. The equation \(\partial_1\phi=0\) says \(\phi(e_1)=0\), and \(\partial_2\phi=0\) holds by weight. The functionals dual to \(f_1\) and \(h_1\) give a basis of this two-dimensional cohomology group. Order the \(e_s\) first in a PBW basis. For \(b=f_1\) or \(h_1\), put \(b^*\) in the top column. In the empty-multiset column the coefficient dual to \(e_4 b\), multiplied by \(\epsilon_3\), has internal differential \(-b^*\epsilon_3\wedge\epsilon_4\). This cancels the top arrow. The layer calculation above ensures survival in the smooth limit.

Now map a shifted trivial module into \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\). Each \(C\langle c\rangle\) is injective in \(\mathcal S\), since a map \(M\to C\langle c\rangle\) amounts to a functional on \(M_c\). The finite stages are bounded complexes of injectives, and their projections split in each degree. Applying the complex of maps from any acyclic source gives a degreewise surjective tower of acyclic vector-space complexes with acyclic limit. Hence \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is K-injective. Maps from \(k\langle u\rangle\) pick out the constant functionals at the specified total energy. Their internal differential vanishes. At \(u=-2\) the entire complex consists of \(\epsilon_1\) in degree zero mapping to \(-\epsilon_1\wedge\epsilon_3\wedge\epsilon_4\) in degree one. This arrow is an isomorphism.

At any energy, write \(O\) for the indices omitted from an exterior word in column \(\alpha\). Its energy on constants is \(-\sum_{a\in\alpha}a-\sum_{s\in O}s\), so each energy contains finitely many such words. An arrow moves an omitted \(a\ge3\) from \(O\) to \(\alpha\). Fix the omissions in \(\{1,2\}\) and the multiset \(\mu=\alpha\sqcup(O\setminus\{1,2\})\). If \(\mu\) is nonempty, its largest entry pairs the words in which it is omitted with the words reached by adjoining it. Indeed, in a reachable sorted multiset the \(i\)-th entry is at most \(i+3\). Removing its largest entry therefore returns to a column from which that entry could be added, and all the other omissions still fit there. The inverse signed arrow on the second word of each pair and zero on the first define a contraction because the other terms cancel in the squares of arrows. For \(\mu\) empty the top arrow pairs the four remaining exterior words isomorphically. This proves vanishing for every \(u,j\). The objects with zero maps to every shift of \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\) form a localizing subcategory containing all the cells. If the cells generated \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\), its identity would vanish, contrary to \(H^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{lin}})_{-2}\ne0\). Joint torsion is closed under sums, subquotients and extensions (each \(\mathfrak a^r\) is finitely generated as a left ideal), so the cohomology condition contains the subcategory generated by the cells. ∎

The [Koszul tower](#atlas-c-induction-common-tail-generation-residual) also distinguishes two uses of torsion. In its K-injective model the vectors killed by a power of \(\mathfrak a\) form the direct sum of the columns. Filtering that subcomplex by annihilating power gives successive quotients built from copies of the acyclic complex of constants. This gives \(R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}X_{\mathrm{lin}}=0\). Its cellular approximation is zero by orthogonality. In (6.13) the object itself is cellular. The [tower of tensor products of fibres of extension classes of strictly increasing degrees](18-open-problems.md#problem-physical-adjunction) is likewise cellular even though its intrinsic torsion counit fails.

Over \(\mathbb C\), the same linear tower also supplies a counterexample after forgetting energy. Write \(U\) for forgetting the grading. For an ungraded smooth module \(N\), put one copy of \(N\) in each integer energy of \(PN\) and take their algebraic direct sum. A current \(x_j\) takes sheet \(e\) to sheet \(e-j\) by its given action on \(N\). Each vector occupies finitely many sheets, so the maximum of their cutoffs gives smoothness. The functors \(U\) and \(P\) are exact, preserve coproducts and are adjoint, \(U\dashv P\), also on the ordinary unbounded derived categories. In \(PUM\) the difference of the sheet index and the original energy is constant under the currents. Grouping by that difference gives \(PUM=\bigoplus_s M\langle s\rangle\). In particular \(P\mathbb C\) is a sum of graded cells and \(M\) is a summand of \(PUM\). Thus cellularity of \(UX_{\mathrm{lin}}\) would imply cellularity of \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\). Exact forgetting retains its nonzero, jointly torsion cohomology.

One can also compute all maps from the ungraded cell into this particular object. Let \(V=\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb C\langle s\rangle\) have trivial current action and cochain degree zero. Adjunction computes these maps by the graded cell in energy zero mapping into \(PUX_{\mathrm{lin}}=X_{\mathrm{lin}}\otimes V\). To compute the latter target, tensor each finite stage with \(V\) before taking its smooth inverse limit. The terms are still injective: the smooth graded category has noetherian generators given by the shifted regular finite-jet enveloping algebras, so sums of injectives are injective. The same bounds on the tail-invariant layers identify the cohomology of this limit with \(H^*(X_{\mathrm{lin}})\otimes V\), by the natural map from \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\otimes V\). The resulting K-injective model can have infinitely many summands contributing to the complex of constants at a fixed energy. That complex is a product over the same fixed multisets and omissions in \(\{1,2\}\); the signed contractions above act separately on its factors. Its cohomology vanishes. Thus \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb C,UX_{\mathrm{lin}}[j])=0\) for every \(j\) in the ungraded smooth category.

What would it take to carry this failure of generation to affine modules? For an actual smooth affine \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) complex \(M\) at a fixed raw level, let \(\operatorname{Res}M\) be its restriction to \(t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t]\). Work over \(\mathbb C\) in the algebraic energy grading. Smooth induction is exact by the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem and takes the trivial module to the regular-top \(W\). Thus maps from the shifted \(W\)'s to \(M\) are computed by maps from the shifted trivial modules to \(\operatorname{Res}M\). The module \(\operatorname{Res}W\) is jointly torsion and concentrated in degree zero, so the bounded-below cellularity theorem puts it in \(\mathcal L\). Since restriction is exact and preserves coproducts, it carries the affine cellular part of \(M\) into \(\mathcal L\) and its right-orthogonal remainder into \(\mathcal L^\perp\). If \(\operatorname{Res}M\) is cellular, that remainder restricts to an object in both subcategories and is zero by conservativity. For such affine inputs this proves
\[
 M\in\mathscr C\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Res}M\in\mathcal L .
\]
{#eq-ch06-affine-positive-current-restriction}
Joint torsion is the same vectorwise condition after restriction. A smooth affine lift of \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\) would therefore give a torsion-cohomology object outside \(\mathscr C\). Inducing \(X_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and restricting it back gives \(T_\ell X_{\mathrm{lin}}=\operatorname{Res}(A_\ell\otimes_{U_+}X_{\mathrm{lin}})\), with the full regular horizontal algebra. In the normalization \((e,f)=1,\ (h,h)=2\), the energy-\(-2\) cell has a nonzero degree-zero map to \(T_\ell X_{\mathrm{lin}}\) for every complex raw level \(\ell\). For this restricted induction, maps in degree two vanish at every cell energy. A degree-one map exists at some energy exactly for integral \(\ell\le-6\). The degree-zero map rules out vacuum orthogonality at every level. Whether this induced complex is cellular remains open. At the critical level another choice of operators gives an affine complex whose regular-top cell maps all vanish.

**Theorem (a critical affine complex orthogonal to the regular top).** Let \(\mathfrak g\) be finite-dimensional, complex and simple, with long roots of squared length \(2\). Put \(U_+=U(t\mathfrak g[t])\), and let \(W=A_\ell\otimes_{U_+}\mathbb C\) be the affine module with regular horizontal top. At raw level \(\ell=-h^\vee\), in the ordinary unbounded derived category of algebraically energy-graded smooth affine modules, \(\mathscr C=\operatorname{Loc}\{W\langle u\rangle:u\in\mathbb Z\}\) is strictly contained in \(D_{\mathfrak a}\). There is a nonzero \(Z\in D_{\mathfrak a}\) with \(\operatorname{RHom}(W\langle u\rangle,Z)=0\) for every \(u\). Both its cellular counit and its intrinsic torsion counit have zero source.

*Proof.* For \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), take the opposite right vacuum \(\mathbb C\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{sl}_2[t^{-1}])}A_\ell\). The nonpositive modes kill its generator \(v\), and its restriction to \(U_+\) is the regular right module. Consider the quadratic current \(S_2=h_1^2/2+e_1f_1+f_1e_1\). Right multiplication of its three terms on \(v\) by \(e_{-1}\) gives respectively \(2ve_1\), \((\ell+2)ve_1\) and \(\ell ve_1\). Their sum is \(2(\ell+2)ve_1\). More generally, for form-dual bases \(u^a,u_a\) put
\[
 S_m=\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}\sum_a (u^a)_r(u_a)_{m-r},\qquad m\ge2 .
\]
Invariance of the form gives \((vS_m)y_0=0\). On commuting \(y_{-1}\) through the two factors the interior terms cancel in adjacent pairs. The two central contractions contribute \(2\ell\) and the adjoint Casimir contributes \(4\), so \((vS_m)y_{-1}=2(\ell+2)vy_{m-1}\). At \(\ell=-2\) the vector \(vS_m\) is another vector killed by all nonpositive modes. Sending \(v\) to \(vS_m\) thus defines an affine endomorphism, given on \(U_+\) by left multiplication.

The even quadratics \(S_{2i}\) commute. After evaluation at distinct points, the partial fractions of the quadratic generating series have coefficients given by the individual Casimirs and multiples of the Gaudin Hamiltonians. The latter commute because terms on disjoint factors commute and the terms on each triple cancel by invariance of the quadratic tensor. The Vandermonde matrix of the evaluation points detects each finite set of modes in the leading PBW symbol. In the quotient by \(\mathfrak n_K\) the quadratics vanish for \(i\ge K\). For \(i<K\), order the length-two symbols first by the smallest sum of squared mode indices and then by the largest number of Cartan factors. The leading monomial of \(S_{2i}\) is \(h_i^2/2\). Separating the even powers of each \(h_i\) gives a triangular basis over the polynomial algebra of the surviving quadratics.

The restricted dual of the opposite vacuum has underlying positive-current module \(C\). Use the transposes of these left multiplications as its Koszul operators, giving \(\epsilon_i\) energy \(2i\). This time the top is \(C\) alone in degree \(-1\). It maps by \(\epsilon_1\wedge\epsilon_2\) into the first two-generator column. At a multiset \(\alpha\) reached from the empty one, put \(N=|\alpha|+2\), place an \(r\)-form in degree \(r-N\), and shift energy by \(-\sum_{i=1}^N2i-\sum_{a\in\alpha}2a\). For \(1\le a\le N\) attach the column adjoining \(a\) by the same signed right wedge with \(\epsilon_a\wedge\epsilon_{N+1}\). These are affine maps. In the graded product a mode \(y_j\) sends a family killed by \(\mathfrak n_K\) to one killed by \(\mathfrak n_{K+\max(0,-j)}\), by the affine bracket. Thus the common-tail product is an affine submodule, and taking the smooth inverse limit \(Z\) gives that same product after restriction. The finite-jet Koszul calculation now uses the triangular basis for the quadratics. It gives \(H^{-1}(\operatorname{Res}Z)=C\) and jointly torsion cohomology in every layer.

On mapping out of a positive-current cell, the internal operators kill the constants. Fix the multiset formed by the branch indices together with the omitted exterior indices. If it is nonempty, inserting or deleting its largest index contracts the complex of constants. The top arrow pairs the empty case. As in the linear tower, the split stage projections make the restricted limit K-injective. Induction–restriction adjunction gives the asserted vanishing for all shifts of \(W\). The identity on \(Z\) survives since its restriction has nonzero cohomology, so \(Z\) lies outside \(\mathscr C\). Orthogonality gives zero cellular approximation. Every object in the image of \(D(\mathcal T)\) has a representative with torsion terms and is cellular, so (6.12) also gives \(R\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}Z=0\). For general simple \(\mathfrak g\) the adjoint Casimir is \(2h^\vee\), and the critical cancellation takes place at \(\ell=-h^\vee\). The square of a Cartan basis vector of nonzero norm supplies the leading monomial in each even weight, giving the same construction. ∎

Forgetting the energy grading of \(Z\) also gives strict inclusion at the critical level in the ordinary ungraded smooth affine category. The sheet construction above works for negative modes as well: both sides of the bracket of modes \(r,s\) shift a sheet by \(-(r+s)\), and its central term occurs at \(r+s=0\) and acts on that sheet by the given level. A finite sum of homogeneous vectors again has one positive-current cutoff. Thus \(P(UW)=\bigoplus_s W\langle s\rangle\). If \(UZ\) were generated by \(UW\), the exact coproduct-preserving functor \(P\) would put \(P(UZ)\) in \(\mathscr C\), together with its summand \(Z\). This contradicts the theorem. Forgetting the grading preserves the nonzero torsion cohomology of \(Z\). To calculate the maps from \(UW\), one needs the energy bounds for the quadratic tower itself.

Write \(Y_p\) for the positive-current restrictions of the finite stages and \(Y=\operatorname{Res}Z\). As above, give \(V\) one trivial summand in each energy. At the tail \(\mathfrak n_K=t^K\mathfrak g[t]\), the triangular basis makes the truncated enveloping algebra free as a left module over the polynomial algebra in the first \(\min(N,K-1)\) even quadratics of a column of size \(N\). Their dual Koszul cochains have cohomology only in form degree zero. The remaining exterior generators contribute through form degree \(\max(0,N+1-K)\). In total degrees the resulting interval is \([-N,\max(-N,1-K)]\). For a fixed degree \(d\), choose \(1-K<d-2\). Every column added at \(N>2-d\) then lies cohomologically below \(d-2\), uniformly in energy. This makes the tail-invariant stage cohomologies in degrees \(d-1,d\) constant from one finite stage onwards, including after tensoring by \(V\). Indeed, each vector of a stage tensor has only finitely many coefficients in \(V\), so testing the tail tests each coefficient. The split stage projections and the inverse-limit sequence now identify the natural map \(Y\otimes V\longrightarrow\lim_p(Y_p\otimes V)\) as a quasi-isomorphism after taking the union over \(K\).

The limit on the right is K-injective in the graded smooth positive-current category by the same split-stage argument. In cell energy \(u\), a fixed multiset \(\mu\) of branch and omitted indices gives its finite complex of constants tensored with \(V_{u+\sum_{i\in\mu}2i}\), counting multiplicities. The full complex in that energy is the product over \(\mu\). Its contraction acts separately on every factor, including the top arrow for the empty multiset. Thus the ungraded trivial cell has zero derived maps to \(UY\) by \(U\dashv P\). Exact smooth induction takes that cell to \(UW\), giving
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{D(\operatorname{Sm}_{\mathrm{un}}(A_\ell))}
                (UW,UZ[j])=0\qquad(j\in\mathbb Z,\ \ell=-h^\vee).
\]
For this tower one can also compare the separately formed ungraded limit with the forgotten graded one. Applying the exact conservative right adjoint \(P\) to \(UY\to\lim_p UY_p\) gives the tensor comparison just proved. The map is therefore a quasi-isomorphism. The terms have different support conditions: in the column labelled by \(r\) copies of \(1\), the constant functional times the full exterior word has cochain degree zero and original energy \(-2r\). Taking these vectors together over all \(r\) gives a family killed by every positive current in the ungraded limit. A vector of \(UY\) occupies only finitely many original energies.

This [critical affine example](#atlas-c-induction-full-torsion-comparison) leaves the unbounded comparison at other raw levels open.

### Energy bounds for the smooth unit and the torsion counit {#ch06-energy-window}

There is a second way to control the truncations for positive-current \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\). In (6.13) infinitely many layers contributed at energy zero because the energy shift of each layer exactly cancelled the cost of reaching its resolution degree. Suppose instead that each \(H^qX\) has an energy floor \(b_q\), with \(b_q=+\infty\) for a zero group, in the algebraic integer-energy grading. At a fixed total cochain degree \(t\), a term of resolution degree \(p\) uses \(H^{t-p}X\). The energy cost is \(e_p=p(p+1)/2\) as before. If, for every \(t\),
\[
 b_{t-p}+e_p\longrightarrow+\infty\qquad(p\to\infty),
\]
then \(X\to GFX\) and \(iR\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}X\to X\) are equivalences, and \(X\) belongs to the localizing subcategory generated by the energy-shifted trivial modules. There is no common-tail hypothesis here.

The estimate comes from the minimal resolution for the tail \(t^N\mathfrak{sl}_2[t]\). At homological degree \(p\) its generators lower energy by at least \(Np+\binom p2\ge e_p\). For a cohomology layer with floor \(b_q\), a degree-preserving map from the resolution shifted to energy \(e\) can contribute to \(\operatorname{Ext}^p\) only if \(e\ge b_q+e_p\). This bound also applies to finite augmentation quotients by filtering them by shifted trivial modules. Thus for fixed output degree and energy the tests for both functors stabilize at a single finite truncation, uniformly in the tail and the quotient. The smooth unit follows first from the ambient module truncations. Its identification then permits the same finite calculation for the torsion counit.

For instance \(b_q\ge-cq^2-C|q|-D_0\) in sufficiently negative degrees suffices if \(0\le c<1/2\). At the quadratic boundary in (6.13) the layers instead have \(b_{-p}=-e_p\). Infinitely many align in energy zero, and the quotient of a product by a direct sum displayed there measures the failure of the torsion comparison. The object in (6.13) is still cellular. For the [Koszul tower](#ch06-theorem-a-complex-orthogonal-to-all-positive-current-cells) the lowest energy of \(H^{-n}(X_{\mathrm{lin}})\) is \(-n^2-4n+2\) for \(n\ge2\). At \(t=0\) the sum of this floor and \(e_n=n(n+1)/2\) tends to \(-\infty\). This tower lies outside the energy-window criterion.

For affine currents the comparison under (6.1) at nonrational shifted level is \(D(Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g))\simeq\operatorname{Loc}(W)\), with all energy shifts when graded. To use it for a physical line we need its affine module and a proof that the module lies in this sector. To use it for fusion we also need a comparison on the product of two lines, natural in maps between the input modules.

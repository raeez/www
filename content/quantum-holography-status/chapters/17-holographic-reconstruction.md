# From boundary operations to bulk reconstruction

A defect transports boundary conditions and the maps between them. Suppose two interfaces carry the same generating boundary condition to the same line. Do they agree on every boundary condition built from that generator? A summand of the generator already decides the question.

::: {.example #ch17-two-actions-on-a-line title="two actions on the same line"}
Over a field \(k\), let \(A=k\times k\) and write \(e_1=(1,0)\), \(e_2=(0,1)\). For each \(i=1,2\) give the line \(P_i=k\) its usual right scalar action and let \(A\) act on the left by its \(i\)-th coordinate. Tensoring a complex \(M\) of right \(A\)-modules with \(P_i\) takes its \(i\)-th summand. Explicitly,
\[
 F_i(M)=M\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}P_i \simeq M e_i .
\]
The isomorphism sends \(m\otimes c\) to \(c\,m e_i\). The ordinary tensor computes the derived tensor here because \(P_i\simeq A e_i\) is projective. Both functors send the free right module \(A\) to \(k\). Apply them now to its endomorphism \(a\mapsto e_1a\). On \(P_1\) this acts as the identity, on \(P_2\) as zero. Its image \(e_1A\) consequently goes to \(k\) under \(F_1\) and to zero under \(F_2\). The two functors have inequivalent values on this boundary condition. Forgetting the left action identifies their kernels as right \(k\)-modules.
:::

For an operation of several variables, what replaces the two characters of \(A\)? An endomorphism of any one input acts on the output at the generators. These actions specify how to apply the operation to the arrows of a free resolution. Work in presentable stable \(k\)-linear categories, with their enrichment over complexes of \(k\)-vector spaces and their homotopy colimits. For \(r\ge1\) inputs indexed by \(1\le i\le r\) and the output indexed by \(i=0\), write \(\mathcal S_i=\operatorname{Loc}(G_i)\) for the full stable subcategory generated by \(G_i\) under colimits and retracts. Assume \(G_i\) is compact in \(\mathcal S_i\). Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_iX &= \operatorname{RHom}(G_i,X),\\
 E_i &= R_iG_i,\\
 E_{\rm in} &= E_1\otimes_k\cdots\otimes_k E_r .
\end{aligned}
\]
Composition is the multiplication of \(E_i\). Precomposing \(f:G_i\to X\) with \(a:G_i\to G_i\) gives the right action \(f\cdot a\) on \(R_iX\). Thus \(R_iG_i\) is the free right \(E_i\)-module. The adjunction with \(M\mapsto M\otimes_{E_i}^{\mathbf L}G_i\) identifies \(\mathcal S_i\) with the derived category of right \(E_i\)-modules, including its mapping complexes. Indeed the unit and counit are equivalences on \(E_i\) and \(G_i\). Both functors preserve colimits there, the mapping functor by exactness and compactness, so these equivalences extend throughout the generated categories. [J. Lurie, *Higher Algebra*, Theorem 7.1.2.1](https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/HA.pdf#page=1206)

::: {.proposition #ch17-proposition-operation-kernels title="operation kernels"}
Suppose \(\mu,\nu:\prod_{i=1}^r\mathcal S_i\to\mathcal S_0\) are enriched \(k\)-multilinear operations preserving colimits in each input. The kernel \(P_\mu=R_0\mu(G_1,\ldots,G_r)\) carries a left \(E_{\rm in}\)-action by applying \(\mu\) to input endomorphisms, as well as its right \(E_0\)-action. For a tuple \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_r)\), form the right \(E_{\rm in}\)-module \(M=\bigotimes_{i=1}^r R_iX_i\), taking tensors over \(k\). Then
\[
 R_0\mu(X)\simeq M\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{E_{\rm in}}P_\mu .
\] {#operation-kernels}
Restriction to the full enriched subcategories on the generators identifies the space of homotopy-coherent enriched natural transformations with the derived mapping space of bimodules,
\[
 \operatorname{Map}_k(\mu,\nu)
 \simeq \operatorname{Map}_{E_{\rm in}\text{-}E_0}(P_\mu,P_\nu).
\]
This equivalence includes homotopies between maps and all higher homotopies.
:::

::: {.proof #ch17-proof-2}
Write \(M_i=R_iX_i\). Its free bar resolution in simplicial degree \(n\) is \(M_i\otimes_k E_i^{\otimes_k n}\otimes_k E_i\). Evaluate the operation on these resolutions. On the tuple of free generators it gives \(P_\mu\). The face which acts on a free generator now acts on \(P_\mu\) from the left. The opposite face acts on \(M_i\) from the right. Realising the resolutions one variable at a time therefore gives the first formula. On the full enriched subcategories spanned by the generators, a transformation specifies its component at their tuple and its coherent naturality along strings of input endomorphisms. Together these data give a map of derived bimodules.

Write \(A=E_{\rm in}\), \(B=E_0\), \(P=P_\mu\) and \(Q=P_\nu\). The free left \(A\)-module bar resolution of \(P\) has terms \(A^{\otimes_k(n+1)}\otimes_k P\), each with its right \(B\)-action. Mapping it to \(Q\) gives the cosimplicial spaces
\[
 \mathcal H^n=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathrm{Mod}_B}
       (A^{\otimes_k n}\otimes_k P,Q).
\]
Their totalisation is \(\operatorname{Map}_{A\text{-}B}(P,Q)\). Projection to cosimplicial degree zero records the underlying map \(P\to Q\) of right modules. The two outer cofaces use the left actions on \(P\) and \(Q\), and the inner cofaces multiply neighbouring inputs. Thus the higher components record naturality along strings of endomorphisms. Conversely, tensoring a bimodule map with the resolved inputs gives a transformation. Restriction and this extension are inverse on the free modules and hence on their resolutions, naturally also for families of maps and their homotopies. This gives the second formula. ∎
:::

For instance, composing a unary operation with \(E\)-\(D\) kernel \(P\) and one with \(D\)-\(C\) kernel \(Q\), in that order, gives the kernel \(P\otimes_D^{\mathbf L}Q\). This description applies on the generated subcategories. If an operation takes values in a larger ambient category, its \(R_0\)-image is still given by the first formula provided \(R_0\mu\) itself preserves colimits separately. Recovering the output from that image requires the evaluation back to the ambient category to be an equivalence on that output. [B. Toën, *The homotopy theory of dg-categories and derived Morita theory*, Corollary 7.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0408337#page=40)

::: example {#ch17-transformation-on-the-residue-field title="A transformation detected by the residue field"}
Let \(A=\mathbb Q[x]\). Which input detects a map from the identity functor on derived right \(A\)-modules to its shift? Form the bimodule \(E=Ae\oplus Af\), free on the left, with right action
\[
 e p=p e,\qquad f p=p f+p'e\qquad (p\in A).
\]
The Leibniz rule gives \((fp)q=f(pq)\). The submodule \(Ae\) and the quotient generated by the image of \(f\) are both the diagonal bimodule \(A\). The connecting map of this extension, tensored with each input, gives a natural transformation \(\mathrm{Id}\to\mathrm{Id}[1]\).

On the free right module \(A\), the quotient has the right-linear section \(p\mapsto fp\), so the component of the transformation is zero. A bimodule section would send \(1\) to \(f+he\) for some \(h\in A\). Its failure to commute with \(x\) is the element
\[
 (f+he)x-x(f+he)=e .
\]
Now take the right module \(k=A/(x)\). The sequence splits on the left, so tensoring it with \(k\) computes the derived extension term by term. In the middle term \(\bar f x=\bar e\) and \(\bar e x=0\). Every lift \(\bar f+c\bar e\) of \(1\) has nonzero product with \(x\), so the sequence \(0\to k\to A/(x^2)\to k\to0\) has no right-linear section. The same transformation has a nonzero component on \(k\).
:::

::: {.example #ch17-double-dual-and-sums title="a functor that fails to preserve sums"}
On complexes of vector spaces the double dual \(V\mapsto V^{**}\), with \(V^*=\operatorname{Hom}_k(V,k)\), agrees with the identity on the generator \(k\) and its scalar endomorphisms. Take \(V=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}k\) in degree zero. Then \(V^*=k^{\mathbb N}\), and evaluation sends each vector in \(V\) to a finite linear combination of coordinate functions on \(k^{\mathbb N}\). The constant sequence of ones has nonzero class in \(k^{\mathbb N}/k^{(\mathbb N)}\). Extend that class to a basis and take its coordinate functional. Pulled back to \(k^{\mathbb N}\), the functional vanishes on every coordinate vector but takes the value one on the constant sequence. Thus it lies outside the image of \(V\to V^{**}\). This map is precisely the comparison from the direct sum of the double duals to the double dual of the sum.
:::

Which operations does a given bulk theory actually induce? A scalar count can omit precisely the action one wants to compare. If a zero has multiplicity two, it contributes twice to an arithmetic trace. Let \(N\) be multiplication by \(z\) on \(\mathbb C[z]/(z^2)\). In the basis \(1,z\), translation \(e^{tN}=1+tN\) fixes \(z\) and sends \(1\) to \(1+tz\). Its trace is two for every \(t\). Applying the translation to \(1\) detects the entry that the trace loses. What boundary input would distinguish a bulk field from zero?

Take a disk \(V\) in the boundary of the free abelian half-space with response \(\kappa\partial_z\). We can leave the disk empty or insert a current \(v\) there. To compare the two readings, choose a smooth normal profile of integral one below height \(\varepsilon\). It includes the current complex \(\mathcal A_\kappa(V)\) in the collar by a cochain map \(i^q\). Place a regular bulk observable \(f\) in an open set \(U\) above this profile. Extend \(f\) by zero and multiply it by the included boundary insertion. A contraction in the Batalin–Vilkovisky differential between the two inputs is supported where their points coincide. Their disjoint normal supports therefore give the graded Leibniz rule for this product. The quantum projection \(p^q\) integrates out the normal fields. Its Wick pairing can connect the two heights. On smooth compactly supported kernels at each finite field number the operation is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{U;V}:\operatorname{Obs}_{\mathrm{bulk}}(U)&\longrightarrow
\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal A_\kappa(V)),\\
\beta_{U;V}(f)(v)&=p^q\bigl(\operatorname{ext}_U(f)\,i^q(v)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Write \(d_\partial\) for the boundary differential and abbreviate \(\beta_{U;V}\) to \(\beta\). For a homogeneous bulk input \(f\), the Leibniz rule and the two cochain maps \(i^q,p^q\) give
\[
d_\partial\beta(f)-(-1)^{|f|}\beta(f)d_\partial=\beta(d_{\mathrm{bulk}}f).
\]
When \(f\) is closed, this identity shows that \(\beta(f)\) takes boundary cocycles to cocycles and coboundaries to coboundaries. It then induces an operation on boundary cohomology.

Choose a linear bulk test \(u_1\) in the field component killed by the linear projection. Give its normal one-form integral one and choose the tangential pairing so that \(u_1\) contracts with the included current \(v\) with coefficient one. Now insert \(u=\kappa u_1\). On the empty disk there is no field to contract with \(u\), and the projection gives zero. On the occupied disk the uncontracted term still contains \(u\) and is killed by the projection. A single Wick contraction, however, consumes both fields. The result is \(\hbar\kappa\) times the unit, with one factor of \(\hbar\) for the contraction. On the square of the same even current there are two possible partners, giving \(2\hbar\kappa v\). Thus at \(\hbar\kappa\ne0\) this boundary detects the chosen bulk operation on cochains. To represent a bulk cohomology class the test must also be closed. [The supported bulk action](07-actions-and-centres.md#ch07-supported-bulk-action)

Recognition asks how much of a bulk field is determined by its actions as the boundary and its insertions vary. A defect gives a related test. Carry two boundary conditions across it and compare the maps between them before and after transport. Does every map between the transported modules come from a unique map between the original boundary conditions? Even when this holds, one can join two lines before crossing or after crossing. Agreement of those products is another condition, and a filling must also assign an amplitude to the empty insertion.

## Interfaces give an explicit reconstruction map {#interfaces-and-reconstruction}

Let \(A=M_n(k)\) act from the left on the column \(P=k^n\) over a field \(k\). For an \(A\)-module \(M\), the diagonal matrix \(E_{11}\) picks out a subspace \(E_{11}M\). When \(n\ge2\), can we recover a vector in the second row from that subspace? Multiplication by \(E_{21}\) moves a vector from the first row to the second, and \(E_{12}\) moves it back. This gives the first instance of the recovery map below. The column also has a commuting right action by scalars. It can therefore transport an arbitrary vector space \(L\) to \(P\otimes_k L\), with matrices acting on the first factor.

**Definition (interface bimodule).** In the algebraic model of a defect between differential graded algebras \(A\) and \(B\), the interface is a complex \(P\) with commuting left \(A\)- and right \(B\)-actions compatible with its differential. It carries a left \(B\)-module \(L\) across the defect by tensoring over \(B\).

The derived functors
\[
F=P\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-:D(B)\longrightarrow D(A),
\qquad
G=R\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,-):D(A)\longrightarrow D(B)
\tag{17.1}
\]
form an adjunction on derived categories of left modules with their mapping complexes. In particular, a map \(P\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L\to M\) corresponds to a map \(L\to G(M)\). For ordinary algebras and modules concentrated in degree zero, the underived correspondence sends \(f\) to \(\ell\mapsto(p\mapsto f(p\otimes\ell))\). On complexes, after resolving the arguments, the adjoint of a degree-zero chain map has value \(p\mapsto(-1)^{|p||\ell|} f(p\otimes\ell)\) on homogeneous \(\ell\). The right \(B\)-action on \(P\) gives the left \(B\)-action on \(G(M)\) by precomposition. In the ordinary degree-zero calculation the unit sends \(\ell\in L\) to the map \(p\mapsto p\otimes\ell\), and the counit sends \(p\otimes h\) to \(h(p)\). Their derived forms are
\[
\eta_L:L\longrightarrow GF(L),
\qquad
\varepsilon_M:FG(M)\longrightarrow M.
\tag{17.2}
\]
**Lemma (unit and counit).** \(F\) is fully faithful exactly when \(\eta_L\) is an equivalence for every \(L\). Under that hypothesis the essential image, the objects equivalent to some \(F(L)\), consists precisely of those \(M\) for which \(\varepsilon_M\) is an equivalence.

*Proof.* For the first assertion apply the adjunction to maps \(F(L')\to F(L)\). Under this identification the map on mapping complexes is postcomposition by \(\eta_L\). If it is an equivalence for every \(L'\), then the cone of \(\eta_L\) receives no derived maps from any \(L'\). Taking \(L'\) to be that cone kills its identity, so the cone is zero. Conversely, an invertible unit induces the required equivalence on maps. For the second, the triangle identity gives \(\varepsilon_{F(L)}F(\eta_L)=1\), while an invertible \(\varepsilon_M\) itself exhibits \(M\simeq F(G(M))\). ∎

A derived Morita equivalence requires the counit to be invertible throughout \(D(A)\).

**Example (matrix units).** For the column \(P=k^n\), put \(A=M_n(k)\), \(B=k\), and \(e=E_{11}\), where \(E_{ij}\) sends the \(j\)-th basis vector \(v_j\) to \(v_i\) and kills the others. An \(A\)-linear map \(h:P\to M\) is determined by \(m=h(v_1)\), since \(v_i=E_{i1}v_1\). The equality \(ev_1=v_1\) puts \(m\) in \(eM\). Conversely any \(m\in eM\) defines such a map by \(h(v_i)=E_{i1}m\). Since the column \(P\simeq Ae\) is a summand of the free left module \(A\), this calculation computes \(G(M)\) on complexes as well. Evaluating \(h\) gives the counit
\[
\varepsilon_M:k^n\otimes eM\longrightarrow M,
\qquad v_i\otimes m\longmapsto E_{i1}m.
\tag{17.3}
\]
Its inverse is
\[
M\longrightarrow k^n\otimes eM,
\qquad m\longmapsto\sum_i v_i\otimes E_{1i}m.
\]
The two composites are identities because \(E_{1j}E_{i1}=\delta_{ij}e\) and \(\sum_iE_{i1}E_{1i}=1_A\). The unit sends a vector \(v\) to \(v_1\otimes v\) in \(e(k^n\otimes V)\), so it is also an isomorphism. This proves the equivalence of module categories directly, including its maps.

The centre of the upper triangular two-by-two matrices is \(k\), just as for \(M_2(k)\). Evaluating the two diagonal entries gives two nonisomorphic simple modules for the triangular algebra. The matrix-unit calculation gives just one simple module for \(M_2(k)\), up to isomorphism. Thus an identification of these centres leaves undetermined even the simple boundary conditions.

The two diagonal characters of the triangular algebra already distinguish boundary conditions that its centre cannot separate. For a field theory the analogous comparison starts with a particular defect. It must supply \(P\), its differential and both actions on their allowed supports. The defect must supply the comparison \(L\to GF(L)\) and the evaluation \(FG(M)\to M\) in (17.2). In the affine model the column is replaced by an induced module. Which smooth modules can be recovered from it? [Derived adjunctions](06-duality-and-induction.md#adjunction-comparison-maps)

## What the affine theorem reconstructs {#affine-reconstruction-scope}

Take a simple complex Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\), normalize its long roots to square length two, and write \(h^\vee\) for its dual Coxeter number. Let \(s\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Q\) be the shifted level and \(B=Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\) the polynomial differential graded line algebra. The affine mode algebra \(A\) has raw level \(s-h^\vee\). The module
\[
W=A\otimes_{U(t\mathfrak g[t])}\mathbb C
\]
is generated by a vector killed by \(t\mathfrak g[t]\). An affine module is smooth if some current tail \(t^N\mathfrak g[t]\) kills each given vector. For an ordinary smooth module \(M\) with the energy grading forgotten, induction identifies \(A\)-linear maps \(W\to M\) with the vectors killed by all strictly positive currents. The correspondence evaluates a map on the inducing vector. An energy-preserving map from \(W\) selects an invariant of energy zero, and shifts of \(W\) select the other energies. For a degree-zero chain map into a complex the image must also be a degree-zero cycle. The zero modes can still act on the inducing vector of \(W\). Their words give its energy-zero space, the regular module over \(R=U(\mathfrak g)\). For example a nonzero \(x\in\mathfrak g\) sends the horizontal unit to \(x\). In the scalar-top vacuum the same zero mode kills the generating vector. A choice between these two inputs changes which maps the induction functor sees.

What does an action on a resolution of \(W\) have to preserve? On a vector killed by this tail, a positive polynomial current is read modulo \(t^N\), so only the coefficients of \(t,\ldots,t^{N-1}\) act. A negative mode can lower a positive mode index. To compare a finite diagram of such operators at output order \(N\), the input must be read at one sufficiently large order for every composite in the diagram. Compatibility as the order increases is one of the smooth realization hypotheses below.

**Conditional theorem (affine induction).** Assume a coherent smooth right-\(B\) realization \(\mathcal W\) of \(W\), the prescribed homogeneous endomorphism comparison, compatible Taylor cutoffs for finite diagrams on its Chevalley resolution, and the enhanced tensor–Hom adjunction. Then the unit for \(F=\mathcal W\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-\) is invertible on every ordinary unbounded source complex and gives
\[
D(B)\simeq\operatorname{Loc}(W).
\tag{17.4}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Loc}(W)\) is generated by \(W\) inside the smooth affine target under shifts, cones, coproducts and retracts, including all internal energy shifts in the graded convention. For a smooth complex \(M\), let \(C_M\) be the cone of the counit \(\varepsilon_M:FGM\to M\) in the smooth affine category. The triangle identity gives \(G(\varepsilon_M)\eta_{GM}=1_{GM}\). The unit is invertible by the theorem, so \(G(\varepsilon_M)\) is an equivalence and \(G(C_M)\simeq0\). By adjunction the mapping complex from any \(F(L)\) to \(C_M\) vanishes. Applying maps from \(F(L)\) to the counit triangle therefore identifies maps into \(FGM\) with maps into \(M\). Since the objects \(F(L)\) exhaust the generated sector up to equivalence, the counit has this factorization property for the entire sector. This is the coreflection in (17.4). [The realization hypotheses](06-duality-and-induction.md#ch06-affine-currents-and-the-algebra-acting-on-their-resolution) · [The original counit](06-duality-and-induction.md#ch06-the-image-and-its-original-counit)

Why does an argument with finite weight steps apply to a complex unbounded in cohomological degree? The descending filtration used here counts positive factors in the line algebra \(B\). Its length is bounded independently of the cohomological degrees of the module. Let \(I=\ker(B\to B_0=R)\) and write \(D\) for the number of positive roots of \(\mathfrak g\). The derived tensors are taken over the weight-zero algebra \(R\). One has
\[
I^{\otimes^{\mathbf L}_R(D+1)}\simeq0,
\qquad I^{\otimes^{\mathbf L}_R D}\not\simeq0.
\tag{17.5}
\]
Each surviving positive factor raises energy and moves strictly up the finite Bruhat order in its integral linkage block. The length can rise at most \(D\) times. A block in which all simple integral roots have positive mode realizes a longest word through actual embeddings of affine Verma modules, giving the nonzero power. For \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\), the three positive roots give a surviving cube and a vanishing fourth power. Equation (17.5) yields a tower with at most \(D+1\) coefficient layers inflated from \(R\). The original unit is already invertible on each such layer, so exact triangles give the conclusion on the source itself. [The sharp exponent](#atlas-c-ur-10)

At other levels one must distinguish two ways an object could disappear. Reduction to weight zero applies \(R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-\), while transport across the algebraic interface applies \(F\). A telescope is the homotopy colimit of a sequence of modules and transition maps. If the transition maps disappear after reduction to \(R\), their telescope there is zero. To infer that transport kills the original object one would need information about the images of the same transitions under \(F\). Under the smooth realization at raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) level \(-3\), a nonzero telescope \(L\) satisfies \(R\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}L\simeq0\), \(F(L)\not\simeq0\) and \(\eta_L:L\xrightarrow{\sim}GF(L)\). Reduction to \(R\) loses a module that the affine realization detects. At critical raw level \(-h^\vee\), assume the smooth realization, its compatibility with forgetting smoothness and its lower-energy unit comparison. There a different telescope \(L'\ne0\) satisfies \(F(L')\simeq0\). Its realizing maps connect distinct cohomological degrees and vanish on cohomology, although the source extension products survive. Determining \(\ker F\) and the full image at rational shifted levels, or identifying \(\operatorname{Loc}(W)\) with a specified unbounded category of torsion modules, remains open in general. [The level \(-3\) telescope](06-duality-and-induction.md#ch06-raw-level-minus-three-distinguishes-the-two-quotients) · [Cellularity and derived torsion](06-duality-and-induction.md#ch06-cellularity-and-derived-torsion)

An all-level equivalence uses a different induction functor. Write \(k=-s\), so that (17.4) uses \(Y_k\) at raw level \(-k-h^\vee\). Twisted induction uses raw level \(k-h^\vee\) and termwise algebraically smooth modules for the even modes of \(Y_k\). It inverts the maps sent by twisted induction to affine quasi-isomorphisms and has restricted twisted coinduction as inverse. Its inverted maps form a proper subclass of ordinary quasi-isomorphisms. At \(k=0\) the raw levels coincide, but this change of source localization still distinguishes the two adjunctions. ## Products must survive passage through the interface {#products-through-an-interface}

For two lines one can join first and transport the result, or transport each line and then join. Even the full unit in (17.4) supplies no identification of these operations. If \(\mathcal C\) and \(\mathcal D\) have specified tensor products, their comparison starts with a tensorator
\[
\gamma_{L,M}:F(L)\otimes_{\mathcal D}F(M)
\longrightarrow F(L\otimes_{\mathcal C}M)
\tag{17.6}
\]
and a unit comparison. For three modules \(L,M,N\), one may transport the product of \(L,M\) first and then join \(N\), or transport \(M,N\) first and then join \(L\). The associativity diagram equates these composites after the two categories' reassociation maps. The unit diagrams concern inserting the tensor unit. A strong monoidal functor requires these maps to be equivalences. A braided comparison also includes the exchange square and its compatibility with the associator.

Suppose the transport and both products act on sectors with compact generators as above. Write \(E,D\) for the endomorphism algebras of the source and target generators. Assume \(F\) and the products are enriched \(k\)-linear in each variable and preserve colimits separately. Denote their kernels by \(P_F,P_{\mathcal C},P_{\mathcal D}\), respectively. The first composite in (17.6) transports twice and then joins. Its two copies of \(P_F\) therefore supply the two right \(D\)-actions for the product in \(\mathcal D\). Joining first uses \(P_{\mathcal C}\), whose right \(E\)-action supplies the input to \(F\). The tensorator in (17.6) is equivalently a map of \((E\otimes_k E)\)-\(D\) bimodules
\[
\begin{gathered}
 (P_F\otimes_k P_F)\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{D\otimes_k D}P_{\mathcal D}\\
 \big\downarrow\\
 P_{\mathcal C}\otimes_E^{\mathbf L}P_F .
\end{gathered}
\]
Both the map and the homotopy comparing its two substitutions on three inputs enter the monoidal structure. For the ungraded affine sector (17.4), the specified right action identifies \(B^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\operatorname{RHom}_{\operatorname{Sm}(A)}(W,W)\). Right modules over this endomorphism algebra are left \(B\)-modules. The graded convention uses all energy shifts. [The proposition on operation kernels](#ch17-proposition-operation-kernels)

**Example (the two vertices).** Take arrows \(X_1\xrightarrow{a}X_2\) of \(k[x]\)-complexes. The arrows \(k\xrightarrow{0}k\) and \(k\xrightarrow{1}k\) at \(x=0\) have identical vertex modules. To distinguish them, use the probes \(G_1=(k\xrightarrow{1}k)\) and \(G_2=(0\to k)\), with \(k=k[x]/(x)\). Resolving \(k\) by multiplication by \(x\) identifies the two mapping complexes from the probes with \(\operatorname{fib}(x:X_i\to X_i)\). Precomposition by the map \(G_2\to G_1\) which is the identity at the second vertex induces \(a\) on those fibres. It distinguishes zero from the identity even here.

In particular, observing the two fibres without the map between probes cannot recover the arrow. Together the probes generate the diagrams with \(x\)-power-torsion cohomology at both vertices. A diagram is right-orthogonal to \(G_1\) exactly when \(x\) acts invertibly on \(X_1\) in the derived category. Orthogonality to both probes requires this at both vertices.

Write \(H\) for the algebra of lower triangular two-by-two matrices over \(k\), so these arrows are left \(H[x]\)-modules. The double centralizer of a probe \(G\) is its derived endomorphism algebra over \(\operatorname{RHom}_{H[x]}(G,G)\). At the origin \(G_1\) is the two-dimensional column and its endomorphisms as an \(H\)-module are scalars. The two columns together are the regular \(H\)-module, whose right multiplications have centralizer \(H\) acting on the left. The Koszul resolution at \(x=0\) contributes an exterior generator \(\epsilon\) in cohomological degree one. Its augmentation module has a free resolution with generators \(v_n\) of degree zero, \(dv_0=0\) and \(dv_n=\epsilon v_{n-1}\) for \(n>0\). Lowering \(n\) represents \(x\), and ordinary Hom permits an arbitrary series in this operator. The resulting double centralizers are \(M_2(k[[x]])\) for \(G_1\) and \(H[[x]]\) for \(G_1\oplus G_2\). The upper-right matrix unit acts in the first double centralizer. Requiring it to commute with the morphism between the two probes removes it from the second. The collection of boundary conditions and its maps both enter reconstruction.

For local fields there is a further input to (17.6), the separation of the insertion points. In the spectral construction, start with a flat Hopf algebra over a characteristic-zero base with bijective antipode, compatible translation, and a unitary spectral form \(\rho_z\) with values in the specified Laurent polynomials. The levels \(\eta_i\) preserve both translation and the form. The product \(\boxtimes_\rho\) contracts fields belonging to different factors. The coproducts \(D_{\eta_1,\eta_2}=(\eta_2\otimes1)\Delta:V_{\eta_1\eta_2}\to V_{\eta_1}\boxtimes_\rho V_{\eta_2}\) preserve those fields. The field hexagon holds on the common space of expansions and the counit is defined at the identity level. [The spectral construction](12-chiral-quantum-groups.md#ch12-a-spectral-construction-of-products-and-coproducts)

For nonrational shifted level in \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), let \(q\) measure the common radial scale of a finite configuration and \(F\) be its field of shape parameters. Under the coherent realization, full unit, bounded-below cellularity and two-term inflated descent hypotheses, the comparison with the independently defined radial current complex holds for every finite tuple of ordinary unbounded line modules. One extends scalars to \(F\) before taking Laurent series in \(q\). Rational evaluation and its affine restriction preserve the outgoing action and all stated incoming actions. The original kernels compose along any finite tree on this common domain. [The radial comparison](08-completions.md#ch08-a-comparison-for-any-finite-number-of-radial-inputs)

That conclusion does not define substitution on every separately completed intermediate tensor. The series
\[
\sum_{n\geq0}q^n\lambda^{-n^2}
\]
is a valid element of \(\mathbb C(\lambda)((q))\), since every \(\lambda^{-n^2}\) is a rational shape coefficient. After \(\lambda=zq\), the \(n\)-th term is \(z^{-n^2}q^{n-n^2}\). For \(n\ge2\) its \(q\)-order decreases strictly to \(-\infty\). The proposed substituted series is outside the iterated Laurent space. A larger tensor comparison must impose and prove the support estimates that exclude this failure, or choose a different independently justified domain.

The failed series substitution retains rational functions of the shape. To take the actual fibre where two points coincide one must decide when the equation of their diagonal is to be inverted. For an integral complex \(C\) over \(R\) with diagonal equation \(d\), specialization is the derived fibre \((R/(d))\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}C\). Applied to \(C[d^{-1}]\) this gives zero, since multiplication by \(d\) on that tensor is both zero and invertible. The finite integral quotients have collision maps before \(d\) is inverted. Comparing them with radial fusion requires maps through specialization and completion. ### Ordered fields on algebraic states {#ch17-native-r3-fields}

The product of two fields gives a more direct test at finite quantum precision. Insert two states in the four-current system of \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\), exchange their positions, and let the resulting fields act on a third algebraic state. The currents have colours \(H,E,F\) in \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) and the trace colour \(I\), each with its ghost. Work over
\[
R_3=\mathbb C[\hbar]/(\hbar^3).
\]
Write \(M_3=V^{KB}(\mathfrak{gl}_2)\otimes_{R_3}F_0\) for the complex of algebraic current and ghost vacuum states with its corrected differential \(d_M\). The parameter \(K\) is the numerical current level, and \(T\) translates fields.

**Theorem (fields on algebraic states).** At each \(K\), including \(0\) and \(-2\), the ordered field \(\mathbb Y_-\), translation, vacuum and exchange give a differential graded quantum vertex superalgebra on \(M_3\). A single locality exponent for the first two states works for every final state. The opposite order gives a weakly associative chain field \(\mathbb Y_+\) homotopic to \(\mathbb Y_-\). A full quantum vertex structure for the opposite exchange would require its own identities. [The all-input field theorem](#atlas-c-native-r3-all-input-qva)

The two ordered fields already exist on algebraic states. Now put closed states at both incoming points and take the output modulo boundaries. Is the exchange still visible? Write \(S_M(u)\) for the exchange on the two incoming states at separation \(u\), and \(d_M^\otimes\) for their tensor differential. There is an algebraically finite Laurent homotopy \(\Lambda(u)\) with
\[
[d_M^{\otimes},\Lambda(u)]=S_M(u)-1.
\]
For cycle representatives of the first two inputs, a finite power of the separation, depending on those representatives, clears its poles. The difference of the two exchanged products then becomes a coefficientwise boundary for every final cycle. Consequently \(H^\bullet(M_3,d_M)\) carries an ordinary local vertex superalgebra, with \(R_3\)-bilinear fields and the original faithful central \(R_3\)-action. Both ordered fields induce that same operation. The argument needs no Künneth identification over \(R_3\). Ordinary locality permits nonzero singular products. [Cohomology fields](#atlas-c-native-r3-ordinary-cohomology).

To determine the products themselves, compute the states at a fixed \(K\ne-2\). Here \(I_m\) is the \(m\)-th trace-current creation state and \(\xi^I_j\) the \(j\)-th divided translation of the degree-one trace ghost. Let \(\omega_\hbar\) denote the cycle lifting the trace-free cubic ghost. An algebraic chain frame preserves the minimal inclusion and identifies
\[
H^\bullet(M_3)\cong
(R_3\mathbf1\oplus R_3[\omega_\hbar])
\otimes_{\mathbb C}
\left(\mathbb C[I_1,I_2,\ldots]
\otimes\Lambda(\xi^I_0,\xi^I_1,\ldots)\right),
\qquad |\omega_\hbar|=3.
\]
Write \(b=[\omega_\hbar]\) and let \(L\) denote the displayed trace factor. Let \(q_\hbar\) be the degree-two generator of the trace-free small complex. On its translates the differential is \(d(f(T)q_\hbar)=2Tf(T)\omega_\hbar\). Thus \(T\omega_\hbar=d(q_\hbar/2)\), which gives \(Tb=0\). Injectivity of multiplication by \(T\) on \(R_3[T]\) says something further. No nonzero \(f(T)q_\hbar\) supplies another cycle. The product of \(b\) with itself has degree six and stays in the trace-free factor, where the degree-six cohomology vanishes. The chain field factors with the commutative differential field of \(L\). Consequently all cohomology products at fixed \(K\ne-2\) have the form \(Y(x,u)y=(e^{uT}x)y\), with \(b^2=0\). This includes numerical level zero.

At the critical level \(K=-2\), let \(S\) be the quadratic Sugawara state of the trace-free current algebra. Its jets \(s_j=T^jS/j!\) and degree-one partners \(b_j\), obtained by differentiating the classical differential with respect to level, have weight \(j+2\). Together with the constant degree-three ghost class \(\omega\) they give a different state calculation. As a graded \(R_3\)-module, the full algebraic critical state cohomology is free on the classical monomials of
\[
\mathbb C[s_j:j\geq0]\otimes\Lambda(b_j:j\geq0)
\otimes\Lambda(\omega)\otimes L .
\]
The invariant \(S\) lifts as an actual cycle. Dividing its differential by \(K+2\) before specializing gives a lift of the partner, and applying the binary field to the lifted cycles gives a cycle above each classical basis monomial. One can choose a translation-linear change of state coordinates intertwining the differentials through precision three. This determines the state module and its precision reductions. Determining the singular products and independently produced marked operations at this level remains a further comparison.

### Retained kernels and the first rooted comparison {#ch17-retained-first-root}

To compare an endpoint operation before and after the exchange, first insert a current state \(w\), then pair the output ghosts with a chosen functional. An output proportional to a single ghost has zero vacuum coefficient, yet its coefficient against the dual of that ghost can be nonzero. We call \(w\) the **rooted input** because it stays in the second position.

At a cutoff retaining \(\xi^I_0\), the two functionals read a term \(v\otimes\xi^I_0\) as \(0\) and \(v\), respectively. Pulling the second functional back to a larger ghost-mode quotient must give the same answer. We therefore need the output at every cutoff, together with its projections to the smaller quotients.

**Definition (retained kernel).** Let \(V=M_3^0\), the ghost-free current states. In the completed ghost algebra \(\mathcal G\), let \(\widehat I_L\) be the closed left ideal generated by modes with index at least \(L\) and put \(F_L=\mathcal G/\widehat I_L\). A degree-\(n\) retained kernel assigns to each algebraic \(w\in V\) a compatible family
\[
T_L(w)\in(V\otimes F_L^n)((u)).
\]
These \(R_3\)-linear families form the retained kernel space \(\mathcal E^n_{\rm ret}\) for \(n\ge0\); put \(\mathcal E^n_{\rm ret}=0\) for \(n<0\). Their Laurent lower bound may depend on \(L\) and \(w\). The combined current–ghost differential \(\mathscr D\) on these kernels squares to zero. The ghost differential itself is evaluated at a larger cutoff before projection; the individual \(F_L\) are not thereby dg quotient algebras. A retained cochain has an additional state input: in degree \(j\) it belongs to
\[
\mathcal Q^{\mathrm{ret},j}
=\prod_{p\ge0}\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}
\bigl(M_3^p,\mathcal E^{p+j}_{\rm ret}\bigr),
\qquad
\delta\Psi=\mathscr D\Psi-(-1)^j\Psi d_M.
\]
There are two independent choices here. The inserted state \(x\in M_3^p\) chooses the kernel \(\Psi(x)\), of ghost degree \(p+j\). On a current \(w\in V\), its value at cutoff \(L\) has coefficients in \(V\otimes F_L^{p+j}\). A functional on its ghosts can then read a coefficient. The term \(-(-1)^j\Psi d_M\) in the Hom differential records the differential of the inserted state.

At every numerical \(K\), the dressed field gives a retained cocycle \(\mathcal Y_-\). Restricting the exchange homotopy gives a second cocycle \(\mathcal Y^\sharp\) with the opposite scalar field. Its polarization agrees with \(\mathbb Y_+\) up to a coboundary in the kernel of projection to ghost degree zero. A coefficient now acts on both sides of an insertion. Write \(A_{\rm cur}\) for the full smooth current algebra and \(A_{{\rm cur},R}\) for its right quotient at cutoff \(R\). A middle coefficient is a functional \(F_L^n\to A_{\rm cur}\). Keeping track of its order relative to the right coefficient gives the source
\[
N_{\rm rt}^{1-n}
=\varprojlim_R\operatorname*{colim}_{L\ge1}
\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}(F_L^n,A_{\rm cur})
 \otimes_{R_3,\mathrm{alg}}A_{{\rm cur},R}\right).
\]
For example, to pair \(f\otimes b\) with \(\Psi(x)\), act first by \(b\) on \(w\). In each Laurent coefficient of \(\Psi(x)_L(bw)\), a term \(v\otimes\gamma\) then gives \(f(\gamma)v\). The order places the right current before the kernel and the left current after ghost extraction. Source coefficients here are independent of \(u\). Each right quotient admits one middle ghost cutoff and finite right support in Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt monomials. Both may vary with the quotient. The recipient is the shifted scalar Hom complex
\[
Q_3=\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}
\bigl(M_3,\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}(V,V((u)))\bigr)[-1],
\]
with the pointwise Laurent topology in its two algebraic inputs. Its Laurent floor need not be uniform over all inputs. Homogeneity supplies the finite ghost box and Laurent floor needed for the pairing. The two evaluations \(E^\flat,E^\sharp:N_{\rm rt}\to Q_3\) retain both endpoint actions and both translation identities, and an actual bimodule homotopy joins them. At \(K=-2\) they are new rooted maps defined by the field, not an extension of the noncritical marked evaluation. [Retained kernels and rooted evaluations](#atlas-c-native-r3-retained-rooted-evaluations).

At a fixed \(K\ne-2\), including \(K=0\), let \(E_3\) be the prescribed pure-root evaluation, with retained cochain \(\Phi\in\mathcal Q^{\mathrm{ret},0}\), and put \(\Delta=\Phi-\mathcal Y^\sharp\). Their scalar kernels agree. Use the translation-compatible state retract with its full trace factor: if \(i\) and \(p\) are its inclusion and projection, its degree-minus-one homotopy \(k\) satisfies \(d_Mk+kd_M=1-ip\). Write \(\Delta_1\) for the restriction to inserted ghost degree one. The degree-minus-one retained cochain
\[
\kappa^{(1)}|_{M_3^2}=\Delta_1k|_{M_3^2},
\qquad
\kappa^{(1)}|_{M_3^p}=0\quad(p\ne2)
\]
has zero scalar pairing and obeys both translation laws. With \(\delta\) the retained Hom differential,
\[
\mathcal Y^{\sharp,1}=\mathcal Y^\sharp+\delta\kappa^{(1)}
\]
agrees with \(\Phi\) on inserted ghost degrees zero and one. The induced evaluation therefore agrees with \(E_3\) on the subcomplex of middle degrees at most one, and is globally homotopic to the evaluation defined by \(\mathcal Y^\sharp\) on \(N_{\rm rt}\). [The first rooted comparison](#atlas-c-native-r3-first-root-alignment).

The contraction explains why this correction exists. Its degree-one small image consists of trace-current polynomials times one trace ghost. The two kernels agree there, while the cocycle identity makes \(\Delta_1d=0\) on degree zero. The contraction identity then gives \(\Delta_1=\Delta_1kd\). The full trace factor is essential to this argument.

Agreement for inserted ghost degrees zero and one leaves the values on higher ghost monomials undetermined. The retract reduces that question to equality on its small complex, with the trace ghosts still present.

### A marked comparison in every retained degree {#ch17-all-retained-marked}

The short comparison tested inserted ghost degrees zero and one. Let the inserted state \(x\) now have any ghost degree, keeping the whole algebraic current state \(w\in V=M_3^0\) in the rooted slot. Denote the two bulk retained kernels by \(\mathcal Y_-\) and \(\mathcal Y^\sharp\) and their exchange homotopy by \(h^\Lambda\), so that \(\delta h^\Lambda=\mathcal Y^\sharp-\mathcal Y_-\). At a fixed noncritical level \(K\ne-2\) the maps \(i,p,k\) contract the state complex onto the small complex and act as the identity on its trace factor. On that complex the prescribed pure-root kernel \(\Phi\) agrees with \(\mathcal Y_-\). This agreement holds as an operator on every such \(w\). On the cubic generator \(\omega_\hbar\) it follows from invariance of its leading term. On \(q_\hbar\) the current mode of index one differentiates the shifted exchange kernel and supplies the order-two term. Multiplication by a trace-current polynomial and insertion of a trace-ghost monomial preserve both equalities. With \(\Delta=\Phi-\mathcal Y^\sharp\), put
\[
K_{\rm rt}=\Delta k-h^\Lambda ip .
\]
The first term has differential \(\Delta(1-ip)\). The second has differential \((\mathcal Y_--\mathcal Y^\sharp)ip=\Delta ip\). Adding the two terms gives \(\delta K_{\rm rt}=\Delta(1-ip)+\Delta ip=\Delta\). Thus the comparison holds in every inserted degree as a kernel on arbitrary algebraic rooted currents. Its scalar part vanishes because \(\Delta_0=0\) and the degree-one small states have only a trace ghost, paired with a nonnegative trace current which annihilates the rooted input. The homotopy satisfies both translation identities.

A marked coefficient can itself be a Laurent series. At each fixed right quotient and algebraic inserted input require one lower Laurent bound, one ghost cutoff and a common finite set of right current monomials for the whole series. For a series starting at exponent \(\nu\), let \(T_0\) bound the exponents of the retained kernel from below on this finite list of right translates of the rooted state. At output exponent \(t\) the convolution uses only \(\nu\leq j\leq t-T_0\). Homogeneity bounds the ghost indices in each of those coefficients. This supplies the continuity estimate which would fail if new right-current orders or unbounded cutoffs could enter at every coefficient.

On the trace-only inserted summand extend the rooted homotopy by contraction with the dual trace monomials. Each fixed algebraic input meets only finitely many.

On the positive traceless inserted summand the two evaluations use the same prescribed state operation \(\mathcal J\) and Casimir correction \(\Xi\). This gives a homotopy on the indicated whole-series stages of the restricted marked source. The Cartan coordinate isomorphism carries it to the resolved source. On the original unsimplified source one may precompose with the specified current projection. The algebraic homotopy uses the equality of these prescriptions on that summand. A physical junction must compute \(\mathcal J+\Xi\) in each spatial order from its own inputs. This includes the relative maps for a unit in the rooted input, the inserted input and the output, with their prescribed homotopies, as well as the tensorator. ### The endpoint obstruction has a specified acting algebra {#ch17-native-endpoint-obstruction}

In the retained pairing a ghost functional reads the output of a field. Consider the converse proposal. A marking is now to produce a two-input field whose scalar output can be measured after the fact. Such a field accepts both state inputs and has its own ghost-valued output. To recover the marked scalar operation, pass to the ghost-free quotient in the second input and in the output. This prescription is well defined precisely when a change of representative by a positive-ghost second input changes the output only in positive ghost degree.

**Definition (scalar corner).** Put \(F^1M_3=M_3^{\ge1}\) and identify \(M_3/F^1M_3\) with the ghost-free state space \(V\) with zero differential. Let \(\mathcal B\) consist of two-input Laurent cochains \(G:M_3\otimes M_3\to M_3((u))\) whose output lies in \((F^1M_3)((u))\) whenever the second input lies in \(F^1M_3\). Passing to the quotient on this rooted input and on the output gives the corner map
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal K\longrightarrow\mathcal B
\xrightarrow{\;\pi\;}
\mathcal C=\operatorname{Hom}_{R_3}(M_3\otimes V,V((u)))
\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C=Q_3[1]\), and \(\mathcal K\) consists of cochains whose every output has positive ghost degree. The sequence is exact and splits on graded modules. A chain section would have to lift every closed scalar cochain. The raising current below gives a scalar cochain for which this fails. Write \(W_-,W_+\) for the two scalar fields obtained by applying \(\pi\) to the ordered bulk fields. Each of these individual fields *does* have a closed lift.

For the endpoint one must lift a map on a whole module. Let \(A_3\) act on \(V\) by its prescribed current action, and put
\[
 A=\operatorname{End}^{\ge0}_{R_3}(M_3)
 \times_{\operatorname{End}_{R_3}(V)}A_3,\qquad q:A\longrightarrow A_3.
\]
An element of this differential graded fibre product consists of an endomorphism of nonnegative ghost degree and a current coefficient with the same induced action on \(M_3/F^1M_3\). On a two-input cochain the left action is on its output and the right action is on its rooted argument, with the Koszul sign. Both preserve the corner sequence. On the shifted marked source \(\mathcal N_3[1]\), let both actions factor through \(q\). This choice is called inflation along \(q\).

**Proposition (obstruction for the inflated endpoint).** At every fixed \(K\ne-2\) through \(R_3\), let \(E_3,\overline E_3\) be the two ordered marked evaluations. For \(A^e=A\otimes_{R_3}A^{\rm op}\), write \(\beta_A:\mathcal C\to\mathcal K[1]\) for the connecting morphism of the corner sequence. Then
\[
\beta_AE_3[1]=\beta_A\overline E_3[1]\ne0
\quad\text{in}\quad
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(A^e)}
\bigl(\mathcal N_3[1],\mathcal K[1]\bigr).
\]

*Proof.* The two evaluations are homotopic as marked bimodule maps, hence have the same connecting class. To detect it, take the closed trace creator \(c\) in \(\ker q\), with \(c^2=0\). The source action factors through \(q\), so \(c\) annihilates the class of the marked unit. Evaluate that unit in the scalar corner and let \(G\) be any closed bulk lift of the resulting normalized class. Evaluating \(G\) and then \(cG\) on the two vacua gives
\[
G(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)=\mathbf1,\qquad
[cG](\mathbf1,\mathbf1)=[\xi^I_0]\ne0 .
\]
An equivariant lift would send \(c\) applied to the unit to \(c\) applied to \(G\), yet the first class is zero and the second is the nonzero trace-ghost class displayed above. Restriction to the exterior algebra on \(c\) detects a split free cyclic \(R_3\)-submodule. A derived module morphism and the linear part of a standard uncurved \(A_\infty\)-module map both induce module maps on cohomology. Exactness of the corner triangle therefore gives the nonzero connecting class, even after an equivalent resolution of the source. [The endpoint obstruction](#atlas-c-native-quotient-inflated-endpoint-obstruction) ∎

The same vacuum evaluation detects more than one creator. At every numerical \(K\), for a closed \(G\in\mathcal B^0\) with \([\pi G]=[W_\pm]\), multiplication of the output by exterior trace monomials gives a split copy of \(\Lambda_{R_3}(\xi^I_0,\xi^I_1,\ldots)\) in \(H^\bullet(\mathcal B)\). Its positive-degree part splits into \(H^\bullet(\mathcal K)\). This is a splitting of graded modules. Suppose a differential graded algebra preserves this corner sequence and contains a closed degree-one element acting on outputs by a trace ghost. If a module map \(S\to\mathcal C\) carries a degree-zero cycle to \([W_\pm]\) and \(H^1(S)=0\), the same argument obstructs a derived or standard uncurved \(A_\infty\)-module lift. A source with nonzero \(H^1\) still has to provide the correct action on its unit. [Trace classes in the corner](#atlas-c-native-r3-trace-classes-source-obstruction)

Suppose a source with closed unit \(e\) carries the exterior algebra on the trace creators \(c_j\). A normalized endpoint intertwining these actions with multiplication by \(i_j=\xi^I_j\) makes \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha[e]\) a split injection on cohomology. A left inverse evaluates the endpoint on the two vacua, takes the regular coefficient and projects to the pure trace ghosts.

The split injection detects an exterior orbit. To identify a particular generator of that orbit, keep the trace currents and translation fixed and ask which changes of the first ghost could escape a measurement at lower precision. On the algebraic trace factor \(d=0\), and the weights of \(I_m,i_j,\hbar\) are \(m,j,-1\). An \(R_3\)-linear algebra automorphism \(U\) fixing the trace currents, commuting with \(T\), preserving ghost degree and adjusted weight, and equal to the identity modulo \(\hbar^2\), is uniquely given by
\[
 U(i_0)=i_0+\hbar^2w,\qquad
 w=\sum_{j=1}^4\lambda_j b_j,\qquad
 (b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)=(i_2,I_1i_1,I_2i_0,I_1^2i_0).
\]
These are all the monomials of ghost degree one and weight two. Translation gives \(U(i_j)=i_j+\hbar^2T^jw/j!\). Every choice of \(\lambda_j\in\mathbb C\) extends to the free supercommutative trace algebra and has inverse given by \(-w\).

At every numerical \(K\) there is a corrected closed traceless cubic ghost \(\Omega_K\) with coefficient one on \(\omega=h_0e_0f_0\). Take the closed inputs \(I_1U(i_0)\) and \(I_1\Omega_K\). In the regular product with the trace ghost first, let \(m_*\) be twice the coefficient of \(I_1^2i_0\omega\), and \(m_j\) the coefficient of \(I_1^2b_j\omega\). The two orders give
\[
 (m_*,m_1,\ldots,m_4)=\pm(2,\hbar^2\lambda_1,\ldots,\hbar^2\lambda_4).
\]
The trace factors multiply, and reversing the two odd inputs gives the minus sign. Each coefficient descends to cohomology. At its fixed trace monomial the classical traceless differential into the weight-zero top ghost vanishes, and higher terms would require negative input weight. In either orientation the \(\hbar^2\)-coefficients of \(2m_j/m_*\) recover the four \(\lambda_j\), even with a common invertible cup factor. For the even insertion \(I_1U(i_0i_1)\) the two orders have the same sign. The correction to \(i_0i_1\) is \(\hbar^2(wi_1+i_0Tw)\), whose coefficients on \(i_0i_3,i_1i_2\) are \(3\hbar^2\lambda_1,-\hbar^2\lambda_1\). An independently named creator acting on the unit by \(i_0\) forces all four \(\lambda_j\) to vanish. A physical determination requires its own marked preparation, output functionals, and absolute cup and cap on that pair.

There is an obstruction already after forgetting the bimodule action on a different pair of marked inputs. At noncritical level write \(e\) for the scalar mark and \(a_E=J_E(-1)\) for the creation mode of the raising current. Both \(a_E e\) and \(e a_E\) are degree-zero cycles of the prescribed marked source. For either ordering the constant Laurent coefficient of the corresponding scalar-corner evaluation at the two vacua is the degree-zero state \(v_E=a_E\mathbf1\). Boundaries in the corner cannot change this vacuum value. Any closed bulk lift would therefore require \(d_Mv_E=0\). If \(E_1,H_1\) denote negative-current states and \(h_0,f_0,f_1\) the corresponding ghost modes, its classical part is
\[
d_Mv_E\bmod\hbar=2E_1h_0-H_1f_0+Kf_1 .
\]
The coefficient of \(E_1h_0\) is two, independently of \(K\). With the prescribed source differential, even an underlying chain map would send a closed marked current to a state with nonzero differential. Changing the trace frame leaves this traceless term untouched. A different source differential must account for this term on the marked insertion.

The retained pairing changes the role of the ghost. If \(f\) is a functional on middle ghost coefficients, multiplication by \(c\) on a retained output pairs with the functional \(\gamma\mapsto f(c\gamma)\). On the vacuum functional this contraction vanishes. The same current \(a_E\) may act as a coefficient on an input to the retained kernel. These formulas give a pairing with a different variance from the excluded covariant lift.

At noncritical level the marked endpoint-bilinear homotopy restores the reversed scalar field, both Ward laws, the prescribed minimal pairings and the comparison diagrams for the Cartan coordinates, the current projection and the proper unit-relative mapping cone. A scalar correction aligns the two scalar homotopies while preserving translation. The two scalar fields in the corner therefore have zero connecting class. It was the lift of an entire map of marked bimodules with its inflated action that failed the creator test. A marked preparation can distinguish these requirements by applying \(c\) before or after the proposed lift. A physical adjunction would still have to realize its right adjoint and counit, both ordered products and their tensorator on whole coefficient series with the bounds of the [marked comparison](#ch17-all-retained-marked). [The marked endpoint homotopy](#atlas-c-native-r3-endpoint-bilinear-homotopy)

## A bulk action and a universal centre {#bulk-action-and-centre}

In the ordinary associative case, Hochschild cochains
\[
C^\bullet(B,B)=R\operatorname{Hom}_{B\otimes B^{\mathrm{op}}}(B,B)
\]
act coherently on derived \(B\)-modules. For example a bimodule morphism \(B\to B[n]\) induces \(M\simeq B\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}M\to M[n]\) for each \(M\). In the chiral case an action also has restrictions to configuration opens, factorization products, a vacuum and homotopies on collision strata. One must specify that action functor before asking for its representing object.

On the affine line, take \(C\) to be the Chevalley coalgebra of \(kp_1\oplus kp_2\), with \(p_ip_j=\delta_{ij}p_i\). Let \(m\) be its multiplication at the same configuration and \(u_B:J\to C\) its unit. Here \(J\) is the unit for the same-configuration tensor \(\otimes^!\). A pointed factorizing coalgebra \((F,u:J\to F)\) can parametrize operations on \(C\). A character is a coalgebra map \(g:F\to C\) with a path \(gu\simeq u_B\). An action is a map of coalgebras \(\alpha:F\otimes^! C\to C\) with a path \(\alpha(u\otimes^!1_C)\simeq1_C\). Write \(\mathcal X(F,u)\) and \(\mathcal A_C(F,u)\) for their spaces, retaining the paths and higher homotopies. Multiplying by \(g\) gives an action. Inserting \(u_B\) in the \(C\)-input of \(\alpha\) gives a character. These operations define
\[
\Theta:\mathcal X(F,u)\longrightarrow\mathcal A_C(F,u),
\qquad
\Psi:\mathcal A_C(F,u)\longrightarrow\mathcal X(F,u),
\qquad \Psi\Theta\simeq1.
\tag{17.7}
\]
Starting with \(g\), evaluate the action it defines on the boundary vacuum. The unit identity returns \(g\). Starting instead with \(\alpha\), that evaluation retains only \(\alpha(-,u_B)\). For another boundary input \(b\), the reconstructed action sends \((f,b)\) to \(m(\alpha(f,u_B),b)\). Recognition requires a homotopy from this reconstructed action to \(\alpha\) on all inputs \(b\). Such a homotopy gives \(\Theta\Psi\simeq1\) if it is natural in the configuration and compatible with collisions and refinements. Recognition holds for arbitrary pointings on genuinely unital Chevalley coalgebras, their specified module extensions, and the nonunital families covered by the horizontal-representative or commutative-connection theorems. For arbitrary coassociative actors the specified three-block boundary has a filling. The comparison of two mixed primitives on \(z_0\ne z_1\) uses a coherent cocommutative structure on the actor and is proved on that separated locus. [Pointed actions](07-actions-and-centres.md#ch07-a-factorizing-actor-and-its-pointing) · [The collision restrictions](07-actions-and-centres.md#ch07-the-remaining-comparison)

A chiral centre representing this action functor would carry a universal action from which every admitted action is pulled back. Existence depends on the boundary. For the full category of pointed factorizing actions on the Chevalley coalgebra of the nonzero-level Heisenberg boundary, two maps built from exterior actors and the derivation \(\partial_{p_1}\) remain distinct after every map out of their target. The vacuum would force the corresponding degree-zero operation to be a module map. Differentiation in \(p_1\) cannot be linear over multiplication by \(p_1\), since their commutator is the identity. A terminal action would equalize them up to homotopy. This rules out a representing action for that particular functor. For a boundary whose action functor *is* represented by \(Z_{\mathrm{ch}}\), a physical reconstruction still needs a morphism \(r:\mathcal O_{\mathrm{bulk}}\to Z_{\mathrm{ch}}\) inducing its independently defined supported bulk insertions, products and restrictions. [The Heisenberg obstruction](07-actions-and-centres.md#ch07-the-remaining-comparison)

There is a reconstruction theorem with different input. Fix an ordinary unital chiral algebra \(A\) on a smooth separated complex curve \(X\). Take its complete coefficient-family categories \(\mathcal C(A)\) over classical parameter schemes, with the functor \(F\) evaluating their vacuum coefficients. For \(j:X^2\setminus\Delta\hookrightarrow X^2\), the two-point coefficient identifies fields on the separated locus, extends them across the diagonal, and there identifies the collision coefficient. Taking the diagonal residue between the two identifications recovers \(j_*j^*(A\boxtimes A)\to\Delta_*A\). This gives \(\operatorname{Rec}(\mathcal C(A),F)\cong A\) with ordinary base change for fixed \(A\). Applying this to derived parameter families or to independent bulk observables would require a comparison on those inputs. [The ordinary categorical round trip](13-geometric-reconstruction.md#ch13-the-ordinary-categorical-round-trip)

## States and amplitudes impose another comparison {#states-lines-and-caps}

An action tells us what an insertion does to a state. To put a number on that action we must also specify the state and its normalization. In a finite Gaussian integral with \(Z(1)\ne0\), dividing by the integral of the empty insertion gives a functional \(\omega\) with \(\omega(1)=1\). Thus
\[
Z(O)=Z(1)\,\omega(O).
\tag{17.8}
\]
For example, doubling the Gaussian measure doubles \(Z(1)\) and \(Z(O)\) for every polynomial \(O\). Even knowledge of every normalized moment would leave this factor undetermined. In a family of fillings the empty-insertion amplitude can be a vector in a line. Comparing two fillings then requires an identification of their lines, and comparing transport in the family also requires their connections. The following solid-torus constructions supply the normalized state and its local vacuum normalization under different hypotheses.

On the solid torus of modulus \(0<|q|<1\), fix the metric and density, \(\kappa\ne0\) and \(\hbar>0\). Take a smooth integrable source \(\mu\) with zero normal trace in the small \(C^1\) inverse neighbourhood and supply a smooth Ward filling \(T_\mu\), based by \(T_0=0\) in families through zero. The normalized even fields on the continued finite cycles converge to a centered Radon Gaussian. The full odd and mixed moments obey the graded Wick rule. The probability law is on the even fields. [The normalized numerical Gaussian](11-states-and-lines.md#ch11-a-numerical-state-from-the-original-cycles)

The weighted coefficient heat construction gives a based local vacuum line with a nonzero finite-part section. It uses its own reflected realization, spectral gap, smooth source bounds and finite action integrals. In a finite spectral frame, the counterterm is added to the covariant connection because the response one-form enters that connection with a minus sign. The finite-part section retains a vacuum normalization on that family. A bare infinite determinant would require convergence, and global transport would require holonomy data. [The based heat line](11-states-and-lines.md#ch11-original-coefficient-heat-and-a-based-local-vacuum-line)

**Definition (cap).** A cap is a chosen filling \(M\) of a boundary, with fields, boundary conditions and normalization preparing a vector or functional in the boundary state space. When amplitudes take values in an anomaly line, specifying that line and its identification across the cut is part of the preparation.

For a bulk observable \(O\), an exact cap comparison would give
\[
Z_M^{\mathrm{bulk}}(O)
=Z_M^{\partial}\bigl(r_M(O)\bigr),
\tag{17.9}
\]
where \(r_M\) sends a bulk observable to a boundary observable on the cut. An isomorphism of amplitude lines lets one compare the two sides. The filling must determine \(Z_M^\partial\) independently. Gluing along a common boundary must intertwine its state-space pairing with the bulk integral, including the value on \(1\).

In the marked Gaussian half-space both the change of fields and a disk preparation can be computed. Fix numerical \(\hbar,\kappa\ne0\), \(k=\hbar\kappa\), the marking \(\zeta=+i\) and an interior collar profile of integral one. Use oriented transverse coordinate \(t\) and tangential complex coordinate \(z\). In the mixed presentation the tests for the two fields \(\Phi,\eta\) are partial forms in \(dt,d\bar z\). In abelian Chern–Simons theory they are full spatial forms. Every spatial form splits uniquely into a partial form and \(dz\) times a partial form. If \(s\) denotes suspension of the linear tests, the comparison is
\[
s(\alpha e_\Phi)\longmapsto-\kappa^{-1}dz\wedge\alpha,\qquad
s(\beta e_\eta)\longmapsto(-1)^{\deg\beta+1}\beta .
\]
The mixed boundary condition kills the tangential trace of \(\beta\), precisely the condition that the spatial form restrict to a multiple of \(dz\). The change of variables identifies the unary differentials. On a mixed Wick contraction the factor \(k\kappa^{-1}=\hbar\) gives the correct quantum coefficient with the signs from the displayed map. Thus the two complexes of regular compact kernels agree at every fixed field number, with disjoint products and the empty unit. On a proper oriented graph ray the one-form tested by the Wilson source is \(\eta-\kappa^{-1}\Phi\,dz\). The second term contributes when the ray moves tangentially. The ray comparison transports the specified Cauchy propagator, framing and outgoing subtraction with these tests. [The marked Gaussian change of variables](#atlas-c-hz-cs)

At level one, prepare a neutral pair of charges \((a,-a)\) at \((r,0)\), with \(a\) real and \(0<r<1\). Removing the outgoing mutual divergence in the specified framing leaves the scalar \(r^{-a^2}\) multiplying a coherent oscillator state. Independently couple the harmonic disk with its zero mode fixed to the real source \(2a\operatorname{Re}h(r)\) in its probability weight, where \(h(r)=\sum_{n\ge1} Z_n r^n/n\). Completing the square gives the complex amplitude \(Z_n\) mean \(ar^n\) and variance \(n\). This law equals the squared modulus of the ray wavefunction divided by its squared norm. For a real collar parameter \(0<q<1\), the normalised one-sided pairing of the holomorphic ray state is \(((1-r^2)/(1-qr^2))^{a^2}\). At each fixed \(r\) the ray scalar cancels from both normalized readings. Yet moving the insertion changes that scalar, with \(d_r\log(r^{-a^2})=-a^2\,dr/r\). A filling that agrees on the normalized disk law must still reproduce this dependence to agree on the amplitude. [A neutral ray pair and a sourced disk](11-states-and-lines.md#ch11-neutral-ray-pair)

At \(\hbar\kappa=1\), the boundary creation modes \(J_{-j}=p_j\) prepare \((p_1^2\pm p_2)/2\) from the vacuum. Both states have norm one and energy two in units of the first harmonic. Include their closed annular representatives into the collar. A unit-strength positive-height cochain probe removes one \(p_j\) by \(\partial_{p_j}\). The positive current mode is \(J_j=j\partial_{p_j}\), so for mode two the current has twice that probe's strength. The marked field operation \(O=J_{-1}^2J_2+J_{-2}J_1^2\) is \(2S\) on this shell, where \(S\) exchanges the two unit occupation vectors. The prepared states have sharp \(S\)-readings \(+1\) and \(-1\). On the independently prepared disk their single-mode laws agree. The joint reading \(2\operatorname{Re}(Z_1^2\bar Z_2)\) fluctuates and has means \(+2\) and \(-2\), equal to the expectations of \(O\). In the gravitational theory the Ward identity for ordinary zero-height insertions holds on the fixed finite stages. ## Independent realizations test what reconstruction preserves {#independent-realizations}

In the particle model of the M2 junction at zero Cherednik parameter, let \(W_\hbar\) be the Weyl algebra \([x,y]=\hbar\), with numerical \(\hbar\ne0\), and take \(N\ge1\). The flux fixes the central generator \(J(1)=N\) in the enveloping algebra \(U_N\) of its Lie algebra. A current \(J(f)\) acts on the particle algebra \(A_N=(W_\hbar^{\otimes N})^{S_N}\) by summing \(f\) over the particles. Joining two interfaces gives the derived tensor product \(A_N\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{U_N}A_N\). Multiplication is an isomorphism in degree zero, and \(\operatorname{Tor}^{U_N}_1(A_N,A_N)=0\). Yet capping with the scalar central-extension class \(\omega\) gives a split bimodule surjection \(\operatorname{Tor}^{U_N}_2(A_N,A_N)\twoheadrightarrow A_N\). This flux summand in degree \(-2\) prevents derived multiplication from being a quasi-isomorphism. [The junction and its flux class](14-branes.md#ch14-what-a-joined-interface-remembers)

Closing a boundary can discard a product even in degree zero. For the relative Donaldson–Thomas operator \(\widehat B^D_{\beta,d}(r)\) on compact K3 states, closing the cylinder gives \((\beta\cdot D)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}\widehat B^D_{\beta,d}(r)\) for \(\beta\cdot D\ne0\). The primitive generating series gives the inverse Igusa form with its stated normalization.

For the coefficient \((\beta,d,m)=(C,1,0)\) of a rational section \(C\), a three-boundary insertion on middle cohomology measures \(q(a,C)q(b,C)q(c,C)\), with \(q\) the intersection form. Choose isotropic \(u,v\) perpendicular to the section and fibre, with \(q(u,v)=1\). On three copies of \(v\) the section cubic is zero. Adding to the first-order product the cyclic Hochschild cocycle with paired cubic \(q(a,u)q(b,u)q(c,u)\) changes that reading to one. Its fibre row vanishes by perpendicularity and its multiplication traces vanish since \(q(u,u)=0\). Thus even the unclosed fibre-marked cylinder and these traces admit both readings. [The compact operator](15-hall-and-compact.md#ch15-relative-donaldsonthomas-theory-produces-an-operator-first) · [The section cubic](15-hall-and-compact.md#ch15-a-cyclic-product-that-all-these-traces-can-miss)

The arithmetic trace has a further restriction. Choose the exponential weight \(a>1/2\) of the arithmetic test algebra. On the theta quotient \(K_s\), for \(\tfrac12<s<\min(a,1)\), one has \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K_s}\Pi_{s,a}(f)=W(f)\) on the arithmetic test algebra. The ordinary positive representation \(\pi_{\rm crit}\) retains only real centered zeros \(w_\rho=-i(\rho-\tfrac12)\). It omits exactly \(\sum_{w_\rho\notin\mathbb R}m_\rho\widehat f(w_\rho)\), with \(m_\rho\) the multiplicity. Vanishing of that difference on all tests is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis. Preserving the normal derivatives of arithmetic jets imposes a further condition on any local field comparison. [The trace and the positive representation](16-arithmetic.md#ch16-a-positive-representation-and-its-exact-missing-remainder)

A local collision presents the same question before any trace is taken. Knowing the complex of fields away from the collision and the vector space at the collision still leaves the map that continues a field between them. For constructible complexes that datum can be written explicitly. Let \(i:\{x\}\hookrightarrow X\) be a closed stratum and \(j:U=X\setminus\{x\}\hookrightarrow X\) its open complement. Given a constructible complex \(L\) on \(U\) and a complex \(V\) at \(x\), an attaching morphism \(\alpha:V\to i^*Rj_*L\) determines the glued complex by the homotopy pullback
\[
Rj_*L\mathop{\times}^{h}_{i_*i^*Rj_*L} i_*V .
\]
Restriction to \(U\) recovers \(L\), and restriction to the point recovers \(V\). The first projection of the pullback induces precisely \(\alpha\) on that stalk. For the four charge-one rays at level two, take the clean continuation of the separated coefficient over proper collisions. At the deepest collision the degree-two link \(H^2(i^*Rj_*L)\) has dimension two. In the family of perverse quotients by a supported line, the image of the attaching map recovers that line. Its image distinguishes perverse extensions whose stalk and costalk dimensions all agree. For identical rays fix the coordinate frames and the positive half-exchange \(+i\). The permutation action on the link in degree two splits as the trivial character plus the sign character. Exactly their two lines in the projective family admit this equivariance. Ordinary inverse image from the coarse unordered space would require every stabilizer to act trivially on each stalk. Within this family that selects the quotient by the trivial line, the pullback of the downstairs middle extension. A physical collision still has to supply that descent condition or determine the attaching map by a different operation. [The four-point attachment](#atlas-c-cl-05)

These examples make three distinct demands on a physical reconstruction. At a collision the field theory must determine the map \(\alpha:V\to i^*Rj_*L\) on supported boundary actions. At an interface its two ordered products must be identified on spaces where the second product can actually be applied. At a filling it must supply (17.9) and the gluing law in the amplitude line. Contracting a K3 cylinder before varying its three boundary states would already lose a possible cubic reading.

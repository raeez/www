# The strongest remaining questions

Joining two circles by a pair of pants defines a product on their states. Does the algebra of that product determine the amplitude for closing a circle with a disk? Even a theory with only two possible fields on a connected surface distinguishes these questions.

::: {.example #ch18-example-cutting-a-two-label-surface title="cutting a two-label surface"}
Let a field on a compact oriented surface be a locally constant label \(1\) or \(2\). Fix dimensionless weights \(w_1=1\), \(w_2=s>0\). A connected component of Euler characteristic \(\chi\) carrying label \(i\) contributes \(w_i^\chi\). Multiply over components and sum over fields with the specified boundary labels. Under gluing one sums over matching labels on each cut circle. These label pairs are precisely the fields on the glued surface, and the Euler characteristics add because a circle has Euler characteristic zero.

Write \(v_i\) for the complex circle state with label \(i\). A pair of pants is connected and has Euler characteristic \(-1\), so \(v_i v_j=\delta_{ij}w_i^{-1}v_i\). In the basis \(e_i=w_i v_i\) this gives \(e_i e_j=\delta_{ij}e_i\). Thus for every \(s\) the circle algebra is \(A=\mathbb C e_1\oplus\mathbb C e_2\), with unit \(e_1+e_2\). Consider its right modules. Taking maps from the projective probe \(e_1A\) returns \(M e_1\) for any complex \(M\). Tensoring back with \(e_1A\) recovers precisely that summand of \(M\). For \(M=e_2A\) this counit is \(0\to e_2A\). Admitting the second probe gives \(M e_1\oplus M e_2=M\), with both projections and all module maps, for every \(s\).

A disk has Euler characteristic one. Its incoming amplitude in the label basis is \(w_i\), so its functional in the idempotent basis is \(\varepsilon_s(e_i)=w_i^2\). Direct summation over the fields on a sphere gives \(1+s^2\). Cutting the sphere into two disks gives the same number by summing \(w_i w_i\) over their common label.

An uncut cylinder has Euler characteristic zero. It carries each label to itself with amplitude one. Present the same cylinder by gluing the pairing \(\beta_s(a,b)=\varepsilon_s(ab)\) on two incoming circles to the inverse pairing on two outgoing circles, along one circle of each. In the idempotent basis the inverse pairing is \(C_s=e_1\otimes e_1+s^{-2}e_2\otimes e_2\). Use instead the inverse pairing \(C_t\) of a theory with weight \(t>0\), identifying the two idempotent bases. The resulting cylinder sends \(e_1\) to \(e_1\), whereas
\[
   \bigl(\beta_s(e_2,-)\otimes 1\bigr) C_t
       =\frac{s^2}{t^2}\,e_2 .
\]
The first probe would accept this cut for every \(t\). The second enforces \(t=s\). The disk sum fixes the value of \(s\) in each theory. [J. Kock, *Frobenius algebras and 2D topological quantum field theories*, short version, §§1.1.8 and 2.1.4](https://mat.uab.es/~kock/TQFT/FS.pdf#page=8)
:::

For chiral algebras a round trip also starts with specified operations. Fix an ordinary unital chiral algebra \(A\) on a smooth separated complex curve. From its complete coefficient families over classical parameter schemes and their vacuum evaluation, one reconstructs the two-point coefficient and takes its diagonal residue. This recovers the original chiral product. A physical comparison starts instead with fields prepared on the other side of a cut. These must supply a map to the reconstructed operations on their actual supports. At a collision that map can be tested even when all dimensions agree. [The ordinary categorical round trip](13-geometric-reconstruction.md#ch13-the-ordinary-categorical-round-trip)

::: {.example #ch18-four-ray-attachment title="four rays at total collision"}
Bring four charge-one rays together at level two, with their coordinate frames fixed. On the transverse slice \(X=\mathbb C^3\) the outgoing charge-four coefficient at distinct positions is a rank-one local system. A full turn of a block of \(m\) rays has exponent \(\binom m2/2\) in units of \(2\pi i\). Thus a pair or triple has multiplier \(-1\), whereas all four together have multiplier \(+1\). On a proper normal circle the coefficient differential is monodromy minus the identity, up to orientation. Multiplication by \(-2\) is invertible. For two disjoint pairs there are two independent rotations on the same fibre. Either circle differential contracts the cochains, even though rotating both pairs together acts by \(+1\). In this way the link cochains with the stated coefficient are acyclic at every proper collision. On the punctured slice extension by zero therefore agrees with derived direct image. Call this clean extension \(L\), put \(j:X\setminus\{0\}\hookrightarrow X\), and let \(i\) include the origin. The link \(K=i^*Rj_*L\) records fields on a punctured neighbourhood of the total collision. Here \(V=H^2K\) has dimension two.

What can an observer determine by counting states at the collision? For every line \(W\subset V\), take the perverse quotient \(P_W=j_!L[3]/i_*W\). The shift is the complex dimension of the slice. Its stalk is \(W[1]\), and its costalk in degrees zero and one is \(V/W\) and \(H^3K\), respectively. The dimensions agree for every \(W\). The attaching map tells the extensions apart. For example, fix a basis of the two-dimensional \(V\) and choose each basis line in turn. A dimension counts one state in either stalk. Following that state out to the punctured neighbourhood tests which of the two lines it occupies. In degree \(-1\) the stalk maps into \(H^{-1}(K[3])=V\). The next arrow in the stalk–link–costalk sequence is the quotient \(V\to V/W\), so exactness identifies the image of the attaching map with \(W\). An automorphism of the separated rank-one coefficient acts by a scalar and fixes each line in \(V\). Thus two different lines give distinguishable extensions even with identical state counts.

Exchanging identical rays imposes a different condition. Fix the positive half-exchange as \(+i\). Permutation equivariance leaves the trivial and sign lines. Ordinary pullback from the coarse unordered space requires the stabilizer at the collision to act trivially on the stalk and selects the quotient by the trivial line. Within this family there is a unique answer under that additional hypothesis. [The four-ray calculation](04-factorization.md#ch04-gaussian-collision)

The Gaussian theory computes an attaching map for one measured coefficient. Work at finite field number with the four rays vertical on the support of the compact spacetime tests and with the prescribed outgoing subtraction. On the unit, Wick contraction gives \(\prod_{a<b}(z_a-z_b)^{1/2}\) on the chosen branch, where the \(z_a\) are the ray positions. On the real oriented resolution of the collisions, divide this scalar by \(\prod_A r_A^{\binom{|A|}{2}/2}\), where \(r_A\) is the positive normal radius of cluster \(A\). The remaining branch \(\Phi\) retains its angular phase. At total collision it multiplies the coincident charge-four Wick functional. Write \(G\) for the translated Wick functional normalized by \(G(\mathbf1)=1\). Including \(-d\log\Phi\) in the flat transport of test functionals makes \(\Phi G\) horizontal, and \(\psi\mapsto\psi(\mathbf1)G\) projects onto its line. Allowing smooth position forms to take unrestricted boundary values gives the summand \(Rj_*L[3]\) after proper direct image. Its stalk attaches to the link by the identity \(K[3]\to K[3]\), and its point costalk is zero. In degree \(-1\) its image is all of \(V\). [The absolute Wick line](reference.html#s-four-ray-absolute-wick-line)

For the full Batalin–Vilkovisky complex of supported bulk and boundary cochains, the attaching map must also act on coincident incoming distributions, respect their position supports and agree with physical sewing. That comparison remains open. One can ask for an equally discriminating operation at an interface. Carry two independently prepared modules across a defect, first separately and then joined. At a filling, cut a second time and pair the prepared states, keeping their vacuum amplitude in its line. Full faithfulness tests the maps between transported modules. Joining the modules tests their product. The empty insertion tests the normalization of the cut. These comparisons use different inputs even for one boundary.

## The physical adjunction and its full domain {#problem-physical-adjunction}

For a simple complex Lie algebra normalize long roots to square length two. Let \(s\) be the shifted level, \(\ell=s-h^\vee\) the raw level of the affine mode algebra \(A_\ell\), and \(B=Y_{-s}(\mathfrak g)\) the polynomial differential graded line algebra. In a smooth module a sufficiently high current tail kills each given vector. Inducing from the trivial module for the strictly positive currents gives \(W\) with horizontal top \(U(\mathfrak g)\). Write \(\mathcal W\) for its specified coherent right-\(B\) realization. Transport a line module \(L\) across the prescribed interface, and compare its image with a smooth boundary module \(M\). A right adjoint would describe each map from that transported line to \(M\) by a map from \(L\) on the original side. The issue is whether the interface gives this correspondence on mapping complexes and whether the algebraic adjunction below computes it. [The affine model](17-holographic-reconstruction.md#affine-reconstruction-scope)

**Problem 18.1.** Construct the adjunction \(F_{\mathrm{phys}}\dashv G_{\mathrm{phys}}\) of the prescribed physical interface. After identifying its source and target, compare it with \(\Phi=\mathcal W\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}-:D(B)\rightleftarrows D(\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell)):\Psi=\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell)}(\mathcal W,-)\). Give a natural equivalence \(F_{\mathrm{phys}}\to\Phi\) and its right mate \(\Psi\to G_{\mathrm{phys}}\) on the original complexes. Determine the essential image and compare the tensorators for each of the two spatial orders.

Suppose the physical transport has been defined and, like \(\Phi\), is enriched, preserves colimits and lands in the chosen sector with compact generator. The free source here is the *left* \(B\)-module \(B\), whose endomorphisms are right multiplications. They induce a right \(B\)-action on its transported object. A comparison \(F_{\mathrm{phys}}(B)\simeq\mathcal W\) that intertwines this action coherently extends to every line module by its free resolution. The two ways of joining and transporting two lines have their own kernels. The physical interface must provide the map between them for each spatial order. [Operation kernels](17-holographic-reconstruction.md#ch17-proposition-operation-kernels) · [The two composite kernels](17-holographic-reconstruction.md#products-through-an-interface)

The algebraic unit is known for every simple complex Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\) at nonrational shifted level under the smooth realization hypotheses of Chapter 6. These require compatible cutoffs for the right action on the resolution, the prescribed comparison of smooth derived endomorphisms, and the tensor–Hom adjunction on mapping complexes. With \(R=U(\mathfrak g)\), positive-weight ideal \(I=\ker(B\to R)\) and \(D\) the number of positive roots, the first vanishing derived tensor power of \(I\) over \(R\) is precisely \(D+1\). The filtration by positive tensor factors therefore has at most \(D+1\) coefficient layers, even on a complex with unbounded cohomology. The unit is already invertible on the weight-zero layers, so the exact triangles give its invertibility on each ordinary unbounded source. This identifies the image as the localizing category generated by the module with regular horizontal top, including internal shifts in the graded convention.

At raw \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) level \(-3\), the weight-zero quotient kills a particular nonzero telescope on which the original unit is an equivalence. A classification of *all* sources there is open. Under the coherent smooth right action and adjunction, affine-free lift and cutoff hypotheses, there are \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) telescopes with nonzero source and zero affine realization at each raw level \(-p-2\) for \(p\ge2\) integral. In every simple type another such kernel occurs at \(-2h^\vee\) under the corresponding hypotheses. These are failures of the original unit on particular inputs. The all-level twisted adjunction in [Chapter 17](17-holographic-reconstruction.md#affine-reconstruction-scope) changes the level convention and the class of maps inverted on its source. [The original unit and counit](06-duality-and-induction.md#ch06-the-image-and-its-original-counit) · [Negative integral kernels](#atlas-c-induction-negative-integer-unit-kernel) · [All simple types at \(-2h^\vee\)](#atlas-c-induction-all-simple-negative-unit-kernel)

A single endpoint tests more than equality of vacuum values. Work with the four colours \(H,E,F,I\) of the \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\) current and their ghosts over \(R_3=\mathbb C[\hbar]/(\hbar^3)\). At fixed noncritical numerical level \(K\ne-2\), insert the closed trace creator \(c\) beside the two vacua. In the marked source with action inflated from the current quotient, \(c\) kills the unit. On a normalized bulk lift the vacuum pair gives \(\mathbf1\), and acting on the output gives \([c\mathbf1]=[\xi^I_0]\ne0\). A covariant lift for this action would have to identify these classes. Replacing the source by an equivalent resolution preserves the contradiction. A source intended to intertwine output creation must instead realize the trace-ghost action on its unit and the differential of its marked currents. [The endpoint calculation](17-holographic-reconstruction.md#ch17-native-endpoint-obstruction)

Keeping the ghost output until a coefficient functional pairs with it changes the map. Moving \(c\) to that functional contracts its argument. At fixed \(K\ne-2\) the discrepancy between the two normalized retained kernels is a coboundary in every inserted ghost degree, with a ghost-free algebraic current at the root. For marked Laurent coefficients the comparison uses one Laurent floor, one ghost cutoff and finite right support for the whole series at each input and quotient. On positive traceless inserted states the state and Casimir operations are prescribed on both sides. A physical theory must derive them from its two ordered products. At \(K=-2\) the ordered rooted fields still exist, and the critical state complex has a translation-compatible linear model with additional generators. The marked products on those generators require an independent comparison. [The marked comparison](17-holographic-reconstruction.md#ch17-all-retained-marked)

For a proposed physical adjunction, compare the unit and counit on the cut and then compare the maps taking two input modules to their joined output in each order. An interface constructed from geometric attaching maps must give the same maps on these preparations. At \(13\mid2\), the first and third positions meet while the second stays separated. Retain a positive traceless insertion, a trace creator and the first derivative normal to \(z_1=z_3\).

A separate three-ghost calculation tests how a first normal derivative constrains the product. Put primitive coadjoint colours for \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) in slots \(1,2,3\) at \(u,t,0\). With \(u\ne0\) fixed, \(t\to0\) brings the last two slots together. Fold them by translating the second colour through \(t\) before multiplying. The nonconsecutive \(13\mid2\) marking above has its own normal \(z_1-z_3\). For the invariant Casimir \(\Omega=H\otimes H/2+E\otimes F+F\otimes E\), put \(A=\Omega_{12}/(u-t)\) and \(B=\Omega_{13}/u\), expanding in \(t/u\). In the unflipped crossing word \(e^{\hbar A}e^{\hbar B}\), the first transverse jet forces the fold's equivariant first-order colour correction to be \(-\Omega/(2t)\) modulo scalars. Reversing the word forces \(+\Omega/(2t)\).

Matching this contact leaves a choice of binary product. At \(K=-1\), calibrate the scalar factor and spectral shift of the pairwise Casimir exponential to agree with the original product on every primitive colour pair in its specified order. Now test a single ghost \(h_0\) against the composite \(e_0f_0\), where \(h,e,f\) are dual to \(H,E,F\) and the subscript denotes the zero jet. Write \(\omega=h_0e_0f_0\). The coefficient of \(\hbar^2t^{-2}\) is \(5\omega\) in the original product and \(4\omega\) when the Casimir acts diagonally on the composite. On the exterior product the grouped Casimir has eigenvalue \(-4\), giving \(16/8+1+1=4\). The original product has two separate pair contributions and gives \(16/8+2(3/2)=5\). The closed insertion \(c_\omega=H[0]\wedge E[0]\wedge F[0]\) pairs the difference of these coefficients to \(+\mathrm{id}\) in either order. Since \(h_0\) is not closed, this tests the specified products on cochains. A physical comparison must also match both input vacua and the outgoing vacuum. [The marked actions and their inputs](07-actions-and-centres.md#ch07-marked-retained-action)

Determining the image even of the algebraic functor involves a choice of derived category. Write \(\mathscr C=\operatorname{Loc}(W)\), let \(\mathcal T\) be the abelian category of smooth modules in which a power of the positive-current augmentation ideal kills each given vector, and let \(D_{\mathcal T}(\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell))\) consist of complexes with cohomology in \(\mathcal T\). An object of \(D(\mathcal T)\) has a representative with torsion in every term. For an object of \(D_{\mathcal T}(\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell))\) the torsion condition is imposed only after taking cohomology. Passing from the latter condition to a termwise torsion representative, with its maps, is part of the question. Here a complex in the affine target is cellular if it belongs to the category generated by \(W\) under shifts, cones, coproducts and retracts, with internal shifts in the graded convention. How does the canonical functor \(D(\mathcal T)\to D_{\mathcal T}(\operatorname{Sm}(A_\ell))\) compare with \(\mathscr C\) inside the smooth derived category? Termwise torsion and bounded-below torsion cohomology give cellularity on their stated domains. For instance, write \(\mathbb C\langle u\rangle\) for the trivial positive-current module in energy \(u\) and put \(E_p=p(p+1)/2\). For \(t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t]\) take nonzero extension classes \(\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\langle-E_{p_j}\rangle[p_j]\) with \(2\le p_1<p_2<\cdots\). The homotopy inverse limit of the finite tensor products of their fibres is cellular. The cone of its intrinsic torsion counit splits as a direct sum of shifted cohomology modules. Shifted cells detect in that cone a product of extension spaces modulo their direct sum. Thus the intrinsic counit can fail on an object recovered by cellularization.

For the smooth positive-current algebra \(t\mathfrak{sl}_2[t]\), suppose a single tail \(t^L\mathfrak{sl}_2[t]\) annihilates every sufficiently low cohomology module. In the algebraic energy grading this makes the smooth-vector completion unit invertible. If the cohomology is jointly augmentation-torsion in every degree, the common tail gives cellularity in both the graded and ungraded categories. For graded cellularity it even suffices that the tail annihilate the part of the low cohomology in energies \(u\le a\) for one fixed \(a\). [The common-tail criterion](06-duality-and-induction.md#ch06-common-tail-completeness)

Without the common-tail hypothesis, must every nonzero complex with jointly torsion cohomology receive a nonzero map from some cohomological shift of a trivial module concentrated in a single energy? Over a field \(k\) of characteristic zero, put \(\mathfrak n=t\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)[t]\). Work in the unbounded derived category \(D(\mathcal S)\) of smooth modules with algebraic integer energy grading and energy-preserving maps. A mode \(xt^j\) lowers energy by \(j\). Write \(k\langle u\rangle\) for the trivial module in energy \(u\). Take the inverse limit \(X\) of the finite stages of the Koszul tower formed from the linear currents \(et^j\), with \(e,f,h\) the standard generators of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\). Its top column resolves a module in cohomological degree \(-1\), and a column indexed by a multiset of size \(r\) resolves a module in degree \(-r-2\). The product at a fixed energy consists of families killed by one common current tail. Every \(H^{-n}(X)\), \(n\ge1\), is nonzero and jointly augmentation-torsion. Two classes in \(H^{-1}(X)\) at energy \(-2\) project in the top column to the coefficient functionals dual to \(ft\) and \(ht\).

A map from \(k\langle u\rangle\) picks out an energy-\(u\) vector killed by every positive current. The tower is K-injective, so this subcomplex of \(X\) computes the derived maps from the cell. At each energy its signed Koszul faces pair to give a contraction. Hence \(\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathcal S)}(k\langle u\rangle,X[m])=0\) for all integers \(u,m\). Vanishing extends to the localizing subcategory generated by the cells because maps out of a coproduct form a product. If \(X\) belonged to that subcategory, its identity would vanish. Thus the cells generate a proper subcategory of the complexes with jointly augmentation-torsion cohomology. On this \(X\) both the derived torsion counit and the cellular counit have source zero. [The positive-current Koszul tower and its cell test](06-duality-and-induction.md#ch06-theorem-a-complex-orthogonal-to-all-positive-current-cells)

Over \(\mathbb C\) one can also test the ungraded trivial module against this same complex after forgetting energy. Let \(U\) forget the grading. Its right adjoint \(P\) puts a copy of an ungraded module in each integer energy, takes their algebraic direct sum, and lets \(xt^j\) act from sheet \(a\) to sheet \(a-j\). Both functors are exact. Grouping the vectors of \(PUX\) by the difference between their two energy labels identifies it with \(X\otimes_{\mathbb C}V\), where \(V=\bigoplus_{a\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb C\langle a\rangle\) has trivial current action and cohomological degree zero. Applying the fixed-tail cohomology calculation to the finite Koszul stages tensored with \(V\) identifies their smooth limit with \(X\otimes V\) in the derived category. Its complex of maps from \(\mathbb C\langle0\rangle\) is a product of the same finite face complexes, each with its contraction. By adjunction every ungraded map from \(\mathbb C\) to \(UX[m]\) therefore vanishes. Exact forgetting preserves the nonzero jointly torsion cohomology of \(X\), so generation fails in the ordinary ungraded smooth category as well. This test takes the graded smooth limit before forgetting energy.

Returning to the affine algebra changes the object. Over \(\mathbb C\) put \(U_+=U(\mathfrak n)\) and induce \(X\) to \(A_\ell\otimes_{U_+}X\). On restriction a current \(y\in\mathfrak n\) acts by \(y(a\otimes x)=[y,a]\otimes x+a\otimes yx\). The bracket on the affine factor includes the central term at level \(\ell\). At every complex raw level \(\ell\), degree-zero maps from \(\mathbb C\langle-2\rangle\) into this restriction have infinite-dimensional image on the top column. Degree-two maps vanish at every cell energy. A degree-one map survives at some energy precisely when \(\ell\) is an integer at most \(-6\). Even one nonzero degree-zero map rules out right-orthogonality to the cells. Whether the restricted induced object belongs to the subcategory they generate remains open. [The affine restriction test](06-duality-and-induction.md#eq-ch06-affine-positive-current-restriction)

A different tower gives a counterexample in the affine category itself. Normalize \((e,f)=1\), \((h,h)=2\), and write \(x_j=xt^j\). Let \(v\) generate the right vacuum killed by the nonpositive modes. For the quadratic element \(S_2=h_1^2/2+e_1f_1+f_1e_1\), the affine commutator gives \((vS_2)e_{-1}=2(\ell+2)ve_1\). At \(\ell=-2\) the commuting even-weight quadratics give singular vacuum vectors. Their transposes on the restricted graded dual of the right vacuum define a Koszul tower \(Z\) of smooth graded affine modules. Its restriction has jointly augmentation-torsion cohomology, with \(H^{-1}(\operatorname{Res}Z)\) equal to the graded dual of \(U_+\). All derived maps from the positive-current cells into \(\operatorname{Res}Z\) vanish. Induction–restriction adjunction therefore gives zero derived maps from every energy shift of \(W\) into \(Z\). This nonzero \(Z\) lies outside \(\mathscr C\). With long roots of squared length two, the quadratic Casimir gives the same strict inclusion for any finite-dimensional complex simple \(\mathfrak g\) at \(\ell=-h^\vee\). [The critical affine tower](06-duality-and-induction.md#ch06-theorem-a-critical-affine-complex-orthogonal-to-the-regular-top)

Does the regular top detect the critical tower after one forgets energy? Write \(Z_p\) for the finite stages and put \(Y_p=\operatorname{Res}Z_p\). Among the first \(N\) quadratics, only the first \(\min(N,K-1)\) act on the coefficients killed by \(t^K\mathfrak g[t]\). The Koszul calculation puts the cohomology of that branch in degrees from \(-N\) to \(\max(-N,1-K)\). For a fixed degree \(d\), choose \(1-K<d-2\). Adding sufficiently deep branches then leaves \(H^{d-1}\) and \(H^d\) unchanged in every energy at once. This bound still holds after tensoring the stages with the space \(V\) that has one trivial summand in every energy. Their smooth limit therefore represents \(P(U\operatorname{Res}Z)\). At each energy its complex of maps from the trivial cell is a product of finite face complexes. The contraction in each factor gives a contraction of their product. The sheet adjunction and then affine induction give \(\operatorname{Hom}(UW,UZ[m])=0\) for every integer \(m\) in the ordinary ungraded smooth affine derived category. Since \(H^{-1}(\operatorname{Res}Z)\) survives forgetting, the subcategory generated by \(UW\) is proper there too.

The same bound identifies \(U\operatorname{Res}Z\to\varprojlim_p UY_p\) up to quasi-isomorphism. Indeed, the exact right adjoint \(P\) preserves the limit and detects quasi-isomorphisms. The two cochain complexes have different support rules. In degree zero the ungraded limit contains a family of top exterior forms in energies \(-2r\), one in each branch indexed by \(r\) copies of the first quadratic. Their constant coefficients are all killed by the positive currents. A vector of the forgotten graded complex has only finitely many original energies. This comparison concerns the critical quadratic tower. At noncritical levels the unbounded affine generation question remains open.

## General chiral quantum groups and completed curved duality {#problem-chiral-quantum-groups}

Knowing which operators a single probe carries leaves open how they act on a pair. On the augmented polynomial algebra \(k[x,y,z]\) in characteristic zero give \(x,y\) weight one and \(z\) weight two. Keep \(x,y\) primitive, so either operator \(a=x,y\) acts on two probes by \(a\otimes1+1\otimes a\). One coproduct makes \(z\) primitive. A second adds \(x\otimes y\) to \(z\otimes1+1\otimes z\). Take two one-dimensional probes on which \((x,y,z)\) acts by \((1,0,0)\) and \((0,1,0)\), in that order. The first coproduct gives \(z\)-action zero on their tensor product. The second gives one. Reversing the probes changes the second reading to zero. Indeed \(\Delta(z)-\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(z)=x\otimes y-y\otimes x\) for the second coproduct, while the first is cocommutative. The multiplication and its associative bar complex agree in the two examples. On configurations the additional operation must say how fields in different output factors interact at the same positions. [Coproducts on a fixed algebra](12-chiral-quantum-groups.md#ch12-associative-products-on-configurations)

**Problem 18.2.** For an associative chiral algebra \(A\) on ordered configurations, construct its same-position product \(\diamond\) and a coproduct \(A\to A\diamond A\), or a level-indexed family of such morphisms. Require the coproduct to preserve the chiral \(E_1\)-multiplication, exchange, vacuum and iterated collisions. For the completed curved dual, specify the category of contramodules and the classes of morphisms inverted on both sides for which the bar adjunction becomes an equivalence.

The coproduct can be constructed when the input also specifies a flat Hopf algebra with bijective antipode, compatible translation, a unitary spectral form \(\rho_z\) with the specified Laurent values, and levels \(\eta_i\) preserving that form and translation. The product \(\boxtimes_\rho\) contracts fields across the output factors. Its level maps \(D_{\eta_1,\eta_2}:V_{\eta_1\eta_2}\to V_{\eta_1}\boxtimes_\rho V_{\eta_2}\) are coassociative with counit at the identity level. On their common expansions, moving a field past a product agrees with moving it successively past its two factors. This is the field hexagon. For the rational determinant-one double, the integral divided-state fields and exchange agree with the Etingof–Kazhdan quantum affine fields over \(\mathbb C[[\hbar]]\) in every rank \(N\ge2\) at every numerical level \(K\).

The hexagon compares two ways to move a field past a product. It presupposes the value of that product. What state is actually produced by colliding two insertions of the spin-three field in the principal \(W_3\) example for \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\)? In the universal \(W_3\) algebra at central charge \(c\), let \(L\) be the stress field, \(W\) the spin-three generator and \(T\) translation. For \(22+5c\ne0\), the coefficient of the double pole in \(W(z)W(w)\) is
\[
\frac{32}{22+5c}\left(:LL:-\frac3{10}T^2L\right)+\frac3{10}T^2L .
\]
The normal product \(:LL:\) creates a quadratic stress state. Transposing a presentation that contains it must retain derivatives in the products of the dual fields. For a universal algebra freely generated by finitely many even fields of half-integer weights at least one over a field of characteristic zero, homogeneous quadratic–linear–scalar brackets admit this derivative-corrected curved transpose.

At \(c=-22/5\) the displayed normalization of \(W\) is singular. Put \(a=(22+5c)/16\). Away from \(a=0\), the spin-three generator \(U\) of the regular universal algebra satisfies \(U=\sqrt a\,W\) with a compatible choice of sign. The two insertions multiply the displayed double pole by \(a\). Its coefficient becomes \(2:LL:+\tfrac3{10}(a-2)T^2L\), which specializes at \(a=0\) in the regular presentation.

To read that pole on the vacuum, write \(U(z)=\sum_m U_m z^{-m-3}\) and \(L(z)=\sum_m L_m z^{-m-2}\). The state of \(U\) is \(U_{-3}\mathbf1\). Acting on it by \(U_{-1}\) extracts the double pole, and \(U_{-1}\mathbf1=0\). The field \(:LL:\) creates \(L_{-2}^2\mathbf1\), while translating \(L\) twice creates \(2L_{-4}\mathbf1\). Thus at every finite \(c\),
\[
[U_{-1},U_{-3}]\mathbf1
=2L_{-2}^2\mathbf1+\tfrac35(a-2)L_{-4}\mathbf1 .

How much of the two-source response requires propagation through the cut? **Definition (connected Ward response).** For either preparation let \(Z_\ell(\alpha,\beta)\) be its vacuum amplitude at the two source strengths, with \(Z_\ell(0,0)\ne0\). Its connected response is
\[
 C_\ell=\left.\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta\log
 \frac{Z_\ell(\alpha,\beta)Z_\ell(0,0)}
      {Z_\ell(\alpha,0)Z_\ell(0,\beta)}\right|_{(0,0)} .
\]
Since the supports are disjoint, local scalar counterterms and separate normalizations of the halves cancel. Two uncoupled compact test planes give \(C_\ell=0\). A two-variable complex Gaussian with diagonal entries \(1+\alpha,1+\beta\) and common off-diagonal entry \(r\in(-1,1)\) has partition function proportional to \(((1+\alpha)(1+\beta)-r^2)^{-1}\). Differentiating the negative logarithm of this determinant gives \(C_\ell=r^2/(1-r^2)^2\), positive for \(r\ne0\). For the sewn preparation, let \(B\) be the fixed meridian pairing and let \(L_0,R_0\) be the two base states, with \(B_0=B(L_0,R_0)\ne0\). Writing primes for their variations with respect to the source on their own half, differentiation gives
\[
 C_\ell^{\rm sew}=\frac{B(L'_0,R'_0)}{B_0}
 -\frac{B(L'_0,R_0)B(L_0,R'_0)}{B_0^2}.
\]
The independent whole-filling calculation must give \(C_\ell^{\rm whole}\) from its own Green operator on the propagating fields and ghost partners, with the variations of its measure and domain. The difference under regulator removal tests sewing on this observable. An absolute cap also needs a fixed nonzero reference vector and phase. A line junction gives a cut with an algebra on either side. In the protected particle model fix an integer \(N\ge1\), numerical \(\hbar\ne0\) and zero Cherednik parameter. ## Arithmetic positivity and local operations {#problem-arithmetic}

On logarithmic scale \(u\), compose two smooth compact tests by convolution and put \(g^*(u)=\overline{g(-u)}\). Let \(\widehat f(z)=\int f(u)e^{izu}\,du\). The complete Weil functional \(W\) includes the prime powers in both orientations, its two polar terms and its real-place contribution. For a nontrivial zeta zero \(\rho\) of multiplicity \(m_\rho\), put \(w_\rho=-i(\rho-\tfrac12)\). In the explicit formula for \(W(g*g^*)\), a real \(w_\rho\) contributes \(m_\rho|\widehat g(w_\rho)|^2\). A nonreal one contributes \(m_\rho\widehat g(w_\rho)\overline{\widehat g(\bar w_\rho)}\). [The complete functional and the positivity criterion](16-arithmetic.md#ch16-the-complete-arithmetic-functional)

For \(a>1/2\), the convolution algebra \(\mathcal A_a\) consists of smooth functions whose derivatives of every order are integrable against \(e^{a|u|}\). Its closed ideal \(\mathcal J_a\) is generated by the normalized theta sums of even Schwartz tests with value and integral zero. In a Hilbert-space star representation with trace-class values, the trace on \(g*g^*\) would be the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the operator for \(g\). [The theta ideal and quotient](16-arithmetic.md#ch16-a-geometric-construction-of-the-full-trace)

**Problem 18.10.** Establish positivity of the complete Weil functional for the Riemann zeta function,
\[
W(g*g^*)\ge0\qquad\text{for every }g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C),
\]
or construct a continuous Hilbert-space \(*\)-representation \(\pi:\mathcal A_a/\mathcal J_a\to\mathfrak S_1(\mathcal H)\) with \(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H}\pi(f)=W(f)\) throughout the stated arithmetic test algebra.

The theta quotient gives the complete trace. On the quotient at \(\tfrac12<s<\min(a,1)\), a nonzero translation by \(t\) has spectral outer radius \(e^{s|t|}>1\). An equivalent Hilbert norm preserves this spectrum and cannot make that translation unitary. The positive representation \(\pi_{\rm crit}\) instead includes one copy of each real centered zero for each multiplicity layer. Its trace differs by
\[
W(f)-\operatorname{Tr}\pi_{\mathrm{crit}}(f)
=\sum_{w_\rho\notin\mathbb R}m_\rho\widehat f(w_\rho).
\tag{18.3}
\]
If every centered zero is real, the sum in (18.3) is empty. Conversely, one can isolate a nonreal spectral point by its transform values in the arithmetic test algebra. Its multiplicity would then give a nonzero remainder. Thus equality with this particular positive trace on every test is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis. For every complex \(L^2\) test supported in an interval of width at most \(31/10\), the full form already has a positive lower bound. The estimate includes both parities, the polar terms, the real place, the prime powers and the coupling to the omitted modes. On a larger window let \(A\) denote the finite block of the form for an orthogonal projection with range in the operator domain, \(K\) the coupling from it to the tail, and \(D\) the tail operator. Fix a vector \(u\) in the finite block and allow its tail \(v\) to vary. If \(D\ge dI\) for \(d>0\), the least value of the full form occurs at \(v=-D^{-1}Ku\). Substitution subtracts \(K^*D^{-1}K\) from \(A\). It follows that a lower bound for this Schur complement controls the whole window. Even a bound on the negative part of the whole-window infimum growing subexponentially along one sequence of half-widths tending to infinity would force every centred zero to be real. The separated zero packets in [the proof for unbounded windows](#ch16-proof-sparse-weil-windows) test every sufficiently large width. [The fixed-window bound and the off-block term](16-arithmetic.md#ch16-whole-window-positivity-and-the-off-block-term)

There is a second choice of tests. Fix the real, normalized compact bump \(\phi\) whose Fourier–Laplace transform has no zeros in the critical strip, constructed by exponential tilting of an infinite convolution independently of the zeta zeros. Put \(R_t\phi(u)=\phi(u-t)\), \(g_n=\phi+R_{\log n}\phi\) and \(Q(g)=W(g*g^*)\). Translation preserves each diagonal contribution, so \(Q(g_n)=2Q(\phi)+2\operatorname{Re}W(\phi^**R_{\log n}\phi)\). The cross term contains the prime-power contributions at these separations. Eventual nonnegativity of \(Q(g_n)\) for this one fixed shape is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis. An estimate in which the bump narrows as \(n\) grows changes the test. [The fixed shape and its translates](16-arithmetic.md#ch16-fixed-shape-translates)

An integral arithmetic point offers a local test before taking any trace. Fix a prime \(p\) and height \(h\ge1\). In the jet polynomial ring \(\mathbb Z_p[x_0,\ldots,x_{h-1}]\), \(R_h\) is the \(p\)-adic completion and \(B_h\) is the completion at the ideal \((p,x_i^p-x_i:i<h)\) of the rational residue locus. The map \(j_h:R_h\hookrightarrow B_h\) retains formal normal coordinates near each residue class. For the identity Frobenius lift on \(\mathbb Z_p\), an arithmetic point \(a\) has first coordinates \(x_0=a,\ x_1=(a-a^p)/p\) when \(h\ge2\). Substitution makes \(h_0=x_0^p+px_1-x_0\) vanish at every such point. The first normal derivation of congruence translation has \(D_0x_0=p,\ D_0x_1=-p x_0^{p-1}\), so \(D_0h_0=-p\), nonzero modulo \(p^2\). Scalar point values would identify this polynomial with zero before the derivative could act. [Integral points and normal operators](16-arithmetic.md#ch16-what-integral-arithmetic-points-forget)

**Problem 18.11.** At a prime \(p\) and finite jet height \(h\), compare the arithmetic collision complexes for \(j_h:R_h\hookrightarrow B_h\) with the collision maps of a local field algebra. The complexes use the factors \([A\to A[f_i^{-1}]]\) for the specified diagonal equations \(f_i\) at each finite precision, followed by \(p\)-adic completion. Require compatibility with the integral normal operators, residue maps, height-raising Frobenius \(\varphi_h:R_h\to R_{h+1}\) and vacuum. On the complex side, identify the common domain of trace-class operators on which the ordinary trace gives the Weil functional. Specify also the continuous jet pairings that detect nilpotent directions at a multiple zero. A comparison between these two coefficient theories must specify the changes of scalars it uses.

Two obstructions restrict the change of coefficients in Problem 18.11. For \(p=2\), use the integer lifts at the parent \(0\) and child \((0,1)\). On full complex formal disks the prescribed Frobenius would send \(z\) to \(u^2+2(1+v)\). This sends the unit \(z-2\) of \(\mathbb C[[z]]\) to the nonunit \(u^2+2v\). At the child \((0,0)\) the substitution has zero constant term and does define a map. A separate obstruction concerns norms. In a nonzero unital complex Banach algebra the relation \([D_0,X]=p1\) gives \([D_0,X^n]=npX^{n-1}\). All the powers of \(X\) are nonzero, by this same identity. Taking norms and cancelling \(\|X^{n-1}\|\) would give \(np\le2\|D_0\|\,\|X\|\) for every \(n\). On a nonzero Banach or Hilbert space any operator realisation retaining this relation must admit an unbounded operator, with a specified common invariant domain for its products. The integral normal derivative and its residue modulo \(p^2\) remain on the integral side.

At finite precision use the actual diagonal equations, change the coefficient ring along \(j_h\) before localization, and then take the \(p\)-adic inverse limit. The comparison is termwise injective. On forest-supported partial diagonals with no extra denominator the supported cohomology comparison is injective and preserves the ordered residues. On the complex side continuous integer-summation duality, the full theta trace and completed bar homotopies are available. The linking operators give a cyclic vector and nonlocal creation fields. To reach a local theory requires a locality identity on the specified coefficient spaces. The ordinary trace counts multiplicity, while the dual jet functionals can detect its nilpotent directions. The graded trace of the theta complex is defined on its proper trace-class ideal. On the integral side, let \(M_f\) denote multiplication by \(f\in B_h\). A \(\mathbb Z_p\)-linear cyclic trace \(\tau\) kills that commutator. Its nonzero integer multiplier is a power of \(p\) times a unit of \(\mathbb Z_p\). In a \(p\)-torsionfree target this forces \(\tau(M_fD)=0\). The products \(M_fD\) span the algebra generated by the multiplications and all finite-weight normal operators, so \(\tau\) vanishes on the whole algebra. If the finite sums are dense in an operator completion, continuity gives the same conclusion for such a trace with Hausdorff target. Finite weighted evaluations at arithmetic marks give cyclic traces on the commutative coefficient algebra. On the stated forest-supported modules one takes the ordered residue before applying those evaluations. A local field realization must still exhibit a common trace domain after change of scalars.

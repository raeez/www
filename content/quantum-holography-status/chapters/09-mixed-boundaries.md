# Ordered boundary operations and mixed theories

Place two boundary insertions at relative complex position \(z=z_1-z_2\) and height \(t=t_2-t_1\). Suppose the complex pole is present only when the second insertion is higher. Its ordered residue is \(H(t)\,\bar\partial\operatorname{PV}(1/z)\), where \(H=\mathbf1_{t>0}\) and \(\operatorname{PV}(1/z)\) is the principal-value extension of \(1/z\). Normalize the complex residue by \(\bar\partial\operatorname{PV}(1/z)=\pi\delta_{\mathbb C}(z)\,d\bar z\).

Use a transverse test equal to one near the complex pole. A smooth height test \(\varphi\) of integral one supported in \(t>0\) detects the residue with coefficient \(\pi\), even though the two insertions never meet. Apply the same test to the height derivative of the ordered residue. Pairing the derivative of \(H\) against \(\varphi\) gives \(-\int_0^\infty\varphi'(t)\,dt=\varphi(0)\). The support of \(\varphi\) lies above the wall, so \(\varphi(0)=0\). The same test detects the ordered residue and gives zero on its height derivative. A compact test equal to one at zero instead gives the coefficient \(\pi\) of the oriented contact
\[
(d_t+\bar\partial)\bigl[H(t)\,\bar\partial\operatorname{PV}(1/z)\bigr]
=\pi\delta(t)\delta_{\mathbb C}(z)\,dt\wedge d\bar z .
\]
The product of the two deltas is defined because the height and complex coordinate are independent. In a product of fields the Ward identity compares the differential of the product with the differentials of its inputs. A calculation in the open height chamber can determine the ordered pole and still miss the term that identity carries on the wall.

## A boundary current from a mixed field theory {#ch09-a-boundary-current-from-a-mixed-field-theory}

The chamber test fixed the support of the pole. What fixes its strength for two actual fields? On a half-space the boundary condition decides which field we may insert, and the quadratic action determines its contraction. Take the abelian theory on \(M=\mathbb C_z\times\mathbb R_{\geq0,t}\) with boundary \(t=0\). Separate the two differentials

\[
d'=\bar\partial+d_t,\qquad \partial=dz\wedge\partial_z.
\]

A holomorphic–topological theory is holomorphic in \(z\) and topological in the real direction. In its cohomological description, the antiholomorphic and real derivatives enter \(d'\); holomorphic derivatives remain genuine operations.

An abelian example has fields \((\alpha,\beta)\), where \(\alpha\) is a form generated by \(d\bar z,dt\), while \(\beta\) has one additional factor \(dz\). An ordinary form of degree \(p\) in either summand has cohomological degree \(p-1\). The differential is

\[
Q_\kappa(\alpha,\beta)
 =\bigl(d'\alpha,d'\beta+\kappa\partial\alpha\bigr).
\]

Since \(d'\partial+\partial d'=0\), its square is zero for every \(\kappa\). In cohomological degree zero, \(a\) has a \(d\bar z\) component and a \(dt\) component. It is a partial connection in precisely these two directions. The other field \(b\) has type \((1,0)\) along the complex curve. Their quadratic action density is

\[
\mathcal L_\kappa=b\wedge d'a+\frac{\kappa}{2}a\wedge\partial a.
\]

At \(\kappa=0\), this is the holomorphic BF presentation: the field \(b\) multiplies the partial curvature \(F=d'a\).

The integrated action requires compact support. The Batalin–Vilkovisky pairing pairs the two field summands by wedge product and integration and is independent of \(\kappa\). This pairing includes the odd ghost and antifield components of the complex; retaining only \(a,b\) would omit the differential needed for the quantum identities.

Let \(\iota\) be the inclusion of \(t=0\). Impose the boundary condition \(\iota^*\alpha=0\) and leave the tangential value of \(\beta\) free. This sets the tangential part of the first field to zero while allowing a boundary current from the second. A compactly supported contraction in the normal direction identifies the boundary observables with their supported bulk realization. This is an instance of the free bulk–boundary construction of Gwilliam, Rabinovich and Williams, [*Factorization algebras and abelian CS/WZW-type correspondences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07888). With residue integration normalized by \((2\pi i)^{-1}\int\), the resulting current has singular product

\[
J(z)J(w)\sim-\frac{\hbar\kappa}{(z-w)^2}.
\]

Insert currents at \(z,w\) in a boundary disk. A contour about \(w\) applied to \(J(z)J(w)\,dz\) alone would take the simple-pole coefficient. Multiplying first by \(z-w\) selects the double pole. With the residue normalization above the free theory predicts \(-\hbar\kappa\) times the identity.

The same coefficient changes the order of two boundary operations. Around the origin let \(J_m=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint z^mJ(z)\,dz\), with positive contour orientation. Moving the \(z\)-contour past the \(w\)-contour picks up the pole at \(z=w\). Its residue against \(z^m\) is \(-\hbar\kappa\,m w^{m-1}\), so the remaining \(w\)-residue gives
\[
 [J_m,J_n]=-\hbar\kappa\,m\,\delta_{m+n,0}\,\mathrm{id}.
\]
In the boundary vacuum \(\Omega\), \(J_1\Omega=0\). Inserting \(J_{-1}\) first and then \(J_1\) returns \(-\hbar\kappa\,\Omega\). Reversing the insertions gives zero. At known nonzero \(\hbar\) this ordered pair of operations determines the coefficient \(\kappa\) of \(a\wedge\partial a/2\) in the half-space action. For formal \(\hbar\), the coefficient of \(\hbar\) in the vacuum commutator is \(-\kappa\).

The same coefficient can be measured with compactly supported forms. Choose a smooth cutoff \(\chi_a\) supported inside the disk and equal to one near \(a\). The form \(u_a=-\bar\partial\chi_a/(z-a)\) is smooth since its numerator vanishes near \(a\). For every holomorphic function \(F\) on the disk, Stokes' theorem on an annulus gives
\[
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int u_a\wedge F(z)\,dz=F(a).
\]
A smooth bump equal to one at \(a\) and supported where \(\chi_a=1\) pairs to zero with \(u_a\). The evaluation formula uses holomorphy.

For distinct \(a,b\), take disjoint small disks and choose a common outer radial cutoff equal to one on both. The product represented on its outer annulus is regular at \(a=b\). Comparing the two products in the quantum differential gives a contraction \(+\hbar\kappa/(a-b)^2\). In cohomology the separated product is the regular product minus this term. At \(\kappa=0\) its coefficient vanishes, while the bulk pairing stays nondegenerate and all polynomial current fields and holomorphic jets remain. [The boundary map and current at every level](#ref-ch09-01).

## Fields as joint currents {#ch09-fields-as-joint-currents}

Integrating through the collar has given a current on the boundary disk. To ask what happens while two endpoints change their *relative height*, we must retain their heights during the contraction. Use a finite Gaussian model on \(\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb C_z\) whose Wick pairings will fix the normalization. For a finite set \(I\) of insertions label the external positions by \(X_I=(\mathbb R\times\mathbb C)^I\). Coincidence of two coordinates in \(X_I\) places the endpoints together. It does not identify the two internal variables against which their fields are tested. Their product therefore takes values in joint distributions in these variables.

This coefficient complex has two polarizations, called \(x\) and \(p\). An internal mixed form of degree \(r\) has cohomological degree \(r-2\) in the \(x\)-polarization and \(r-1\) in the \(p\)-polarization. Put \(M=\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb C_z\) and \(\overline M=(-\infty,+\infty]\times\mathbb C_z\). An \(x\)-particle may be supported at an endpoint. A \(p\)-particle may occupy the whole ray above it. This requires a different support condition for the two variables.

**Definition (upward-proper support).** On each compact set of external parameters, after an external cutoff equal to one nearby, the closure of the joint coefficient support lies in one compact subset of \(M^{I_x}\times\overline M^{I_p}\), where \(I_x,I_p\) label the internal variables. Thus the \(p\)-heights share a lower bound and all complex coordinates have bounded support. Above one upper collar height for this compact set, the current is independent of each such \(p\)-height and has no differential in that height. A bounded family on a fixed compact shares the carrier, the collar height and a finite distribution-order bound after truncating the collars. Particle number is finite, and these operations are defined over \(\mathbb C[\hbar]\).

On one coefficient variable, the internal differential is

\[
Q(f_x)=(d'f)_x,\qquad Q(g_p)=-(d'g)_p,\qquad
\delta(f_x)=-(\partial_zf)_p,\qquad \delta(g_p)=0.
\]

With external forms placed first, the full differential is

\[
\mathcal D=d_{X_I}+Q+\delta,
\qquad
(Q+\delta)(\omega\otimes n)
 =(-1)^{|\omega|}\omega\otimes(Q+\delta)n.
\]

The notation \(d_{X_I}\) denotes \(\sum_i(dt_i\,\partial_{t_i}+d\bar z_i\,\partial_{\bar z_i})\) in the external position variables. The coefficient differential and its shifts are part of this formula.

At position \(i\), \(\widehat W_i\) integrates the \(p\)-field on the upward ray, \(\widehat p_i\) evaluates that field at the endpoint, and the degree-zero current \(\widehat J_i\) combines the \(x\)-point field with the holomorphic derivative of the ray. They obey

\[
\mathcal D\widehat W_i=-\widehat p_i,\qquad
\mathcal D\widehat p_i=\mathcal D\widehat J_i=0.
\]

The first identity is the endpoint term from differentiating the upward ray. Each field is a smooth family of distributions in the internal variables. Normal ordering means multiplication in independent internal variables followed by particle symmetrization. It introduces no extra contraction at coincident external endpoints.

For \(n_{ij}=(t_j-t_i,z_i-z_j)\), the nonzero cross contractions are

\[
\langle W_i,J_j\rangle=-\hbar b_1(n_{ij}),\qquad
\langle p_i,J_j\rangle=\hbar Q_1(n_{ij}),\qquad
\langle J_i,J_j\rangle=\hbar E_2(z_i-z_j).
\]

Here \(E_m=(-1)^{m-1}\partial_z^{m-1}\operatorname{PV}(1/z)/(m-1)!\) extends \(z^{-m}\). The one-form \(Q_1\) is the bulk contribution from differentiating the ray with respect to its endpoint. Its two components will be found together with the residue on the axis. [Ordered poles and their complete differential](#ch09-ordered-poles-and-their-complete-differential). Off \(z=0\) the ray kernel is

\[
b_1(t,z)=\frac{1}{2z}
 \left(1+\frac{t}{\sqrt{t^2+|z|^2}}\right).
\]

Fix \(t\ne0\) and write \(R=(t^2+|z|^2)^{1/2}\). For \(t<0\), rationalizing \(R+t\) gives \(b_1=\bar z/[2R(R-t)]\), which is smooth at \(z=0\). For \(t>0\) the same calculation gives \(b_1-1/z=-\bar z/[2R(R+t)]\), also smooth there. The pole has coefficient one on the upper side and zero on the lower side. This jump is the source of the ordered residue in the differential.

A Wick contraction now has two kinds of variables. Its scalar kernel \(K\) is a distribution in the external positions \(q\), while the unmatched fields form a smooth family \(F(q)\) of distributions in the internal variables. For a compact joint test \(\varphi\), pair its internal variables with \(F(q)\) first. The resulting smooth function of \(q\) can be tested against the finite-order distribution \(K\). Thus

\[
\langle KF,\varphi\rangle
 =\left\langle K,\ q\longmapsto
       \langle F(q),\varphi(q,\cdot)\rangle\right\rangle.
\]

Uniform support, collar and finite-order estimates make this a joint distribution. Two Wick sums can give the same pairing on every joint test. To use that current as an input, contracting either sum with a third block must give the same joint current. This is the descent property for the Gaussian product on disjoint sets of labels. [Composition on actual Gaussian images](#ref-ch09-03).

## Ordered poles and their complete differential {#ch09-ordered-poles-and-their-complete-differential}

The two expansions of \(b_1\) differ by \(1/z\) along the positive part of the axis. To differentiate a higher pole, first take its holomorphic derivatives as a distribution. The pole \(E_m\) then gives a residue \(r_m\) on the axis. Differentiating its height gate \(H(t)\) gives a current \(C_m\) where both coordinates vanish. With the opening orientation these currents are

\[
r_m(z)=\frac{\pi(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}
 \partial_z^{m-1}\delta_{\mathbb C}(z)\,d\bar z,
\qquad
C_m(t,z)=\delta(t)\,dt\wedge r_m(z).
\]

Thus \(\bar\partial E_m=r_m\). The first bulk kernel is the distributional one-form represented off the origin by

\[
Q_1(t,z)=
\frac{\bar z}{2(t^2+|z|^2)^{3/2}}\,dt
-\frac{t}{4(t^2+|z|^2)^{3/2}}\,d\bar z.
\]

For every \(m\geq1\), define the higher kernels by holomorphic distributional differentiation:

\[
b_m=\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\partial_z^{m-1}b_1,
\qquad
Q_m=\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\partial_z^{m-1}Q_1.
\]

This is the same normalization as \(E_m\). The scalar ray identities are

\[
D b_m=Q_m+H(t)r_m,\qquad DQ_m=-C_m,
\qquad D=d_t+\bar\partial.
\]

**Example (the ray and its wall).** Apply \(D\) once more to \(b_1\). A test supported in the open upper chamber sees no derivative of the gate. A test at the common endpoint sees \(D[H(t)r_1]=C_1\) with exactly the orientation of the opening calculation. The endpoint form contributes \(DQ_1=-C_1\), so the sum is zero. The two tests account distributionally for \(D^2b_1=0\).

For three insertions the complex differences satisfy \(z_1-z_3=(z_1-z_2)+(z_2-z_3)\). The three singular directions can therefore meet with only two independent complex normals. Separate extensions of individual poles do not determine their product there. We need a class of extensions of the whole kernel that controls its behavior at each intersection.

A gate is a factor \(H(\ell)\) imposing a real height inequality \(\ell>0\). For one ray its boundary is \(\delta(\ell)\,d\ell\). When several arguments share a normal coordinate, extending their product requires control at every intersection.

Work on a coordinate space \(V=V_t\times V_z\), with \(V_t\) real and \(V_z\) complex. Let \(\mathscr L\) be the finite intersection-closed lattice of proper subspaces generated by the complex collision loci, mixed collision loci and gate walls. Its members are products of real-linear and complex-linear subspaces. At \(y\in V\), let \(L(y)\) be the intersection of the lattice members containing \(y\), or \(V\) if there are none, and let \(\pi_y:V\to V/L(y)\) be the quotient. If its real factor has dimension \(p_y\) and its complex factor has complex dimension \(k_y\), the quotient has mixed dimension \(p_y+k_y\).

**Definition (tame and subcritical normal currents).** A normal current \(\omega\) is bihomogeneous of bidegree \((\alpha,\beta)\) if, including its form factors,

\[
g_{\lambda,\theta}^*\omega
 =\lambda^\alpha e^{i\beta\theta}\omega,
\qquad
g_{\lambda,\theta}(t,z)=(\lambda t,\lambda e^{i\theta}z),
\quad\lambda>0.
\]

Its excess is \(\alpha-\beta\). A current is *tame* if near each \(y\) it has a finite presentation

\[
\sum_j\eta_j\wedge\pi_y^*\omega_j,
\]

where \(\eta_j\) are smooth mixed forms and \(\omega_j\) are bihomogeneous normal currents of nonnegative excess. It is *subcritical* if, at every point of the lattice union, these models can be chosen with \(\deg\omega_j<p_y+k_y\). This bounds the normal model degree, which can be smaller than the total degree after multiplication by \(\eta_j\). The whole scalar arrangement has a unique extension in this class agreeing with its regular kernel; see *Subcritical Q cycles and finite native Gaussian kernels*, equation 1.4, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 3.2. For uniqueness, a difference of two extensions on a minimal remaining normal stratum must be supported at the normal origin. Such a current of nonnegative excess needs every real and antiholomorphic normal differential. The subcritical degree excludes it. This fixes the scalar extension within the stated class. Its selection by an independent physical construction is a separate question. [Subcritical normal extension](#ref-ch09-04).

A supporting flat records which real coordinates are equal and which complex coordinates are equal. It is thus described by a partition of each set of coordinates. Compatibility means that the real partition refines the complex one: equality in height is imposed only within a complex block. Scalar rays and marked one-forms \(Q_m\) join distinct complex blocks. Gates \(H(h)\) compare real blocks inside a complex block. One also allows finite holomorphic derivatives of the top normal delta currents.

Call an edge marked when its contraction supplies a \(Q_m\) one-form to the ordered wedge. A wedge of these forms uses a normal degree for each mark. To count the available directions for a set \(A\) of marked edges, let \(p_A\) be the real rank of its height forms and \(k_A\) the complex rank of its complex forms. The nonzero admissible case requires \(|A|<p_A+k_A\) for every nonempty marked subset; violating this inequality gives the zero whole kernel. A marked set is closed if it contains every marked edge whose height and complex forms both lie in the respective spans for this set. A critical contact comes from a nonempty closed set with

\[
|A|=p_A+k_A-1.
\]

To see why equality forces closure, add a mark whose two normals already lie in the respective spans. The number of marks increases by one, while both ranks stay fixed. This contradicts the strict inequality for the enlarged subset. For example, \(Q_1(s,u)\wedge Q_1(t,u)\) has two marks, two independent height forms \(s,t\), and only one complex normal \(u\). Here \(2=2+1-1\), so the two marks together can contribute a contact. With

\[
\Omega_2=\pi\delta(s)\delta(t)\delta_{\mathbb C}(u)
 \,ds\wedge dt\wedge d\bar u,
\]

the complete gated current satisfies

\[
D\bigl[H(t-s)Q_1(s,u)\wedge Q_1(t,u)\bigr]
 =-\frac12\partial_u\Omega_2.
\]

The factor \(1/2\) is determined by the two complementary chambers. Their currents sum to the ungated current, whose derivative is \(-\partial_u\Omega_2\); interchanging \(s,t\) makes their two coefficients equal. This computation never assigns a value to \(H(0)\). A ray weight changes the residue: on the corresponding whole arrangement,

\[
D\bigl[Q_1(s,u)\wedge Q_1(t,u)b_1(s,u)\bigr]
 =\frac14\partial_u^2\Omega_2.
\]

The added ray changes both the normal derivative and its coefficient. The annular moment must therefore be taken on the whole weighted kernel. [Scalar-weighted critical mixed normals](#ref-ch09-06).

Let \(\mathcal M_I\) consist of finite linear combinations of these compatible scalar currents multiplied by finite normally ordered \(W,p,J\) families and smooth external forms, including all allowed holomorphic normal jets. Two expressions give the same element when their values agree on every joint test. To vary the heights within this space, its differential must still be a sum of such currents. Differentiating a single ray gave a marked one-form and an ordered residue. The two-height example shows why, with several rays, a differential can also be supported on a joint flat.

**Theorem — finite marked Ward calculus.** With the stated compatible flats, internal gates, finite pole and jet orders, upward-proper coefficient support and canonical scalar extension, \(\mathcal D\mathcal M_I\subseteq\mathcal M_I\). For a scalar coefficient \(K\) with \(q\) marked factors, its derivative consists of four finite sums:

\[
DK=\mathcal B(K)+\mathcal S(K)+\mathcal V(K)+\mathcal C(K).
\]

Here \(\mathcal B\) replaces a ray by an additional mark with sign \((-1)^q\); \(\mathcal S\) takes the residues of entire complex-parallel scalar classes; \(\mathcal V\) takes the oriented real boundaries of whole simplified gates; and \(\mathcal C\) is the sum over closed critical marked flats. For each such flat the coefficients in \(\mathcal C(K)\) are annular moments of the whole weighted, gated normal kernel. The derivative of \(KF\) also includes \((-1)^qK\mathcal DF\). Old normal degrees contribute their Koszul sign, and every residue differentiates the entire remaining coefficient and field family to the required finite normal order.

To find what differentiation can add, restrict first away from the collision flats. There the ordinary product rule applies. At a flat, the factors with the same vanishing complex normal must be taken together before taking their residue. This leaves the other factors as tangent coefficients. Where marked factors share real and complex normals, their joint angular moment gives the additional contact, as in the example with normals \(s,t,u\). Finite-order estimates justify each transpose against a joint test. Since the formula equals the actual distributional differential, it respects every relation between presentations of the same current. The precise statement is *The finite marked Ward calculus on compatible collision flats*, Theorems 4.1 and 7.1.

For two disjoint sets of labels the Gaussian product \(\star\) sums all matchings between the two blocks, keeps each unmatched field, and symmetrizes its coefficient particles with the graded signs. It closes on this space and is associative with those signs. Its Ward defect

\[
\mathfrak W(X,Y)=\mathcal D(X\star Y)
 -(\mathcal DX)\star Y-(-1)^{|X|}X\star(\mathcal DY)
\]

is supported on complex collisions between a label of \(X\) and a label of \(Y\). For example, the endpoint field \(\widehat p_1\) and current \(\widehat J_2\) are closed. Their Wick product is \(:\widehat p_1\widehat J_2:+\hbar Q_1(n_{12})\). Its normally ordered term is closed, so the entire Ward defect is
\[
\mathfrak W(\widehat p_1,\widehat J_2)=-\hbar C_1(n_{12}).
\]
Here \(C_1\) is supported where both height and complex position coincide. Two closed inputs have produced a product whose differential is a contact current.

There are three single contractions. Combining \(1,2\) first produces \(Q_{12}\) before the third field enters. Combining \(2,3\) first produces \(E_{23}\) before the first field enters. Both orders give the joint current
\[
 :\widehat p_1\widehat J_2\widehat J_3:
 +\hbar Q_{12}\widehat J_3+\hbar Q_{13}\widehat J_2
 +\hbar E_{23}\widehat p_1 .
\]
On a test supported where \(z_3\) stays away from both \(z_1\) and \(z_2\), the only contribution to its differential is \(-\hbar C_{12}\widehat J_3\). Indeed \(DQ_{13}=-C_{13}\) and \(DE_{23}=r_2(z_2-z_3)\) both vanish on that support, and the three remaining fields are closed. A test that meets the first two endpoints can read the contact with the third field still present. The mapping cone keeps this term available for gluing, together with its sign.

Take the normal test function \(u\chi(u)\), where \(\chi\) is smooth, compactly supported and equal to one near zero. Since \(r_2=-\pi\partial_u\delta_{\mathbb C}\,d\bar u\), the derivative of the test at zero gives \(+\pi\hbar\) when the remaining field and tangent factors are paired to one. A correction supported at equal heights cannot cancel this reading.

## Relative currents and a radial homotopy {#ch09-relative-currents-and-a-radial-homotopy}

**Definition (relative current complex).** Let \(Z_I=\bigcup_{i<j}\{t_i=t_j,\ z_i=z_j\}\), and let \(\mathcal M_Z\subseteq\mathcal M_I\) consist of currents supported on \(Z_I\). The relative complex is the mapping cone \(\operatorname{Cone}(\mathcal M_Z\hookrightarrow\mathcal M_I)\) with differential

\[
\mathcal F_{\mathcal M}^n
 =\mathcal M_I^n\oplus\mathcal M_Z^{n+1},
\qquad
d_F(\alpha,\gamma)=(\mathcal D\alpha+\gamma,-\mathcal D\gamma).
\]

For the two-input product, set \(X=\widehat p_1\star\widehat J_2\). The pair \((X,\hbar C_1(n_{12}))\) is closed in this cone because its first differential component is \(-\hbar C_1+\hbar C_1=0\), and the second is zero because the contact current is closed. Thus one can retain both the product and the term that corrects the Leibniz rule, even under a later operation that meets the diagonal. More generally, if \(G\) is a gated complex normal current and \(F\) its smooth tangent coefficient, the pair

\[
(GF,-DG\,F)
\]

stores the negative of the scalar wall term in the shifted summand when \(DG\) is supported on the mixed diagonal. In the first component of \(d_F\), it cancels the \(DG\) in the derivative of \(GF\). Terms differentiating the tangent coefficient \(F\) persist. They have to be retained in any further contraction.

The decomposition by supporting flats supplies a projection \(P_Z\) onto the summands supported on \(Z_I\). A complex collision at strictly separated heights stays in \(1-P_Z\). Differentiating a height gate can move a term into the image of \(P_Z\), so \(P_Z\) need not commute with \(\mathcal D\). For an ambient current \(x\), the desired first component is \((1-P_Z)x\). To make its differential agree with the first component of \(E(\mathcal D x)\), the second component must contribute \((1-P_Z)\mathcal D x-\mathcal D(1-P_Z)x\). This gives the chain map

\[
E(x)=\bigl((1-P_Z)x,\mathcal DP_Zx-P_Z\mathcal Dx\bigr).
\]

With \(\mathsf R(\alpha,\gamma)=E(\alpha)\) and
\(\mathsf H(\alpha,\gamma)=(0,P_Z\alpha)\), direct calculation gives

\[
\mathsf R^2=\mathsf R,\qquad
1-\mathsf R=d_F\mathsf H+\mathsf H d_F.
\]

For a current \(z\) supported on \(Z_I\), both \(z\) and \(\mathcal Dz\) are fixed by \(P_Z\), so \(E(z)=0\). Hence \(E((1-P_Z)x)=E(x)\), which gives the idempotence of \(\mathsf R\). The homotopy \(\mathsf H\) thus retracts the displayed relative complex onto \(E(\mathcal M_I)\). A complex collision at separated heights remains in the first component.

The retraction holds the external positions fixed. We can also bring three heights together while leaving all the other insertions in place. For this operation the primitive must come from the moving fields themselves. Vary their heights on an interval and integrate the coefficient of its parameter differential. Consider the three-field circuit
\(:W_1^2:,\ :W_2J_2:,\ :J_3^2:\), together with any finite disjoint set of mutually contracting, initially unmarked \(W,J\) spectators. Set

\[
t_0=t_3,\quad \tau=t_2-t_1,\quad \sigma=t_3-t_2,
\qquad u=z_1-z_2,\quad v=z_2-z_3.
\]

Scale the circuit heights by

\[
t_1\mapsto t_0-\lambda(\tau+\sigma),\qquad
t_2\mapsto t_0-\lambda\sigma,\qquad
t_3\mapsto t_0,\qquad 0<\lambda\leq1,
\]

leaving spectator heights and all complex coordinates unchanged. At positive scale this gives a distributional pullback. Write \(\mathcal P\) for this pullback on the parameter cylinder. Its endpoint \(\operatorname{sp}_0\) is the horizontal restriction of the limiting total form. The cylinder integral also uses the coefficient of \(d\lambda\). Both require estimates as the height map loses rank at zero. In particular,

\[
d(t_0-\lambda\sigma)
 =dt_0-\lambda\,d\sigma-\sigma\,d\lambda.
\]

A single height difference illustrates which parameter terms survive. For \(\lambda>0\) the zero set of \(\lambda\ell\) is the same oriented wall as the zero set of the linear form \(\ell\). Its entire distributional one-form is unchanged, since

\[
\delta(\lambda\ell)\,d(\lambda\ell)
 =\lambda^{-1}\delta(\ell)
    (\lambda\,d\ell+\ell\,d\lambda)
 =\delta(\ell)\,d\ell.
\]

The last equality uses \(\ell\delta(\ell)=0\); it does not discard the parameter differential by convention.

During this motion a spectator can meet one of the moving fields. The relative support \(Z\) was chosen for the three circuit mixed diagonals, times the full spectator parameter space. A new spectator contact can occur in the ambient first component. The second component must stay supported on that same \(Z\) throughout the motion.

**Theorem — radial circuit transport.** Let \(\mathscr S\) be the span of the complete first and successive circuit contacts, their stated spectator compositions, smooth form multiples and finite holomorphic derivatives. On \(\mathscr S\) the positive-scale pullback has a unique continuous joint-current extension to zero. It extends as a chain family to
\(\mathscr V=\mathscr S+d_F\mathscr S\). Its proper parameter integral gives

\[
H_F(\alpha,\gamma)=(h\alpha,-h\gamma),\qquad
h=\int_0^1\iota_{\partial_\lambda}\mathcal P\,d\lambda,
\]

\[
d_FH_F+H_Fd_F=\operatorname{id}-\operatorname{sp}_0.
\]

Both endpoints and the homotopy take values in the original ambient relative current complex with the same \(Z\). The three successive contact branches retain their full sum-zero relation, including unmatched fields, vacuum terms and all finite normal jets.

The source restriction makes the limit possible. Unmarked tangent coefficients are strongly \(C^1\) on the required joint-current stages. Positive-scale equality of two presentations then extends uniquely to zero, so the construction descends to actual currents. The chain identity on \(\mathscr S+d_F\mathscr S\) uses the combined full derivative, including the \(p\)-descendants. It requires no separate endpoint value for every term in that derivative.

**Counterexample (a second height derivative).** Take the whole kernel \(K_t=[b_1(t,v)^2]\) with no independent height in the test. Pair its symmetric second difference quotient \((K_t+K_{-t}-2K_0)/t^2\) with \(v^2\chi(|v|^2)\), for a smooth compact cutoff equal to one near zero. At \(\rho=|v|\) the two linear terms in \(t/\sqrt{t^2+\rho^2}\) cancel. The radial profile that remains is

\[
\frac{1}{2(t^2+\rho^2)}.
\]

With planar area measure the integral near zero is \(2\pi\int_0^{\rho_0}\rho\,d\rho/[2(t^2+\rho^2)]=\pi\log(1/|t|)+O(1)\) for fixed \(\rho_0>0\). Thus a general \(C^2\) trace without a retained real normal is false.

Place two cluster endpoints at the same complex position and at heights \(t_0-h_1,t_0-h_2\). A single spectator at complex separation \(v\) and height \(t_0+s\) gives the two ray heights \(s+h_1,s+h_2\). Now the joint test includes integration in \(s\). At \(\rho=|v|>0\), the height derivative of the profile \(v\,b_1(t,v)\) is \(a_\rho(t)=\rho^2/[2(t^2+\rho^2)^{3/2}]\). This function has integral one and maximum \(1/(2\rho)\). Hence
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R} a_\rho(s+h_1)a_\rho(s+h_2)\,ds
 \le \frac{1}{2\rho},
\]
even when \(h_1=h_2\). After the same angular test, the planar factor \(\rho\,d\rho\) makes this bound integrable at zero.

Take the canonical whole product of finitely many scalar ray kernels and holomorphic poles before inserting any height gates. Suppose the height difference on each ray has a nonzero real linear part in the spectator heights integrated against the joint test. Two rays may share that linear form, as they do here. The unmarked current is then strongly \(C^2\) in the moving cluster heights. Replacing one native ray by its full form \(Q_m\) gives a continuous trace. [Height traces, Theorems 6.1 and 6.2](#ref-ch09-07).

The radial circuit theorem uses the \(C^1\) estimate on its unmarked tangent coefficients and transports the combined differential. Its endpoint retains the original gates, walls and labels. [Radial circuit transport, Theorem 1.1](#ref-ch09-08).

### Two odd insertions at the fourth point {#ch09-two-marked-height-operation}

The height homotopy integrates the coefficient of \(d\lambda\). Restricting to the final height slice loses this coefficient altogether. Can it be detected even when the final slice gives zero? Add two odd fields at one further point and take the ordered inputs
\[
:W_1^2:,\qquad :W_2J_2:,\qquad :J_3^2:,\qquad :P P':,
\qquad P=p_4,\quad P'=\partial_{z_4}p_4 .
\]
The first three are the circuit above. Both slots of the fourth input are odd. At every \(\lambda>0\) the height map is a submersion, so one can pull back the entire four-input operation and its differential as joint currents. The pullback includes \(d(t_j'-t_4)\) for the moving heights \(t'_j\), the fields left uncontracted by each matching, and both components of each old relative contact.

Write \(T\) for the three-input bulk Wick sum, \(Q_j=Q_1(t_j-t_4,z_4-z_j)\) and \(Q'_j=\partial_{z_4}Q_j\). In a Wick summand let \(D_j\) remove one unmatched \(J_j\), summed over its occurrences. The four-input bulk sum before pullback is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 :T PP':\ &+\hbar\sum_{j=2,3}
       \bigl(Q_j :(D_j T)P':-Q'_j :(D_j T)P:\bigr)\\
 &+\hbar^2\sum_{j,k=2,3}Q_j\wedge Q'_k :(D_jD_k T): .
\end{aligned}
\]
Moving the singly contracted form from \(P'\) past the earlier odd \(P\) gives the minus sign. In a full matching the two forms are kept in spectator-slot order, so both assignments to \(J_2,J_3\) carry a plus. Each summand is extended as one joint current. The symbol \(D_j\) counts matching choices before extension. When an old term has a normal derivative, its transpose acts on every new kernel and on the remaining field family.

Consider the first contact of labels \(2,3\). On the sheet \(v=z_2-z_3=0\), put \(y=t_0-t_4,\ w=z_4-z_3,\ a=\lambda\sigma\), where \(\sigma=t_3-t_2\). The two odd contractions into the distinct targets \(J_2,J_3\) leave two \(W_1\)'s. With
\[
A=Q_1(y-a,w),\qquad B=Q_1(y,w),\qquad
K=A\wedge\partial_w B+B\wedge\partial_w A,
\]
their contribution to the relative pair before radial transport is
\[
U=2\hbar^3\bigl(-r_1(v)\wedge[H(\sigma)K]\, :W_1^2:,\,0\bigr)
\]
with \(a=\sigma\) initially. The factor \(2\) counts the two choices of a \(J_3\). On the old real wall the marked normals agree and the canonical repeated-normal wedge vanishes. This accounts for the zero in the second component.

On a fixed height slice the tangent part of \(K\) vanishes in the limit \(a\to0\). A test of the height homotopy also sees the coefficient of \(da\wedge d\bar w\), since changing \(\lambda\) changes \(a\). For \(a\ne0\) the two singularities are separated. Put \(R_A^2=(y-a)^2+|w|^2,\ R_B^2=y^2+|w|^2\). Direct substitution of \(Q_1\) gives this coefficient as
\[
K_{a\bar w}=\frac{3a(2y-a)y\bar w^2}{16R_A^5R_B^5}.
\]
The denominator is invariant under \(w\mapsto e^{i\theta}w\), while the numerator contains \(\bar w^2\). The constant and linear Taylor terms of a transverse test integrate to zero around each circle. The first term with the compensating angular dependence is \(w^2\). At \(a=1\), reflecting \(y\) about \(1/2\) puts this moment in the form
\[
\frac3{32}\int_{\mathbb R\times\mathbb C}
\frac{(2y-1)^2|w|^4}{R_A^5R_B^5}\,dy\,d^2w
=\frac{\pi}{6}.
\]
Combining the denominators with a beta parameter \(t\) and shifting \(y\) to \(y-t\) gives a radial integral in three real dimensions. The two even numerator terms then combine by \(4t(1-t)+(2t-1)^2=1\). Under \((y,w)=|a|(y',w')\), the kernel coefficient has degree \(-5\), the test monomial \(w^2\) has degree two and the volume has degree three. The moment is therefore independent of \(|a|\). Changing \((a,y)\) to \((-a,-y)\) reverses its sign. Angular Taylor subtraction on compact tests bounds the higher remainders and gives the distributional limits
\[
K_{a\bar w}\ \longrightarrow\
\pm\frac{\pi}{12}\,\delta(y)\partial_w^2\delta_{\mathbb C}(w)
\qquad(a\to0^\pm).
\]
The coefficient is half the tested moment because \(\partial_w^2w^2=2\). [The moment and the total height family](#ref-ch09-20).

Now \(da=\sigma d\lambda+\lambda d\sigma\). The horizontal endpoint of \(U\) is zero, while its coefficient with \(d\lambda\) placed first is
\[
2\hbar^3\,\frac{\pi}{12}H(\sigma)|\sigma|\,r_1(v)\wedge
\delta(y)\partial_w^2\delta_{\mathbb C}(w)\,d\bar w\ :W(t_0,z_1)^2: .
\]
The sign uses the passage of \(d\lambda\) past \(r_1(v)\). Two internal tests supported above the endpoints detect the two upward \(W\)-tails. A transverse test equal to \(w^2\) near zero then measures a nonzero multiple of \(\delta(t_0-t_4)\) in the chamber \(\sigma>0\). Every earlier unmarked contact and its admitted descendants gives a continuous dependence on this height after the same tests. Thus this contact strictly enlarges the domain at nonzero \(\hbar\), and also over \(\mathbb C[\hbar]\).

**Theorem (transport of the two-mark contact).** Adjoin \(U\), its smooth multiples, finite holomorphic derivatives and full \(d_F\)-descendants to \(\mathscr V\). The continuous cylinder limit on this larger space respects relations of joint currents because it is the ordinary pullback at every positive \(\lambda\). Applying Stokes on \([\epsilon,1]\) and passing to the limit recovers \(d_FH_F+H_Fd_F=1-\operatorname{sp}_0\) in the same fixed relative recipient. Any further disjoint spectator made solely of \(W\)'s and their finite holomorphic jets can be appended to this sector. Its only possible contractions would need an unmatched \(J\), so its product with this contact only adjoins the new particles and takes their graded symmetrization.

At positive scale the full four-input operation is a distributional pullback along a submersion. At zero scale the theorem above controls \(U\) and its stated descendants, including the \(d\lambda\) coefficient detected by the two ray tests. Treating a different sheet or a bulk term with another singular ray requires an estimate at its own shared normals. A general composition would also have to descend through the relations among the original bulk and ray sources and place its outputs in the domain of the next contraction. Interacting field theories require their own boundary condition, propagator and renormalized kernels with a valid Stokes formula.

## Changing endpoint profiles {#ch09-changing-endpoint-profiles}

The height test used an actual ray representative. On a disk with an interval collar, integration along the interval loses the choice of representative even for a smooth field. How much of a product survives that integration? Write \(p\) for integration along the interval \(I\). For a compact profile \(\theta_a\) with \(\int_I\theta_a(t)\,dt=1\), the section is \(s_a(\alpha)=\alpha\,\theta_a(t)\,dt\), so \(ps_a=1\). Changing the profile from \(a\) to \(b\) gives

\[
T_{ba}=1+(s_b-s_a)p,\qquad
T_{cb}T_{ba}=T_{ca},\qquad T_{ab}=T_{ba}^{-1}.
\]

Both profiles integrate to one, so \(p(s_b-s_a)=0\). Consequently \(pT_{ba}=p\) and \(T_{ba}s_a=s_b\). A linear disk field keeps its integral when its interval representative is changed. For the product of two fields there is one more term to compare, the scalar obtained by contracting the pair.

For a planar disk \(D\) and interval \(I\), take the endpoint coefficient complex \(\mathcal E_c=\Omega_c^\bullet(D\times I)[2]\) with
\(b(u,v)=\nu\int u\wedge v\), with \(\nu\ne0\). Put
\(A=(s_b-s_a)p\) and \(T=1+A\). Since both \(Au\) and \(Av\) contain \(dt\),

\[
b(Tu,Tv)-b(u,v)=b(Au,v)+b(u,Av).
\]

This defect can be nonzero. Choose a compact planar function \(\phi\), a compact planar two-form \(\psi\), and real distinct profiles, with \(\int_D\phi\psi\ne0\). For
\(u=\phi(\theta_b-\theta_a)\) and \(v=\psi\theta_a\,dt\), it equals

\[
\nu\left(\int_D\phi\psi\right)
       \left(\int_I(\theta_b-\theta_a)^2dt\right)\ne0.
\]

Here \(pu=0\), so \(Tu=u\). The shear replaces \(v=\psi\theta_a\,dt\) by \(Tv=\psi\theta_b\,dt\), and both integrate to \(\psi\). Thus each field keeps its own interval integral while \(b(Tu,Tv)\) differs from \(b(u,v)\). Applying \(T\) separately to the fields in every monomial therefore fails to intertwine the unchanged quantum differential in general. A transport of the quantum product must correct what happens to a *pair*.

Let \(\Pi_a,\Pi_b\) be signed primitives of the respective local quantum pairings. Thus \([d,\partial_{\Pi_a}]\) is the second-order pairing operator in the source differential, and similarly at the target. The operator \(\partial_\Pi\) contracts pairs of symmetric field factors with \(\Pi\), and \(E_a=\exp(\partial_{\Pi_a})\) changes from quantum to creation coordinates. In creation coordinates the differential is \(d\). The product still contracts fields from different inputs with a closed bilinear pairing. Write \(c_a,c_b\) for these cross pairings, including the contributions of all the cuts in the given word.

**Proposition (Gaussian transport between profiles).** For a bounded chain isomorphism \(T\), write \(Q_T=T^*\Pi_b-\Pi_a\) for the difference of the two primitives on source fields. If \(T^*c_b=c_a\), the map that transports the quantum product and differential is

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_T&=E_b^{-1}\operatorname{Sym}(T)E_a\\
   &=\operatorname{Sym}(T)\exp\!\bigl(-\partial_{Q_T}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Pair contraction removes two fields. At each fixed number of fields its exponential therefore terminates. Kernels are completed in the projective tensor topology at fixed field number and summed algebraically over field number. On this domain \(G_T\) transports each source \(\eta\) to \(T\eta\) and preserves the full source–target matrix differential. A word operation \(f\) in these coordinates has linear part \(T_Y^{-1}fT_X\) and the corresponding pairing correction. Agreement with \(f\) on a single field requires \(T_Y f=fT_X\). Once this equality holds, evaluating on two fields tests the remaining scalar difference of the contraction primitives. Both conditions must hold to recover the same operation. Independent changes in two disjoint input bands generally cannot be absorbed by one output profile shear. These statements are proved in *Local pairing defects and profile-labelled Gaussian operations*.

Cutting a word in more than one place can give several orders of evaluation. A refinement homotopy compares the maps for those orders. With several choices the comparisons have faces of their own, indexed by a finite cube. To apply such a homotopy to completed words one needs an estimate on how much Laurent order it can lose. The normalized finite cubes and their evaluation mates act on mixed words with one Laurent lower bound before all state-energy and bar-length products. Profile paths and exponent distributions are algebraic at fixed indices. Finite coalgebra supports are chosen from right to left, before the still-free state products to their left; terminal coefficient and form slots remain independent until balancing. Constant finite-dimensional commuting charge profiles, including Jordan profiles, retain their full matrix Hom complexes on this domain.

What happens if one completes only in the energy of the state and allows the Laurent lower bound to vary with that energy? An input can then move out to arbitrarily high energy and still contribute the same unit after contraction. In the neutral creation algebra, let \(J_{i,n}\) have energy \(n+1\), let \(c_{i,n}\) be a ghost of energy \(n\), and let \(s\) denote bar suspension. The specified normalized binary refinement cell satisfies

\[
\overline K\!\left(
 sc_{1,0}\mid
 s\!\left(3(z_1-z_3)^{n+1}(z_2-z_3)^{n+1}
             c_{2,0}J_{3,n}^{\,2}\right)\right)=s1.
\]

Here \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) are the separated positions, and \(s1\) is the length-one bar state of the algebra unit. It survives normalization of this cell. The inputs tend to zero in the product over energy blocks, while the output is the same nonzero unit. Hence this cell has no continuous extension preserving those values to a Hausdorff target that retains the unit. In the logarithmic frame \(z_i=b+t^{-1}t_i\), the multiplier has order \(-2(n+1)\). The sequence therefore has no common Laurent lower bound.

There is room to change the homotopy on the separated domain. Write \(a=c_{1,0}\), \(b_n=c_{2,0}J_{3,n}^2\) and \(b_n'=c_{2,0}c_{3,n+1}J_{3,n}\). The current differential of \(b_n\) has the component \(-2\kappa(n+1)b_n'\). Let \(\lambda_n\) be the reciprocal of the scalar multiplying \(b_n\) in the displayed word. At \(\kappa\ne0\) take the degree-minus-two comparison whose only generating component is
\[
T(sa\mid s b_n')=\frac{\lambda_n}{2\kappa(n+1)}\,s1 .
\]
Extend it along the bar coproduct between the two fixed maps of histories. The suspension sign in the source differential then gives \((\partial T)(sa\mid s b_n)=-\lambda_n s1\). On the separated domain with one Laurent lower bound, \(\overline K+\partial T\) cancels the displayed row and has the same boundary as \(\overline K\). The primitive \(T\) itself is discontinuous in the regular product topology. Deciding which homotopies extend across the collision therefore requires the whole revised operation and the actual topology of its source.

## Two complex dimensions {#ch09-two-complex-dimensions}

The boundary level on a complex curve was read from a two-current contraction. To test a local current anomaly on a complex surface, consider instead three current insertions in a free matter loop. The scalar coefficient uses the trace on the matter states averaged over the orders of the three currents. The volume form \(dz_1\wedge dz_2\) couples two holomorphic directions. The possible scalar anomaly is governed by a symmetric cubic invariant of the matter representation. Higher current algebras and their free-field realizations are developed by Gwilliam and Williams in [*Higher Kac–Moody algebras and symmetries of holomorphic field theories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06534).

A concrete family uses internal formal variables \(x,y\), distinct from the spatial \(z_1,z_2\). For an integer \(N\ge1\), put \(\mathfrak m=(x,y)\subset\mathbb C[[x,y]]\) and

\[
V_N=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{N+1},\qquad
\mathfrak g_N=\mathfrak m^2/\mathfrak m^{N+2},\qquad
\rho_N(f)g=f_xg_y-f_yg_x.
\]

The estimate
\(\{\mathfrak m^a,\mathfrak m^b\}\subseteq\mathfrak m^{a+b-2}\)
makes the action well defined. Quadratic Hamiltonians preserve internal degree; higher ones raise it. On the first jet, the quadratic \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) action has
\(h=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), so \(\operatorname{Tr}(h^2)=2\) while \(\operatorname{Tr}(h^3)=0\). The full symmetric cubic trace vanishes on every \(V_N\). Any product containing a degree-raising operator has zero diagonal blocks and hence zero trace. On each fixed degree it remains to take the trace of three quadratic Hamiltonians. Their matrices are skew-adjoint for the invariant bilinear form. Taking the adjoint reverses their order and supplies three minus signs, so the trace of each ordered triple cancels the trace of its reverse in the symmetric sum. [The cubic invariant and the finite current](#ref-ch09-13).

For every integer \(N\ge1\) and positive heat scale, the vanishing of the polarized cubic invariant \((a,b,c)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{V_N}(\rho_N(a)\rho_N(b)\rho_N(c))_{\mathrm{sym}}\), the average over the six orders, cancels the scalar wheel anomaly. This gives a nontrivial quantum higher Lie current map from
\(\Omega_c^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb C^2,\mathfrak g_N)\)
to the compact quadratic distribution observables of the free matter theory. Its components satisfy the master equation, with formal \(\hbar\) and completion in background weight. With \(N=0\) the same quotients give \(V_0=\mathfrak g_0=0\), so every positive-arity current is zero. [*Hamiltonian jets and quantum currents*, finite-current theorem](#ref-ch09-13). The cancellation concerns complete scalar wheels; individual ordered traces need not vanish.

The ambient observables cannot simply be projected by deleting higher matter modes. For a removed dual pair \(b,g\) with Batalin–Vilkovisky bracket \(\{b,g\}=1\), deletion gives \(r(b)=r(g)=0\) but \(r(1)=1\). It is not a bracket-preserving map. Thus compatibility of the finite jet currents must be established on the inverse systems they generate, using the degree-triangular action above.

There is a further geometric distinction. The internal matrix action fixes the spatial point. For actual holomorphic \(F,g\), let \(H_F=(\partial_{z_1}F)\partial_{z_2}-(\partial_{z_2}F)\partial_{z_1}\). Taylor expansion in internal jet variables \(\xi_1,\xi_2\) gives

\[
j_N^+(H_Fg)
 =H_F(z)\cdot\partial_zj_N^+g
   +\rho_N(F_z^{\geq2})j_N^+g.
\]

Here \(j_N^+\) is the positive Taylor jet through order \(N\), and \(F_z^{\geq2}\) is the Taylor part of \(F\) of degrees two through \(N+1\). If \(F=z_1\), \(g=z_2^2\), and \(N=1\), the matrix term vanishes but the transport term sends \(2z_2\xi_2\) to \(2\xi_2\). A comparison with geometric Hamiltonian currents must retain that transport term and the relations defining holonomic jets.

## Compact comparison and prescribed lower homotopies {#ch09-compact-comparison-and-prescribed-lower-homotopies}

A scalar trace can give the same answer on two distinct currents. To decide whether projecting a collision normal changes the compact evaluation, one must compare the currents themselves on the same input, before tracing.

If a normal current is a boundary \(dy\), a degree-zero evaluation \(B\) sends it to \(d(By)\) when \(B\) commutes with the differential. In the compact comparison, assume that both evaluations are degree-zero cochain maps after the prescribed forward maps from their carriers into the original output complex. Assume also that the incoming degree-one contact is a cocycle for the full source and target differentials. These hypotheses apply on the common domain of the evaluations and the two contraction homotopies.

Two ways of bringing the last pair of fields together on a compact elliptic source give maps into the same space of outputs. One keeps the normal current until the compact evaluation. The other projects the normal first. In the six-field cubic construction, let \(A_\epsilon:N\to\mathcal Y\) be the original compact output and \(E_\epsilon:J_q\to\mathcal Y\) the separately specified evaluation. Here \(N\) retains the unreduced normal variables, \(J_q\) is the complex after projecting the selected collision normal \(q\), and \(\epsilon\) is the regularization parameter of the compact maps. The signed contraction \(N\rightleftarrows J_q\) has section \(j_q\), projection \(g_q\), and homotopy \(H_q\). A further root–central contraction \(J_q\rightleftarrows\mathscr G\) has section \(j_r\), projection \(g_r\), and homotopy \(h_r\). The target retains the central outputs, both harmonic components and the full parameter-form coefficients. Let \(\delta_u\) be the degree-one unreduced contact from the prescribed six-field source, and \(\delta_c=g_q\delta_u\) its projected contact. The two contraction identities are \(\partial H_q=1-j_qg_q\) and \(\partial h_r=1-j_rg_r\). Inserting the first in \(A_\epsilon\delta_u\), then the second in \((A_\epsilon j_q-E_\epsilon)\delta_c\), gives on their common domain

\[
A_\epsilon\delta_u-E_\epsilon\delta_c
 =\partial\mathcal L_\epsilon+\gamma_\epsilon g_r\delta_c,
\qquad
\gamma_\epsilon=A_\epsilon j_qj_r-E_\epsilon j_r,
\]

\[
\mathcal L_\epsilon
 =A_\epsilon H_q\delta_u+(A_\epsilon j_q-E_\epsilon)h_r\delta_c.
\]

Write \(X\) for the six-field source. For a homogeneous map \(f:P\to Q\), the Hom differential is \(\partial f=d_Q f-(-1)^{|f|}f d_P\). Thus the contact cocycle equation is \(\partial\delta_u=d_N\delta_u+\delta_u d_X=0\). The projections and sections are degree-zero cochain maps, so \(\partial\delta_c=g_q\partial\delta_u=0\) and \(R=A_\epsilon j_q-E_\epsilon\) is closed. For a degree-zero evaluation \(B\), a degree-minus-one homotopy \(h\), and a degree-one contact \(\delta\), the graded product rule gives
\[
\partial(Bh\delta)
 =(\partial B)h\delta+B(\partial h)\delta-Bh(\partial\delta).
\]
For the two summands of \(\mathcal L_\epsilon\), the first and last terms vanish by closedness. The middle terms are \(A_\epsilon(1-j_qg_q)\delta_u\) and \(R(1-j_rg_r)\delta_c\). Adding \(\gamma_\epsilon g_r\delta_c=Rj_rg_r\delta_c\) gives the difference of the two compact evaluations on their contacts. If contact terms cancel by a prescribed homotopy, its boundary must account for the surviving terms of the product rule. [*Reducing the compact discrepancy to its actual supported marking*, §1, (S.1)–(S.8)](#ref-ch09-14).

Both summands of \(\mathcal L_\epsilon\) have degree zero. On any input \(x\in X\), its boundary therefore has value \(d_{\mathcal Y}\mathcal L_\epsilon(x)-\mathcal L_\epsilon(d_Xx)\). The remaining calculation evaluates \(\gamma_\epsilon\colon\mathscr G\to\mathcal Y\) on the states \(g_r\delta_c(x)\in\mathscr G\). A trace on \(\mathcal Y\) can annihilate a nonzero current \(\gamma_\epsilon(g_r\delta_c(x))\).

Finite polynomial Green-boundary limits and their four-supported compositions are available with fixed finite polynomial and jet degrees, the original order of width limits and all source-contact terms. Selected scalar unit and section rows also have vanishing objectwise first-comparison values. That scalar sector is not a subcomplex: its coefficient differential produces antiholomorphic descendants. Those descendants remain in the Hom identities.

One descendant can be measured before tracing. After taking the first contact in the normal \(U\), use \(V\) for the new two-dimensional complex normal. Put \(V=(V_1,V_2)\), \(r^2=|V_1|^2+|V_2|^2\), and normalize the one-edge form with \(C=(4\pi^2)^{-1}\) by
\[
\eta=C\,\frac{\bar V_1d\bar V_2-\bar V_2d\bar V_1}{r^4},\qquad
s_j=\frac{\bar V_j}{r^2}.
\]
Then \(\bar\partial(-s_1)=V_2\eta/C\) and \(\bar\partial(-s_2)=-V_1\eta/C\), distributionally also at the origin. The boundary integral on a small three-sphere is \(O(r^2)\). In the zero-jet contact row, projecting the selected \(q\)-normal first sends this coefficient descendant to \(+\zeta_j(c_Vc_x)/2\) in each of the two specified \(q\)-first orders. Here \(c_V,c_x\) are the point currents of the two independent normal factors and \(\zeta_j\) is the first outgoing \(q\)-delta derivative in direction \(j\). The coefficient follows from \(V_i\partial_{V_k}c_V=-\delta_{ik}c_V\). Projecting the graph normals first gives zero in the specified graph-first order. Testing with a compact function that agrees near zero with the \(j\)-th linear \(q\)-coordinate detects its delta derivative. A test constant near zero annihilates it. This distinguishes the prescribed projection orders on a concrete input. Computing the compact marking on that input still requires \(A_\epsilon\).

The cylinder map from the canonical normal complex to the original normal complex is a separate required arrow, especially on unsupported homotopy outputs. A coordinate isomorphism of finite normal currents does not define it. A cubic compatibility for an operation \(\Omega=\sum_i\hbar^i\Omega_i\) and map \(P=\sum_j\hbar^jP_j\) retains the entire coefficient

\[
\Omega_0P_3+\Omega_1P_2+\Omega_2P_1+\Omega_3P_0.
\]

For this elliptic operation \(\Omega_3=0\). Even so, both \(\Omega_1P_2\) and \(\Omega_2P_1\) contribute beside \(\Omega_0P_3\). The same multiplication of series applies to the homotopy equation, where the homotopies at orders zero, one and two have already been fixed. If the Hom differential is \(\partial_0+\hbar\partial_1+\cdots\), correcting lower coefficients \(S_0,S_1,S_2\) contributes

\[
-\partial_1S_2-\partial_2S_1-\partial_3S_0
\]

at order \(\hbar^3\). A scalar test may annihilate the discrepancy. The coefficient of \(\hbar^3\) in the Hom equation still contains the three displayed terms from the prescribed lower homotopies. To extend the comparison through order \(\hbar^3\), its remaining discrepancy must be a Hom boundary with those three lower coefficients fixed. The remaining question is an equality of specified maps with their supported domains and lower homotopies.

On a punctured sphere cut between two pairs of fields. The circle carries intermediate states, with a representation assigned to each possible momentum. At a fixed momentum the sum over its energy levels must give a state on which the next three-point amplitude can act. To compare two choices of cut we must also know which functions of momentum can be paired with the same spectral tests. [Finite analytic sewings](10-analytic-sewing.md#ch10-finite-uncut-sewings).

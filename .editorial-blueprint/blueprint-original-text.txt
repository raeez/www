012 h1: Executive Summary
013 p: This document presents a comprehensive blueprint for establishing the Laboratory for Incentivization, Coordination and Cooperation (LICC) as a jointly-sponsored research institution between the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and the Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI). LICC will pioneer the emerging field of coordination science—the mathematical and computational study of how economic agents, institutions, and jurisdictions can be designed to achieve optimal collective outcomes through programmable incentive mechanisms.
014 h2: The Opportunity
015 p: The convergence of cryptographic primitives, programmable compliance infrastructure, and the emergence of Special Economic Zones as innovation laboratories has created an unprecedented opportunity to establish a new academic discipline. Current institutional arrangements—from international trade to jurisdictional governance—operate on frameworks designed for a pre-digital era. LICC will develop the theoretical foundations and practical tools to design "programmable institutions" that can operate at software speed while maintaining the legitimacy and stability of traditional governance structures.
016 h2: Why MIT-MBZUAI?
017 p: This dual-sponsorship model uniquely combines MIT's world-class theoretical expertise in mechanism design, institutional economics, and network science with MBZUAI's applied AI infrastructure and direct pathways to government implementation through UAE's 27 free zones. The recently announced MIT-MBZUAI Collaborative Research Program (October 2025) provides an existing institutional framework that can be expanded to accommodate LICC's coordination science mandate.

033 h1: Part I: Institutional Context and Strategic Rationale
034 h2: 1.1 What is LICC?
035 p: The Laboratory for Incentivization, Coordination and Cooperation (LICC) is a proposed interdisciplinary research institute dedicated to the mathematical, computational, and empirical study of coordination mechanisms across scales—from individual economic transactions to international institutional design. LICC's research agenda spans:
036 li: Cryptoeconomic Mechanism Design: Formal methods for designing incentive-compatible protocols that align individual rationality with collective welfare in decentralized systems
037 li: Spectral Coordination Theory: Mathematical frameworks using spectral graph theory and category theory to analyze multi-agent coordination dynamics
038 li: Jurisdictional Competition and Decentralization: Empirical and theoretical analysis of how regulatory competition shapes institutional evolution
039 li: Programmable Compliance Infrastructure: Technical architectures for "Smart Assets" that carry compliance, identity, and operational intelligence across jurisdictions
040 li: Multi-Scale Coordination Dynamics: Understanding how coordination mechanisms interact across individual, organizational, and societal scales
041 h2: 1.2 MIT: Institutional Profile and Strategic Assets
042 p: The Massachusetts Institute of Technology represents the world's premier institution for the theoretical foundations LICC requires. MIT's research enterprise spans $2.1 billion annually, with industry-sponsored research totaling $175 million from over 300 companies in fiscal year 2024—approximately 20% of total MIT research expenditures. According to the National Science Foundation, MIT consistently ranks first or second in industry-funded research and development expenditures among universities without a medical school.

118 h1: Part II: Dual-Sponsorship Model Analysis
119 h2: 2.1 Structural Options
120 p: Three primary structural configurations exist for establishing LICC within the MIT-MBZUAI framework. Each offers distinct advantages and constraints that must be evaluated against LICC's long-term research independence requirements.
121 h3: Option A: Expansion of Existing MIT-MBZUAI Collaboration
122 p: Mechanism: Propose LICC as an additional research theme within the existing MIT-MBZUAI Collaborative Research Program, positioning "Coordination Science" as a fourth pillar alongside scientific discovery, human thriving, and planetary health.
123 p: Advantages
124 p: Constraints
125 p: Uses existing infrastructure and governance mechanisms
126 p: Must convince current directors (Isola, Song) to expand scope beyond AI/ML focus
127 p: MBZUAI funding already committed under 5-year agreement
128 p: May be constrained by existing program priorities and steering committee composition
129 p: Steering committee pathway provides clear decision process
130 p: Limited independence for LICC-specific research agenda
131 p: Fastest path to initial research funding
132 p: Risk of being absorbed into broader AI research portfolio
133 p: Assessment: High resource access, low autonomy. Appropriate for bootstrap phase (Months 1-12) but insufficient for LICC's long-term independence requirements.
134 h3: Option B: Affiliated Independent Institute (RECOMMENDED)
135 p: Mechanism: Establish LICC as a legally distinct research institute with formal affiliation agreements with both MIT and MBZUAI. LICC maintains independent governance while leveraging institutional resources through partnership agreements.
136 p: Advantages
137 p: Constraints
138 p: Full research agenda autonomy protected by affiliation agreement
139 p: Must secure separate funding streams beyond MIT-MBZUAI program
140 p: Broader hiring flexibility across disciplines (economics, mathematics, law)
141 p: Less direct access to MBZUAI computing infrastructure
142 p: Multi-institutional partnerships enabled (Santa Fe Institute, ETH Zürich, etc.)
143 p: Requires independent governance structure and administration
144 p: Geopolitical insulation through neutral governance
145 p: Longer timeline to full operations (18-24 months)
146 p: Preserves LICC brand independently of either institution
147 p: More complex negotiation with both universities
148 p: Assessment: Optimal for LICC's interdisciplinary mandate and long-term independence. Recommended structure for mature LICC operations.
149 h3: Option C: Joint Venture with UAE Government
150 p: Mechanism: Establish LICC as a trilateral joint venture between MIT, MBZUAI, and UAE government entities (ADGM, Dubai Free Zone Council, or similar). LICC operates as a quasi-governmental research institution with direct regulatory pathway.
151 p: Advantages
152 p: Constraints
153 p: Direct regulatory pathway for implementation research
154 p: Complex governance structure with multiple stakeholders
155 p: Government funding mandate ensures long-term sustainability
156 p: Potential for political interference in research agenda
157 p: Living laboratory access through 27 UAE free zones
158 p: Slower decision-making due to government processes
159 p: Maximum legitimacy for policy-relevant research
160 p: UAE-specific focus may limit global research applicability
161 p: Assessment: Maximum implementation leverage, maximum complexity. Appropriate for Phase 2 after proof-of-concept through Options A or B.
162 h2: 2.2 Recommended Hybrid Strategy
163 p: The optimal path combines elements of all three options through a phased approach that prioritizes speed-to-launch while preserving long-term independence:
164 p: Phase 1 (Months 1-12): Bootstrap through Option A. Launch initial research projects within MIT-MBZUAI program framework. Build faculty relationships and demonstrate research value.
165 p: Phase 2 (Months 12-24): Transition to Option B. Establish LICC as affiliated independent institute while maintaining MIT-MBZUAI partnership. Secure independent funding and governance.
166 p: Phase 3 (Months 24-48): Selective Option C elements. Formalize government partnerships for implementation research. Expand to multi-jurisdictional scope beyond UAE.
167 h2: 2.3 Critical Gap Analysis
168 p: Understanding the complementary strengths and weaknesses of each institution is essential for designing an effective dual-sponsorship structure.
169 h3: What MIT Provides That MBZUAI Lacks
170 li: Domain Expertise in Mechanism Design: MBZUAI has virtually no faculty in game theory, mathematical economics, or auction theory. MIT's Daskalakis, Acemoglu, and affiliated researchers represent the global frontier.
171 li: Network Science and Social Physics: Pentland's Connection Science initiative has no MBZUAI equivalent. This is essential for understanding coordination dynamics at scale.
172 li: Institutional Economics: Acemoglu's Nobel-winning research on how institutions shape prosperity provides the empirical foundation LICC requires.
173 li: Cryptographic Foundations: Micali's Turing Award work on zero-knowledge proofs and verifiable computation underlies programmable compliance infrastructure.
174 li: Publication Prestige: MIT publications carry greater weight in economics journals (Econometrica, AER, QJE) than MBZUAI's ML-focused venues (NeurIPS, ICLR, CVPR).
175 h3: What MBZUAI Provides That MIT Lacks
176 li: Direct Government Channel: MBZUAI's board includes Sultan Ahmed Al Jaber (UAE Minister of Industry) and Khaldoon Al Mubarak (Mubadala CEO). MIT has no equivalent government access.
177 li: Implementation Laboratory: UAE's 27 free zones provide a living laboratory for testing coordination mechanisms. MIT research remains theoretical without such testing grounds.
178 li: Sovereign Wealth Funding: MBZUAI's state funding model provides long-term stability that MIT's grant-dependent research cannot match.
179 li: Applied AI Infrastructure: MBZUAI's Foundation Models Hub and computing infrastructure exceed what MIT can provide for applied coordination research.
180 li: Speed of Decision-Making: MBZUAI's young institution can move faster than MIT's established bureaucracy for new initiative approval.

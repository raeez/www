012 h1: Executive Summary
013 p: The Laboratory for Incentivization, Coordination and Cooperation (LICC) is proposed as a joint research institution. Its sponsors would be the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and the Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI). Coordination science studies how economic agents, institutions, and jurisdictions can be designed for optimal collective outcomes through programmable incentive mechanisms. LICC will develop this emerging field using mathematical and computational methods.
014 h2: The Opportunity
015 p: Cryptographic primitives, programmable compliance infrastructure, and Special Economic Zones used for experimentation now support the development of a new academic discipline. Institutions governing international trade and jurisdictions still use frameworks designed before the digital era. LICC will develop the theory and tools for "programmable institutions" that operate at software speed while preserving the legitimacy and stability of traditional governance.
016 h2: Why MIT-MBZUAI?
017 p: The dual-sponsorship model combines MIT's theoretical expertise in mechanism design, institutional economics, and network science with MBZUAI's applied AI infrastructure. MBZUAI also provides direct routes to government implementation through the UAE's 27 free zones. The MIT-MBZUAI Collaborative Research Program, announced in October 2025, provides an institutional framework that could expand to include LICC's coordination science mandate.
018 p: The recommended sequence begins with research inside the MIT-MBZUAI collaboration, then establishes LICC as an independently governed affiliated institute. Selected government partnerships would follow to support implementation research.

034 h1: Part I: Institutional Context and Rationale
035 h2: 1.1 What is LICC?
036 p: LICC is a proposed interdisciplinary research institute for the mathematical, computational, and empirical study of coordination mechanisms. Its scope ranges from individual economic transactions to international institutional design. The research agenda includes:
037 li: Cryptoeconomic Mechanism Design: Formal methods for designing incentive-compatible protocols that align individual rationality with collective welfare in decentralized systems
038 li: Spectral Coordination Theory: Mathematical frameworks using spectral graph theory and category theory to analyze multi-agent coordination dynamics
039 li: Jurisdictional Competition and Decentralization: Empirical and theoretical analysis of how regulatory competition shapes institutional evolution
040 li: Programmable Compliance Infrastructure: Technical architectures for "Smart Assets" that carry compliance, identity, and operational intelligence across jurisdictions
041 li: Multi-Scale Coordination Dynamics: Study of how coordination mechanisms interact across individual, organizational, and societal scales
042 p: The first programs would study UAE free zone federation coordination, Smart Asset compliance architecture, and jurisdictional competition. Appendix B specifies the work for Years 1-3.
043 h2: 1.2 MIT: Institutional Profile and Research Resources
044 p: MIT has expertise in the theoretical foundations LICC requires. MIT's research enterprise totals $2.1 billion annually. In fiscal year 2024, industry-sponsored research totaled $175 million from over 300 companies, approximately 20% of total MIT research expenditures. According to the National Science Foundation, MIT consistently ranks first or second in industry-funded research and development expenditures among universities without a medical school.

120 h2: 1.5 Complementary Strengths and Gaps
121 p: The dual-sponsorship structure must account for the expertise, resources, and access each institution provides or lacks.
122 h3: MIT's Contributions
123 li: Domain Expertise in Mechanism Design: MBZUAI has virtually no faculty in game theory, mathematical economics, or auction theory. MIT's Daskalakis, Acemoglu, and affiliated researchers lead research in these fields.
124 li: Network Science and Social Physics: Pentland's Connection Science initiative has no MBZUAI equivalent. Its work is essential to understanding coordination dynamics at scale.
125 li: Institutional Economics: Acemoglu's Nobel-winning research on how institutions shape prosperity provides the empirical foundation LICC requires.
126 li: Cryptographic Foundations: Micali's Turing Award work on zero-knowledge proofs and verifiable computation underlies programmable compliance infrastructure.
127 li: Publication Prestige: MIT publications carry greater weight in economics journals (Econometrica, AER, QJE) than MBZUAI's ML-focused venues (NeurIPS, ICLR, CVPR).
128 h3: MBZUAI's Contributions
129 li: Direct Government Channel: MBZUAI's board includes Sultan Ahmed Al Jaber (UAE Minister of Industry) and Khaldoon Al Mubarak (Mubadala CEO). MIT has no equivalent government access.
130 li: Implementation Laboratory: The UAE's 27 free zones provide sites for testing coordination mechanisms. MIT research remains theoretical without such testing grounds.
131 li: Sovereign Wealth Funding: MBZUAI's state funding model provides long-term stability that MIT's grant-dependent research cannot match.
132 li: Applied AI Infrastructure: MBZUAI's Foundation Models Hub and computing infrastructure exceed what MIT can provide for applied coordination research.
133 li: Speed of Decision-Making: As a young institution, MBZUAI can approve new initiatives faster than MIT's established bureaucracy.
134 h1: Part II: Dual-Sponsorship Model Analysis
135 h2: 2.1 Structural Options
136 p: Three structures could establish LICC within the MIT-MBZUAI framework. Each has advantages and constraints that must be assessed against LICC's long-term research independence requirements.
137 h3: Option A: Expansion of Existing MIT-MBZUAI Collaboration
138 p: Mechanism: Propose LICC as an additional research theme within the MIT-MBZUAI Collaborative Research Program. "Coordination Science" would become a fourth research area alongside scientific discovery, human thriving, and planetary health.
139 p: Advantages
140 p: Constraints
141 p: Uses existing infrastructure and governance mechanisms
142 p: Must convince current directors (Isola, Song) to expand scope beyond AI/ML focus
143 p: MBZUAI funding already committed under 5-year agreement
144 p: May be constrained by existing program priorities and steering committee composition
145 p: Steering committee provides a clear decision process
146 p: Limited independence for LICC-specific research agenda
147 p: Fastest path to initial research funding
148 p: Risk of being absorbed into broader AI research portfolio
149 p: Assessment: High access to resources, low autonomy. Suitable for the bootstrap phase (Months 1-12), but insufficient for LICC's long-term independence requirements.
150 h3: Option B: Affiliated Independent Institute (RECOMMENDED)
151 p: Mechanism: Establish LICC as a legally distinct research institute with formal affiliation agreements with MIT and MBZUAI. It would govern itself independently and access institutional resources through partnership agreements.
152 p: Advantages
153 p: Constraints
154 p: Full research agenda autonomy protected by affiliation agreement
155 p: Must secure separate funding streams beyond MIT-MBZUAI program
156 p: Broader hiring flexibility across disciplines (economics, mathematics, law)
157 p: Less direct access to MBZUAI computing infrastructure
158 p: Allows partnerships with other institutions (Santa Fe Institute, ETH Zürich, etc.)
159 p: Requires independent governance structure and administration
160 p: Geopolitical insulation through neutral governance
161 p: Longer timeline to full operations (18-24 months)
162 p: Preserves LICC brand independently of either institution
163 p: More complex negotiation with both universities
164 p: Assessment: Best suited to LICC's interdisciplinary mandate and long-term independence, and recommended for mature operations. Appendix C sets out the proposed governance.
165 h3: Option C: Joint Venture with UAE Government
166 p: Mechanism: Establish LICC as a trilateral joint venture between MIT, MBZUAI, and UAE government entities (ADGM, Dubai Free Zone Council, or similar). LICC would operate as a quasi-governmental research institution with a direct regulatory pathway.
167 p: Advantages
168 p: Constraints
169 p: Direct regulatory pathway for implementation research
170 p: Complex governance structure with multiple stakeholders
171 p: Government funding mandate ensures long-term sustainability
172 p: Potential for political interference in research agenda
173 p: Access to 27 UAE free zones for research and testing
174 p: Slower decision-making due to government processes
175 p: Maximum legitimacy for policy-relevant research
176 p: UAE-specific focus may limit global research applicability
177 p: Assessment: Greatest capacity to support implementation, with the most complex structure. Suitable for Phase 2 after proof-of-concept through Options A or B.
178 h2: 2.2 Recommended Hybrid Strategy
179 p: The recommended approach combines all three options in phases to launch quickly while preserving long-term independence:
180 p: Phase 1 (Months 1-12): Start through Option A. Launch initial research projects within the MIT-MBZUAI program to build faculty relationships and demonstrate research value.
181 p: Phase 2 (Months 12-24): Move to Option B. Establish LICC as an affiliated independent institute, maintain the MIT-MBZUAI partnership, and secure independent funding and governance.
182 p: Phase 3 (Months 24-48): Adopt selected elements of Option C. Formalize government partnerships for implementation research and expand research to jurisdictions beyond the UAE.
